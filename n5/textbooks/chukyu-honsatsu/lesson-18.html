第18課 — 地球に生きる (Chikyuu ni ikiru — Living on Earth)

Source
できる日本語 中級 本冊 (アルク), printed p.235–246 (PDF pages 235–246; the PDF page number is the same as the printed one)
Lesson goal
地球に生きる1人としてできることは何か考えて、日々の生活を見直すことができる。Chikyuu ni ikiru hitori to shite dekiru koto wa nani ka kangaete, hibi no seikatsu o minaosu koto ga dekiru. Think about what you can do as one person living on Earth, and rethink your daily life. (Built from the できる！ page, p.246.)

1 Lesson Goals (チャレンジ！ can-do list)

話してみよう (warm-up, p.235)

The opening picture puts the Earth in the middle, surrounded by environmental problems: a factory giving off 二酸化炭素 (nisanka tanso, carbon dioxide) and 大気汚染 (taiki osen, air pollution), cars with 排気ガス (haiki gasu, exhaust fumes), bags of rubbish for ごみ問題 (gomi mondai, the waste problem), a polar bear on melting ice for 地球温暖化 (chikyuu ondanka, global warming), trees disappearing for 森林の減少 (shinrin no genshou, loss of forests), 砂漠化 (sabakuka, desertification), 野生生物の減少 (yasei seibutsu no genshou, decline of wildlife) and dead fish for 海洋汚染 (kaiyou osen, ocean pollution). Talk about: which of these problems affect your country most?

  • 1. ニュースを聞いて、身近な環境問題とその対策を知ることができる。Nyuusu o kiite, mijika na kankyou mondai to sono taisaku o shiru koto ga dekiru. I can listen to the news and learn about everyday environmental problems and what is being done about them.
    HI: मैं समाचार सुनकर रोज़मर्रा की पर्यावरण समस्याओं और उनके उपायों के बारे में जान सकता हूँ। GU: હું સમાચાર સાંભળીને રોજબરોજની પર્યાવરણ સમસ્યાઓ અને તેના ઉપાયો વિશે જાણી શકું છું.
  • 2. 不要になった物を処分する際、希望する人にわかりやすく物の説明をして、譲ることができる。Fuyou ni natta mono o shobun suru sai, kibou suru hito ni wakariyasuku mono no setsumei o shite, yuzuru koto ga dekiru. When I get rid of something I no longer need, I can describe it clearly to someone who wants it and hand it on.
    HI: जब मैं बेकार हुई चीज़ हटाता हूँ, तो उसे चाहने वाले को साफ़-साफ़ समझाकर दे सकता हूँ। GU: જ્યારે હું બિનજરૂરી વસ્તુ કાઢું છું, ત્યારે તે ઇચ્છતી વ્યક્તિને સ્પષ્ટ સમજાવીને આપી શકું છું.
  • 3. 環境の変化に影響を受けている動植物の問題について書かれた記事を読んで、理解することができる。Kankyou no henka ni eikyou o ukete iru doushokubutsu no mondai ni tsuite kakareta kiji o yonde, rikai suru koto ga dekiru. I can read and understand an article about plants and animals affected by environmental change.
    HI: मैं पर्यावरण बदलाव से प्रभावित पौधों और जानवरों की समस्या पर लिखा लेख पढ़कर समझ सकता हूँ। GU: હું પર્યાવરણના ફેરફારથી અસર પામતા છોડ અને પ્રાણીઓની સમસ્યા વિશેનો લેખ વાંચીને સમજી શકું છું.
  • 4. 自分の周りの守りたい自然について述べることができる。Jibun no mawari no mamoritai shizen ni tsuite noberu koto ga dekiru. I can talk about the nature around me that I want to protect.
    HI: मैं अपने आसपास की उस प्रकृति के बारे में बता सकता हूँ जिसे मैं बचाना चाहता हूँ। GU: હું મારી આસપાસની એ પ્રકૃતિ વિશે કહી શકું છું જેને હું બચાવવા માંગું છું.

Task 1 · 耳でキャッチ (Ⓑ32, Ⓑ33)

テレビでエコライフフェアの様子が放送されていました。Terebi de eko raifu fea no yousu ga housou sarete imashita.

Setting: you are watching TV and see a report from an "Eco-Life Fair". The pictures show plants climbing up a balcony and big green bitter gourds (ゴーヤ, gooya). The audio is the interview printed as 使ってみよう 1 (summarised in section 3).
EN: On 5 June, World Environment Day, a reporter talks to an environmental adviser at a fair in a park. Task 3 · 見つけた！

Material (summary of the panel, p.237)

私たちはトキについて調べましたWatashitachi wa toki ni tsuite shirabemashita

Setting: at Sakura University's festival, the wildlife research club has a panel about the トキ (toki, the crested ibis, scientific name Nipponia nippon).
EN: The ibis used to live in 里山 (satoyama), the managed hills and fields near villages all over Japan. People grew rice and vegetables and looked after the woods, so there was plenty of food and nesting space. But the birds trampled rice plants looking for food, so they were treated as pests. They lived everywhere until the end of the Edo period. In the Meiji era, hunting for their beautiful feathers boomed and they were over-hunted. Later, as industrialisation spread, rice fields disappeared, and nature destruction and pollution cut their numbers further, until they survived only on the Noto Peninsula (Ishikawa) and Sado Island (Niigata). In 1981 the last five wild birds on Sado were taken into protection, so wild ibis vanished from Japan's skies. The club concludes that humans put their own lives first, took the ibis's habitat, and pushed it to the edge of extinction, and that once a species is extinct nothing can be done, so we must act before that.

8 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

FormMeaningConnectionKey differenceUse when
〜上にue ni
vs 〜上でue de		besides / after普通形＋上に／V-た＋上で上に = adds a second fact of the same kind; 上で (Lesson 1) = after doing X, then Y.安い上に新鮮 (and also) · 確認した上で (after checking).
〜(よ)うにも〜ないyou ni mo nai
vs 〜ようがないyou ga nai		can'tV-意向形＋にも／V-マス形＋ようがないうにも〜ない = I want to but circumstances stop me; ようがない = there's no method at all.帰ろうにも帰れない · 連絡のしようがない.
〜あげくageku
vs 〜た結果ta kekka		after / as a resultV-た＋あげく／結果あげく = long, troublesome process with a bad end; 結果 = neutral result.Complaining → あげく. Reporting → 結果.
〜わけがないwake ga nai
vs 〜に違いないni chigainai		impossible / must be普通形＋わけがない／に違いないOpposites: わけがない = "can't be"; に違いない = "must be". Both are the speaker's strong judgement.飛ぶわけがない ↔ 何かあったに違いない.
〜向きmuki
vs 〜向けmuke (added)		suited to / aimed atN＋向き／N＋向け向き = happens to suit; 向け = designed for that group.学生向きの自転車 · 子ども向けの番組.
Watch out: 〜つつ(も) and 〜にほかならない are written forms. In a speech or essay (Task 4, the essay contest) they sound good; in a chat with a friend use のに／けど and まさに〜だ／〜だからだ instead. And remember 〜っぱなし almost always carries criticism: 水を出しっぱなし is "you left the water running (and you shouldn't have)".
