1 Lesson Goals (チャレンジ！ can-do list)

話してみよう (warm-up, p.183)

The opening page shows the results of a survey of international students (日本語学校 中級・上級クラスアンケート): 日本に来て疑問に思ったり、驚いたりしたこと (Nihon ni kite gimon ni omottari, odoroitari shita koto, "things that puzzled or surprised us after coming to Japan"). In shops: people eating alone; saying ごちそうさま even after paying; always splitting the bill (割り勘). In town: office workers all dressed alike; lots of vending machines. On TV: the same news repeated all day; many scenes of people eating. On transport: quiet trains; people doing make-up on the train; too many announcements. Other: people don't say no clearly; they say すみません straight away. Talk about: what surprised you in Japan (or what would)?
HI: सर्वे में विदेशी छात्रों ने बताया कि जापान में उन्हें क्या अजीब लगा: अकेले खाना, पैसे देकर भी "गोचिसोसामा" कहना, बिल बाँटना, एक जैसे कपड़े, बहुत सारी वेंडिंग मशीनें, ट्रेन में चुप्पी और मेकअप, साफ़ "ना" न कहना, तुरंत "सुमिमासेन" कहना। GU: સર્વેમાં વિદેશી વિદ્યાર્થીઓએ કહ્યું કે જાપાનમાં તેમને શું નવાઈભર્યું લાગ્યું: એકલા જમવું, પૈસા આપીને પણ "ગોચિસોસામા" કહેવું, બિલ વહેંચવું, એકસરખાં કપડાં, ઘણાં વેન્ડિંગ મશીન, ટ્રેનમાં શાંતિ અને મેકઅપ, સ્પષ્ટ "ના" ન કહેવું, તરત "સુમિમાસેન" કહેવું.

  • 1. 投書を読んで、自分と異なる習慣などについて理解することができる。Tousho o yonde, jibun to kotonaru shuukan nado ni tsuite rikai suru koto ga dekiru. I can read a letter to a newspaper and understand customs that differ from mine.
    HI: मैं अख़बार में छपा पाठक-पत्र पढ़कर अपने से अलग रीति-रिवाज़ समझ सकता हूँ। GU: હું છાપામાં છપાયેલો વાચક-પત્ર વાંચીને મારાથી જુદા રિવાજો સમજી શકું છું.
  • 2. 客からの無理な注文に対して、それができない事情を説明し、丁寧に対応することができる。Kyaku kara no muri na chuumon ni taishite, sore ga dekinai jijou o setsumei shi, teinei ni taiou suru koto ga dekiru. I can respond politely to an impossible request from a customer, explaining why it can't be done.
    HI: ग्राहक की असंभव माँग पर मैं विनम्रता से कारण बताकर जवाब दे सकता हूँ। GU: ગ્રાહકની અશક્ય માંગ સામે હું નમ્રતાથી કારણ સમજાવીને જવાબ આપી શકું છું.
  • 3. 異文化適応についての解説を聞き、グラフを見ながら心の変化について理解することができる。Ibunka tekiou ni tsuite no kaisetsu o kiki, gurafu o minagara kokoro no henka ni tsuite rikai suru koto ga dekiru. I can listen to an explanation of cross-cultural adjustment and follow the emotional changes on a graph.
    HI: मैं विदेशी संस्कृति में ढलने पर व्याख्या सुनकर, ग्राफ़ देखते हुए मन के बदलाव समझ सकता हूँ। GU: હું વિદેશી સંસ્કૃતિમાં ગોઠવાવા વિશેનું સમજૂતી સાંભળીને, ગ્રાફ જોતાં મનના ફેરફાર સમજી શકું છું.
  • 4. 自分の国と日本との違いについて様子や状況を少し詳しく描写することができる。Jibun no kuni to Nihon to no chigai ni tsuite yousu ya joukyou o sukoshi kuwashiku byousha suru koto ga dekiru. I can describe differences between my country and Japan in some detail.
    HI: मैं अपने देश और जापान के अंतर को थोड़ा विस्तार से बयान कर सकता हूँ। GU: હું મારા દેશ અને જાપાન વચ્ચેના તફાવતને થોડી વિગતે વર્ણવી શકું છું.

Task 1 · 見つけた！

Material (summary of two newspaper letters, p.184)

自分がしていないことも謝る日本人 ／「すみません」から思うことJibun ga shite inai koto mo ayamaru nihonjin / "Sumimasen" kara omou koto

Setting: reading the paper, you find a letter from an international student who thinks the same way you do; the following week a reply appears.
EN: Letter 1 is from スターニャ (Sutaanya), 22, from Thailand. She works part-time at a convenience store while studying for university entrance, and says the job teaches her not only Japanese but Japanese customs and ways of thinking. One day a customer's basket knocked a shelf and goods fell. Her senior rushed over saying "すみません" and started picking them up. She finds it strange that someone apologises for something the customer did, and asks why Japanese people apologise even for things they didn't do. Letter 2 is from 中川美智子 (Nakagawa Michiko), 48, a housewife in Tokyo. The same day she had a similar experience in a clothes shop: a blouse slid off its hanger onto the floor, and a clerk ran over, said "すみません" and put it back. She felt it was a pleasant shop that cares about its customers, and thinks the convenience-store clerk also said すみません out of valuing the customer.
HI: पहला पत्र थाईलैंड की 22 साल की स्तान्या का है। वह कॉन्बिनी में पार्ट-टाइम काम करती है और कहती है कि इससे जापानी भाषा के साथ जापानी आदतें भी समझ आती हैं। एक ग्राहक की टोकरी से सामान गिरा, तो उसकी सीनियर "सुमिमासेन" कहकर उठाने लगी। उसे अजीब लगा कि जो ग़लती अपनी नहीं, उसके लिए भी माफ़ी क्यों। दूसरा पत्र टोक्यो की 48 साल की गृहिणी नाकागावा मिचिको का है: कपड़ों की दुकान में ब्लाउज़ ज़मीन पर गिरा तो कर्मचारी ने "सुमिमासेन" कहकर उसे ठीक किया। उन्हें लगा यह ग्राहक का ख़याल रखने वाली दुकान है, और कॉन्बिनी वाले ने भी ग्राहक को अहमियत देने के भाव से "सुमिमासेन" कहा होगा।
GU: પહેલો પત્ર થાઇલેન્ડની 22 વર્ષની સ્તાન્યાનો છે. તે કોન્બિનીમાં પાર્ટ-ટાઇમ કામ કરે છે અને કહે છે કે તેનાથી જાપાની ભાષા સાથે જાપાની આદતો પણ સમજાય છે. એક ગ્રાહકની ટોપલીથી સામાન પડ્યો, તો તેની સિનિયર "સુમિમાસેન" કહીને ઉપાડવા લાગી. તેને નવાઈ લાગી કે પોતાની ભૂલ ન હોય તો પણ માફી કેમ. બીજો પત્ર ટોક્યોની 48 વર્ષની ગૃહિણી નાકાગાવા મિચિકોનો છે: કપડાંની દુકાનમાં બ્લાઉઝ નીચે પડ્યો તો કર્મચારીએ "સુમિમાસેન" કહીને પાછો મૂક્યો. તેમને લાગ્યું કે આ ગ્રાહકની કાળજી રાખતી દુકાન છે, અને કોન્બિનીવાળાએ પણ ગ્રાહકને મહત્ત્વ આપવાના ભાવથી "સુમિમાસેન" કહ્યું હશે.

使ってみよう 3: the culture-shock curve (Ⓑ16, p.189)

Model dialogue (summary)

An アナウンサー (anaunsaa, announcer) asks the expert 長井 (Nagai) why people who move abroad first find everything fun and then feel uneasy. Nagai shows the U-curve of cross-cultural adjustment, using the example of a Japanese person studying in China. For about the first three months people are excited and see the culture and people very clearly: most students want to learn everything, so it's a happy, active time, the "honeymoon" (point ①). Then, as they get used to life, instead of enjoying it they get depressed, because they see things they can't understand or accept: this is culture shock. After a while, with some trigger, their mood rises again, and it rises further as going home approaches (point ③). Differences exist, but this can happen to anyone, and not only when entering a new environment.

江戸しぐさに学ぶEdo shigusa ni manabu (Learning from Edo manners)

EN: When someone walks towards you on a crowded street, what do you do? In the Edo period (1603–1867) the custom was to turn your shoulder back so neither of you had to stop, so as not to waste the other person's time. About 400 years ago Edo (today's Tokyo) packed huge numbers of people into a much smaller area than modern Tokyo; it is said to have been more populous than Paris or London, the biggest city in the world. People came from all over Japan, so strangers with different cultures and lifestyles had to live side by side, and they developed everyday wisdom to avoid trouble: these are 江戸しぐさ (Edo manners). Examples: 傘かしげ (kasa kashige), tilting your umbrella outward for a moment when passing someone so raindrops don't fall on either of you; 七三歩き (shichisan aruki), splitting the road 3:7 and walking in the 3 at the edge, leaving the 7 for people in a hurry, people carrying goods or the sick, which began with giving way to 飛脚 (couriers); and うかつあやまり (ukatsu ayamari), apologising on the spot for any trouble caused by carelessness. If someone steps on your foot, instead of getting angry, the Edo way is for the one stepped on to say "sorry, I was careless to have my foot out". These ideas are now used in school education and company training: the wisdom of Edo people who tried to keep relationships smooth lives on today.
HI: भीड़ वाली सड़क पर कोई सामने से आए तो क्या करेंगे? एदो काल (1603–1867) में कंधा पीछे करके चलना शिष्टाचार था, ताकि कोई रुके नहीं और दूसरे का समय बर्बाद न हो। 400 साल पहले का एदो (आज का टोक्यो) आज से कहीं छोटी जगह में बहुत घनी आबादी वाला, दुनिया का सबसे बड़ा शहर माना जाता है। देश भर से अलग-अलग संस्कृति के लोग आए, इसलिए झगड़े से बचने की समझ बनी: "एदो शिगुसा"। उदाहरण: "कासा काशिगे" — रास्ते में मिलते समय छाता बाहर की ओर झुकाना; "शिचिसान आरुकी" — सड़क को 3:7 में बाँटकर किनारे के 3 हिस्से में चलना, बाकी जल्दी वालों, सामान ढोने वालों, बीमारों के लिए; "उकात्सु आयामारी" — लापरवाही के लिए तुरंत माफ़ी, यहाँ तक कि जिसका पैर दबा वह भी कहे "मेरी भी ग़लती थी"। आज ये विचार स्कूल और कंपनी प्रशिक्षण में इस्तेमाल होते हैं।

8 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

FormMeaningConnectionKey differenceUse when
〜ばかりでなくbakari de naku
vs 〜どころかdokoro ka		not only / far from普通形＋ばかりでなく／どころかばかりでなく adds a second, similar point; どころか rejects the first point and gives the opposite or a far stronger one.速いばかりか安全 (both true) · 早く着くどころか遅刻 (the opposite).
〜(か)と思うとka to omou to
vs 〜たとたんta totan		right afterV-た＋かと思うと／とたんBoth mean "as soon as". かと思うと describes something observed in others; たとたん can be your own action and stresses surprise.Watching a scene → かと思うと. "The moment I did X, Y happened" → たとたん.
〜によってni yotte
vs 〜によってはni yotte wa		depending onN＋によって(は)によって = varies across the whole group; によっては = in some cases (〜こともある).人によって違う · 人によってはアレルギーが出る.
〜からkara
vs 〜からにはkara ni wa		because / since普通形＋から(に)はから gives any reason; からには leads to a duty, resolve or natural conclusion.給料をもらうからには責任を持つべきだ.
〜てからte kara
vs 〜て以来te irai		after / sinceV-て＋から／以来てから = one-off order of events; て以来 = a state continuing from then until now.一人暮らしを始めて以来ずっと自炊している.
〜げge
vs 〜そうsou (added)		looks ~イA／ナA＋げ／そうBoth describe appearance. げ is literary and about a person's mood; そう is everyday and also predicts (雨が降りそう).Written description of feelings → げ. Conversation → そう.
Watch out: this lesson has two なんて. 謝るなんて不思議だ ("apologising, of all things…") puts なんて after the thing you find odd. なんてきれいなんだろう ("how beautiful!") puts なんて first and ends with んだろう. Also, 〜得る reads うる or える, but the negative is always 得ない (えない).
