1 Lesson Goals (チャレンジ！ can-do list)

話してみよう (warm-up, p.261)

Six people describe small moments of happiness in speech bubbles: getting a free piece of fried chicken (唐揚げ, karaage) at the convenience store makes you feel you gained something; when your make-up or hairstyle is just right, you feel today will be a good day; when the supermarket checkout queue moves fast, you feel lucky; "having money does give peace of mind"; getting lots of comments on your blog makes you happy to feel connected to everyone; and 「休みの日ソファでコーヒーを飲みながら、お気に入りの本を読むのって、幸せ。」(Yasumi no hi sofa de koohii o nominagara, o-ki-ni-iri no hon o yomu no tte, shiawase.) "Reading a favourite book on the sofa with a coffee on a day off: that's happiness." Talk about: when do you feel happy?

HI: छह लोग छोटी-छोटी ख़ुशियाँ बताते हैं: मुफ़्त चिकन मिलना, बाल ठीक बनना, लाइन जल्दी चलना, पैसे से निश्चिंतता, ब्लॉग पर कमेंट, छुट्टी के दिन कॉफ़ी के साथ पसंदीदा किताब। आप कब ख़ुश होते हैं? GU: છ લોકો નાની નાની ખુશીઓ કહે છે: મફત ચિકન મળવું, વાળ બરાબર બનવા, લાઇન ઝડપથી ચાલવી, પૈસાથી નિશ્ચિંતતા, બ્લૉગ પર કમેન્ટ, રજાના દિવસે કૉફી સાથે મનગમતી ચોપડી. તમે ક્યારે ખુશ થાઓ છો?

  • 1. 自分の価値観を伝えるために、あると幸せだと思うものについて優先順位を考えながら話すことができる。Jibun no kachikan o tsutaeru tame ni, aru to shiawase da to omou mono ni tsuite yuusen jun'i o kangaenagara hanasu koto ga dekiru. To show my values, I can talk about the things that make me happy, putting them in order of priority.
    HI: अपने मूल्य बताने के लिए, मैं उन चीज़ों के बारे में प्राथमिकता के क्रम से बोल सकता हूँ जिनके होने से मैं ख़ुश रहता हूँ। GU: મારાં મૂલ્યો જણાવવા, જે વસ્તુઓ હોવાથી હું ખુશ રહું છું તેના વિશે પ્રાથમિકતાના ક્રમે બોલી શકું છું.
  • 2. 雑誌の記事を読んで、幸せを数値化することについて考えるきっかけを得ることができる。Zasshi no kiji o yonde, shiawase o suuchika suru koto ni tsuite kangaeru kikkake o eru koto ga dekiru. I can read a magazine article and get a starting point for thinking about putting happiness into numbers.
    HI: मैं पत्रिका का लेख पढ़कर ख़ुशी को अंकों में मापने के बारे में सोचने की शुरुआत कर सकता हूँ। GU: હું મેગેઝિનનો લેખ વાંચીને ખુશીને આંકડામાં માપવા વિશે વિચારવાની શરૂઆત કરી શકું છું.
  • 3. 経営者へのインタビューを聞いて、その理念を知ることができる。Keieisha e no intabyuu o kiite, sono rinen o shiru koto ga dekiru. I can listen to an interview with a company head and learn his management philosophy.
    HI: मैं किसी कंपनी प्रमुख का इंटरव्यू सुनकर उसका प्रबंधन-दर्शन जान सकता हूँ। GU: હું કંપનીના વડાનો ઇન્ટરવ્યૂ સાંભળીને તેમનું સંચાલન-દર્શન જાણી શકું છું.
  • 4. 人物紹介の記事を読んで、その人の決意や行動を理解することができる。Jinbutsu shoukai no kiji o yonde, sono hito no ketsui ya koudou o rikai suru koto ga dekiru. I can read a profile article and understand the person's resolve and actions.
    HI: मैं किसी व्यक्ति-परिचय का लेख पढ़कर उसके संकल्प और कामों को समझ सकता हूँ। GU: હું વ્યક્તિ-પરિચયનો લેખ વાંચીને તેના સંકલ્પ અને કાર્યો સમજી શકું છું.
  • 5. 団体で店を利用するときに、幹事として希望を伝えて予約することができる。Dantai de mise o riyou suru toki ni, kanji to shite kibou o tsutaete yoyaku suru koto ga dekiru. When a group uses a restaurant, I can act as organiser, explain what we want and make a booking.
    HI: समूह में किसी रेस्तराँ जाते समय, आयोजक बनकर अपनी इच्छाएँ बताकर बुकिंग कर सकता हूँ। GU: જૂથમાં કોઈ રેસ્ટોરાં જતી વખતે, આયોજક બનીને અમારી ઇચ્છાઓ જણાવીને બુકિંગ કરી શકું છું.

Task 1 · 伝えてみよう (Ⓑ39)

これがあると幸せだと思うものについて、五つぐらい挙げてその理由とともに話してください。Kore ga aru to shiawase da to omou mono ni tsuite, itsutsu gurai agete sono riyuu to tomo ni hanashite kudasai.

EN: Name about five things that you think make you happy when you have them, and talk about them with your reasons. The book gives a numbered list 1–5 to fill in, and a thought bubble of ideas: 友達, 仕事, 安全, お金, 家族, 時間, 情報, 健康, 夢, ペット (tomodachi, shigoto, anzen, okane, kazoku, jikan, jouhou, kenkou, yume, petto): friends, work, safety, money, family, time, information, health, dreams, pets.
HI: ऐसी लगभग पाँच चीज़ें बताइए जिनके होने से आप ख़ुश होते हैं, और कारण के साथ बोलिए। सुझाव: दोस्त, काम, सुरक्षा, पैसा, परिवार, समय, जानकारी, स्वास्थ्य, सपने, पालतू जानवर।
GU: એવી લગભગ પાંચ વસ્તુઓ જણાવો જે હોવાથી તમે ખુશ થાઓ છો, અને કારણ સાથે બોલો. સૂચનો: મિત્રો, કામ, સલામતી, પૈસા, કુટુંબ, સમય, માહિતી, આરોગ્ય, સપનાં, પાળેલાં પ્રાણી.

(our answer, not in book) 私にとって何よりも大切なのは家族です。家族がいてこそ、今の私があります。次は健康です。健康あっての仕事ですから。3番目は時間で、好きなことをする時間ほど幸せなものはありません。4番目は友達、5番目はお金です。お金はあるにこしたことはありませんが、たくさんあれば幸せだというものでもないと思います。Watashi ni totte nani yori mo taisetsu na no wa kazoku desu. Kazoku ga ite koso, ima no watashi ga arimasu. Tsugi wa kenkou desu. Kenkou atte no shigoto desu kara. Sanbanme wa jikan de, suki na koto o suru jikan hodo shiawase na mono wa arimasen. Yonbanme wa tomodachi, gobanme wa okane desu. Okane wa aru ni koshita koto wa arimasen ga, takusan areba shiawase da to iu mono de mo nai to omoimasu.

EN: The most important thing to me is my family. It is because I have them that I am who I am. Next is health, because work depends on health. Third is time: nothing makes me as happy as time to do what I like. Fourth is friends and fifth is money. It's better to have money, but I don't think having a lot automatically makes you happy.
HI: मेरे लिए सबसे ज़रूरी परिवार है; उन्हीं की वजह से मैं आज ऐसा हूँ। फिर स्वास्थ्य, क्योंकि काम स्वास्थ्य पर ही टिका है। तीसरा समय: पसंद का काम करने के समय जितनी ख़ुशी कुछ नहीं देता। चौथा दोस्त, पाँचवाँ पैसा। पैसा हो तो अच्छा है, पर बहुत पैसा होने से ही कोई ख़ुश नहीं हो जाता।
GU: મારા માટે સૌથી મહત્ત્વનું કુટુંબ છે; તેમને કારણે જ હું આજે આવો છું. પછી આરોગ્ય, કારણ કે કામ આરોગ્ય પર જ ટકે છે. ત્રીજું સમય: ગમતું કામ કરવાના સમય જેટલી ખુશી બીજું કંઈ નથી આપતું. ચોથું મિત્રો, પાંચમું પૈસા. પૈસા હોય તો સારું, પણ ઘણા પૈસા હોવાથી જ કોઈ ખુશ થઈ જતું નથી.

Task 2 · 見つけた！

Material (summary of the magazine article, p.262–263)

幸せは測れるのかShiawase wa hakareru no ka

Setting: you see a magazine article titled "Can happiness be measured?"
EN: If a survey asked you to rate your happiness from 0 to 10, some people would give 10 because they can do what they want, and others 2 because they're lonely without a partner. Turning such a subjective feeling into numbers is very hard, yet it is already being done and used in community- and nation-building; these are called 幸福度調査 (koufukudo chousa, happiness surveys) and are carried out around the world. There are two kinds of measure: objective ones based on data such as income and life expectancy, and subjective ones based on surveys; recently, measures combining both are increasing. On the World Map of Happiness, Japan ranked only 90th of 178 countries in 2006: high on data such as income and life expectancy, but low on psychological satisfaction. The writer suggests Japan has focused so much on economic growth that it has not looked at satisfaction in daily life. Now Japan is trying to measure happiness its own way; the Cabinet Office says it is meaningful to focus on "how each individual feels as they live", which policy has not looked at before. Some say measuring something as personal and vague as happiness is pointless, but the Great East Japan Earthquake of March 2011 made many people realise that happiness is not just an individual matter but is tied to many social factors. Thinking about happiness may help us see a better future. (A footnote explains the World Map of Happiness, made by social psychologist Adrian White at the University of Leicester.)
HI: अगर सर्वे में ख़ुशी को 0 से 10 अंक देने हों, तो कोई 10 देगा, कोई 2। ख़ुशी जैसी व्यक्तिगत भावना को अंकों में बदलना मुश्किल है, फिर भी दुनिया भर में "ख़ुशी सर्वेक्षण" होते हैं। मापने के दो तरीक़े हैं: आय और औसत आयु जैसे आँकड़ों पर आधारित वस्तुनिष्ठ, और सर्वे पर आधारित व्यक्तिनिष्ठ; अब दोनों को मिलाने वाले बढ़ रहे हैं। 2006 में जापान 178 देशों में 90वें स्थान पर था: आँकड़े ऊँचे, पर मन की संतुष्टि कम। लेखक के अनुसार जापान ने आर्थिक विकास पर इतना ज़ोर दिया कि रोज़मर्रा की संतुष्टि पर ध्यान नहीं दिया। अब जापान अपने तरीक़े से ख़ुशी मापना चाहता है। 2011 के भूकंप के बाद लोगों ने समझा कि ख़ुशी सिर्फ़ निजी मामला नहीं, समाज से जुड़ी है।
GU: જો સર્વેમાં ખુશીને 0 થી 10 અંક આપવાના હોય, તો કોઈ 10 આપે, કોઈ 2. ખુશી જેવી અંગત લાગણીને આંકડામાં ફેરવવી મુશ્કેલ છે, છતાં દુનિયાભરમાં "ખુશી સર્વેક્ષણ" થાય છે. માપવાની બે રીત છે: આવક અને સરેરાશ આયુષ્ય જેવા આંકડા પર આધારિત વસ્તુલક્ષી, અને સર્વે પર આધારિત વ્યક્તિલક્ષી; હવે બંનેને ભેગી કરતી રીતો વધી રહી છે. 2006માં જાપાન 178 દેશોમાં 90મા સ્થાને હતું: આંકડા ઊંચા, પણ મનનો સંતોષ ઓછો. લેખક મુજબ જાપાને આર્થિક વિકાસ પર એટલો ભાર મૂક્યો કે રોજિંદા સંતોષ પર ધ્યાન ન આપ્યું. હવે જાપાન પોતાની રીતે ખુશી માપવા માગે છે. 2011ના ભૂકંપ પછી લોકોને સમજાયું કે ખુશી માત્ર અંગત બાબત નથી, સમાજ સાથે જોડાયેલી છે.

Key lines quoted: 「この幸せという主観的な感覚を数値化するのはとても難しい。」(Kono shiawase to iu shukanteki na kankaku o suuchika suru no wa totemo muzukashii.) · 「所得や寿命などのデータでは高い値を示しても、心理的な満足度が低いという。」(Shotoku ya jumyou nado no deeta de wa takai atai o shimeshite mo, shinriteki na manzokudo ga hikui to iu.)

使ってみよう 3: a company head's philosophy (Ⓑ40, p.268–269)

Model interview (summary)

A TV caster (キ) interviews 塚越寛 (Tsukakoshi Hiroshi), chairman of 伊那食品工業 (Ina Shokuhin Kougyou). When he was put in charge at 21, the company could have gone bankrupt at any moment, but he resolved never to give up: staff even stayed overnight at the factory to fix or install machines, and he read specialist books until they fell apart, studying even if it meant staying up all night. So it must keep growing steadily, little by little; forced rapid growth risks failure, and if the company goes bankrupt there is no employee happiness. The caster names this 年輪経営 (nenrin keiei, "tree-ring management"): a tree adds a ring each year, growing only a little but never stopping. The caster adds that many companies fail after pushing too hard for profit.

8 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

FormMeaningConnectionKey differenceUse when
〜てでもte de mo
vs 〜てまでte made		even if it means ~V-テ形＋でも／V-テ形＋までてでも = I'm willing to pay the price (positive resolve); てまで = going that far is too much (often criticism or doubt).並んででも食べたい · 並んでまで食べようとは思わない.
〜にこしたことはないni koshita koto wa nai
vs 〜というものではないto iu mono de wa nai		it's best to ~ / it doesn't follow that ~普通形＋にこしたことはない／というものではないThe first approves a common-sense preference; the second denies a too-simple belief. Together they make a balanced opinion.お金はあるにこしたことはないが、あれば幸せだというものでもない.
〜あまりamari
vs 〜末(に)sue ni		out of too much ~ / after (a long) ~普通形／Nの＋あまり／V-タ形・Nの＋末あまり = an excess of feeling or focus causes an (unwanted) result; 末 = a result reached after a long process.緊張のあまり話せなかった · 考えた末に決めた.
〜に基づいてni motozuite
vs 〜のもとでno moto de		based on / underN＋に基づいて／N＋のもと(で)基づいて = data, facts or rules are the foundation; のもと = under someone's support, guidance or conditions.データに基づいた調査 · 村人の協力のもと.
〜まいmai
vs 〜ないつもりだnai tsumori da		will not ~V-辞書形＋まい／V-ない＋つもりだまい = strong, literary resolve, often with 二度と／決して; ないつもり = an everyday plan.二度と行くまいと思った · 今日は出かけないつもりだ.
Watch out: this lesson has three "extreme effort" forms. 〜てでも (Tsukakoshi: 徹夜してでも勉強しよう) shows willing sacrifice; 〜てまで (Kobayashi: 安定した生活を捨ててまで) asks whether going that far was worth it; 〜末に shows where a long effort finally led (無理をした末に失敗した). And 〜ないものでしょうか is the polite way to ask for something difficult, as Natapon does for the private room.
