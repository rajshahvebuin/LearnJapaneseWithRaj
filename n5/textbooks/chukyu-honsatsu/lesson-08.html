第8課 — 気持ちを伝える (Kimochi o tsutaeru — Conveying your feelings)

Source
できる日本語 中級 本冊 (アルク), printed p.101–114 (PDF pages 101–114; the PDF page number is the same as the printed one)
Lesson goal
場面に応じて自分の気持ちをうまく伝えたり、相手の気持ちを受け止めたりして、周りの人と気持ちよくコミュニケーションを取ることができる。Bamen ni oujite jibun no kimochi o umaku tsutaetari, aite no kimochi o uketometari shite, mawari no hito to kimochi yoku komyunikeeshon o toru koto ga dekiru. Communicate smoothly with the people around you by putting your feelings across well for the situation and taking in other people's feelings. (Goal from the 目次, p.5.)
Grammar points

1 Lesson Goals (チャレンジ！ can-do list)

話してみよう (warm-up, p.101)

The opening page has no printed question, just nine faces showing different feelings: a woman with a tear (sad), a man with arms folded and jagged lines around him (angry), a girl clutching her hands (nervous or scared), a boy humming (cheerful), a man grinning (happy), a woman laughing over a book, a woman holding a broken plate (upset, troubled), a boy squeezing his eyes shut (frustrated, about to cry), and a man frowning at his watch (impatient). Name each feeling in Japanese and talk about how you show it. HI: पहले पन्ने पर नौ चेहरे हैं जो दुख, ग़ुस्सा, घबराहट, ख़ुशी, हँसी, परेशानी, खीज और बेसब्री दिखाते हैं; हर भावना का जापानी नाम बताएँ और चर्चा करें कि आप उसे कैसे जताते हैं। GU: પહેલા પાના પર નવ ચહેરા છે જે દુઃખ, ગુસ્સો, ગભરાટ, આનંદ, હાસ્ય, મૂંઝવણ, ચીડ અને અધીરાઈ બતાવે છે; દરેક લાગણીનું જાપાની નામ કહો અને ચર્ચા કરો કે તમે તે કેવી રીતે વ્યક્ત કરો છો.

    HI: मैं कारण बताकर सामने वाले से विनम्रता से अनुमति माँग सकता हूँ। GU: હું કારણ જણાવીને સામેની વ્યક્તિ પાસે નમ્રતાથી પરવાનગી માગી શકું છું.
  • 2. メッセージの伝え方について知って、自分の生活に生かすことができる。Messeeji no tsutaekata ni tsuite shitte, jibun no seikatsu ni ikasu koto ga dekiru. I can learn how to send messages and use it in my own life.
    HI: मैं संदेश भेजने के तरीक़े जानकर उन्हें अपनी ज़िंदगी में काम ला सकता हूँ। GU: હું સંદેશ મોકલવાની રીત જાણીને તેને મારા જીવનમાં ઉપયોગમાં લઈ શકું છું.
  • 3. 相手の愚痴を聞いて気持ちを理解することができる。Aite no guchi o kiite kimochi o rikai suru koto ga dekiru. I can listen to someone's complaints and understand how they feel.
    HI: मैं किसी की शिकायतें (भड़ास) सुनकर उसकी भावना समझ सकता हूँ। GU: હું કોઈની ફરિયાદો (બળાપો) સાંભળીને તેની લાગણી સમજી શકું છું.
  • 4. メールを読んで、送った人の励ましの気持ちを理解することができる。Meeru o yonde, okutta hito no hagemashi no kimochi o rikai suru koto ga dekiru. I can read an e-mail and understand the sender's wish to encourage me.
    HI: मैं ई-मेल पढ़कर भेजने वाले की हौसला बढ़ाने की भावना समझ सकता हूँ। GU: હું ઈ-મેલ વાંચીને મોકલનારની હિંમત આપવાની લાગણી સમજી શકું છું.
  • 5. 気持ちの伝え方について自分の考えを理由とともに述べることができる。Kimochi no tsutaekata ni tsuite jibun no kangae o riyuu to tomo ni noberu koto ga dekiru. I can give my opinion, with reasons, on how to convey feelings.
    HI: मैं भावनाएँ जताने के तरीक़े पर अपनी राय कारण सहित बता सकता हूँ। GU: હું લાગણી વ્યક્ત કરવાની રીત વિશે મારો મત કારણ સાથે કહી શકું છું.

Task 2 · 見つけた！

Material (summary of the magazine article, p.102)

一筆箋で伝わる気持ちIppitsusen de tsutawaru kimochi (Feelings that come across in a one-line note)

Setting: you find a magazine article about ways of sending messages. A 一筆箋 (ippitsusen) is a long, narrow notepaper, about 18 cm by 8 cm.
EN: Unlike a letter, there are no stiff greetings or fixed rules, so you can go from a simple greeting straight to the point, as if writing an e-mail. It is a casual, handwritten way to show your feelings. When to use it: when you give a present such as a birthday gift or want to say a quick thank-you (e.g. happy birthday, thanks for the other day, here is a snack from my country), and at work when sending goods, documents or materials. The person who receives it will think "how thoughtful" or "how polite" and get a good impression of you. Choosing a design that suits the person and the content makes your feelings come across even more.
HI: इप्पित्सुसेन लगभग 18×8 सेमी का लंबा-पतला लेटर पेपर है। चिट्ठी जैसे औपचारिक अभिवादन या नियम नहीं होते, सीधे मुद्दे पर आ सकते हैं, ई-मेल की तरह। उपहार देते समय या छोटा-सा धन्यवाद कहने के लिए, और काम पर सामान, काग़ज़ात या सामग्री भेजते समय इसका उपयोग होता है। पाने वाला सोचेगा "कितना ध्यान रखने वाला / विनम्र इंसान है"। व्यक्ति और विषय के हिसाब से चित्र वाला काग़ज़ चुनें तो भावना और अच्छी तरह पहुँचती है।
GU: ઇપ્પિત્સુસેન લગભગ 18×8 સેમીનો લાંબો-સાંકડો લેટર પેપર છે. પત્ર જેવાં ઔપચારિક અભિવાદન કે નિયમો નથી, સીધા મુદ્દા પર આવી શકાય, ઈ-મેલની જેમ. ભેટ આપતી વખતે કે નાનો આભાર કહેવા, અને કામ પર માલ, કાગળો કે સામગ્રી મોકલતી વખતે તેનો ઉપયોગ થાય છે. મેળવનાર વિચારશે "કેટલું ધ્યાન રાખનાર / નમ્ર માણસ છે". વ્યક્તિ અને વિષય પ્રમાણે ચિત્રવાળો કાગળ પસંદ કરો તો લાગણી વધુ સારી રીતે પહોંચે.

Key lines quoted: 手書きの一筆箋で手軽にあなたの気持ちを伝えましょう。(Tegaki no ippitsusen de tegaru ni anata no kimochi o tsutaemashou.) · 受け取った人は「気が利く人だな」「丁寧な人だな」などと思って、あなたにいい印象を持つでしょう。(Uketotta hito wa "ki ga kiku hito da na" "teinei na hito da na" nado to omotte, anata ni ii inshou o motsu deshou.)

The speaker says that when telling someone they like them, they meet the person and say it directly, for two reasons. 包むTsutsumu (Wrapping)

EN: International students in Japan find many Japanese customs surprising, and one is wrapping. A student buying a birthday present for his younger brother was asked lots of questions about wrapping, didn't follow, and said "I'll leave it to you". The clerk put the gift in a box, wrapped it in pretty paper, added a ribbon and put it in a paper bag; he thought it a bit wasteful, since the paper and ribbon just get thrown away. Japanese shops do often wrap gifts carefully, there are shops selling wrapping goods, and some people take wrapping classes. Why? In the Heian period, nobles are said to have wrapped gifts in paper. Paper was made by soaking it in water again and again to remove dirt, so people believed paper protected the gift from bad or dirty things. Paper was also very expensive, so wrapping a gift in it showed you valued the person. So in Japan wrapping has long expressed care for the other person and the wish to please them. Recipients look at a nicely wrapped gift before opening it carefully, and some even reuse the paper as a book cover. Next time you give or receive a present, try thinking of everything, wrapping included, as the gift.

8 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

FormMeaningConnectionKey differenceUse when
〜おかげでokage de
vs 〜せいでsei de		thanks to / because ofNの／普通形＋おかげで／せいでおかげで = good result, gratitude; せいで = bad result, blame. Ironic おかげで for a bad result exists but sounds sarcastic.友達のおかげで合格した ✓ · 雨のせいで中止 ✓.
〜くせにkuse ni
vs 〜のにno ni (added)		even though普通形(ナAな／Nの)＋くせに／のにくせに = criticism of another person, same subject, strong feeling; のに = general surprise or disappointment, any subject.Complaining about someone → くせに. Neutral "although" → のに.
〜ものですからmono desu kara
vs 〜からkara		because普通形(ナAな／Nな)＋ものですからものですから = an excuse, sounds apologetic and soft; から is a plain reason and can sound like you're blaming circumstances.Apologising for being late or unable → ものですから.
〜ことはないkoto wa nai
vs 〜なくてもいいnakute mo ii (added)		no need toV-辞書形＋ことはない／V-なくてもいいことはない adds reassurance or advice ("don't bother / don't worry"); なくてもいい just states it isn't required.Comforting a friend → 気にすることはない.
〜にしたらni shitara
vs 〜にとってni totte (added)		for ~ / from ~'s viewN＋にしたら／にとってにしたら imagines that person's feelings, usually someone else's; にとって is a neutral "for ~" and also works for yourself and things.店長にしたら心配だろう ✓ · 私にとって大切な人 ✓.
〜たびにtabi ni
vs 〜ときtoki		every time / whenV-辞書形／Nの＋たびにたびに = every single time, a repeated pattern; とき can be one occasion.会うたびに背が伸びている = every time I see him.
Watch out: this lesson has two kinds of (さ)せる. 休ませていただけませんか (【1-2】) asks permission for you to act. お客さんを怒らせる (【3-4】) means you cause someone's feeling. If the sentence ends in いただけませんか／ください, it's a permission request; if the object is a person with a feeling verb (驚く, 喜ぶ, 怒る), it's "make someone feel".
