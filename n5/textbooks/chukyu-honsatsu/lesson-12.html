第12課 — 心と体の健康 (Kokoro to karada no kenkou — Health of mind and body)

Source

Lesson goal
異なる環境において自分の目標を達成するために、心と体の健康を保って生活することができる。Kotonaru kankyou ni oite jibun no mokuhyou o tassei suru tame ni, kokoro to karada no kenkou o tamotte seikatsu suru koto ga dekiru. 1 Lesson Goals (チャレンジ！ can-do list)

話してみよう (warm-up, p.157)

健康チェックをしてみよう！(Kenkou chekku o shite miyou!) Do a health check: tick the items that apply to you. There are 12 items about eating, exercise, habits and mood, for example 朝食を食べないことが多い。(Choushoku o tabenai koto ga ooi.) "I often skip breakfast", 階段を上ると息が切れる。(Kaidan o noboru to iki ga kireru.) "I get out of breath on stairs", and ストレス解消の方法が見つからない。(Sutoresu kaishou no houhou ga mitsukaranai.) "I can't find a way to relieve stress". Others cover picky eating, irregular mealtimes, no exercise for a week, tiring easily, smoking, wanting a drink in the evening, skin problems, getting irritated over small things, and not feeling motivated. A doctor says: チェック項目が多いほど要注意です！(Chekku koumoku ga ooi hodo youchuui desu!) "The more ticks, the more careful you need to be!" HI: स्वास्थ्य जाँच कीजिए: जितने ज़्यादा निशान, उतनी ज़्यादा सावधानी ज़रूरी। GU: આરોગ્ય તપાસ કરો: જેટલાં વધુ નિશાન, તેટલી વધુ સાવચેતી જરૂરી.

  • 1. 気持ちが落ち込んだとき、身近な人に状況を話してアドバイスをもらうことができる。Kimochi ga ochikonda toki, mijika na hito ni joukyou o hanashite adobaisu o morau koto ga dekiru. When I feel down, I can tell someone close to me about my situation and get advice.
    HI: जब मन उदास हो, मैं किसी क़रीबी को अपनी हालत बताकर सलाह ले सकता हूँ। GU: જ્યારે મન ઉદાસ હોય, ત્યારે હું નજીકની વ્યક્તિને મારી સ્થિતિ કહીને સલાહ લઈ શકું છું.
  • 2. テレビ番組を見て、運動習慣と体力にどのような関係があるかについて知ることができる。Terebi bangumi o mite, undou shuukan to tairyoku ni dono you na kankei ga aru ka ni tsuite shiru koto ga dekiru. I can watch a TV programme and learn how exercise habits relate to physical fitness.
    HI: मैं टीवी कार्यक्रम देखकर जान सकता हूँ कि व्यायाम की आदत और शारीरिक क्षमता में क्या संबंध है। GU: હું ટીવી કાર્યક્રમ જોઈને જાણી શકું છું કે કસરતની ટેવ અને શારીરિક ક્ષમતા વચ્ચે શો સંબંધ છે.
  • 3. 記事を読んで、睡眠に関する情報を得ることができる。Kiji o yonde, suimin ni kansuru jouhou o eru koto ga dekiru. I can read an article and get information about sleep.
    HI: मैं लेख पढ़कर नींद से जुड़ी जानकारी पा सकता हूँ। GU: હું લેખ વાંચીને ઊંઘ વિશેની માહિતી મેળવી શકું છું.
  • 4. 新聞の投書欄を読んで、健康について自分の意見を言うことができる。Shinbun no toushoran o yonde, kenkou ni tsuite jibun no iken o iu koto ga dekiru. I can read the letters-to-the-editor column of a newspaper and give my opinion about health.
    HI: मैं अख़बार का पाठकों के पत्र वाला कॉलम पढ़कर स्वास्थ्य पर अपनी राय दे सकता हूँ। GU: હું છાપાની વાચકોના પત્રોની કૉલમ વાંચીને આરોગ્ય વિશે મારો મત આપી શકું છું.

Task 3 · 見つけた！

Material (summary of the article, p.159)

快適な眠りを手に入れるには？Kaiteki na nemuri o te ni ireru ni wa?

Setting: 最近あまり眠れないと思っていたら、睡眠に関係のある記事を見つけました。Saikin amari nemurenai to omotte itara, suimin ni kankei no aru kiji o mitsukemashita. You've felt you can't sleep well lately, and you find an article about sleep.
EN: A survey of 1,029 Japanese men and women in their 20s to 40s (Wacoal, Feb 2012) found only 28.9% were satisfied with their sleep; the other 71.1% were not, with worries like not recovering from tiredness or waking up badly. An office worker says work pressure and worry about the future make it hard to fall asleep; a housewife busy with childcare is always short of sleep. A chart shows a night's sleep rhythm: REM sleep ("body sleep") and non-REM sleep ("brain sleep") alternate through the night. The second part says the key to good sleep is sleeping in multiples of 1.5 hours (90 minutes): if you wake at the changeover, you feel you slept well. Recent research shows that matching the body clock to our daily schedule leads to good health, and the method is surprisingly simple: just eat breakfast every morning, which resets the clock. If you skip breakfast, the clock gradually drifts from real time; your head and body don't work properly, you feel jet-lagged, and you can become unwell. vs 〜とは限らないto wa kagiranai		just because / not necessarily普通形＋からといって／とは限らないからといって starts the sentence (the reason); とは限らない ends it (the partial denial). They often go together.専門家だからといって、正しいとは限らない.
〜に対して (L11)ni taishite
vs 〜に対して (L12)		whereas / towardsN＋に対してL11 = contrast between two facts; L12 = target of an action or feeling (〜に対する愛情).Two opposite facts → contrast. Survey, criticism, feelings aimed at someone → target.
〜さえ〜ばsae ~ba
vs 〜だけdake		as long as / onlyN＋さえ＋V-条件形さえ〜ば = this one condition is enough for the result; だけ just limits (時間だけある)."All you need is ~" → さえ〜ば.
〜なんて／なんかnante / nanka
vs 〜などnado		the likes of ~N(＋助詞)＋なんてなんて／なんか are spoken and carry feeling (contempt, surprise, modesty); など is the written / polite form, and also means "etc."Casual dismissal → なんて. Formal listing → など.
Watch out: 〜ばかりだ here takes the dictionary form (増えるばかりだ, "keeps increasing"), not the た-form (来たばかり, "just arrived"). And 〜とは限らない is only a partial "no": 金持ちが幸せとは限らない means "rich people aren't necessarily happy", not "rich people are never happy".
