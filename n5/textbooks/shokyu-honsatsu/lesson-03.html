Culture note: the Japanese school year starts in April (入学式 nyuugakushiki, the entrance ceremony), just when the cherry blossoms bloom. Big breaks are ゴールデンウィーク (early May), 夏休み (summer) and 冬休み (New Year).

Word order tip: in Japanese the verb always comes last, like Hindi and Gujarati: 私は毎朝コーヒーを飲みます = मैं हर सुबह कॉफ़ी पीता हूँ.

【ポイント16】 V ます／ません

small topics 2 · 3
ReadingV masu / V masen
Meaning (EN)"(I) do V / will do V" — "(I) don't V / won't V"
Meaning (HI)V करता/करती हूँ, करूँगा/करूँगी — V नहीं करता/करती
Meaning (GU)V કરું છું, કરીશ — V નથી કરતો/કરતી
How it works〜ます is the polite verb ending. The same form covers habits ("every day I eat…") and the future ("in summer I will go…"). For "not", change ます to ません. For a question, add か.
FormPatternExample
AffirmativeV ます食べます。Tabemasu. — I eat / will eat.
NegativeV ません食べません。Tabemasen. — I don't eat / won't eat.
QuestionV ますか食べますか。Tabemasu ka. — Do you eat?
Past(added, not in book — Lesson 5) V ました食べました。Tabemashita. — I ate.

Answer a yes/no question by repeating just the verb: はい、食べます／いいえ、食べません. Don't answer with only はい, そうです — that is for です-sentences.

Biggest trap: へ vs で. Hindi में and Gujarati માં cover both, so learners mix them up. Ask yourself: am I moving towards the place (→ へ), or doing something there (→ で)? 図書館へ行きます。図書館で本を読みます。 And don't add に to 毎日: × 毎日に学校へ行きます (the book itself marks this as wrong on p.271).
