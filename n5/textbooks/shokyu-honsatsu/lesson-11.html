This lesson has two halves: polite talk about your life (small topics 1–2), and then casual "friend" Japanese (small topic 3), where です／ます disappear. Both are needed: polite for new people, casual for close friends.

2 Sentence Patterns (ポイント 98–103, p.278–279)

The lesson pages flag the points in the corner: small topic 1 = 98, 99, 100, 101, 102; small topic 2 = 101; small topic 3 = 103. The book gives each pattern with examples at the back (p.278–279). Below, each one is explained in plain words, with every book example plus the model lines from the 言ってみよう drills.

Verb forms you need here. て form (飲んで, 見て, して) for ポイント98. た form = the plain past: it is the て form with て→た, で→だ (飲んで → 飲んだ, 見て → 見た, 行って → 行った). It's used in ポイント99, 101 and 103. Dictionary form (飲む, 見る, 行く) and ない form (飲まない) appear in 101 and 103.

【ポイント100】 N1 は ＿＿が、N2 は ＿＿

small topic 1
ReadingN1 wa ___ ga, N2 wa ___
Meaning (EN)"N1 is …, but N2 is …" — a contrast
Meaning (HI)N1 तो … है, लेकिन N2 … है
Meaning (GU)N1 તો … છે, પણ N2 … છે
How it worksが after a sentence means "but". Putting は on both things you compare makes the contrast clear, like Hindi "तो". は can replace を and が, and is added after other particles: へ→へは, に→には, で→では, と→とは (the book's box on p.278).
ParticleBecomesExample

がが → は会話は好きですが、作文は好きじゃありません。Kaiwa wa suki desu ga, sakubun wa suki ja arimasen.
へへ → へは遠いところへはまだ行っていません。Tooi tokoro e wa mada itte imasen.
