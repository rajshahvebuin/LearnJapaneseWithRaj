1 Reading Strategy

Skill focus

✿実際に起きたことかどうか考えようJissai ni okita koto ka dou ka kangaeyou — Try to understand what actually happened!

実際にはしなかった過去の仮定Jissai ni wa shinakatta kako no katei — A hypothetical about the past that didn't actually happen:

SentenceWhat it means actually happened
もしあの飛行機に乗っていたら、事故にあっていただろう。Moshi ano hikouki ni notte itara, jiko ni atte ita darou. — "If I had gotten on that plane, I would have been in an accident." 実際は飛行機に乗っていない／実際は事故にあっていないJissai wa hikouki ni notte inai / jissai wa jiko ni atte inai. — In reality, I didn't get on the plane, and in reality I wasn't in an accident.
あのときもっと勉強していたら、今ごろ博士になっていただろう。Ano toki motto benkyou shite itara, imagoro hakase ni natte ita darou. — "If I had studied harder back then, I would be a PhD by now." 実際は勉強が足りなかった／実際は博士になっていないJissai wa benkyou ga tarinakatta / jissai wa hakase ni natte inai. — In reality, I didn't study enough, and in reality I am not a PhD.

乗らなくてよかったね！Noranakute yokatta ne! — "Good thing you didn't get on it!" ／ あのときもっと勉強すればよかった……Ano toki motto benkyou sureba yokatta... — "I wish I had studied more back then..."

Key idea: 「〜ていたら、〜ただろう」("if X had happened, Y would have happened") describes a past that never actually occurred — both the condition and the result are the opposite of reality.

HI: 「〜ていたら、〜ただろう」("अगर X हुआ होता, तो Y हुआ होता") असल में कभी न घटी हुई घटना बताता है — शर्त और नतीजा दोनों असलियत के उल्टे होते हैं। GU: 「〜ていたら、〜ただろう」("જો X થયું હોત, તો Y થયું હોત") ખરેખર ક્યારેય ન બનેલી ઘટના દર્શાવે છે — શરત અને પરિણામ બંને વાસ્તવિકતાથી ઊલટાં હોય છે.

練習 (Renshuu — practice: read the dialogue, then judge each statement)

Dialogue

Demo, kono aida yonda hon ni yoru to, Okinawa de wa kodomo ga kushami o suru to, soba ni iru hito ga nanika iu rashii desu yo. Sore o iwanai to kodomo ga yuurei ni tsurete ikareru toka.

Why: #1 overstates a rare, optional remark ("だれかが噂している" is mentioned as something people sometimes say, not a mandatory rule) as an obligation ("言わなければならない"). #2 is contradicted by the whole dialogue — Okinawan/old Japanese customs did have such an expression. #4 restates the custom as if it were a proven fact happening today, when the passage only reports it as a surviving tradition/belief (言い伝え), not literal cause-and-effect. #3 and #5 both match the teacher's own words ("この間読んだ本によると" — a recent discovery for the teacher — and the underlying belief that not saying the phrase leads to misfortune). 2 Passage Questions

問題 from p.63: an essay about the writer's discovery of Japan's own "Bless you"-equivalent traditions, followed by 3 comprehension questions with 4 options each.

Essay

「①日本人はだれかがくしゃみをしても、何も言わないんですか。」"① Nihonjin wa dareka ga kushami o shite mo, nani mo iwanai ndesu ka."

もし、こんな質問を教室でされていたら、なんと答えていただろう。そういう習慣はありませんね、と簡単に答えてしまっていただろう。つまり、英語の"Bless you!"に当たるようなものがないのか、という質問だ。日本にもちゃんとそういう習慣が過去にあり、さらに現在でも沖縄に残っていることを知ったのは、つい最近のことだ。Moshi, konna shitsumon o kyoushitsu de sarete itara, nan to kotaete ita darou. Sou iu shuukan wa arimasen ne, to kantan ni kotaete shimatte ita darou. Tsumari, eigo no "Bless you!" ni ataru you na mono ga nai no ka, to iu shitsumon da. Nihon ni mo chanto sou iu shuukan ga kako ni ari, sara ni genzai demo Okinawa ni nokotte iru koto o shitta no wa, tsui saikin no koto da.

沖縄では子どもがくしゃみをすると、そばにいる人が「クスクェー」と言うらしい。言わないと子どもが幽霊に連れていかれるという言い伝え注1によるものだそうだ。調べてみると、『徒然草』という有名な書物（1330年頃）にも、くしゃみが出たときに「くさめくさめ」と唱えない注2と死んでしまうという記述があることがわかった。Okinawa de wa kodomo ga kushami o suru to, soba ni iru hito ga "kusukwee" to iu rashii. Iwanai to kodomo ga yuurei ni tsurete ikareru to iu iitsutae (chuu 1) ni yoru mono da sou da. Shirabete miru to, "Tsurezuregusa" to iu yuumei na shomotsu (sen sanbyaku sanjuu-nen goro) ni mo, kushami ga deta toki ni "kusame kusame" to tonaenai (chuu 2) to shinde shimau to iu kijutsu ga aru koto ga wakatta.

日本人であり、日本語を外国人に教える自分が、沖縄の言葉も知らず、日本語の古語も知らないことを恥ずかしく思った。②日本人の日本語知らずには要注意だ。Nihonjin de ari, nihongo o gaikokujin ni oshieru jibun ga, Okinawa no kotoba mo shirazu, nihongo no kogo mo shiranai koto o hazukashiku omotta. ② Nihonjin no nihongo shirazu ni wa youchuui da.

注1 言い伝え：a tradition chuu 1: iitsutae — "a tradition, an old saying" ・ 注2 唱える：to chant chuu 2: tonaeru — "to chant"

EN: "① Do Japanese people not say anything when someone sneezes?" If I had been asked such a question in class, what would I have answered? I probably would have simply said, "There's no such custom" — that is, whether there's nothing equivalent to the English "Bless you!" It was only quite recently that I learned Japan, too, properly had such a custom in the past, and that it still survives today in Okinawa. In Okinawa, when a child sneezes, it seems the person nearby says "Kusukwee." This apparently comes from a tradition that if you don't say it, the child will be taken away by a ghost. On looking into it, I found that a famous book called the Tsurezuregusa (around the year 1330) also contains a passage saying that if you don't chant "kusame kusame" when you sneeze, you will die. Being Japanese myself, and someone who teaches Japanese to foreigners, I felt ashamed that I knew neither the Okinawan word nor this piece of old Japanese. ② One must be careful of Japanese people not knowing their own Japanese.
HI: "① क्या जापानी लोग किसी के छींकने पर कुछ नहीं कहते?" अगर मुझसे क्लास में ऐसा सवाल पूछा गया होता, तो मैं क्या जवाब देता? शायद मैं आसानी से कह देता, "ऐसी कोई परंपरा नहीं है" — यानी, अंग्रेज़ी के "Bless you!" जैसा कुछ भी नहीं है क्या। मुझे बस हाल ही में पता चला कि जापान में भी पहले ऐसी परंपरा वाकई थी, और आज भी ओकिनावा में बची हुई है। ओकिनावा में जब कोई बच्चा छींकता है, तो पास खड़ा व्यक्ति "कुसुक्वे" कहता है, ऐसा लगता है। मान्यता है कि न कहने पर बच्चे को भूत ले जाता है। खोजबीन करने पर पता चला कि 'सुरेज़ुरेगुसा' नाम की एक मशहूर किताब (लगभग सन् 1330) में भी लिखा है कि छींक आने पर "कुसामे कुसामे" न बोलो तो मर जाओगे। जापानी होते हुए और विदेशियों को जापानी पढ़ाते हुए भी, मुझे न ओकिनावा का शब्द पता था, न यह पुराना जापानी शब्द — इस पर मुझे शर्म महसूस हुई। ② जापानियों के अपनी ही भाषा न जानने से सावधान रहना ज़रूरी है।
