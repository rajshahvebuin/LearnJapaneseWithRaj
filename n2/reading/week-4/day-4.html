Notes
Week 4 opens the book's two literary weeks (エッセイ・小説) — the strategy boxes shift from vocabulary/table skills to reading strategy, matching this site's N1 Reading format. This day's ✿ tip is about katakana words that are NOT foreign loanwords but native Japanese words written in katakana for stylistic reasons.

1 Reading Strategy

Key idea

カタカナで書かれた言葉に注意！②　— 日本語の場合Katakana de kakareta kotoba ni chuui! Ni — Nihongo no baai

Katakana words do not necessarily have a foreign origin — many are Japanese words simply written in katakana for effect. Watch for three common reasons:

  • 1. Onomatopoeia / mimicry. ワンワンwanwan (a dog's bark), トントンtonton (a knocking/tapping sound), ツルツルtsurutsuru (smooth/slippery) — words built from sounds or sensory impressions (音や様子から作られたoto ya yousu kara tsukurareta).
    HI: ध्वनि या एहसास से बने शब्द, जैसे भौंकने या खटखटाने की आवाज़। GU: અવાજ કે અહેસાસ પરથી બનેલા શબ્દો, જેમ કે ભસવાનો કે ખખડાવવાનો અવાજ.
  • 2. Stylistic effect in writing. ゴミgomi (trash), バカbaka (fool), コメkome (rice) — written in katakana for visual emphasis or a sharper tone in a sentence (文章で効果を出すためにbunshou de kouka o dasu tame ni).
    HI: वाक्य में असर या ज़ोर डालने के लिए काताकाना में लिखे गए साधारण जापानी शब्द। GU: વાક્યમાં અસર કે ભાર આપવા માટે કાટાકાનામાં લખાયેલા સાદા જાપાનીઝ શબ્દો.
  • 3. Custom, often because the kanji is hard. バラbara (rose), ウサギusagi (rabbit), マンガmanga (comics), ハチhachi (bee) — these have kanji, but by convention they're usually written in katakana instead (漢字が難しいなどの理由で習慣的にkanji ga muzukashii nado no riyuu de shuukanteki ni).
    HI: इनके कांजी होते हैं, पर मुश्किल होने या रिवाज के कारण आमतौर पर काताकाना में ही लिखे जाते हैं। GU: આના કાંજી હોય છે, પણ અઘરા હોવાથી કે રિવાજ મુજબ સામાન્ય રીતે કાટાકાનામાં જ લખાય છે.

★ カタカナは外来語とは限りません。日本語である場合も多いです。Katakana wa gairaigo to wa kagirimasen. Nihongo de aru baai mo ooi desu. — "Katakana words do not necessarily have a foreign origin. There are many of Japanese origin."

EN: A: "Isn't the area with the bench along the road I always take kind of full of trash? Kids from our school hang out there talking, but they leave their food trash behind..." B: "Yeah, everyone just leaves it under the bench or around the trees. Hey, what if our club made a trash can and put it there? We could ask the art club to paint it." A: "Oh, that could work."
HI: A: "जिस रास्ते से मैं हमेशा जाता हूँ, वहाँ बेंच के आसपास कुछ ज़्यादा ही कचरा नहीं लगता? हमारे स्कूल के बच्चे भी वहाँ बैठकर बातें करते हैं, पर खाने का कचरा वहीं छोड़ जाते हैं..." B: "हाँ, सब बेंच के नीचे या पेड़ों के आसपास फेंक देते हैं। सुन, अगर हमारा क्लब कचरा पेटी बनाकर वहाँ रख दे तो? पेंटिंग के लिए आर्ट क्लब से कह देंगे।" A: "अरे, यह अच्छा रहेगा।"
GU: A: "હું જે રસ્તેથી હંમેશા જાઉં છું, ત્યાં બાંકડાની આસપાસ થોડું વધારે કચરો નથી લાગતું? અમારી શાળાના બાળકો પણ ત્યાં બેસીને વાતો કરે છે, પણ ખાવાનો કચરો ત્યાં જ છોડી જાય છે..." B: "હા, બધા બાંકડા નીચે કે ઝાડની આસપાસ ફેંકી દે છે. સાંભળ, જો અમારો ક્લબ કચરાપેટી બનાવીને ત્યાં મૂકે તો? રંગકામ માટે આર્ટ ક્લબને કહીશું." A: "અરે, આ સારું રહેશે."
#StatementTrue/False
1AさんとBさんは同じクラブのメンバーである。A-san to B-san wa onaji kurabu no menbaa de aru. — A and B are members of the same club.True
2Bさんは美術部のメンバーである。B-san wa bijutsu-bu no menbaa de aru. — B is a member of the art club.False
3学校のベンチの周りは、ゴミでいっぱいである。Gakkou no benchi no mawari wa, gomi de ippai de aru. — The area around the school's bench is full of trash.False
4Aさんたちは自分たちでゴミ箱を作るつもりである。A-san-tachi wa jibun-tachi de gomibako o tsukuru tsumori de aru. — A and friends plan to make the trash can themselves.True
5ベンチにかく絵を美術部に頼む予定である。Benchi ni kaku e o bijutsu-bu ni tanomu yotei de aru. — They plan to ask the art club to paint the bench.False

Why: B proposes it as "うちのクラブで" ("with OUR club") — a phrase that only makes sense between two members of the same club, so #1 is true. B explicitly hands the painting job to a separate 美術部 (art club), which only makes sense if B is NOT already in it — so #2 is false. The bench is on "いつも通る道" (a road A always walks along), never confirmed as school property, even though school kids hang out there — #3 quietly swaps "a bench on my route" for "the school's bench," so it's false. #4 restates B's proposal ("うちのクラブでゴミ箱作って") which A agrees to — true. #5 swaps the target of the painting: the art club is asked to paint the new トラッシュ can (ゴミ箱), not the bench itself — false.

2 Passage Questions

The main 問題 (p.67) is a short essay, 手作りのゴミ箱 (Tezukuri no gomibako — "The Handmade Trash Can"), followed by 問1 and 問2. Words with a dotted underline are clickable/tappable (or hover on desktop) for a popup with romaji + English/Hindi/Gujarati meaning.

Passage

手作りのゴミ箱 (Tezukuri no gomibako)

駅に行く道の歩道にベンチがひとつあります。すぐそばには木が数本植えられていて、お年寄りがひと休みするのにちょうどいい場所です。学校帰りの高校生もよく座っておしゃべりをしています。Eki ni iku michi no hodou ni benchi ga hitotsu arimasu. Sugu soba ni wa ki ga suuhon uerarete ite, otoshiyori ga hito-yasumi suru no ni choudo ii basho desu. Gakkou-gaeri no koukousei mo yoku suwatte oshaberi o shite imasu.

以前は、その高校生たちが残していったものらしく*、ベンチの周りはジュースの空き缶や弁当の空き箱などのゴミが散らかっていて、あまり気持ちのいい場所とは言えませんでした。けれども、いつだったか、木で作ったゴミ箱が置かれました。4個、それぞれペンキでいろいろな色に塗られ、「燃えるゴミ」「燃えないゴミ」「空きカン」「空きビン」という文字が書かれ、かわいい絵もついていました。缶でできた吸いがら注2入れもありました。Izen wa, sono koukousei-tachi ga nokoshite itta mono rashiku, benchi no mawari wa juusu no akikan ya bentou no akibako nado no gomi ga chirakatte ite, amari kimochi no ii basho to wa iemasen deshita. Keredomo, itsu datta ka, ki de tsukutta gomibako ga okaremashita. Yon-ko, sorezore penki de iroiro na iro ni nurare, "moeru gomi" "moenai gomi" "aki-kan" "aki-bin" to iu moji ga kakare, kawaii e mo tsuite imashita. Kan de dekita suigara ire mo arimashita.

そのゴミ箱が置かれてからは、①前よりも人が集まってきています。そこに座っていた人たちの話では、近くの高校の生徒たちが作ったということです。最近の高校生の態度が悪いのにはあきれていたのですが、②そんな高校生ばかりではないとわかり、本当に心が温まりました。Sono gomibako ga okarete kara wa, mae yori mo hito ga atsumatte kite imasu. Soko ni suwatte ita hitotachi no hanashi de wa, chikaku no koukou no seito-tachi ga tsukutta to iu koto desu. Saikin no koukousei no taido ga warui no ni wa akirete ita no desu ga, sonna koukousei bakari de wa nai to wakari, hontou ni kokoro ga atatamarimashita.

注1 手作り：hand-made 　·　注2 吸いがら：a cigarette butt　·　* footnote: "apparently there was stuff left behind by the high school students"

EN: There's a bench on the sidewalk along the road to the station. A few trees are planted right beside it, making it a perfect spot for the elderly to rest, and high schoolers on their way home from school often sit there chatting too. Previously, apparently left behind by those high schoolers, the area around the bench was littered with empty juice cans and empty lunch boxes, and it wasn't a very pleasant place. But at some point, a handmade wooden trash can was placed there — four of them, each painted a different color, labeled "burnable trash," "non-burnable trash," "empty cans," "empty bottles," with cute pictures on them too. There was even a can-made cigarette-butt receptacle. Since the trash cans were placed there, more people have been gathering than before. According to people sitting there, students from a nearby high school made them. I had been appalled at how poorly today's high schoolers behave, but learning that not all high schoolers are like that truly warmed my heart.
HI: स्टेशन जाने वाली सड़क के फुटपाथ पर एक बेंच है। पास ही कुछ पेड़ लगे हैं, जिससे यह बुज़ुर्गों के आराम करने के लिए एकदम सही जगह बन जाती है, और स्कूल से लौटते हाई-स्कूल के बच्चे भी अक्सर वहाँ बैठकर बातें करते हैं। पहले, शायद उन्हीं हाई-स्कूल के बच्चों द्वारा छोड़ा गया, बेंच के आसपास जूस के खाली डिब्बे और खाने के खाली बक्से बिखरे रहते थे, और यह ज़्यादा सुहावनी जगह नहीं थी। पर किसी समय, लकड़ी से बनी कचरा पेटियाँ वहाँ रखी गईं — चार, हर एक अलग रंग में रंगी हुई, "जलने वाला कचरा", "न जलने वाला कचरा", "खाली डिब्बे", "खाली बोतलें" लिखा हुआ, साथ में प्यारी तस्वीरें भी। डिब्बे से बनी सिगरेट की राख रखने की जगह भी थी। कचरा पेटियाँ रखे जाने के बाद से, पहले से ज़्यादा लोग वहाँ जमा होने लगे हैं। वहाँ बैठे लोगों के अनुसार, पास के हाई स्कूल के छात्रों ने इन्हें बनाया। मैं हाल के हाई-स्कूल छात्रों के बुरे व्यवहार से हैरान थी, पर यह जानकर कि सभी हाई-स्कूल के छात्र वैसे नहीं होते, दिल को सच में सुकून मिला।
GU: સ્ટેશન જવાના રસ્તાના ફૂટપાથ પર એક બાંકડો છે. નજીકમાં જ થોડાં ઝાડ વાવેલાં છે, જેથી તે વૃદ્ધો માટે આરામ કરવાની એકદમ યોગ્ય જગ્યા બને છે, અને શાળાએથી પાછા ફરતા હાઈસ્કૂલના વિદ્યાર્થીઓ પણ ઘણી વાર ત્યાં બેસીને વાતો કરે છે. પહેલાં, કદાચ એ જ હાઈસ્કૂલના વિદ્યાર્થીઓ દ્વારા છોડાયેલા, બાંકડાની આસપાસ જ્યુસના ખાલી ડબ્બા અને ખાવાના ખાલી બોક્સ વિખરાયેલા રહેતા, અને એ બહુ સુખદ જગ્યા નહોતી. પણ ક્યારેક, લાકડામાંથી બનેલી કચરાપેટીઓ ત્યાં મુકાઈ — ચાર, દરેક અલગ રંગે રંગાયેલી, "બળે તેવો કચરો", "ન બળે તેવો કચરો", "ખાલી ડબ્બા", "ખાલી બોટલો" લખેલું, સાથે સુંદર ચિત્રો પણ. ડબ્બામાંથી બનેલી સિગારેટની રાખ મૂકવાની જગ્યા પણ હતી. કચરાપેટીઓ મુકાયા પછીથી, પહેલા કરતાં વધુ લોકો ત્યાં ભેગા થવા લાગ્યા છે. ત્યાં બેઠેલા લોકોના જણાવ્યા મુજબ, નજીકની હાઈસ્કૂલના વિદ્યાર્થીઓએ તે બનાવ્યાં. હું તાજેતરના હાઈસ્કૂલ વિદ્યાર્થીઓના ખરાબ વર્તનથી નિરાશ હતી, પણ એ જાણીને કે બધા હાઈસ્કૂલ વિદ્યાર્થીઓ એવા નથી હોતા, દિલને ખરેખર હૂંફ મળી.
Q1

Same passage — underline ①

①前よりも人が集まってきていますとあるが、なぜ前よりも人が集まってくるのか。① Mae yori mo hito ga atsumatte kite imasu to aru ga, naze mae yori mo hito ga atsumatte kuru no ka.

EN: The passage says "more people have been gathering than before" (underline ①) — why are more people gathering than before?
OptionJapaneseEnglish
1ゴミを捨てるため。Gomi o suteru tame.In order to throw away trash.
2清潔で気持ちのいい場所になったから。Seiketsu de kimochi no ii basho ni natta kara.Because it became a clean, pleasant place.
3高校生たちの態度が良くなったから。Koukousei-tachi no taido ga yoku natta kara.Because the high schoolers' attitude improved.
4タバコを吸う場所ができたから。Tabako o suu basho ga dekita kara.Because a smoking spot was created.

Why (booklet-confirmed, 問1: 2): The passage's whole arc is: messy/unpleasant bench area → handmade trash cans appear → the area becomes clean → more people gather. Option 1 mistakes the trash cans' purpose for the reason people gather (they gather because the place is now pleasant, not specifically to throw away trash). Option 3 is never stated — nothing says the specific high schoolers who littered changed their behavior; different students built the cans. Option 4 wrongly generalizes the small cigarette-butt receptacle into "a smoking spot," which isn't what draws the crowd.

Q2

Same passage — underline ②

②そんな高校生とは、どんな高校生のことを言っているのか。② Sonna koukousei to wa, donna koukousei no koto o itte iru no ka.

The sentence reads: "最近の高校生の態度が悪いのにはあきれていたのですが、そんな高校生ばかりではないとわかり…" — "I was appalled at how poorly today's high schoolers behave, but learning not all high schoolers are like that..."

EN: What kind of high schoolers does "そんな高校生" ("high schoolers like that") refer to?
OptionJapaneseEnglish
1ゴミ箱を作った生徒たちGomibako o tsukutta seito-tachiThe students who made the trash can
2学校帰りにベンチに座っておしゃべりをする生徒たちGakkou-gaeri ni benchi ni suwatte oshaberi o suru seito-tachiStudents who sit on the bench chatting on the way home
3心がやさしい生徒たちKokoro ga yasashii seito-tachiKind-hearted students
4態度が悪い生徒たちTaido ga warui seito-tachiBadly-behaved students

Why (booklet-confirmed, 問2: 4): "そんな" is a forward-pointing-back demonstrative here — it reaches back to the clause immediately before it: "最近の高校生の態度が悪い" ("today's high schoolers behave badly"). "そんな高校生ばかりではない" = "not ALL high schoolers are like that [badly-behaved]" — meaning the trash-can-building students proved an exception to the writer's earlier low opinion. Options 1–3 all describe students in a positive or neutral light, but そんな specifically echoes the negative description right before it, not the trash-can builders themselves.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

No vocabulary confusion cluster today — instead, compare which reading strategy unlocks each question:

QuestionStrategy usedWhat the answer choice testsTrap to avoid
練習 #1/#5 Track exactly who does what to what "うちのクラブ" (our club) vs. 美術部 (art club) are two different groups; the painting target is the ゴミ箱, not the ベンチ Don't merge two separate groups/objects just because they appear in the same sentence — check exactly who/what each verb attaches to.
練習 #3 Distinguish "a bench along my route" from "the school's bench" The passage never says the bench belongs to the school, even though school kids use it A person or thing associated with a place isn't the same as that place being owned by them.
Q1 Track cause → effect across the whole passage arc "Clean and pleasant" is the actual cause of more people gathering — not the trash cans' function itself Don't confuse an object's purpose (holding trash) with the broader effect it has (making the area pleasant).
Q2 Resolve a forward-looking そんな by re-reading the clause right before it そんな高校生 = 態度が悪い高校生, the exact phrase used one clause earlier Don't let a demonstrative default to the most recently-mentioned noun (ゴミ箱を作った生徒) — trace it to the specific description it's echoing.
