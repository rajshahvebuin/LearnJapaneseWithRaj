The passage is from 宮沢賢治『銀河鉄道の夜』("Ginga Tetsudou no Yoru," "Night on the Galactic Railroad") — a scene the 練習 dialogue discusses in advance, then the 問題 presents in full.

1 Reading Strategy

Key idea

情景や登場人物の様子、気持ちを読み取ろうJoukei ya toujou jinbutsu no yousu, kimochi o yomitorou

Try to comprehend the scene, the characters' physical states, and their feelings — in novels, emotion is often shown through action or appearance rather than stated outright. The book gives three examples:

  • 先生は意外なように（質問に答えない）カムパネルラを見ていました。Sensei wa igai na you ni (shitsumon ni kotaenai) Kanpanerura o mite imashita.
    …先生はカムパネルラが答えられると思っているから「意外」。…Sensei wa Kanpanerura ga kotaerareru to omotte iru kara "igai." — The teacher looks at Campanella (who doesn't answer the question) as if "surprised" — because the teacher expected Campanella, of all students, to be able to answer.
  • もじもじ立ち上がったままやはり答えができませんでした。Mojimoji tachiagatta mama yahari kotae ga dekimasen deshita.
    ……したいことがはっきりできないときに「もじもじ」する。……Shitai koto ga hakkiri dekinai toki ni "mojimoji" suru. — One "fidgets" (もじもじ) when unable to clearly do what one wants to.
  • ジョバンニはまっ赤になってうなずきました。Jobanni wa massaka ni natte unazukimashita.
    …恥ずかしいときや怒ったときに「真っ赤」になる。…Hazukashii toki ya okotta toki ni "makka" ni naru. — One turns "bright red" when embarrassed or angry.
HI: उपन्यासों में भावनाएँ अक्सर सीधे नहीं बताई जातीं — बल्कि हरकतों या चेहरे की हालत से जताई जाती हैं, जैसे झिझकना या शरमाकर लाल हो जाना। GU: નવલકથાઓમાં લાગણીઓ ઘણી વાર સીધી નથી કહેવાતી — બલ્કે હલનચલન કે ચહેરાની હાલત દ્વારા બતાવાય છે, જેમ કે ખચકાવું અથવા શરમથી લાલ થઈ જવું.

#StatementTrue/False
1娘は「銀河鉄道の夜」という本を読んだことがない。Musume wa "Ginga tetsudou no yoru" to iu hon o yonda koto ga nai. — The daughter has never read "Night on the Galactic Railroad."False
2カンパネルラとジョバンニは本の中の登場人物である。Kanpanerura to Jobanni wa hon no naka no toujou jinbutsu de aru. — Campanella and Giovanni are characters in the book.True
3カンパネルラはジョバンニの友だちである。Kanpanerura wa Jobanni no tomodachi de aru. — Campanella is Giovanni's friend.True
4ジョバンニは病気である。Jobanni wa byouki de aru. — Giovanni is sick.False
5カンパネルラはひどい人だ。Kanpanerura wa hidoi hito da. — Campanella is a cruel person.False

Why: The daughter says she "borrowed it at school today and read it" (今日学校で借りて読んだんだけど) — she HAS read it, so #1 is false. Both mother and daughter discuss Campanella and Giovanni as characters within the book ("主人公の友だち"), confirming #2. The mother explicitly calls Campanella "主人公の友だち" (the protagonist Giovanni's friend), confirming #3. It's Giovanni's mother, not Giovanni himself, who is described as sick ("お母さんも病気で"), so #4 is false. The daughter's closing "ひどい！" (how cruel!) is her reaction to the unfairness of the STORY (Campanella dying despite being kind), not a judgment of Campanella's character — Campanella is described as "優しくて" (kind), so #5 misreads who "ひどい" is aimed at.

GU: "તો બાળકો, શું તમને ખબર છે કે આ ધૂંધળી સફેદ વસ્તુ ખરેખર શું છે, જેને ક્યારેક નદી કહેવાય છે, ક્યારેક વહી ગયેલા દૂધનું નિશાન?" […] કાનપાનેરુરાએ હાથ ઊંચો કર્યો. પછી ચાર-પાંચ બીજાઓએ પણ હાથ ઊંચો કર્યો. જોબાન્નીએ પણ હાથ ઊંચો કરવાનો પ્રયત્ન કર્યો, પણ ઝડપથી રોકી લીધો. તેણે ક્યારેક એક મેગેઝિનમાં વાંચ્યું હતું કે એ બધા તારા છે, પણ હમણાં જોબાન્નીને શાળામાં પણ રોજ ઊંઘ આવે છે, વાંચવાનો સમય અને પુસ્તકો બંને નથી, એટલે તેને લાગે છે જાણે તેને કંઈ પણ બરાબર સમજાતું નથી. પણ શિક્ષિકાએ પહેલેથી જ આ જોઈ લીધું હતું. "જોબાન્ની, તને ખબર છે ને?" જોબાન્ની જોરથી ઊભો થયો, પણ ઊભા થતાં જ તેને સ્પષ્ટ જવાબ આપવો મુશ્કેલ લાગ્યો. […] શિક્ષિકાએ ફરી કહ્યું: "મોટા દૂરબીનથી આકાશગંગાને ધ્યાનથી જુઓ તો તે ખરેખર શું છે?" જોબાન્નીએ ફરી વિચાર્યું "તારા," પણ આ વખતે પણ તે ઝડપથી જવાબ ન આપી શક્યો. શિક્ષિકા થોડી વાર મૂંઝાયેલી દેખાઈ, પછી કાનપાનેરુરા તરફ જોઈને બોલી: "તો કાનપાનેરુરા." પણ આટલા ઉત્સાહથી હાથ ઊંચો કરનાર કાનપાનેરુરા પણ ખચકાતાં ઊભો રહ્યો અને જવાબ ન આપી શક્યો. શિક્ષિકા આશ્ચર્યથી થોડી વાર કાનપાનેરુરાને જોતી રહી, પછી ઝડપથી "ભલે" કહીને પોતે તારાનો નકશો બતાવવા લાગી. EN: The passage says Giovanni "started to raise his hand, but quickly stopped" (underline ①) — why did he stop?
OptionJapaneseEnglish
1急に眠くなってしまったから。Kyuu ni nemuku natte shimatta kara.Because he suddenly felt sleepy.
2カンパネルラが手をあげていたから。Kanpanerura ga te o agete ita kara.Because Campanella had raised his hand.

Why (booklet-confirmed, 問1: 3): The very next sentence explains his hesitation: although Giovanni once read that the white band was all stars, "このごろはジョバンニはまるで毎日教室でも眠く…なんだかどんなこともよくわからないという気持ちがするのでした" (lately he's sleepy every day and feels like he doesn't understand anything well anymore). That's a loss of confidence in his own knowledge, not sudden drowsiness in the moment (option 1 misreads an ongoing state as a one-off event), not about Campanella's hand specifically (option 2, which is simply mentioned in passing), and the "自分のものではない雑誌" detail (option 4) never appears at all in this scene.

OptionJapaneseEnglish
1答えがわかっていたのに先生の質問に答えられなくて悔しかったから。Kotae ga wakatte ita noni sensei no shitsumon ni kotaerarenakute kuyashikatta kara.Because he was frustrated at not being able to answer despite knowing the answer.

4自分のことを先生も友達もだれもわかってくれないことがさびしかったから。Jibun no koto o sensei mo tomodachi mo dare mo wakatte kurenai koto ga sabishikatta kara.Because it was lonely that neither the teacher nor any friend understood him.

Why (booklet-confirmed, 問2: 2): Giovanni works out that Campanella must have deliberately stayed silent out of pity, because Campanella noticed how hard Giovanni's life has become (tiring work, no longer playing or talking with friends). The passage's final line spells out the feeling directly: "自分もカムパネルラもあわれなような気がするのでした" ("he felt that both he and Campanella were pitiable") — matching option 2 exactly. Option 1 mistakes the emotion for frustration at himself, but the tears come from realizing Campanella's kindness, not from personal failure. Option 3 and option 4 both exist as background facts in the story, but neither is what the passage says directly triggers this specific moment of tears — the trigger is his realization about Campanella's silent kindness.
