Notes
The book under review, 『われ笑う、ゆえにわれあり』(土屋賢二著／文春文庫), plays on Descartes' "I think, therefore I am" (我思う、ゆえに我あり) — the review's own title swaps 思う (think) for 笑う (laugh).

1 Reading Strategy

Key idea

引用部分を表す「　」『　』に注意！In'you bubun o arawasu "　" '　' ni chuui! — "Pay attention to「 」『 』, which mark quotations!"

「　」『　』の記号はほかの人の話し言葉や、文章や本のタイトルなどを引用するときに使います。強調したい言葉などにも使います。"　" '　' no kigou wa hoka no hito no hanashi kotoba ya, bunshou ya hon no taitoru nado o in'you suru toki ni tsukaimasu. Kyouchou shitai kotoba nado ni mo tsukaimasu.

  • What they mark. 「 」and『 』indicate what someone else has said or written, or quote the title of a book — and are also used just to emphasize certain words.
    HI: 「 」और『 』किसी और के कहे या लिखे हुए को, या किताब के शीर्षक को उद्धृत करने के लिए इस्तेमाल होते हैं — कभी-कभी किसी शब्द पर ज़ोर देने के लिए भी। GU: 「 」અને『 』બીજા કોઈએ કહેલું કે લખેલું, અથવા પુસ્તકનું શીર્ષક ટાંકવા માટે વપરાય છે — ક્યારેક કોઈ શબ્દ પર ભાર મૂકવા માટે પણ.
  • Why this matters for 書評 (book reviews). A review constantly slides between the reviewer's own voice and quoted phrases lifted straight from the book — the quotation marks are your only signal for which is which, and today's passage title, chapter names, and key claims are all wrapped in them.
    HI: किताब समीक्षा में समीक्षक की अपनी बात और किताब से सीधे उद्धृत वाक्यों के बीच लगातार बदलाव होता रहता है — उद्धरण चिह्न ही यह बताते हैं कि कौन-सा हिस्सा किसका है। GU: પુસ્તક સમીક્ષામાં સમીક્ષકની પોતાની વાત અને પુસ્તકમાંથી સીધા ટાંકેલા વાક્યો વચ્ચે સતત ફેરબદલ થાય છે — અવતરણ ચિહ્નો જ બતાવે છે કે કયો ભાગ કોનો છે.

練習 (warm-up — answer on the next page)

次の会話文を読んで、後の文から正しいものを選ぼう。Tsugi no kaiwabun o yonde, ato no bun kara tadashii mono o erabou.

Dialogue

女の人：土屋先生のエッセイ注1、読んだ？Onna no hito: Tsuchiya sensei no essei, yonda?
男の人：土屋先生って、あの哲学の教授？Otoko no hito: Tsuchiya sensei tte, ano tetsugaku no kyouju?
女の人：そう。あの先生、エッセイも書いているのよ。Onna no hito: Sou. Ano sensei, essei mo kaite iru no yo.
男の人：ふーん。哲学の本じゃないんだ。Otoko no hito: Fuun. Tetsugaku no hon janai n da.
女の人：うん、でもね、「人間の定義注2」を書いているところなんて、笑っちゃうんだけど、読んでいくうちに、結局は哲学を考えさせられるっていう不思議なエッセイなの。でも、文章は難しくないから、すらすら読めて、楽しかったー。Onna no hito: Un, demo ne, "Ningen no teigi" o kaite iru tokoro nante, waracchau n da kedo, yonde iku uchi ni, kekkyoku wa tetsugaku o kangaesaserareru tte iu fushigi na essei na no. Demo, bunshou wa muzukashikunai kara, surasura yomete, tanoshikattaa.
男の人：へえ、読みたいな。貸してよ。Otoko no hito: Hee, yomitai na. Kashite yo.

（注1）エッセイ：an essay — ＊随筆。やわらかい文体で気軽に書いた文章。Zuihitsu. Yawarakai buntai de kigaru ni kaita bunshou. — "A casual, softly-styled piece of writing."　·　（注2）定義：a definition

#Statement (Japanese)EnglishTrue/False
1女の人は哲学の本を読んでいる。Onna no hito wa tetsugaku no hon o yonde iru.The woman is reading a philosophy book.False — she read an essay; the man is the one who assumes/checks it isn't a philosophy book.
2土屋先生のエッセイはおもしろい。Tsuchiya sensei no essei wa omoshiroi.Professor Tsuchiya's essay is interesting.True — she laughed while reading it and calls it 楽しかった (enjoyable).
3土屋先生のエッセイは不思議でわかりにくいところがある。Tsuchiya sensei no essei wa fushigi de wakarinikui tokoro ga aru.The essay is strange and has hard-to-understand parts.False — 不思議 describes the surprising effect (making you think about philosophy despite the humor), not that it's hard to understand; she says the opposite: 文章は難しくない, すらすら読めて.
4女の人は土屋先生のエッセイを簡単に読めた。Onna no hito wa Tsuchiya sensei no essei o kantan ni yometa.The woman could read the essay easily.True — "文章は難しくないから、すらすら読めて" (the writing isn't difficult, so it read smoothly).
5男の人は、哲学の本は読みたくない。Otoko no hito wa, tetsugaku no hon wa yomitakunai.The man doesn't want to read philosophy books.False — not stated; he actually asks to borrow the essay.

Answer (confirmed, printed at the bottom of p.89): 2・4.

2 Passage Questions

Main 問題 from p.89: a book review of 『われ笑う、ゆえにわれあり』, followed by 問1 and 問2. Words with a dotted underline are clickable/tappable (or hover on desktop) for a popup with romaji + English/Hindi/Gujarati meaning.

Passage

Book review

『われ笑う、ゆえにわれあり』土屋賢二著（文春文庫）"Ware warau, yue ni ware ari" Tsuchiya Kenji cho (Bunshun bunko) — "I Laugh, Therefore I Am," by Tsuchiya Kenji (Bunshun Bunko)

作者本人は本書を「ユーモアエッセイ」と称して注1いるが、ユーモアのつもりで読んでいると、いつの間にか鋭い社会批評であったり、冗談だと思えば哲学の問題であったりする。Sakusha honnin wa honsho o "yuumoa essei" to shou shite iru ga, yuumoa no tsumori de yonde iru to, itsu no ma ni ka surudoi shakai hihyou de attari, joudan da to omoeba tetsugaku no mondai de attari suru.

その文章は非常に読みやすい。やさしい言葉や表現を用いているため、哲学の話と気がつかない場合がある。しかし、いつの間にか、本来難しいはずの論理を考えていたりするという①不思議なエッセイである。Sono bunshou wa hijou ni yomiyasui. Yasashii kotoba ya hyougen o mochiite iru tame, tetsugaku no hanashi to ki ga tsukanai baai ga aru. Shikashi, itsu no ma ni ka, honrai muzukashii hazu no ronri o kangaete itari suru to iu ① fushigi na essei de aru.

本書の「人間を定義するのは不可能である」という項目の中で、さまざまな例をあげながら「人間の定義」を論じて注2いるのだが、「我々がどこから見ても人間に見えるが犬から生まれた場合」や、「一生の間に1分間だけカエルになった場合」など、突拍子もない注3条件に笑いながら読み進んでいくと、「定義」とはどういうものかということが、ぼんやりとではあるが見えてくるのである＊。（　②　）にもお勧め注4の一冊である。Honsho no "Ningen o teigi suru no wa fukanou de aru" to iu koumoku no naka de, samazama na rei o agenagara "ningen no teigi" o ronjite iru no da ga, "wareware ga doko kara mite mo ningen ni mieru ga inu kara umareta baai" ya, "isshou no aida ni ippun-kan dake kaeru ni natta baai" nado, toppyoushi mo nai jouken ni warainagara yomisusunde iku to, "teigi" to wa dou iu mono ka to iu koto ga, bon'yari to de wa aru ga miete kuru no de aru. ( ② ) ni mo osusume no issatsu de aru.

（注1）称する：to claim　·　（注2）論じる：to deal with/discuss　·　（注3）突拍子もない：crazy and unrealistic　·　（注4）お勧め：recommended　·　＊ footnote: "You will gradually but vaguely understand what the definition means."

EN: The author himself calls this book a "humor essay," but if you read it expecting humor, before you know it you're reading sharp social commentary — or, if you take it as a joke, a philosophical problem instead. The writing is extremely easy to read; because it uses plain words and expressions, you may not even notice it's discussing philosophy. Yet before you know it, you find yourself pondering logic that should by rights be difficult — a strange (①) kind of essay. In the book's section "Defining a human being is impossible," the author discusses "the definition of a human" through various examples — like "a case where something born from a dog looks human from every angle" or "a case of turning into a frog for exactly one minute during your lifetime." As you laugh your way through these outlandish conditions, what "definition" actually means starts to become visible, if only hazily. It's a recommended read for (②) as well.
HI: लेखक खुद इस किताब को "हास्य निबंध" कहते हैं, पर अगर आप हास्य समझकर पढ़ें, तो पता ही नहीं चलता कि आप कब तीखी सामाजिक टिप्पणी पढ़ने लगे — या मज़ाक समझें तो एक दार्शनिक सवाल बन जाता है। भाषा बेहद आसान है; सरल शब्दों के कारण कभी-कभी पता ही नहीं चलता कि यह दर्शनशास्त्र की बात है। फिर भी, बिना जाने आप कठिन तर्क पर सोचने लगते हैं — एक अजीब (①) निबंध। किताब के "इंसान को परिभाषित करना असंभव है" खंड में, लेखक अजीबोग़रीब उदाहरणों से "इंसान की परिभाषा" पर चर्चा करते हैं — जैसे "कुत्ते से जन्मा कोई प्राणी जो हर तरफ़ से इंसान जैसा दिखे" या "जीवन में सिर्फ़ एक मिनट के लिए मेंढक बन जाना।" इन हास्यास्पद शर्तों पर हँसते हुए पढ़ते-पढ़ते, "परिभाषा" का मतलब धुंधला-सा ही सही, समझ आने लगता है। यह (②) के लिए भी अनुशंसित किताब है।
GU: લેખક પોતે આ પુસ્તકને "હાસ્ય નિબંધ" કહે છે, પણ જો તમે હાસ્ય સમજીને વાંચો, તો ખબર જ નથી પડતી કે ક્યારે તમે તીખી સામાજિક ટિપ્પણી વાંચવા લાગ્યા — અથવા મજાક સમજો તો એક દાર્શનિક પ્રશ્ન બની જાય છે. ભાષા ખૂબ સરળ છે; સાદા શબ્દોને કારણે ક્યારેક ખબર જ નથી પડતી કે આ ફિલસૂફીની વાત છે. છતાં, અજાણતાં તમે અઘરા તર્ક પર વિચારવા લાગો છો — એક વિચિત્ર (①) નિબંધ. પુસ્તકના "માણસને વ્યાખ્યાયિત કરવો અશક્ય છે" વિભાગમાં, લેખક વિચિત્ર ઉદાહરણોથી "માણસની વ્યાખ્યા" ચર્ચે છે — જેમ કે "કૂતરામાંથી જન્મેલું કંઈક જે દરેક રીતે માણસ જેવું દેખાય" અથવા "જીવનમાં ફક્ત એક મિનિટ માટે દેડકો બની જવું." આ હાસ્યાસ્પદ શરતો પર હસતાં હસતાં વાંચતાં, "વ્યાખ્યા" નો અર્થ ઝાંખો પણ સમજાવા લાગે છે. આ (②) માટે પણ ભલામણ કરેલ પુસ્તક છે.

問1　なぜ①不思議なエッセイなのか。Mondai 1: Naze ① fushigi na essei na no ka. — "Why is it a 'strange essay' (①)?"

OptionJapaneseEnglish
1エッセイなのに哲学のことばかり書いてあるから。Essei na noni tetsugaku no koto bakari kaite aru kara.Because even though it's an essay, it only ever talks about philosophy.
2やさしい言葉や表現を用いているから。Yasashii kotoba ya hyougen o mochiite iru kara.Because it uses plain words and expressions.
3知らないうちに、哲学的なことを考えさせられるから。Shiranai uchi ni, tetsugaku-teki na koto o kangaesaserareru kara.Because, without realizing it, you're made to think about philosophical things.
4理解しにくい冗談が多いから。Rikai shinikui joudan ga ooi kara.Because it has many hard-to-understand jokes.

Why (answer confirmed: booklet index 117, "第5週" → "6日目 (p.88～p.89)" → 問1:3): The sentence right before ① says exactly this: 「いつの間にか、本来難しいはずの論理を考えていたりする」("before you know it, you find yourself pondering logic that should by rights be difficult") — the "strangeness" is that a book you picked up for laughs quietly makes you think philosophically. Option 2 describes a true fact about the book, but it's the cause of the surprise, not the surprise itself; options 1 and 4 aren't supported by the passage at all.

問2　（②）に入るものとして最も適当なものはどれか。Mondai 2: ( ② ) ni hairu mono toshite mottomo tekitou na mono wa dore ka. — "What best fits in blank (②)?"

OptionJapaneseEnglish
1ユーモアには興味がないという若者Yuumoa ni wa kyoumi ga nai to iu wakamonoyoung people who have no interest in humor
2哲学には興味がないという若者Tetsugaku ni wa kyoumi ga nai to iu wakamonoyoung people who have no interest in philosophy
3エッセイには興味がないという若者Essei ni wa kyoumi ga nai to iu wakamonoyoung people who have no interest in essays
4条件には興味がないという若者Jouken ni wa kyoumi ga nai to iu wakamonoyoung people who have no interest in "conditions"

Why (answer confirmed: booklet index 117, "第5週" → "6日目 (p.88～p.89)" → 問2:2): The whole review's point is that this book sneaks philosophical thinking past readers who wouldn't otherwise pick up a philosophy book — via humor and easy language. So it's precisely recommended for young readers who think they have no interest in philosophy (②), since the book makes them engage with philosophical ideas without realizing it. Options 1, 3, and 4 name the wrong thing to be uninterested in — the book's whole selling point is delivering philosophy to people who'd normally avoid it, not humor, essays in general, or the "condition" examples themselves.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Compare which part of the passage each question anchors to, and what trap the wrong options set:

QuestionAnchor sentenceWhat the correct answer capturesTrap to avoid
問1 「いつの間にか、本来難しいはずの論理を考えていたりする」 The surprise itself: being made to think philosophically without realizing it Don't confuse the cause of the surprise (easy language, option 2) with the surprise (option 3) itself.
問2 Whole review's throughline: humor smuggling in philosophy The book's target audience is people put off by philosophy, not by humor/essays/the silly examples The blank names what the audience has no interest in — match it to the subject the book quietly teaches (philosophy), not to its delivery method (humor, essays, silly conditions).
Idiom note: 「　」and『　』aren't interchangeable by accident here — the review uses『われ笑う、ゆえにわれあり』(double brackets) for the book's own title, but single「　」for quoted phrases and chapter names inside it (「人間の定義」「人間を定義するのは不可能である」) — double brackets nest around a title that itself might contain quoted material.
