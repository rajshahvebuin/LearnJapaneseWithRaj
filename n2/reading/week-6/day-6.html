1 Reading Strategy

Key idea

複雑な文章を整理して理解しよう③Fukuzatsu na bunshou o seiri shite rikai shiyou ③

Try to understand the complicated sentences by reorganizing them! ③ A narrative-style passage like today's breaks into paragraph blocks:

第4段落Dai-yon danraku

2ラクダの計算を使うと何かを分けるときに必ずうまくいく。Rakuda no keisan o tsukau to nanika o wakeru toki ni kanarazu umaku iku.Using the camel calculation always works out when dividing something.False
3ラクダの計算の話には、借りたラクダを返せた話と返せない話がある。Rakuda no keisan no hanashi ni wa, karita rakuda o kaeseta hanashi to kaesenai hanashi ga aru.The camel-calculation story includes both a version where the borrowed camel was returned and one where it wasn't.True
4父親はラクダの計算を子どもから教えてもらった。Chichioya wa rakuda no keisan o kodomo kara oshiete moratta.The father learned the camel calculation from the child.False
5子どもは父親のヒントでラクダの計算を理解した。Kodomo wa chichioya no hinto de rakuda no keisan o rikai shita.The child understood the camel calculation thanks to the father's hint.True

Why (confirmed on p.107, "3・5"): The child already knows the first (successful) version and only asks about "the part that comes after" — so item 1 (never heard it at all) is false. The second version in the dialogue fails, so item 2 (always works) is false. Together, the two versions the father and child discuss are exactly one that worked and one that didn't — item 3 true. It's the father explaining fractions to the child, not the reverse — item 4 false — and the child's closing "あ、そうか" (oh, I see!), right after following the father's fraction hint, shows she worked it out herself thanks to him — item 5 true.

2 Passage Questions

The 問題 on p.107 is the classic "17-camel inheritance" folktale, used to introduce fractions that don't add up to a whole. Words with a dotted underline are clickable/tappable (or hover on desktop) for a popup with romaji + English/Hindi/Gujarati meaning.

Passage

数学に関する文章 (Suugaku ni kansuru bunshou)

みなさんは、分数が得意だろうか。分数と聞くだけで、嫌だと思う人もいるかもしれない。実はコンピューターでさえ、分数は苦手だとか。でも、①この話を読めば、きっと分数に興味を持つようになるだろう。Minasan wa, bunsuu ga tokui darou ka. Bunsuu to kiku dake de, iya da to omou hito mo iru kamoshirenai. Jitsu wa konpyuutaa de sae, bunsuu wa nigate da toka. Demo, ① kono hanashi o yomeba, kitto bunsuu ni kyoumi o motsu you ni naru darou.

昔、アラビア注1のある村で年老いた商人が3人の息子に次のような言葉を残して亡くなった。「私が死んだらラクダをお前たちにやる。長男は1/2、次男は1/3、三男は1/9とする。」ところが、ラクダの数は17頭だったので、2でも、3でも9でも割り切れない注2。3人はけんかを始めてしまった。Mukashi, Arabia no aru mura de toshioita shounin ga san-nin no musuko ni tsugi no you na kotoba o nokoshite nakunatta. "Watashi ga shindara rakuda o omaetachi ni yaru. Chounan wa nibun-no-ichi, jinan wa sanbun-no-ichi, sannan wa kyuubun-no-ichi to suru." Tokoro ga, rakuda no kazu wa juunana-tou datta node, ni de mo, san de mo kyuu de mo warikirenai. San-nin wa kenka o hajimete shimatta.

そこへ旅の老人がラクダに乗ってやってきた。そしてけんかの理由を聞き、「それなら私のラクダを貸してあげよう。」と言った。父親の残したラクダに1頭足すと18頭になったので、長男は9頭、次男は6頭、三男は2頭で父親の言葉どおりに分けることができた。余った1頭は元通りに旅の老人が連れていった。Soko e tabi no roujin ga rakuda ni notte yatte kita. Soshite kenka no riyuu o kiki, "Sore nara watashi no rakuda o kashite ageyou." to itta. Chichioya no nokoshita rakuda ni ittou tasu to juuhachi-tou ni natta node, chounan wa kyuu-tou, jinan wa roku-tou, sannan wa ni-tou de chichioya no kotoba doori ni wakeru koto ga dekita. Amatta ittou wa mototoori ni tabi no roujin ga tsurete itta.

何年か後、同じように11頭のラクダを持っている老人が「長男には1/2、次男には1/3、三男には1/6のラクダを。」と言い残して死んでしまった。そこで、前述の注3話を思い出した近所の人が自分のラクダを連れてきて解決してみせようとした*。長男には1/2の6頭、次男には1/3の4頭、三男には1/6の2頭。Nan-nen ka nochi, onaji you ni juuichi-tou no rakuda o motte iru roujin ga "Chounan ni wa nibun-no-ichi, jinan ni wa sanbun-no-ichi, sannan ni wa rokubun-no-ichi no rakuda o." to iinokoshite shinde shimatta. Soko de, zenjutsu no hanashi o omoidashita kinjo no hito ga jibun no rakuda o tsurete kite kaiketsu shite miseyou to shita. Chounan ni wa nibun-no-ichi no roku-tou, jinan ni wa sanbun-no-ichi no yon-tou, sannan ni wa rokubun-no-ichi no ni-tou.

ところが、今度は3人のラクダを合計すると6＋4＋2＝12頭となり、連れてきたラクダを持って帰ることができず、損をしてしまった。前の話では1/2＋1/3＋1/9＝17/18になるのに対し、1/2＋1/3＋1/6＝1となり、（②）に気づかなかったのだ。Tokoro ga, kondo wa san-nin no rakuda o goukei suru to roku tasu yon tasu ni de juuni-tou to nari, tsurete kita rakuda o motte kaeru koto ga dekizu, son o shite shimatta. Mae no hanashi de wa nibun-no-ichi tasu sanbun-no-ichi tasu kyuubun-no-ichi wa juunanabun-no-juuhachi ni naru no ni taishi, nibun-no-ichi tasu sanbun-no-ichi tasu rokubun-no-ichi wa ichi to nari, (___) ni kizukanakatta no da.

注1 アラビア：Arabia Chuu 1: Arabia　·　注2 割り切れる：can be divided by ... Chuu 2: warikireru — "to be evenly divisible"　·　注3 前述の：前に述べた Chuu 3: zenjutsu no: mae ni nobeta — "previously mentioned"　·　* footnote translation: "A neighbor who remembered the story he had heard earlier brought his own camel to settle the problem."

EN: Are you good at fractions? Just hearing the word "fraction" makes some people uneasy. Apparently even computers are bad at fractions. But if you read this story, you'll surely become interested in fractions. Long ago, in a certain village in Arabia, an elderly merchant died, leaving these words to his three sons: "When I die, give the camels to you three. The eldest gets 1/2, the second son 1/3, and the third son 1/9." But there were 17 camels, which can't be evenly divided by 2, 3, or 9. The three sons began to quarrel. Then a traveling old man came riding up on his camel. Hearing the reason for the quarrel, he said, "In that case, let me lend you my camel." Adding one camel to the father's 17 made 18, so the eldest got 9, the second son 6, and the third son 2 — dividing it exactly as the father had said. The old traveler took his one leftover camel back and went on his way. Some years later, another old man with 11 camels, in exactly the same way, left these dying words: "Give the eldest 1/2, the second son 1/3, and the third son 1/6 of the camels." A neighbor who remembered the earlier story brought his own camel to try to solve it the same way. The eldest got 1/2 of 6, the second son 1/3 of 4, and the third son 1/6 of 2. But this time, when the three sons' camels were added up, 6+4+2 = 12 — and the neighbor couldn't take his camel back, ending up at a loss. Whereas the earlier story worked out to 1/2+1/3+1/9 = 17/18, this time 1/2+1/3+1/6 = 1 exactly — and nobody had noticed [blank].
HI: क्या आप भिन्न (fraction) में अच्छे हैं? "भिन्न" शब्द सुनते ही कुछ लोगों को बेचैनी होती है। कहते हैं कंप्यूटर भी भिन्न में कमज़ोर होते हैं। पर अगर आप यह कहानी पढ़ें, तो ज़रूर भिन्न में दिलचस्पी लेने लगेंगे। बहुत पुरानी बात है, अरब के एक गाँव में एक बूढ़े व्यापारी की मृत्यु हो गई, जो अपने तीन बेटों के लिए यह वसीयत छोड़ गया: "मेरी मृत्यु के बाद तुम तीनों को ऊँट मिलेंगे। बड़े बेटे को 1/2, दूसरे को 1/3, और तीसरे को 1/9।" पर ऊँट थे 17, जो 2, 3 या 9 से पूरी तरह विभाज्य नहीं होते। तीनों भाई झगड़ने लगे। तभी एक यात्री बूढ़ा अपने ऊँट पर सवार होकर आया। झगड़े की वजह सुनकर उसने कहा, "तो फिर, मैं अपना ऊँट उधार दे देता हूँ।" पिता के 17 ऊँटों में एक और मिलाने से 18 हो गए, तो बड़े को 9, दूसरे को 6, तीसरे को 2 मिले — बिल्कुल पिता की वसीयत के मुताबिक़। बचा हुआ एक ऊँट यात्री बूढ़ा वापस लेकर चला गया। कुछ साल बाद, ठीक वैसे ही, 11 ऊँटों वाले एक और बूढ़े ने मरते समय कहा: "बड़े बेटे को 1/2, दूसरे को 1/3, तीसरे को 1/6 ऊँट देना।" पहले वाली कहानी याद रखने वाले एक पड़ोसी ने अपना ऊँट लाकर वैसे ही हल करने की कोशिश की। बड़े बेटे को 6 (1/2), दूसरे को 4 (1/3), तीसरे को 2 (1/6) मिले। पर इस बार, तीनों भाइयों के ऊँट जोड़ने पर 6+4+2 = 12 निकले — और पड़ोसी अपना ऊँट वापस नहीं ले जा सका, उसे नुकसान झेलना पड़ा। पहली कहानी में 1/2+1/3+1/9 = 17/18 बनता था, जबकि इस बार 1/2+1/3+1/6 पूरा 1 बनता है — और यह बात किसी को समझ नहीं आई [रिक्त स्थान]।
GU: શું તમે અપૂર્ણાંક (fraction)માં સારા છો? "અપૂર્ણાંક" શબ્દ સાંભળતાં જ કેટલાકને અગવડ થાય છે. કહેવાય છે કમ્પ્યુટર પણ અપૂર્ણાંકમાં નબળા હોય છે. પણ જો તમે આ વાર્તા વાંચો, તો ચોક્કસ અપૂર્ણાંકમાં રસ પડવા લાગશે. બહુ જૂની વાત છે, અરબસ્તાનના એક ગામમાં એક વૃદ્ધ વેપારીનું અવસાન થયું, જે પોતાના ત્રણ દીકરાઓ માટે આ વસિયત મૂકી ગયો: "મારા મૃત્યુ પછી તમને ત્રણેયને ઊંટ મળશે. મોટા દીકરાને 1/2, બીજાને 1/3, અને ત્રીજાને 1/9." પણ ઊંટ હતા 17, જે 2, 3 કે 9 વડે પૂરેપૂરા ભાગી શકાય તેવા નહોતા. ત્રણેય ભાઈઓ ઝઘડવા લાગ્યા. એ જ વખતે એક પ્રવાસી વૃદ્ધ પોતાના ઊંટ પર સવાર થઈને આવ્યો. ઝઘડાનું કારણ સાંભળીને તેણે કહ્યું, "તો પછી, હું મારું ઊંટ ઉધાર આપું છું." પિતાના 17 ઊંટમાં એક ઉમેરતાં 18 થયા, તો મોટાને 9, બીજાને 6, ત્રીજાને 2 મળ્યા — બરાબર પિતાની વસિયત મુજબ. બચેલું એક ઊંટ પ્રવાસી વૃદ્ધ પાછું લઈને ચાલ્યો ગયો. થોડાં વર્ષો પછી, બરાબર એ જ રીતે, 11 ઊંટ ધરાવતા બીજા એક વૃદ્ધે મરતી વખતે કહ્યું: "મોટા દીકરાને 1/2, બીજાને 1/3, ત્રીજાને 1/6 ઊંટ આપજો." પહેલી વાર્તા યાદ રાખનાર એક પડોશીએ પોતાનું ઊંટ લાવીને એ જ રીતે ઉકેલવાનો પ્રયત્ન કર્યો. મોટા દીકરાને 6 (1/2), બીજાને 4 (1/3), ત્રીજાને 2 (1/6) મળ્યા. પણ આ વખતે, ત્રણેય ભાઈઓના ઊંટ ભેગા કરતાં 6+4+2 = 12 થયા — અને પડોશી પોતાનું ઊંટ પાછું લઈ ન શક્યો, તેને નુકસાન વેઠવું પડ્યું. પહેલી વાર્તામાં 1/2+1/3+1/9 = 17/18 થતું હતું, જ્યારે આ વખતે 1/2+1/3+1/6 બરાબર 1 થાય છે — અને આ વાત કોઈને સમજાઈ નહીં [ખાલી જગ્યા].
Q1

問1　①この話とはどんな話か。最も適当なものを選びなさい。Toi 1: ① Kono hanashi to wa donna hanashi ka. Mottomo tekitou na mono o erabinasai.

OptionJapaneseEnglish
1分数の苦手な人の話Bunsuu no nigate na hito no hanashiA story about people who are bad at fractions
2ラクダの分配の話Rakuda no bunpai no hanashiA story about dividing up camels
3分数に興味を持った人の話Bunsuu ni kyoumi o motta hito no hanashiA story about a person who became interested in fractions
4アラビアの老人の話Arabia no roujin no hanashiA story about an old man from Arabia

Why (booklet-confirmed, 問1: 2): "この話" points forward to the whole folktale that follows, which is entirely about dividing camels among heirs using fractions — matching option 2. Options 1 and 3 both describe people's feelings about fractions in general (the opening remark), not the story itself; option 4 names only one character (the traveling old man), not the story as a whole.

Q2

問2　（②）に入る最も適当なものはどれか。Toi 2: (②) ni hairu mottomo tekitou na mono wa dore ka.

OptionJapaneseEnglish
1数が少なくなることKazu ga sukunaku naru kotoThat the number becomes smaller
2数が多くなることKazu ga ooku naru kotoThat the number becomes larger
3割り切れて余りがなくなることWarikirete amari ga nakunaru kotoThat it divides evenly, with nothing left over
4また割り切れなくなることMata warikirenaku naru kotoThat it becomes indivisible again

Why (booklet-confirmed, 問2: 3): The passage sets up a direct contrast: the first case's fractions sum to 17/18 (less than 1, leaving a leftover sliver that lets the borrowed camel always be returned), while the second case's fractions sum to exactly 1/2+1/3+1/6 = 1 (dividing evenly, with nothing left over). Because the total was already whole, there was never really a "spare" camel to lend — the neighbor's plan couldn't work the same way, and nobody had noticed this key difference beforehand. That matches option 3. Options 1 and 2 misread the issue as being about camel quantity rather than about whether the fractions divide evenly, and option 4 restates the wrong case (the fractions actually became more exactly divisible, not less).
