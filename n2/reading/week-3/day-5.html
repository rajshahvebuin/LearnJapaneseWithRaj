Notes
This day's theme is 説明文 (explanatory essays), and the ✿ tip is the first of a two-part series on demonstratives (指示語) — most often これ／それ points back to the content that came right before it, though the book warns (and promises to revisit in Week 4 Day 5) that this isn't always true.

1 Reading Strategy

Key idea

指示語に注意！①Shijigo ni chuui! (1)

Pay attention to demonstratives! Words like これ／それ／この／その most often point back to whatever content came immediately before them.

HI: सूचक शब्दों (यह/वह) पर ध्यान दें! ये शब्द अक्सर ठीक पहले आई बात की ओर इशारा करते हैं। GU: સૂચક શબ્દો (આ/તે) પર ધ્યાન આપો! આ શબ્દો મોટે ભાગે તરત પહેલાં આવેલી વાત તરફ ઈશારો કરે છે.

もうすぐサクラが咲く。これを楽しみにしている人は多い。Mou sugu sakura ga saku. Kore o tanoshimi ni shite iru hito wa ooi.

"The cherry blossoms will bloom soon. Many people are looking forward to this." — これ points back to the whole preceding sentence (サクラが咲くこと, "the blossoms blooming"), not just to サクラ itself.

◆ 内容が一文だけでなく長い場合もある。Naiyou ga ichibun dake de naku nagai baai mo aru. — Sometimes the referent is longer than just one sentence.

サクラが咲くと酒が飲める。ぼくには花の美しさよりそれのほうが楽しみなのだ。Sakura ga saku to sake ga nomeru. Boku ni wa hana no utsukushisa yori sore no hou ga tanoshimi na no da.

"When the cherry blossoms bloom, you get to drink sake. For me, that is more of a treat than the beauty of the flowers." — それ points to "getting to drink sake," which you must work out from the content, not from mere proximity — the phrase right before それ is actually 花の美しさ, and yet それ does not refer to that.

すぐ前といっても、「花の美しさ」ではないことは内容から考える。Sugu mae to itte mo, "hana no utsukushisa" de wa nai koto wa naiyou kara kangaeru. — "Even though it's the phrase right before, you have to reason from the content that it's not 'the beauty of the flowers.'" (And demonstratives sometimes point forward instead of back — more on that in Week 4 Day 5.)

練習 (Warm-up — answer confirmed on the next page)

男の人：あれ、ここも禁煙？　この会社も吸えるところがどんどん減っちゃって、居心地悪くなった注1よな。道路でも吸ったら罰金注2取られるっていうし。喫煙者注3はつらいよ。Otoko no hito: Are, koko mo kin'en? Kono kaisha mo sueru tokoro ga dondon hecchatte, igokochi warukunatta yo na. Douro de mo suttara bakkin torareru tte iu shi. Kitsuensha wa tsurai yo.

女の人：じゃ、やめちゃえばいいじゃない。どんどん値上がりしているんでしょ。体に悪いし、それに、周りの人にとっても迷惑だし。Onna no hito: Ja, yamechaeba ii ja nai. Dondon neagari shite irun desho. Karada ni warui shi, sore ni, mawari no hito ni totte mo meiwaku da shi.

男の人：……。でも、だったら、酔っ払いだって迷惑だと思うんだけどなあ。Otoko no hito: ...Demo, dattara, yopparai datte meiwaku da to omoun dakedo naa.

女の人：いえ、お酒より絶対に迷惑よ。煙はいやでも私たちの体に入ってくるんだから。Onna no hito: Ie, osake yori zettai ni meiwaku yo. Kemuri wa iya demo watashitachi no karada ni haitte kurun dakara.

注1 居心地(が)悪い：to feel uncomfortable　注2 罰金：a fine　注3 喫煙者：a smoker — Man: "Huh, no smoking here too? This company's smoking spots keep shrinking — it's gotten uncomfortable. And I hear you get fined for smoking on the street now too. Being a smoker is rough." — Woman: "Then just quit. Prices keep going up too, right? It's bad for your health, and on top of that, it's a nuisance to people around you." — Man: "...But then, I'd think drunk people are a nuisance too." — Woman: "No, it's absolutely more of a nuisance than alcohol. Smoke gets into our bodies whether we like it or not."

#StatementTrue/False
1この会社では、タバコを吸う場所がない。Kono kaisha de wa, tabako o suu basho ga nai. — This company has no place to smoke at all.False
2男の人は道路でタバコを吸って罰金を払った。Otoko no hito wa douro de tabako o sutte bakkin o haratta. — The man smoked on the street and paid a fine.False
3女の人は男の人にタバコをやめるように勧めている。Onna no hito wa otoko no hito ni tabako o yameru you ni susumete iru. — The woman is urging the man to quit smoking.True
4男の人は、喫煙者は酔っ払いほど迷惑をかけていないと言っている。Otoko no hito wa, kitsuensha wa yopparai hodo meiwaku o kakete inai to itte iru. — The man states outright that smokers cause less nuisance than drunk people.False
5女の人は、酔っ払いより喫煙者のほうが迷惑だと思っている。Onna no hito wa, yopparai yori kitsuensha no hou ga meiwaku da to omotte iru. — The woman thinks smokers are more of a nuisance than drunk people.True

Why: The shrinking smoking spots (#1) and the fine (#2) are both things the man merely mentions in passing — spots are shrinking, not gone, and he only says he heard about fines, never that he was actually fined. The woman's "じゃ、やめちゃえばいい" (#3) is a direct suggestion to quit. The man only questions whether drunks aren't equally a nuisance (a tentative "...と思うんだけどなあ") — he never flatly states smokers are less of a problem (#4 overstates his hedge into a claim). The woman, by contrast, flatly declares smoke is 絶対に (absolutely) more of a nuisance than alcohol (#5 true).

2 Passage Questions

The main 問題 (p.53) is a short essay comparing why smoking is restricted more heavily than drinking. Words with a dotted underline are clickable/tappable (or hover on desktop) for a popup with romaji + English/Hindi/Gujarati meaning.

Passage

Essay excerpt (no title given)

近年注1、日本では「分煙」注2「終日禁煙」が当たり前になりつつある。電車やホテル、公共施設注3などでも禁煙になっているところがほとんどである。会社でも喫煙できるスペースは年々狭くなっているようだ。また、路上でタバコを吸えば罰金を払わなければいけない地域もある。Kinnen, Nihon de wa "bun'en" "shuujitsu kin'en" ga atarimae ni narit tsutsu aru. Densha ya hoteru, koukyou shisetsu nado de mo kin'en ni natte iru tokoro ga hotondo de aru. Kaisha de mo kitsuen dekiru supeesu wa nen'nen semaku natte iru you da. Mata, rojou de tabako o sueba bakkin o harawanakereba ikenai chiiki mo aru.

喫煙者はこれをかなり不満に思っていて、なぜ酒はよくてタバコはダメなのかと反論注4をする。しかし、よく考えればやはり①その違いは明らかである。酔っ払いが周りに迷惑をかけることはあっても、酒そのものが周囲の人の体に直接影響を与えることはない*。それに対して喫煙にはやっかいな注5煙が付き物注6だ。タバコの先から出る煙は空気中に広がる。つまり、煙は周囲の人々の肺注7にも入るのである。Kitsuensha wa kore o kanari fuman ni omotte ite, naze sake wa yokute tabako wa dame na no ka to hanron o suru. Shikashi, yoku kangaereba yahari ① sono chigai wa akiraka de aru. Yopparai ga mawari ni meiwaku o kakeru koto wa atte mo, sake sono mono ga shuui no hito no karada ni chokusetsu eikyou o ataeru koto wa nai. Sore ni taishite kitsuen ni wa yakkai na kemuri ga tsukimono da. Tabako no saki kara deru kemuri wa kuuki-chuu ni hirogaru. Tsumari, kemuri wa shuui no hitobito no hai ni mo hairu no de aru.

「タバコ臭い」とか「煙い」ということより、②これが喫煙者が嫌われる大きな理由である。"Tabako kusai" to ka "kemui" to iu koto yori, ② kore ga kitsuensha ga kirawareru ookina riyuu de aru.

注1 近年：recently　注2 分煙：separation of smoking areas　注3 公共施設：a public place　注4 反論：to refute/object　注5 やっかいな：cumbersome　注6 付き物：something which comes with it　注7 肺：a lung　＊footnote: Although drinkers disturb other people, they don't harm their health.

EN: In recent years, "separated smoking areas" and "smoke-free all day" have become the norm in Japan. Trains, hotels, and most public facilities have gone smoke-free. Even at companies, the space where you're allowed to smoke seems to shrink every year. In some areas, smoking on the street even carries a fine. Smokers find this quite unfair, and push back by asking why drinking is fine but smoking isn't. But if you think it through, ①the difference is actually clear. Even though a drunk person can be a nuisance to those around them, alcohol itself never has a direct physical effect on other people's bodies. Smoking, by contrast, inevitably comes with troublesome smoke. The smoke drifting off the end of a cigarette spreads through the air — meaning it enters the lungs of the people around the smoker too. Rather than "cigarettes smell bad" or "the smoke stings," ②this is the real, major reason smokers are disliked.
HI: हाल के वर्षों में, जापान में "धूम्रपान क्षेत्र अलग करना" और "पूरे दिन धूम्रपान-निषेध" सामान्य होता जा रहा है। ट्रेनें, होटल और लगभग सभी सार्वजनिक स्थान धूम्रपान-मुक्त हो गए हैं। कंपनियों में भी धूम्रपान की जगह हर साल सिकुड़ती जा रही है। कुछ इलाकों में तो सड़क पर धूम्रपान करने पर जुर्माना भी लगता है। धूम्रपान करने वाले इसे काफ़ी नाइंसाफ़ी मानते हैं और सवाल उठाते हैं कि शराब ठीक है तो सिगरेट क्यों नहीं। पर अगर ध्यान से सोचें, तो ①यह फ़र्क साफ़ है। शराबी भले आस-पास वालों के लिए परेशानी बने, पर शराब खुद किसी और के शरीर पर सीधा असर कभी नहीं डालती। इसके उलट, धूम्रपान के साथ झंझट भरा धुआँ हमेशा जुड़ा होता है। सिगरेट की नोक से निकलने वाला धुआँ हवा में फैलता है — यानी वह आस-पास के लोगों के फेफड़ों में भी जाता है। "सिगरेट से बदबू आती है" या "धुएँ से जलन होती है" — इससे कहीं ज़्यादा, ②यही धूम्रपान करने वालों के नापसंद किए जाने की असली बड़ी वजह है।
GU: તાજેતરના વર્ષોમાં, જાપાનમાં "ધૂમ્રપાન વિસ્તાર અલગ કરવો" અને "આખો દિવસ ધૂમ્રપાન-નિષેધ" સામાન્ય બનતું જાય છે. ટ્રેનો, હોટેલો અને લગભગ બધાં જાહેર સ્થળો ધૂમ્રપાન-મુક્ત બની ગયાં છે. કંપનીઓમાં પણ ધૂમ્રપાનની જગ્યા દર વર્ષે સંકોચાતી જાય છે. કેટલાક વિસ્તારોમાં તો રસ્તા પર ધૂમ્રપાન કરવા પર દંડ પણ થાય છે. ધૂમ્રપાન કરનારાઓ આને ઘણી અન્યાયી માને છે અને સવાલ ઉઠાવે છે કે દારૂ ચાલે તો સિગારેટ કેમ નહીં. પણ જો ધ્યાનથી વિચારીએ, તો ①આ ફરક સ્પષ્ટ છે. દારૂડિયો ભલે આસપાસના લોકો માટે તકલીફ ઊભી કરે, પણ દારૂ પોતે ક્યારેય કોઈના શરીર પર સીધી અસર નથી કરતો. તેનાથી ઊલટું, ધૂમ્રપાન સાથે ઝંઝટવાળો ધુમાડો હંમેશા જોડાયેલો હોય છે. સિગારેટની ટોચેથી નીકળતો ધુમાડો હવામાં ફેલાય છે — એટલે કે તે આસપાસના લોકોના ફેફસાંમાં પણ જાય છે. "સિગારેટની દુર્ગંધ આવે છે" કે "ધુમાડાથી બળતરા થાય છે" — તેના કરતાં ઘણું વધારે, ②આ જ ધૂમ્રપાન કરનારાઓ નાપસંદ થવાનું ખરું મોટું કારણ છે.
Q1

問1　①その違いとは何の違いか。Toi 1: ① Sono chigai to wa nan no chigai ka.

OptionJapaneseEnglish
1禁煙と罰金Kin'en to bakkinNo-smoking rules and fines
2煙と臭いKemuri to nioiSmoke and smell
3酒とタバコSake to tabakoAlcohol and tobacco
4酔っ払いと喫煙家Yopparai to kitsuenkaA drunk person and a smoker

Why (booklet-confirmed, 問1: 3): The whole preceding sentence sets up smokers asking "why is drinking okay but smoking isn't?" — so "その違い" (the/that difference) refers back to the difference between alcohol and tobacco (酒とタバコ) that makes society treat them differently, which is exactly what the rest of the paragraph goes on to explain.

Q2

問2　②これとは何を指しているか。Toi 2: ② Kore to wa nani o sashite iru ka.

OptionJapaneseEnglish
1タバコを吸うことTabako o suu kotoSmoking cigarettes (the act itself)
2タバコが臭く、煙いことTabako ga kusaku, kemui kotoCigarettes smelling bad and stinging with smoke
3タバコの煙が周りの人の健康を害することTabako no kemuri ga mawari no hito no kenkou o gaisuru kotoCigarette smoke harming the health of people nearby
4タバコを吸える場所が狭くなっていることTabako o sueru basho ga semaku natte iru kotoPlaces where you can smoke shrinking

Why (booklet-confirmed, 問2: 3): これ directly follows a clause that explicitly says "とか「煙い」ということより" ("more than [merely] 'the smell' or 'the sting'") — signaling それ is NOT the surface-level smell/irritation complaint (ruling out option 2). Instead it points back to the paragraph's real point: smoke physically enters bystanders' lungs, i.e. harms their health — option 3. Options 1 and 4 describe smoking or its shrinking space in general, not the specific reason smokers are disliked that the essay just built up to.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

No vocabulary confusion cluster today — instead, compare which reading strategy unlocks each question:

QuestionStrategy usedWhat the answer choice testsTrap to avoid
練習 Separate a hedge ("...と思うんだけどなあ") from a flat claim The man only questions the fairness of the smoking crackdown; the woman flatly declares smoke worse than drinking Don't upgrade the man's tentative musing into a firm claim (#4) — only the woman makes an absolute statement (#5).
Q1 Trace その違い back to the sentence that set up the comparison The previous sentence is built entirely around "why is alcohol OK but tobacco isn't" — that IS the difference in question 禁煙・罰金・酔っ払い・喫煙家 are all details mentioned nearby, but none of them is the actual pair being contrasted.
Q2 Notice the explicit contrast marker "…ということより" right before これ これ is deliberately set up as something bigger/deeper than the smell/sting complaint that was just dismissed Option 2 restates exactly the surface-level complaint the sentence just said this reason is bigger than — a classic "picks the nearby phrase, not the real referent" trap.
