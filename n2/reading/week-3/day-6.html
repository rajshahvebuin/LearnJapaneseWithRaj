Notes
This day's theme is 説明文 (explanatory essays), focused on sentence endings built around ない that don't mean straightforward negation — にほかならない ("is precisely..."), にすぎない ("is nothing more than..."), とは限らない ("isn't necessarily..."), and more. The main passage is a popular-science piece on why a bumpy relief globe is scientifically inaccurate.

1 Reading Strategy

Key idea

「ない」が入った文末表現に注意！"Nai" ga haitta bunmatsu hyougen ni chuui!

Pay attention to sentences ending with an expression that contains ない — many of these don't mean simple negation at all; they're set phrases with their own fixed meaning.

HI: "ない" वाले वाक्य-अंत रूपों पर ध्यान दें! इनमें से कई सीधे निषेध (नहीं) का मतलब नहीं रखते — ये तय अर्थ वाले मुहावरेदार रूप हैं। GU: "ない" વાળા વાક્ય-અંતના રૂપો પર ધ્યાન આપો! તેમાંથી ઘણાનો સીધો નકારનો અર્થ નથી હોતો — તે નિશ્ચિત અર્થવાળા રૂઢિપ્રયોગો છે.
Expression≈ MeansEnglish
〜とは限らない~ to wa kagiranai〜ないかもしれない~nai kamo shirenaiis not necessarily the case
〜にすぎない~ni suginai〜だけだ~dake daonly..., nothing more than...

ほかにもたくさんあるよ！Hoka ni mo takusan aru yo! — "There are lots more besides these!"

練習 (Warm-up — answer confirmed on the next page)

A：あ、この地球儀注1、おもしろいね。でこぼこしている注2。A: A, kono chikyuugi, omoshiroi ne. Dekoboko shite iru.

B：そう、山のところは出っ張って注3いるんだ。でもね、これ変だよ。だって、地球の大きさから考えると、国際宇宙ステーション注4だってこの地球儀の表面から1センチぐらいしか離れていないところを回っていることになるんだよ。この山なんて1センチ以上出ている。だから変なんだよ。つまり、正しい地球儀はツルツルでなければいけないんだよ*。B: Sou, yama no tokoro wa deppatte irun da. Demo ne, kore hen da yo. Datte, chikyuu no ookisa kara kangaeru to, kokusai uchuu suteeshon datte kono chikyuugi no hyoumen kara issenchi gurai shika hanarete inai tokoro o mawatte iru koto ni narun da yo. Kono yama nante issenchi ijou dete iru. Dakara hen nan da yo. Tsumari, tadashii chikyuugi wa tsurutsuru de nakereba ikenain da yo.

注1 地球儀：a globe　注2 でこぼこしている：to have an uneven surface　注3 出っ張る：to stick out　注4 国際宇宙ステーション：an international space station　＊footnote: A good globe must have a very smooth surface. — A: "Oh, this globe is interesting — it's all bumpy." — B: "Yeah, the mountains stick out. But you know, that's actually strange. Because if you think about the Earth's real size, even the International Space Station orbits at a distance that works out to only about 1cm from this globe's surface. But this mountain sticks out more than 1cm! So it doesn't add up. In other words, an accurate globe has to be perfectly smooth."

#StatementTrue/False
1この地球儀は丸くない。Kono chikyuugi wa marukunai. — This globe isn't round.False
2この地球儀の表面はツルツルしていない。Kono chikyuugi no hyoumen wa tsurutsuru shite inai. — This globe's surface isn't smooth.True
3この地球儀は山の位置が間違っている。Kono chikyuugi wa yama no ichi ga machigatte iru. — This globe has the mountains in the wrong locations.False
4この地球儀では山は1センチでなければいけない。Kono chikyuugi de wa yama wa issenchi de nakereba ikenai. — On this globe, mountains must measure exactly 1cm.False
5この地球儀は実際の山の高さを正しく表していない。Kono chikyuugi wa jissai no yama no takasa o tadashiku arawashite inai. — This globe doesn't correctly represent the actual height of mountains.True

Why: B never questions the globe's roundness (#1 false) or the mountains' locations (#3 false) — the whole complaint is about height/texture. B's math shows this globe's mountain sticks out more than 1cm even though, at true scale, it should barely be perceptible (making the surface bumpy rather than smooth, #2 true) — meaning the mountain's height is exaggerated relative to reality (#5 true). B never claims exactly 1cm is the required height (#4 misreads "about 1cm" — the ISS orbit distance — as a rule for mountain height).

2 Passage Questions

The main 問題 (p.55) is a "地球サイエンス" (Earth Science) column titled でこぼこの地球儀 ("The Bumpy Globe"). Words with a dotted underline are clickable/tappable (or hover on desktop) for a popup with romaji + English/Hindi/Gujarati meaning.

Passage

でこぼこの地球儀 (Dekoboko no Chikyuugi — "The Bumpy Globe")

先日、宇宙飛行士を乗せたロシアの宇宙船ソユーズ注1が、また国際宇宙ステーションに向けて飛び立ちました。国際宇宙ステーションは地表注2から約400キロ上空に位置し、地球の周りを1周約90分というスピードで回っています。そこから青く光る地球を見たとき、人はどんな気持ちがするのでしょうか。考えただけでもわくわくします注3。Senjitsu, uchuu hikoushi o noseta Roshia no uchuusen Soyuuzu ga, mata kokusai uchuu suteeshon ni mukete tobitachimashita. Kokusai uchuu suteeshon wa chihyou kara yaku yon-hyaku-kiro joukuu ni ichi shi, chikyuu no mawari o isshuu yaku kyuujuppun to iu supiido de mawatte imasu. Soko kara aoku hikaru chikyuu o mita toki, hito wa donna kimochi ga suru no deshou ka. Kangaeta dake demo wakuwaku shimasu.

ところで、この距離は地球の大きさからいってどのぐらいでしょうか。地球の直径は、およそ12,700キロ。地表から400キロのところというのは1センチとちょっとということになります。これでは、ほとんど地球の表面と変わりません。つまり、国際宇宙ステーションへ宇宙旅行といっても、地球の規模注4から見ると、①地上と変わらないところを回っているにほかならないのです。Tokoro de, kono kyori wa chikyuu no ookisa kara itte dono gurai deshou ka. Chikyuu no chokkei wa, oyoso ichiman-nisen-nanahyaku-kiro. Chihyou kara yon-hyaku-kiro no tokoro to iu no wa issenchi to chotto to iu koto ni narimasu. Kore de wa, hotondo chikyuu no hyoumen to kawarimasen. Tsumari, kokusai uchuu suteeshon e uchuu ryokou to itte mo, chikyuu no kibo kara miru to, ① chijou to kawaranai tokoro o mawatte iru ni hoka naranai no desu.

このことから考えると、海や山を表現したでこぼこした地球儀がありますが、これは全く正しくないことがわかります。地球でいちばん高いエベレスト山も30センチの地球儀では高さはわずか0.2ミリにすぎません。つまり正しい地球儀は②ツルツルでなければならないのです。Kono koto kara kangaeru to, umi ya yama o hyougen shita dekoboko shita chikyuugi ga arimasu ga, kore wa mattaku tadashikunai koto ga wakarimasu. Chikyuu de ichiban takai Eberesuto-san mo sanjus-senchi no chikyuugi de wa takasa wa wazuka rei-ten-ni-miri ni suginasen. Tsumari tadashii chikyuugi wa ② tsurutsuru de nakereba naranai no desu.

注1 ソユーズ：Soyuz: a Russian spacecraft　注2 地表：the surface of the earth　注3 わくわくする：to be excited　注4 規模：a scale

EN: The other day, a Russian spacecraft, Soyuz, carrying astronauts, launched once again toward the International Space Station. The ISS sits about 400km above the Earth's surface, circling the planet once roughly every 90 minutes. What must it feel like to look down at the blue-glowing Earth from up there? Just imagining it is thrilling. By the way, how large is that distance really, relative to the size of the Earth? The Earth's diameter is roughly 12,700km. 400km from the surface works out to just over 1cm at that scale — barely different from the Earth's actual surface. In other words, even a "space trip" to the ISS is, on the scale of the Earth, ①nothing other than orbiting a point that's practically ground level. Given this, some relief globes render oceans and mountains as bumpy, uneven surfaces — but this turns out to be completely inaccurate. Even Mount Everest, the tallest point on Earth, would measure only 0.2mm on a 30cm globe. In other words, an accurate globe ②has to be perfectly smooth.
HI: हाल ही में, अंतरिक्ष यात्रियों को लेकर एक रूसी अंतरिक्ष यान, सोयुज़, फिर से अंतरराष्ट्रीय अंतरिक्ष स्टेशन की ओर रवाना हुआ। यह स्टेशन पृथ्वी की सतह से लगभग 400 किलोमीटर ऊपर स्थित है और लगभग हर 90 मिनट में पृथ्वी का एक चक्कर पूरा करता है। वहाँ से नीली चमकती पृथ्वी को देखकर कैसा महसूस होता होगा? सोचकर ही रोमांच हो आता है। वैसे, पृथ्वी के आकार के हिसाब से यह दूरी कितनी है? पृथ्वी का व्यास लगभग 12,700 किलोमीटर है। सतह से 400 किलोमीटर की दूरी, इस पैमाने पर, महज़ थोड़ी-सी 1 सेंटीमीटर से ज़्यादा बैठती है — यानी पृथ्वी की सतह से लगभग अलग नहीं। मतलब, अंतरराष्ट्रीय अंतरिक्ष स्टेशन तक की "अंतरिक्ष यात्रा" भी, पृथ्वी के स्तर पर देखें तो, ①लगभग ज़मीन जैसी जगह का ही चक्कर काटना है, इसके सिवा कुछ नहीं। इस आधार पर सोचें तो, कुछ ग्लोब समुद्र और पहाड़ों को ऊबड़-खाबड़ दिखाते हैं — पर यह पूरी तरह ग़लत साबित होता है। पृथ्वी की सबसे ऊँची चोटी, एवरेस्ट, भी 30 सेंटीमीटर के ग्लोब पर महज़ 0.2 मिलीमीटर ही होगी। यानी एक सही ग्लोब ②बिल्कुल चिकना होना ही चाहिए।
GU: તાજેતરમાં, અવકાશયાત્રીઓને લઈને એક રશિયન અવકાશયાન, સોયુઝ, ફરીથી આંતરરાષ્ટ્રીય અવકાશ મથક તરફ રવાના થયું. આ મથક પૃથ્વીની સપાટીથી લગભગ 400 કિલોમીટર ઉપર સ્થિત છે અને લગભગ દર 90 મિનિટે પૃથ્વીની એક પ્રદક્ષિણા પૂરી કરે છે. ત્યાંથી ભૂરી ચમકતી પૃથ્વીને જોઈને કેવું લાગતું હશે? વિચારીને જ રોમાંચ થાય છે. વૈસે, પૃથ્વીના કદના હિસાબે આ અંતર કેટલું છે? પૃથ્વીનો વ્યાસ લગભગ 12,700 કિલોમીટર છે. સપાટીથી 400 કિલોમીટરનું અંતર, આ સ્કેલ પર, ફક્ત થોડું વધારે 1 સેન્ટિમીટર જેટલું બેસે છે — એટલે કે પૃથ્વીની સપાટીથી લગભગ અલગ નહીં. મતલબ, આંતરરાષ્ટ્રીય અવકાશ મથક સુધીની "અવકાશ યાત્રા" પણ, પૃથ્વીના સ્કેલ પર જોઈએ તો, ①લગભગ જમીન જેવી જ જગ્યાની આસપાસ ફરવા સિવાય બીજું કંઈ નથી. આ આધારે વિચારીએ તો, કેટલાક ગ્લોબ સમુદ્ર અને પર્વતોને ખાડાટેકરાવાળા બતાવે છે — પણ આ સંપૂર્ણપણે ખોટું સાબિત થાય છે. પૃથ્વીનું સૌથી ઊંચું શિખર, એવરેસ્ટ, પણ 30 સેન્ટિમીટરના ગ્લોબ પર ફક્ત 0.2 મિલિમીટર જ હશે. મતલબ કે સાચો ગ્લોબ ②એકદમ લીસો જ હોવો જોઈએ.
Q1

問1　①地上と変わらないところを回っているにほかならないとあるが、どういう意味か。Toi 1: ① Chijou to kawaranai tokoro o mawatte iru ni hoka naranai to aru ga, dou iu imi ka.

OptionJapaneseEnglish
1ほとんど地表といっていいところを回っているだけだHotondo chihyou to itte ii tokoro o mawatte iru dake daIt's simply orbiting somewhere you could call almost the Earth's surface itself.
2地球上の変化のないところを回っているだけだChikyuujou no henka no nai tokoro o mawatte iru dake daIt's just orbiting a place on Earth with no [geographic] variation.
3地球の表面を回っているわけではないChikyuu no hyoumen o mawatte iru wake de wa naiIt isn't actually orbiting the Earth's surface.
4地面と同じ場所を回っているわけではないJimen to onaji basho o mawatte iru wake de wa naiIt isn't orbiting the same place as the ground.

Why (booklet-confirmed, 問1: 1): The booklet's own hint: "400キロといっても、地球の規模から見ると大した距離ではない" ("Even though it's 400km, that's not a great distance when viewed on the scale of the Earth") — 400km/12,700km diameter works out to roughly 1cm on a normal-sized globe, which is essentially indistinguishable from "the surface itself." That matches option 1's "ほとんど地表といっていい" (practically the same as the surface). Options 3 and 4 flatly contradict this — the whole point is that the ISS orbit IS effectively at the surface, not somewhere clearly different from it; option 2 misreads "no different from the ground" as "no geographic variation," which isn't the claim being made.

Q2

問2　②ツルツルでなければならないとはどういう意味か。Toi 2: ② Tsurutsuru de nakereba naranai to wa dou iu imi ka.

OptionJapaneseEnglish
1でこぼこしていてはおかしいDekoboko shite ite wa okashiiIt would be wrong/strange for it to be bumpy.
2よく回転するようにしなければならないYoku kaiten suru you ni shinakereba naranaiIt must be made to spin smoothly.
3でこぼこが逆でないとおかしいDekoboko ga gyaku de nai to okashiiIt would be wrong unless the bumps were reversed.
4きれいに磨かなければならないKirei ni migakanakereba naranaiIt must be polished to a shine.

Why (booklet-confirmed, 問2: 1): The whole passage builds toward the conclusion that mountains, which look dramatic in real life, would be practically imperceptible at true globe scale (Everest = 0.2mm on a 30cm globe) — so a scientifically accurate globe shouldn't show bumps at all. "ツルツルでなければならない" (must be smooth) simply means "being bumpy (でこぼこ) is inaccurate" — option 1. Options 2 and 4 reinterpret "smooth" as being about rotation or polish/shine, which the passage never discusses; option 3 invents a "reversed bumps" idea that isn't in the text at all.

練習 Distinguish "wrong shape/position" from "wrong scale" B's complaint is entirely about scale/height, never about roundness or mountain location Don't let "1cm" (the ISS orbit distance) get mistaken for a fixed rule about mountain height (#4).
