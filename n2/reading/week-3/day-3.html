1 Reading Strategy

Skill focus

✿筆者が最も言いたいことを理解しようHissha ga mottomo iitai koto o rikai shiyou — Try to understand what the author wants to say most!

筆者の言いたいことを読み取るときは……Hissha no iitai koto o yomitoru toki wa... — When you're trying to work out what the writer wants to say:

LayerWhat it isShould you stop here?
一般的な意見ippanteki na ikena general/widely-shared opinionNo — this is just background
常識joushikicommon senseNo — the exam isn't testing what "everyone knows"
自分の気持ちjibun no kimochithe author's own feeling/stanceYes — this is the target

★常識や一般的意見ではなく、筆者が文章の中でいちばん言いたいことを考えましょう。★ Joushiki ya ippanteki iken de wa naku, hissha ga bunshou no naka de ichiban iitai koto o kangaemashou. — Rather than common sense or general opinion, think about what the author most wants to say within the passage itself. Think about what the author really wants to say and try to go beyond commonly shared opinions.

2 Passage Questions

問題 from p.49: a short opinion piece built around a Japanese proverb, followed by 2 comprehension questions with 4 options each.

Opinion piece

日本のことわざに「盗人にも三分の理」というのがある。この場合、「三分」は10分の3、「理」は物事の理由を表す。つまり「たとえ泥棒であっても30％くらいは納得のできる理由がある*（だから、どんなことにも無理に理由をつけることはできる）」という意味だ。Nihon no kotowaza ni "nusubito ni mo sanbu no ri" to iu no ga aru. Kono baai, "sanbu" wa juubun no san, "ri" wa monogoto no riyuu o arawasu. Tsumari "tatoe dorobou de atte mo sanjuppaasento kurai wa nattoku no dekiru riyuu ga aru (dakara, donna koto ni mo muri ni riyuu o tsukeru koto wa dekiru)" to iu imi da.

ところが①近ごろの犯罪はどうだろう。Tokoro ga chikagoro no hanzai wa dou darou.

何の不自由もなさそうな普通の少年少女が、特に欲しくもないものを万引きしたり注1、ただ退屈だからという理由で物を壊したりする。「なぜそういうことをした」と聞いても、彼らは「別に」「なんとなく」などと答えることが多い。先日のコンビニ強盗した高校生も、ただスリルを楽しむためにやったということだ。また、最近は「騒ぐと殺す」というような言葉もなく、いきなり暴行注2あるいは殺害注3し、金などを奪う犯罪も多い。これなどは被害者をまったく人間扱いせず、効率注4のみを考えたやり方である**。Nan no fujiyuu mo nasasou na futsuu no shounen shoujo ga, toku ni hoshiku mo nai mono o manbiki shitari, tada taikutsu da kara to iu riyuu de mono o kowashitari suru. "Naze sou iu koto o shita" to kiite mo, karera wa "betsu ni" "nantonaku" nado to kotaeru koto ga ooi. Senjitsu no konbini goutou shita koukousei mo, tada suriru o tanoshimu tame ni yatta to iu koto da. Mata, saikin wa "sawagu to korosu" to iu you na kotoba mo naku, ikinari boukou arui wa satsugai shi, kane nado o ubau hanzai mo ooi. Kore nado wa higaisha o mattaku ningen-atsukai sezu, kouritsu nomi o kangaeta yarikata de aru.

「理」などというものは、どこを探しても「三分」どころか、（②　　）。"Ri" nado to iu mono wa, doko o sagashite mo "sanbu" dokoro ka, (②).

注1 万引きする：to shop-lift　注2 暴行する：to assault　注3 殺害する：to murder　注4 効率：efficiency　* among the reasons for stealing provided by robbers, 30% is reasonable　** in this case, the suspect showed no respect for the victim as a human and just quickly took what he wanted

EN: There's a Japanese proverb, "even a thief has three-tenths of a reason." Here, "three-tenths" means 3 out of 10, and "reason" refers to a justification for something. In other words, it means "even a thief has a reason that's about 30% understandable (which is to say, you can force a reason onto almost anything)." But what about crime these days? Ordinary boys and girls who don't seem to lack for anything will shoplift things they don't even particularly want, or break things simply out of boredom. Even when asked "why did you do that," they often just answer "no reason" or "just because." The high-schooler who recently robbed a convenience store apparently did it purely to enjoy a thrill. Also, these days there's a lot of crime where, without even a warning like "make noise and I'll kill you," the attacker suddenly assaults or kills the victim and takes their money — treating the victim not as a human being at all, but purely as an efficiency calculation. As for "reason" — no matter where you look for it, far from being "three-tenths," (②).
HI: जापान की एक कहावत है, "चोर के पास भी तीन-दसवाँ हिस्सा वजह होती है।" यहाँ "तीन-दसवाँ" का मतलब है 10 में से 3, और "वजह" का मतलब किसी काम का औचित्य। यानी "चोर के पास भी लगभग 30% मान्य वजह होती है (इसलिए किसी भी काम को ज़बरदस्ती वजह दी जा सकती है)।" पर आजकल के अपराधों का क्या? किसी चीज़ की कमी न होने वाले सामान्य लड़के-लड़कियाँ, ऐसी चीज़ें चुराते हैं जो उन्हें ख़ास चाहिए भी नहीं होतीं, या सिर्फ़ बोरियत की वजह से चीज़ें तोड़ देते हैं। "ऐसा क्यों किया" पूछने पर भी अक्सर "यूँ ही" या "कोई ख़ास वजह नहीं" जवाब देते हैं। हाल ही में कॉन्वीनियंस स्टोर लूटने वाला हाई-स्कूल का लड़का भी सिर्फ़ रोमांच के लिए ऐसा किया था। साथ ही, आजकल "शोर मचाया तो मार डालूँगा" जैसी चेतावनी दिए बिना ही सीधे हमला या हत्या करके पैसे लूटने वाले अपराध भी बहुत हैं। यह तो पीड़ित को इंसान समझे बिना, सिर्फ़ दक्षता को ध्यान में रखने वाला तरीक़ा है। "वजह" नाम की चीज़, कहीं भी ढूँढ लो, "तीन-दसवाँ हिस्सा" तो दूर, (②)।
GU: જાપાનની એક કહેવત છે, "ચોર પાસે પણ ત્રણ-દસમા ભાગનું કારણ હોય છે." અહીં "ત્રણ-દસમો" એટલે 10માંથી 3, અને "કારણ" એટલે કોઈ કામનું ઔચિત્ય. એટલે કે "ચોર પાસે પણ લગભગ 30% માન્ય કારણ હોય છે (એટલે કોઈ પણ કામને બળજબરીથી કારણ આપી શકાય)." પણ આજકાલના ગુનાઓનું શું? કોઈ વસ્તુની ખોટ ન હોય તેવા સામાન્ય છોકરા-છોકરીઓ, એવી વસ્તુઓ ચોરે છે જે તેમને ખાસ જોઈતી પણ નથી, અથવા ફક્ત કંટાળાને કારણે વસ્તુઓ તોડી નાખે છે. "એવું કેમ કર્યું" પૂછવા છતાં પણ ઘણીવાર "એમ જ" અથવા "કંઈ ખાસ કારણ નથી" જવાબ આપે છે. તાજેતરમાં કન્વીનિયન્સ સ્ટોર લૂંટનાર હાઈસ્કૂલનો છોકરો પણ ફક્ત રોમાંચ માટે આવું કર્યું હતું. ઉપરાંત, આજકાલ "અવાજ કરીશ તો મારી નાખીશ" જેવી ચેતવણી આપ્યા વગર જ સીધો હુમલો કે હત્યા કરીને પૈસા લૂંટવાના ગુના પણ ઘણા છે. આ તો પીડિતને માણસ ગણ્યા વગર, ફક્ત કાર્યક્ષમતાનો જ વિચાર કરતી પદ્ધતિ છે. "કારણ" નામની વસ્તુ, ગમે ત્યાં શોધો, "ત્રણ-દસમો ભાગ" તો દૂર, (②).

Why: The whole passage is built around one contrast: the old proverb assumes every wrongdoing has some understandable reason (三分の理), but today's crimes — shoplifting out of boredom, robbery for a thrill, violence with no warning — have essentially no reason at all. Options 2, 3, and 4 each pick out a true supporting detail from the passage (money isn't the motive; there's violent crime; the culprits are often ordinary youths) but none of them is the author's overarching point, which is specifically about the absence of reason itself. Confirmed answer (booklet, PDF index 115, "3日目" row): 問1 = 1.

Why: The phrase "「三分」どころか" ("far from even 'three-tenths'") is a rhetorical structure that demands an even more extreme negative than "less than 30%" — it needs a flat denial, not a hedge. Options 1, 2, and 4 all still leave room for a small amount of reason to exist (1%, "might find some," "can't say there's none") — exactly the kind of hedge the author has spent the whole passage arguing against. Only option 3 delivers the total denial that "どころか" sets up. Confirmed answer (booklet, PDF index 115) — note: 1行目の「盗人」は「ぬすっと」とも読む (the word 盗人 in line 1 can also be read "nusutto"): 問2 = 3.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

No vocabulary cluster today — instead compare which reading strategy unlocks each question:

QuestionStrategy usedWhat it testsTrap to avoid
練習 Separate motive from circumstance Whether "wasn't short of money" is correctly read as ruling out a financial motive Don't assume a robbery always implies a money motive — this passage's whole point is that it didn't.
問1 Find the overarching claim, not a supporting detail Distinguishing the author's single main point from three true-but-secondary observations Options 2–4 are all accurate statements about the passage — but accuracy isn't the test here; only one is what the author is most trying to say.
問2 Match the strength of a rhetorical negative ("どころか") Choosing the option that intensifies rather than softens the preceding claim Answers that hedge with "maybe a little" contradict the rhetorical direction of どころか, which demands something stronger than the thing it's dismissing, not a smaller version of it.
