Notes
Scene: a man explains he lent a friend money out of a "we help each other" spirit, but the friend hasn't paid it back; his listener says lending/borrowing money between friends is never a good idea and he should ask for it back directly.
する after a word = the word itself is also a noun that pairs with する to become a verb (e.g. 見聞きする)
Scene

A worried-looking man explains his situation to a listener, who responds with blunt advice.

Him: 困ったときはお互い様だと思って、友達にお金を貸したんだけど、なかなか返してくれないんだ。返さないつもりはないと思うから、もう少し様子を見ようと思って……。Komatta toki wa otagaisama da to omotte, tomodachi ni okane o kashita ndakedo, nakanaka kaeshite kurenai nda. Kaesanai tsumori wa nai to omou kara, mou sukoshi yousu o miyou to omotte...
("Thinking 'we help each other when in trouble,' I lent a friend some money, but he's not paying it back easily. I don't think he intends not to repay it, so I was thinking I'd wait and see a little longer...")

Listener: いくら友達でも、お金の貸し借りはよくないよ。はっきり「返してくれ」と言うべきだよ。Ikura tomodachi demo, okane no kashikari wa yokunai yo. Hakkiri "kaeshite kure" to iu beki da yo.
("No matter how close a friend, lending and borrowing money is never a good idea. You should clearly tell him 'pay me back.'")

困ったときはお互い様です(komatta toki wa otagaisama desu) — "we help each other when in trouble" — and お金の貸し借り(okane no kashikari) — "lending and borrowing money" — are today's two key phrases in action.

Q1

駅から会場までを（a. 行き来　b. 行き帰り）するバスが出ている。Eki kara kaijou made o (a. ikiki / b. ikikaeri) suru basu ga dete iru.

HI: स्टेशन से आयोजन स्थल तक आने-जाने वाली बस चलती है।
GU: સ્ટેશનથી સ્થળ સુધી અવરજવર કરતી બસ ચાલે છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a行き来ikikiback-and-forth traffic/movementआना-जानाઅવરજવર

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Today's 〜たり〜たり compounds all describe a "back-and-forth" pair of actions, but each is locked to its own object — exactly what Q1 and Q2 test. Its fixed phrases also serve very different social functions, tested in Q4 and Q5:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
行き来するikiki suru To go back and forth (traffic/movement) A repeated, ongoing shuttle between two points. "A bus runs between the station and the venue."
行き帰りikikaeri One's own trip there and back A single person's one round trip, not a repeated service. "On my way to/from work, I stop by the store."
出し入れするdashiire suru To take in and out Fits storable objects (futons), not a continuous intensity. "Taking the futons in and out is a hassle."
上げ下げするagesage suru To raise and lower Fits adjustable levels/intensities (volume, blinds). "Adjusting the volume with a button."
いつもお世話になっていますitsumo osewa ni natte imasu Thank you for your ongoing care A one-directional thank-you toward the listener's kindness. Opening a business call/greeting.
困ったときはお互い様ですkomatta toki wa otagaisama desu We help each other when in trouble Frames help as mutual and reciprocal, not one-sided thanks. Reassuring someone that helping them was natural, no thanks needed.
Slang caution: This week's よく聞く俗語 box repeats a running theme from earlier weeks — 超〜, マジ, and ヤバい are extremely common among young speakers but read as too casual (even rude) toward a boss or elder; save them for friends, and note that ヤバい in particular has drifted from its original "dangerous" meaning to also express strong enthusiasm ("ヤバい, おいしい！" = "this is insanely good!").
