Notes
Continues Day 1's compound-verb theme. The opening illustration is a single character's inner monologue rather than a two-person dialogue, and it demonstrates several of today's words directly (見習って, 思いついた, 書き留めて). The word list again runs as one continuous "おぼえましょう" table across both printed pages.
⊙ = 関連する語彙 (a related word/expression)
Scene

A woman holds a notebook and pen, thinking about following her senior colleague's example.

Her (thinking): 先輩を見習って、思いついた考えは、忘れないうちに何でも書き留めておこう。Senpai o minaratte, omoitsuita kangae wa, wasurenai uchi ni nandemo kakitomete okou.
("Following my senior colleague's example, I'll jot down any idea I come up with, whatever it is, before I forget it.")

Three combination verbs appear in one thought: 見習う (learn from/follow someone's example), 思いつく (come up with an idea), 書き留める (jot down) — all covered in today's list.

Q1

インターネットの便利さを（a. 見つめた　b. 見直した）。Intaanetto no benrisa o (a. mitsumeta / b. minaoshita).

Full sentence: インターネットの便利さを見直した。Intaanetto no benrisa o minaoshita.
EN: I came to re-appreciate the convenience of the internet.
HI: मैंने इंटरनेट की सुविधा को फिर से नए सिरे से समझा/सराहा।
GU: મેં ઇન્ટરનેટની સુવિધાને ફરી નવેસરથી સમજી/વખાણી.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a見つめたmitsumetastared at (a physical gaze, not a reconsideration)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
b見直したminaoshitareconsidered/re-examinedदोबारा समझाફરી સમજ્યું

Q2

かっこの中に当て（a. はまる　b. はめる）語句を書きなさい。Kakko no naka ni ate (a. hamaru / b. hameru) goku o kakinasai.

Full sentence: かっこの中に当てはまる語句を書きなさい。Kakko no naka ni atehamaru goku o kakinasai.
EN: Write the word/phrase that fits in the parentheses.
HI: कोष्ठक में फ़िट होने वाला शब्द/वाक्यांश लिखिए।
GU: કૌંસમાં બંધબેસતો શબ્દ/શબ્દસમૂહ લખો.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
aはまるhamarufits (intransitive — the word itself fits)फ़िट होनाબંધબેસવું
bはめるhameruto fit something in (transitive — needs an agent doing the fitting)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Today's list has two clusters of near-synonyms — "call/summon" verbs and "ride past a stop" verbs — plus a "chase" family, all of which the exercises above test directly. Compare them:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
思い込むomoikomu To become convinced of (often wrongly) A standing, sometimes mistaken belief held over time. "I was convinced the illness wouldn't heal."
思いつくomoitsuku To come up with (an idea) A sudden, one-off realization. "I came up with a good idea."
呼び出すyobidasu To summon a specific person to come Targets one particular person (e.g. via phone or PA). "Paging a lost child over the PA."
呼びかけるyobikakeru To call out to (an open appeal) Addressed to whoever is listening, not one named person. "Calling out loudly to a crowd."
乗り越す／乗り過ごすnorikosu / norisugosu To ride past one's intended stop Still on the train, but past where you meant to get off. "Riding past the station."
乗り遅れるnoriokureru To miss the train entirely Never boarding at all, unlike riding past a stop. "Missing the train before it departs."
Chase-family trap: 追いかける (to chase/pursue, an ongoing action), 追いつく (to catch up to, reaching the same point as the target), and 追い越す (to overtake/pass, going beyond the target) form a natural sequence — a police officer 追いかける's a fleeing criminal, hopes to 追いつく them, and a car that wants to pass 追い越す's the one ahead. Don't reach for 追い出す ("to drive/force someone out of a place") when you mean any of these three — it describes eviction, not motion relative to another person.
