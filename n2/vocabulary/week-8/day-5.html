JLPT N2 · Vocabulary · Week 8 — まとめて覚えましょう③ (Matomete oboemashou ③)

5日目 — よく使われる表現③ (Yoku tsukawareru hyougen ③)

Source
日本語総まとめ N2 語彙 (Nihongo Sou-Matome N2 Goi) — printed p.132–133
Theme
Common single-word/phrase expressions covering luck, tolerability, fussiness, waste, "better than nothing," decency, and margin/room-to-spare
Answer key
練習Ⅰ・Ⅱ answers for this day are printed in the "133ページの答え" box at the bottom of p.135 (Day 6's second page) — confirmed, not guessed.
Notes
Scene: a man's inner monologue while his wife (who barely cooks) peers into a cabinet full of dishware she's particular about. Vocabulary here is one continuous "おぼえましょう" list, not split into sub-groups.
⇔ = 反対の意味 (antonym)
⊙ = 関連する語彙 (a related word/expression)
* = マイナス／プラスの意味 (a note on negative/positive nuance)
Scene

A woman peers into a cabinet full of neatly stacked dishware while her husband looks on, thinking to himself:

His thoughts: 彼女、ろくに料理をしないのに、食器にこだわりがあるらしい。Kanojo, roku ni ryouri o shinai noni, shokki ni kodawari ga aru rashii.
("She barely cooks, and yet it seems she's particular about her dishware.")

それより、料理にこだわってほしいんだけどなあ。Sore yori, ryouri ni kodawatte hoshii ndakedo naa.
("I'd rather she be particular about her cooking instead...")

ろくに〜ない(roku ni ~nai) — "hardly/barely does" — and こだわりがある(kodawari ga aru) — "to be particular/fussy about" — are the two idioms this scene is built around.

Q1

赤ちゃんが泣くのは（a. 当たり前　b. まし）です。Akachan ga naku no wa (a. atarimae / b. mashi) desu.

Full sentence: 赤ちゃんが泣くのは当たり前です。Akachan ga naku no wa atarimae desu.
EN: It's only natural for a baby to cry.
HI: बच्चे का रोना स्वाभाविक बात है।
GU: બાળકનું રડવું સ્વાભાવિક બાબત છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a当たり前atarimaenatural/to be expectedस्वाभाविकસ્વાભાવિક
bましmashibetter than the alternative (needs a comparison, not a standalone description)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Several words today shift meaning with context or polarity — exactly what Q1, Q3, Q5, and Q6 test. Compare them:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
こだわる（マイナス）kodawaru (mainasu) To fuss over trivial things Negative — being needlessly picky about something unimportant. "つまらないことにこだわる"
こだわる（プラス）kodawaru (purasu) To be particular/exacting Positive — carefully insisting on quality (e.g. ingredients). "材料にこだわって作る"
もったいないmottainai Wasteful, a shame to lose/discard About discarding something still valuable/usable. "It's a shame to throw this out."
ましmashi Better than the alternative A relative comparison — not great, but preferable to something worse. "Even this is better than nothing."
まとも（な）matomo (na) Proper, decent, sound Describes a thing/person's own quality, not a comparison. "A decent, normal way of doing things."
ろくな〜ないroku na ~nai Nothing decent/worthwhile Only exists as a negative pattern — never used affirmatively. "最近、ろくな仕事がない。"
Same word, opposite spin: こだわる is the trickiest word this week — the book flags it with *マイナスの意味 and *プラスの意味 on facing example rows, because the very same verb can mean "petty and picky" (つまらないことにこだわる) or "admirably particular" (材料にこだわる) depending purely on what it's attached to. Always check the object before judging the tone.
