JLPT N2 · Vocabulary · Week 8 — まとめて覚えましょう③ (Matomete oboemashou ③)

4日目 — よく使われる表現② (Yoku tsukawareru hyougen ②)

Source
日本語総まとめ N2 語彙 (Nihongo Sou-Matome N2 Goi) — printed p.130–131
Theme
体の名称を使った表現② (idioms built from body-part names — 手/手 "hand", 気/気 "spirit/feeling", 腕/腕 "arm/skill", 足/足 "foot/leg")
Answer key
練習Ⅰ・Ⅱ answers for this day are printed in the "131ページの答え" box at the bottom of p.133 (Day 5's second page) — confirmed, not guessed.
Notes
Scene: a boy with a toothache can't focus on anything; his friend recommends a skilled dentist. Header note on p.130: ※「が」→「の」でもOK — in these body-part idioms, the が marking the body part can usually be swapped for の (e.g. 手が空いたら／手の空いたら) without changing the meaning.
⇔ = 反対の意味 (antonym)
⊙ = 関連する語彙 (a related word/expression)
* = 注意すべき点 (a usage note, explained alongside the entry)
Scene

A boy holds his aching cheek, miserable; his friend puts a hand on his shoulder and offers a solution.

Him: 歯が痛くて、勉強も手につかないし、テレビを見る気もしないよ。Ha ga itakute, benkyou mo te ni tsukanai shi, terebi o miru ki mo shinai yo.
("My tooth hurts so much I can't concentrate on studying, and I don't even feel like watching TV.")

Friend: 早く歯医者に行ったほうがいいよ。腕のいい歯医者を紹介するよ。Hayaku haisha ni itta hou ga ii yo. Ude no ii haisha o shoukai suru yo.
("You should go to the dentist soon. I'll introduce you to a skilled dentist.")

手につかない(te ni tsukanai) — "can't concentrate/focus on" — and 気もしない(ki mo shinai) — "don't even feel like it" — plus 腕のいい(ude no ii) — "skilled" — are exactly today's three idiom families in action.

Q1

車を修理に出してしまったので、行きたいけど（a. 手がかかる　b. 足がない）のです。Kuruma o shuuri ni dashite shimatta node, ikitai kedo (a. te ga kakaru / b. ashi ga nai) no desu.

Full sentence: 車を修理に出してしまったので、行きたいけど足がないのです。Kuruma o shuuri ni dashite shimatta node, ikitai kedo ashi ga nai no desu.
EN: Since I sent the car in for repair, I'd like to go, but I have no means of transportation.
HI: गाड़ी मरम्मत के लिए भेज दी है, इसलिए जाना तो चाहता/चाहती हूँ पर कोई साधन नहीं है।
GU: ગાડી સમારકામ માટે મોકલી દીધી છે, એટલે જવું તો છે પણ કોઈ સાધન નથી.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a手がかかるte ga kakaruneeds a lot of care (fits a demanding child/task, not lacking transport)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
b足がないashi ga naino means of transportationकोई साधन नहीं हैકોઈ સાધન નથી

Q2

この机は、少し手を（a. 入れれば　b. かせば）、十分に使えます。Kono tsukue wa, sukoshi te o (a. irereba / b. kaseba), juubun ni tsukaemasu.

Full sentence: この机は、少し手を入れれば、十分に使えます。Kono tsukue wa, sukoshi te o ireba, juubun ni tsukaemasu.

HI: इस मेज़ में थोड़ी मरम्मत कर दें तो यह अभी और इस्तेमाल हो सकती है।
GU: આ ટેબલમાં થોડું સમારકામ કરો તો હજુ સારી રીતે વાપરી શકાય.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a入れればirerebatouch up/repair a bit (手を入れる)थोड़ी मरम्मत करनाથોડું સમારકામ કરવું

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Today's exercises turn on telling apart near-synonym idioms built from the same body part — exactly what Q3, Q5, and Q7/Q8 test. Compare them:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
手が空くte ga aku To become free/available Describes a person becoming available to help or do something. "Come once you're free."
手に入れるte ni ireru To obtain/get hold of Describes successfully acquiring an object. "I finally got my hands on it."
気が重いki ga omoi To feel down/dread something A heavy, reluctant feeling about a known upcoming event. "I dread school starting again."
気にかかるki ni kakaru To weigh on one's mind Lingering worry over something unresolved/uncertain. "I can't stop worrying about my son."
気にくわないki ni kuwanai To dislike Distaste for someone's attitude/behavior, not worry. "I don't like his attitude."
腕がいいude ga ii To be skilled Praises a person's professional skill/expertise. "That dentist is very skilled."
Body-part trap: 腕がいい ("skilled") and 手がいい (not a standard idiom) both involve hands/arms but only 腕 carries the "skill" sense here — Q4 tests exactly this swap. Likewise 手を貸す ("lend a hand" to a person) and 手を入れる ("touch up/repair" an object) share the same 手を〜construction shape but take completely different kinds of objects, as Q2 tests.
