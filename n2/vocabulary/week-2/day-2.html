JLPT N2 · Vocabulary · Week 2 — 仕事は順調ですか？ (Shigoto wa junchou desu ka?)

2日目 — 仕事をしています① (Shigoto o shite imasu ①)

Source
日本語総まとめ N2 語彙 (Nihongo Sou-Matome N2 Goi) — printed p.30–31
Theme
会社の人間関係・勤怠に関する言葉 (workplace relationships, attendance/leave)
Answer key
練習Ⅰ・Ⅱ answers for this day are printed in the "31ページの答え" box at the bottom of p.33 — confirmed, not guessed.
Notes
Scene: a boss wonders if a new subordinate has settled in; the subordinate has, thanks to learning from a senior colleague — but elsewhere, an overworked employee's inner monologue reveals he's exhausted and considering faking illness to leave early.
⊙ = 関連する語彙 (a related word/expression)
⇔ = 反対の意味 (antonym)
Scene

A boss privately wonders if his new subordinate has adjusted to the job; the subordinate reassures him, while elsewhere an overworked employee's thoughts reveal how exhausted he really is.

Boss (thinking): もう仕事には慣れたかな？Mou shigoto ni wa nareta kana?
("I wonder if he's gotten used to the work by now?")

Subordinate: はい、部長。先輩に色々教わりながら、少しずつ慣れてきました。Hai, buchou. Senpai ni iroiro osowari nagara, sukoshizutsu narete kimashita.
("Yes, manager. While learning various things from my senior colleague, I've been gradually getting used to it.")

Another employee (thinking): 忙しくて、コーヒー一飲む間もない。これじゃ、体がもたないし、今日はもうやる気もないし、具合が悪いふりして早退しようかなあ。Isogashikute, koohii hito nomu ma mo nai. Kore ja, karada ga motanai shi, kyou wa mou yaru ki mo nai shi, guai ga warui furi shite soutai shiyou kanaa.
("I'm so busy I don't even have time to drink one coffee. At this rate my body won't hold up, and I've got no motivation left today either... maybe I should pretend to feel sick and leave early.")

具合が悪いふりをする (guai ga warui furi o suru) — "to pretend to feel sick," the classic excuse to leave work early (早退する)

2 Quiz / Exercise Section

練習Ⅰ (6 items, from p.31) and 練習Ⅱ (2 items, from p.31). Answers confirmed from the "31ページの答え" box printed at the bottom of p.33.

練習Ⅰ — choose the correct option

Q1

子どもが私の（a. やりがい　b. 生きがい）です。Kodomo ga watashi no (a. yarigai / b. ikigai) desu.

Full sentence: 子どもが私の生きがいです。Kodomo ga watashi no ikigai desu.
EN: My child is my reason for living.
HI: मेरा बच्चा ही मेरे जीवन का मक़सद है।
GU: મારું બાળક જ મારા જીવનનો હેતુ છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
aやりがいyarigaiworth doing (about a task, not a whole life's purpose)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
b生きがいikigaione's reason for livingजीवन का मक़सदજીવનનો હેતુ

Why: 生きがい ("reason for living") fits a person or thing being someone's whole purpose in life, like a child; やりがい describes a specific task or job feeling worthwhile, not a life's overall purpose.

Full sentence: 忙しくて休むひまがない。Isogashikute yasumu hima ga nai.
EN: I'm so busy I don't have time to rest.
HI: इतना व्यस्त हूँ कि आराम करने का समय नहीं है।
GU: એટલો વ્યસ્ત છું કે આરામ કરવાનો સમય નથી.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a体karadabody (doesn't fit "no time to rest" grammatically)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Today's exercises hinge on telling apart words for "reason to live" vs. "worth doing," and for different kinds/lengths of workplace absence — exactly what Q1 and Q7 above test. Compare them:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
生きがいikigai One's reason for living A whole-life purpose — a person, passion, or pursuit that gives life meaning. "My child/work is my reason for living."
やりがいyarigai Worth doing (about a specific task) Describes a single job/task as fulfilling — smaller in scope than 生きがい. "This is a rewarding job."
席を外すseki o hazusu To step away from one's desk A brief, expected-to-return-soon absence. Answering a phone call for someone momentarily away.
早退するsoutai suru To leave work early Leaving before the workday ends — not coming back that day. Explaining someone left for the day.
休みを取るyasumi o toru To take time off/leave A planned absence of a day or more. Describing vacation or sick leave.
部下buka A workplace subordinate Defined by job hierarchy/rank. Describing who reports to you at work.
後輩kouhai A junior (by school/entry year) Defined by when someone joined a school or company, independent of current rank. Describing a junior from the same school/company class.
Register note: 超忙しい uses 超 as a casual slang intensifier (＝非常に, "extremely") — natural in speech among friends/colleagues, but too casual for formal writing, where 非常に忙しい or とても忙しい would be expected instead.
