Q1

私は将来（　）になるのが夢です。Watashi wa shourai (___) ni naru no ga yume desu.

Full sentence: 私は将来学者になるのが夢です。Watashi wa shourai gakusha ni naru no ga yume desu.
EN: My dream is to become a scholar in the future.
HI: मेरा सपना भविष्य में एक विद्वान बनना है।
GU: મારું સપનું ભવિષ્યમાં એક વિદ્વાન બનવાનું છે.
#JapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
1人物jinbutsua person/figure (you can't grammatically "become" a jinbutsu — everyone already is one)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
2学者gakushaa scholar (a real, aspirational profession)विद्वानવિદ્વાન
3作者sakushaan author (of a specific work — you're only "the 作者" once a work exists; not something you aspire to "become")(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
4人間ningena human being (same problem as 人物 — already true of everyone)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Why (reasoned): "～になるのが夢です" ("my dream is to become ~") needs a profession one aspires to; 学者 ("scholar") is a natural career goal, while 人物/人間 both just mean "a person," which one already is, and 作者 names the author-relationship to a specific work rather than a career path (作家 would be the writer-as-profession word).

1作業sagyouwork/task (too generic, doesn't name the specific act of quoting)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

#JapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
1体重taijuubody weight (doesn't naturally "disappear" with age as a given fact)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Q21

合計goukei

#JapaneseEnglish
1新しい仕事の合計をした。Atarashii shigoto no goukei o shita."I did the [goukei] of a new job" — 合計 needs quantities to sum, not a job itself
2勉強の合計におやつを食べた。Benkyou no goukei ni oyatsu o tabeta."I ate a snack at the [goukei] of studying" — should be 合間 (a break), not 合計
3他校のチームと合計で練習をした。Takou no chiimu to goukei de renshuu o shita."I practiced in [goukei] with another school's team" — should be 合同 (jointly), not 合計
4お買い物金額は合計で3万円になります。Okaimono kingaku wa goukei de san-man-en ni narimasu.The total shopping amount comes to 30,000 yen.

Why (reasoned): Day 5's 合計を出す ("to work out the total") confirms 合計 is for summing numeric amounts — sentence 4 correctly uses it for a total purchase price; 1–3 misapply it where 合間 (a break) or 合同 (joint/combined) would be correct instead.

Q22

名人meijin

#JapaneseEnglish
1このお話は古典的名人です。Kono ohanashi wa kotenteki meijin desu."This story is a classical [meijin]" — a story can't be a "master," a person-only word
2彼はオムレツ作りの名人です。Kare wa omuretsu-zukuri no meijin desu.He is a master at making omelets.
3申込書に名人を書いた。Moushikomisho ni meijin o kaita."I wrote [meijin] on the application form" — should be 氏名/名前 (one's name), not 名人
4最近職場での名人関係がうまくいっていない。Saikin shokuba de no meijin kankei ga umaku itte inai."Recently, [meijin] relations at work aren't going well" — should be 人間関係 (human relations), not 名人関係

Why (reasoned): Day 4's 魚つりの名人 ("a master at fishing") confirms 名人 means "a master/expert" at some skill — sentence 2 matches that exactly; 1, 3, and 4 all confuse it with unrelated words (無関係な用法, 氏名, 人間関係) that merely sound similar.

目下meshita

#JapaneseEnglish
1彼女は部下など目下の人に対してはとても厳しい人です。Kanojo wa buka nado meshita no hito ni taishite wa totemo kibishii hito desu.She is a very strict person toward her subordinates and other juniors.

#JapaneseEnglish

Q25

物事monogoto

#JapaneseEnglish
1家が狭くて、物事があふれている。Ie ga semakute, monogoto ga afurete iru."The house is small, and [monogoto] overflows" — should be 物 (physical objects/clutter), not the abstract 物事
2物事のために、一生懸命勉強した。Monogoto no tame ni, isshoukenmei benkyou shita."For the sake of [monogoto], I studied hard" — nonsensical without a specific goal named
3その物事の作者は、いまや人気作家です。Sono monogoto no sakusha wa, ima ya ninki sakka desu."The author of that [monogoto] is now a popular writer" — should be 物語 (a story), not 物事
4物事には必ず裏と表があります。Monogoto ni wa kanarazu ura to omote ga arimasu.Every matter/thing always has two sides (a front and a back).

Why (reasoned): Day 1's 学校で多くの物事を学ぶ ("to learn many things/matters at school") confirms 物事 means abstract "things/matters" in general — sentence 4's idiom "物事には裏と表がある" ("every matter has two sides") is the classic natural usage; 1 confuses it with physical clutter (物), and 3 confuses it with 物語 ("a story").
