JLPT N2 · Vocabulary · Week 1 — 楽しく暮らしていますか？ (Tanoshiku kurashite imasu ka?)

4日目 — 家事・子育ては大変です (Kaji, kosodate wa taihen desu)

Source
日本語総まとめ N2 語彙 (Nihongo Sou-Matome N2 Goi) — printed p.18–19
Theme
家事・子育て・子どものいたずらに関する言葉 (housework, child-rearing, children's mischief)
Answer key
練習Ⅰ・Ⅱ answers for this day are printed in the "19ページの答え" box at the bottom of p.21 — confirmed, not guessed.
Notes
Scene: a chaotic family evening — siblings fighting, a baby needing a diaper change, dishes to clear, and a child's scribbles discovered on the wall. Vocabulary splits into a housework/child-rearing table (p.18) and a "mischief and rough play" table (p.19).
⊙ = 関連する語彙 (a related word/expression)
(N) = 名詞 (noun)
Scene

A household at its most chaotic: siblings fighting, a baby needing a diaper change, dishes piling up, and a scribble found on the wall.

Father: こら、二人とも、けんかはよせ！Kora, futari tomo, kenka wa yose!
("Hey, both of you, stop fighting!")

Younger child (crying): お兄ちゃんが頭をぶったーOniichan ga atama o buttaa
("Big brother hit me on the head!")

Mother (to the baby): おむつ換えようね。Omutsu kaeyou ne.
("Let's change your diaper, okay?")

Mother (to her daughter): よう子、洗い物を流しに持っていきなさい。Youko, araimono o nagashi ni motte ikinasai.
("Youko, take the dirty dishes to the sink.")

Daughter (at the table): あ〜あ、こんなところに落書きしたのはだれ？Aaa, konna tokoro ni rakugaki shita no wa dare?
("Ugh, who scribbled here?")

けんかはよせ (kenka wa yose) — よす (＝やめる) used as a blunt imperative: "stop it / cut it out"

練習Ⅰ — choose the correct option

Q1

お母さんが赤ちゃんを背中に（a. だっこ　b. おんぶ）している。Okaasan ga akachan o senaka ni (a. dakko / b. onbu) shite iru.

Full sentence: お母さんが赤ちゃんを背中におんぶしている。Okaasan ga akachan o senaka ni onbu shite iru.
EN: The mother is carrying the baby on her back.
HI: माँ बच्चे को अपनी पीठ पर उठाए हुए है।
GU: મા બાળકને પોતાની પીઠ પર ઊંચકીને રાખે છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
aだっこdakkoholding in one's arms (not on the back)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
bおんぶonbucarrying on one's backपीठ पर उठानाપીઠ પર ઊંચકવું

Why: 背中に ("on the back") specifically calls for おんぶ; だっこ is for carrying a baby facing you in your arms — the two aren't interchangeable positions.

Full sentence: サッカーボールを足でけった。Sakkaa booru o ashi de ketta.
EN: I kicked the soccer ball with my foot.
HI: मैंने फ़ुटबॉल को पैर से लात मारी।
GU: મેં ફૂટબોલને પગથી લાત મારી.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
aなぐったnaguttapunched (with a fist, not a foot)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
bけったkettakickedलात मारीલાત મારી

Why: 足でける ("to kick with the foot") is the natural verb for hitting a ball with your foot; なぐる specifically means to punch/strike with a fist, which doesn't fit 足で.

Q5

爪をかむのは（a. よしなさい　b. たたきなさい）。Tsume o kamu no wa (a. yoshinasai / b. tatakinasai).

Full sentence: 爪をかむのはよしなさい。Tsume o kamu no wa yoshinasai.
EN: Stop biting your nails.
HI: नाखून चबाना बंद करो।
GU: નખ કરડવાનું બંધ કરો.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
aよしなさいyoshinasaistop it (a habit/action)बंद करोબંધ કરો
bたたきなさいtatakinasaihit/spank (nonsensical here — you can't "spank" a habit)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Why: よしなさい (from よす＝やめる) is the fixed command for telling someone to stop a habit/action like nail-biting; たたく means "to hit/spank," which can't apply to stopping the act of nail-biting itself.

EN: I got yelled at loudly by my father.
HI: पिता ने मुझे ज़ोर से डाँटा।

OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Today's words split into near-synonym clusters for "hitting," "carrying a baby," and "raising a child badly/well" — exactly what Q1, Q2/Q3/Q6, and Q7/Q8 above test. Compare them:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
なぐるnaguru To punch/strike hard A forceful hit, usually with a fist — physically aggressive. "He punched him in the face."
たたくtataku To hit/spank/tap A lighter, often disciplinary hit (spanking a child), or even a friendly tap. "She spanked the child's bottom."
どなる／怒鳴るdonaru To yell/shout at Entirely verbal — no physical contact at all. "My father yelled at me loudly."
だっこするdakko suru To hold a baby in your arms Face-to-face, cradled in the arms. Comforting or feeding a baby.
おんぶするonbu suru To carry a baby on your back Baby faces outward/backward, hands-free for the carrier. Carrying a baby while doing chores.
子どもを甘やかすkodomo o amayakasu To spoil a child Giving in too much — the parent's failure to set limits. Warning against overindulgence.
子どもをしつけるkodomo o shitsukeru To discipline / instill manners The opposite goal — actively teaching right from wrong. Describing the positive task of upbringing.
Register note: よす／よせ is a blunt, casual imperative for "stop it/cut it out" (as the father snaps けんかはよせ！ at his fighting kids) — it's rougher than やめる and mostly heard as a command, not in polite or written contexts. いたずら ("mischief") and いじめ ("bullying") both describe misbehavior but are not interchangeable severities: calling bullying "いたずら" downplays real harm to a victim, so don't swap them even though both appear as "naughty behavior" in English.
