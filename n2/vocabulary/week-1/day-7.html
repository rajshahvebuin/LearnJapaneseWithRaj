Q1

私は（　）20年の一軒家に住んでいる。Watashi wa (___) nijuu-nen no ikken'ya ni sunde iru.

Full sentence: 私は築20年の一軒家に住んでいる。Watashi wa chiku nijuu-nen no ikken'ya ni sunde iru.
EN: I live in a house that was built 20 years ago.
HI: मैं 20 साल पहले बने एक घर में रहता/रहती हूँ।
GU: હું 20 વર્ષ પહેલાં બંધાયેલા મકાનમાં રહું છું.
#JapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
1経kei(not a real counter here)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
2過ka(not a real counter here)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
3建ken(this is the counter for stories, e.g. 3階建て, not "years since built")(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
4築chikuyears since builtबने हुए (वर्ष)બંધાયાનાં (વર્ષ)

Why: Day 1's 築10年 (chiku juu-nen, "built 10 years ago") shows 築＋number＋年 is the fixed way to say how long ago a building was constructed; 建て is a different counter, for the number of stories.

Q2

私の家の（　）を電車が走っています。Watashi no ie no (___) o densha ga hashitte imasu.

Full sentence: 私の家の真後ろを電車が走っています。Watashi no ie no massushiro o densha ga hashitte imasu.
EN: A train runs right behind my house.
HI: मेरे घर के ठीक पीछे से ट्रेन गुज़रती है।
GU: મારા ઘરની બરાબર પાછળથી ટ્રેન પસાર થાય છે.
#JapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
1真後ろmassushiroright behind (attaches naturally to 家の)ठीक पीछेબરાબર પાછળ
2この付近kono fukinaround here (この already means "this," so 家の＋この is redundant/ungrammatical)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
3この辺りkono atariaround here (same problem as option 2)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
4真ん中mannakaright in the middle (a train can't run through the middle of a house)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Why: 家の真後ろ ("right behind the house") is a normal spot for train tracks to run; この付近／この辺り already contain the demonstrative この ("this"), so they can't naturally follow 家の (which would double-mark "this-ness"), and 真ん中 doesn't make sense for something running past/through a house.

Q3

（　）の新しい冷蔵庫に買い換えたら、電気代が安くなった。(___) no atarashii reizouko ni kaikaetara, denkidai ga yasuku natta.

Full sentence: 省エネの新しい冷蔵庫に買い換えたら、電気代が安くなった。Shou-ene no atarashii reizouko ni kaikaetara, denkidai ga yasuku natta.
EN: When I replaced it with a new energy-saving fridge, the electricity bill got cheaper.
HI: ऊर्जा-बचत वाले नए फ़्रिज से बदलने पर बिजली का बिल कम हो गया।
GU: ઊર્જા-બચત વાળા નવા ફ્રિજથી બદલતાં વીજળીનું બિલ ઘટી ગયું.
#JapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
1リサイクルrisaikururecycling (unrelated to a fridge's own efficiency)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
2省エネshou-eneenergy-savingऊर्जा-बचतઊર્જા-બચત
3節約setsuyakusaving (an action one takes, not an adjective describing the fridge)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
4再利用sairiyoureuse (unrelated to a fridge's efficiency)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Why: Day 2's 省エネ describes a product as energy-saving, exactly modifying 冷蔵庫 here; 節約 describes the act of saving, which doesn't work as a noun-modifier before 新しい冷蔵庫 the same way.

Q4

#JapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
1管理料kanriryoumanagement fee (for an apartment, unrelated to a bank transfer)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
2敷金shikikina rental deposit (unrelated to a bank transfer)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Q5

逃げていた銀行強盗がやっと（　）。Nigete ita ginkou goutou ga yatto (___).

Full sentence: 逃げていた銀行強盗がやっと捕まった。Nigete ita ginkou goutou ga yatto tsukamatta.
EN: The bank robber who had been on the run was finally caught.
HI: भागता हुआ बैंक डाकू आख़िरकार पकड़ा गया।
GU: ભાગતો બેંક લૂંટારો છેવટે પકડાયો.
#JapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
1捕まったtsukamattagot caughtपकड़ा गयाપકડાયો
2片付けたkatazuketatidied up (wrong meaning entirely)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
3引っかかったhikkakattagot hooked/conned (fits a scam victim, not an arrested robber)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
4処分したshobun shitadisposed of (wrong meaning, and wrong voice — the robber isn't disposing of anything)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Why: Day 5's （〜が）捕まる is the intransitive verb for a person ending up caught, exactly fitting 銀行強盗が...捕まった ("the bank robber got caught"); 引っかかる is used for scam victims, a different figurative sense of "getting caught."

Q6

Full sentence: そのごみは、リサイクルの資源になるので、可燃ごみには捨てないでください。Sono gomi wa, risaikuru no shigen ni naru node, kanen gomi ni wa sutenaide kudasai.

GU: આ કચરો રિસાયક્લિંગનું સંસાધન બની શકે છે, એટલે તેને બળે તેવા કચરામાં ન નાખો.
#JapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
1用品youhinsupplies/goods (doesn't fit "リサイクルの〜")(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
2資源shigena resourceसंसाधनસંસાધન
3再利用sairiyoureuse (an action, not "becoming" a category of garbage)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
4不燃funenunburnable (contradicts being told not to throw it as 可燃ごみ specifically because it's recyclable)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Why: Day 2's 資源ごみ ("recyclable garbage") shows リサイクルの資源になる ("becomes a recycling resource") is the natural reason to sort something out of burnable trash.

Q21

つまらないものですがtsumaranai mono desu ga

#JapaneseEnglish
1よくいらっしゃいました。つまらないものですが、お元気でしたか。Yoku irasshaimashita. Tsumaranai mono desu ga, ogenki deshita ka.Welcome! [Humble gift phrase], how have you been? — no gift is being handed over here
2つまらないものですが、どうぞゆっくりしていってください。Tsumaranai mono desu ga, douzo yukkuri shite itte kudasai.[Humble gift phrase], please stay and relax — no gift is being mentioned, said by the wrong person (the host)

Q22

高くつくtakaku tsuku

#JapaneseEnglish
1あのビル、36階建てですが、とても高くつきますね。Ano biru, sanjuu-rok-kai date desu ga, totemo takaku tsukimasu ne.That building is 36 stories, and it's very [takaku tsuku] — misuses it to mean physically "tall"
2チラシを見て遠くのスーパーへ行ったが、交通費がかかってかえって高くついた。Chirashi o mite tooku no suupaa e itta ga, koutsuuhi ga kakatte kaette takaku tsuita.I saw a flyer and went to a distant supermarket, but the transport costs meant it ended up costing more after all.
3富士山は日本でいちばん高くつく山です。Fujisan wa Nihon de ichiban takaku tsuku yama desu.Mt. Fuji is the [takaku tsuku]-est mountain in Japan — misuses it to mean physically "tall"
4オークションで古いテレビが高くついて売れた。Ookushon de furui terebi ga takaku tsuite ureta.At the auction, the old TV sold [takaku tsuite] — misuses it to mean "sold at a high price," not "cost more than expected"

Why: 高くつく specifically means something ends up costing more than intended/expected, usually because of a hidden extra cost — sentence 2's "went somewhere to save money but transport costs made it pricier after all" is the textbook case. Sentences 1 and 3 wrongly use it to mean physical height/tallness, and 4 wrongly uses it for a high selling price.

Q23

甘えるamaeru

#JapaneseEnglish
1子猫が甘えた声で鳴いていた。Koneko ga amaeta koe de naite ita.The kitten was mewing in a coaxing, affection-seeking voice.
2このケーキはとても甘える。Kono keeki wa totemo amaeru.This cake is very [amaeru] — confuses 甘える with 甘い ("sweet-tasting")
3彼は一人っ子なのでとても甘えて育てられたようです。Kare wa hitorikko na node totemo amaete sodaterareta you desu.Being an only child, he seems to have been raised very [amaete] — should be 甘やかされて (was spoiled by his parents), not 甘えて (his own attention-seeking behavior)
4社長は今日は機嫌が悪く、甘えている。Shachou wa kyou wa kigen ga waruku, amaete iru.The president is in a bad mood today and is [amaete iru] — a bad mood isn't the same as acting spoiled/dependent

Why: 甘える means to act sweetly dependent/attention-seeking (as toward a parent), and 甘えた声 ("a coaxing voice") is a common natural phrase; sentence 2 confuses it with 甘い (sweet-tasting), and 3 confuses it with the passive of the different verb 甘やかす (to spoil someone else).

Q24

けるkeru

#JapaneseEnglish
1顔を手でけられて、けがをした。Kao o te de kerarete, kega o shita.I was "kicked" in the face with a hand and got hurt — contradiction: ける is with the foot, not 手で
2いたずらが見つかって、父に大声でけられた。Itazura ga mitsukatte, chichi ni oogoe de kerareta.My mischief was found out, and I was "kicked" loudly by my father — 大声で ("loudly") pairs with どなられた, not a physical kick
3前の席をけらないでください。Mae no seki o keranaide kudasai.Please don't kick the seat in front of you.
4最後まで、ボールをゴールにけられなかった。Saigo made, booru o gooru ni kerarenakatta.Until the end, the ball "was not kicked" into the goal — should be the potential けれなかった ("couldn't kick it"), not the passive けられなかった

Why: ける specifically means to kick with the foot, and sentence 3 ("don't kick the seat in front of you") is a common, natural instruction; 1 wrongly pairs it with 手で ("with a hand"), 2 confuses it with どなる (Day 4's "to yell"), and 4 confuses the passive form with the potential form.

Q25

召し上がるmeshiagaru

#JapaneseEnglish
1ただいまお茶をお召し上がりします。Tadaima ocha o omeshiagari shimasu.I will now [meshiagaru] tea — wrong direction: 召し上がる honors the listener's action, not the speaker's own (should be いただきます)
2寒いので家の中へお召し上がりください。Samui node ie no naka e omeshiagari kudasai.Since it's cold, please [meshiagaru] into the house — wrong verb entirely; should be お入りください ("please come in")
3これはこれは、遠いところまでようこそお召し上がりくださいました。Kore wa kore wa, tooi tokoro made youkoso omeshiagari kudasaimashita.Well, well, thank you for [meshiagaru]-ing all this way — wrong verb; should be いらっしゃいました ("for coming")
4冷めないうちに、どうぞお召し上がりください。Samenai uchi ni, douzo omeshiagari kudasai.Please eat/drink it before it gets cold.

Why: 召し上がる is the honorific verb for "to eat/drink," used only for someone else's (the listener's) action — sentence 4 correctly tells a guest to eat while food is warm; 1 wrongly applies the honorific to the speaker's own action, and 2–3 wrongly use it as if it meant "to enter" or "to come," rather than "to eat/drink."
