2日目 — 引っ越しは大変です (Hikkoshi wa taihen desu)

Source
日本語総まとめ N2 語彙 (Nihongo Sou-Matome N2 Goi) — printed p.14–15
Theme
家電の買い替え・ごみの分別・リサイクルに関する言葉 (replacing appliances, sorting garbage, recycling)
Answer key
練習Ⅰ・Ⅱ answers for this day are printed in the "15ページの答え" box at the bottom of p.17 — confirmed, not guessed.
Notes
Scene: a couple decide to replace their fridge, then struggle with sorting the garbage. Vocabulary splits into a picture-labeled "look and learn" set (packaging/container items) and two "おぼえましょう" sets (appliances/energy-saving, then garbage types and disposal verbs).
⊙ = 関連する語彙 (a related word/expression)
⇔ = 反対の意味 (antonym)
※ = 「が」can be swapped for 「の」 in that phrase (also seen used this way in N2, same as N1)
Scene

A couple is doing chores at home — one is eyeing a new refrigerator, the other is struggling with the garbage.

Person A: 冷蔵庫、買い換えたいなあ。Reizouko, kaikaetai naa. 新しいのは省エネだから、電気代の節約にもなるし……Atarashii no wa shou-ene dakara, denkidai no setsuyaku ni mo naru shi...
("I want to replace the fridge. A new one is energy-saving, so it'd help save on the electricity bill too...")

Person B: ああ、ごみの分別は本当に面倒くさい。Aa, gomi no bunbetsu wa hontou ni mendou kusai. あ、これ、もれてる。A, kore, moreteru.
("Ugh, sorting the garbage is such a pain. Oh — this is leaking.")

練習Ⅰ — choose the correct option

Q1

新しい洗濯機に買い換えたら水道代が（a. 節約　b. 省エネ）できました。Atarashii sentakuki ni kaikaetara suidoudai ga (a. setsuyaku / b. shou-ene) dekimashita.

Full sentence: 新しい洗濯機に買い換えたら水道代が節約できました。Atarashii sentakuki ni kaikaetara suidoudai ga setsuyaku dekimashita.
EN: After replacing it with a new washing machine, I was able to save on the water bill.
HI: नई वॉशिंग मशीन ख़रीदने के बाद पानी का बिल बचाया जा सका।
GU: નવું વોશિંગ મશીન લીધા પછી પાણીનું બિલ બચાવી શકાયું.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a節約setsuyakuto save/economize (money, resources)बचत करनाબચત કરવી
b省エネshou-eneenergy-saving (a description, not a verb one "does" to a bill)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Why: 「〜代が節約できる」("the ~ bill can be saved on") takes 節約, a verb-like noun for saving money; 省エネ describes a product/behavior as energy-saving, but doesn't combine with 〜ができる about a bill directly.

Q2

古くなったテレビを（a. 分別　b. 処分）した。Furuku natta terebi o (a. bunbetsu / b. shobun) shita.

Full sentence: 古くなったテレビを処分した。Furuku natta terebi o shobun shita.
EN: I got rid of the TV that had gotten old.
HI: पुरानी हो चुकी टीवी को निपटा दिया।
GU: જૂનું થઈ ગયેલું ટીવી કાઢી નાખ્યું.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a分別bunbetsusorting (garbage into categories, not disposing of one whole item)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
b処分shobunto dispose of, get rid ofनिपटानाનિકાલ કરવો

Why: 処分する means "to get rid of/dispose of" a single unwanted item like an old TV; 分別する is specifically about sorting garbage into categories (burnable/unburnable/etc.), which doesn't fit "a TV" as a single object being discarded.

HI: कृपया बोतलों को अच्छी तरह धोकर ही रीसाइक्लिंग के लिए बाहर रखें।

OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

This day's exercises hinge on telling apart near-synonyms for "getting rid of something" and "liquid escaping" — exactly what 練習Ⅰ Q2/Q5 and Q3/Q4/Q7 above test. Compare them:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
分別するbunbetsu suru To sort into categories About dividing garbage into groups (burnable/unburnable/recyclable) — needs at least two categories. "Separate A and B before throwing them out."
処分するshobun suru To dispose of, get rid of About getting rid of one unwanted item entirely — no sorting implied. "I got rid of my old TV."
もれるmoreru To leak (out) A slow, structural leak — from a crack, a loose seal, a bag with a hole. Also used figuratively for secrets. "Liquid is leaking from the garbage bag."
こぼれるkoboreru To spill (out) A sudden spill from a tipped or overfilled container — an event, not a slow leak. "The glass tipped over and the drink spilled."
たまるtamaru To accumulate, pool up Gradual buildup over time (garbage, stress, water) — the opposite direction from もれる/こぼれる (things collecting, not escaping). "Garbage has been piling up."
Figurative extension: もれる／もらす aren't just about liquids — 秘密がもれる ("a secret leaks out") and 秘密をもらす ("to leak/let slip a secret") use the exact same verbs for information escaping as for water escaping a container. こぼれる doesn't extend to secrets this way — you can't say 秘密がこぼれる for a leaked secret, only もれる/もらす.
