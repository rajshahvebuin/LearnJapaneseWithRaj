JLPT N2 · Vocabulary · Week 4 — 副詞をたっぷり覚えましょう (Fukushi o tappuri oboemashou)

3日目 — せっせと覚えよう！ (Sesseto oboeyou!)

Source
日本語総まとめ N2 語彙 (Nihongo Sou-Matome N2 Goi) — printed p.64–65
Theme
様子を表す言葉 (adverbs describing manner/state — じっくり／ぎっしり／さっさと／せっせと etc.)
Answer key
練習Ⅰ・Ⅱ answers for this day are printed in the "65ページの答え" box at the bottom of p.67 — confirmed, not guessed.
Notes
Opens with a two-character dialogue: a mother nagging her son the night before an exam, and his exasperated internal reply. The .vd-warmup section below reproduces both sides.
⊙ = 関連する語彙 (a related word/expression)
⇔ = 反対の意味 (antonym)
Scene

The night before an exam, a mother keeps giving her son advice while he grows exasperated.

Mother: いよいよ、明日は試験ね。今日はさっさとお風呂に入って寝なさい。試験のときは、問題をじっくり読んで…Iyoiyo, ashita wa shiken ne. Kyou wa sassato ofuro ni haitte nenasai. Shiken no toki wa, mondai o jikkuri yonde...
("At last, tomorrow is the exam. Today, quickly get in the bath and go to sleep. During the exam, read the questions carefully...")

Son (thinking): 毎日、こんな注意ばかり。もううんざりだよ。Mainichi, konna chuui bakari. Mou unzari da yo.
("Every day, nothing but this kind of nagging. I'm so fed up already.")

Caption: "I'll memorize them diligently!" (坚持不懈地记住吧！／열심히 외우자!)

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

This day is full of similar-sounding "manner" adverbs that trip learners up — exactly what Q1–Q8 above test. Compare them:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
じっくりjikkuri Slowly and carefully (a manner of thinking/reading) Describes the pace and attention of a mental action. "Think it over carefully."
ぎっしりgisshiri Tightly packed (a state of density) Sounds almost identical to じっくり but describes objects crammed together, not a manner of thinking — a classic sound-alike trap. "The schedule is packed tight."
さっさとsassato Quickly, without delay (finishing/leaving fast) Emphasizes speed in getting something done or leaving. "Leave at once."
せっせとsesseto Diligently, steadily (sustained hard work) Also sounds close to さっさと, but describes steady effort over time, not speed. "Work steadily for one's family."
どっとdotto All at once, in a rush (a crowd/wave of fatigue) A sudden surge affecting many people/a large amount at once. "Exhaustion suddenly hit all at once."
すっとsutto Smoothly, abruptly (a single motion); refreshed One smooth/quick action or feeling, not a surge affecting many. "Stand up abruptly." / "Feel refreshed."
ばったりbattari By chance (an unexpected encounter) An accidental, surprising meeting. "Bump into a friend."
こっそりkossori Secretly, stealthily (a deliberate hidden action) An intentional act done to avoid being noticed. "Sneak out." / "Secretly eat a snack at night."
Sound-alike trap: じっくり and ぎっしり differ by a single sound but describe unrelated things — じっくり is about the careful pace of a mental action (thinking, reading), while ぎっしり is about physical density (things packed tightly). Likewise さっさと (fast, get-it-done-and-leave) and せっせと (steady, diligent effort over time) are easy to mix up by ear but describe opposite paces of work.
