JLPT N2 · Vocabulary · Week 4 — 副詞をたっぷり覚えましょう (Fukushi o tappuri oboemashou)

4日目 — さらに覚えよう！ (Sara ni oboeyou! — I'll try to memorize more!)

Source
日本語総まとめ N2 語彙 (Nihongo Sou-Matome N2 Goi) — printed p.66–67
Theme
程度を表す副詞 (adverbs expressing degree — how much, how strongly, or how the degree of something changes)
Answer key
練習Ⅰ・Ⅱ answers for this day are printed in the "67ページの答え" box at the bottom of p.69 — confirmed, not guessed.
Notes
Unlike earlier weeks' dialogue-scene days, Week 4 is a pure adverb week: the opening panel shows a short exchange between two people using the day's degree adverbs, then the vocabulary is grouped by the book itself into 5 sub-categories (a lot / a little / comparison / change / other) rather than by situation.
Scene

Two people comment on how suddenly cold the weather has turned, comparing this winter to last year's.

Him: このごろ、めっきり寒くなったね。明日の朝は、一段と冷えるらしいよ。Kono goro, mekkiri samuku natta ne. Ashita no asa wa, ichidan to hieru rashii yo.
("It's gotten noticeably cold these days, hasn't it. Tomorrow morning is apparently going to be even colder.")

Her: 予報では、今年の冬はわりあい暖かいって言ってたけど、去年よりむしろ寒いよね。Yohou de wa, kotoshi no fuyu wa wariai atatakai tte itteta kedo, kyonen yori mushiro samui yo ne.
("The forecast said this winter would be relatively warm, but if anything, it's colder than last year, isn't it.")

Four degree-adverbs at work here: めっきり(mekkiri) "noticeably", 一段と(ichidan to) "a step further", わりあい(wariai) "relatively", むしろ(mushiro) "rather/if anything" — today's whole lesson is variations on these.

2 Quiz / Exercise Section

練習Ⅰ (6 items, from p.67) and 練習Ⅱ (2 items, from p.67). Answers confirmed from the "67ページの答え" box printed at the bottom of p.69.

練習Ⅰ — choose the correct option

Q1

彼女からのメールが（a. たっぷり　b. めっきり）減ってしまった。Kanojo kara no meeru ga (a. tappuri / b. mekkiri) hette shimatta.

Full sentence: 彼女からのメールがめっきり減ってしまった。Kanojo kara no meeru ga mekkiri hette shimatta.
EN: Emails from her have noticeably decreased.
HI: उससे आने वाले ईमेल काफ़ी हद तक कम हो गए हैं।
GU: તેના તરફથી આવતા ઈમેલ નોંધપાત્ર રીતે ઘટી ગયા છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
aたっぷりtappuriamply (fits an abundant amount, not a noticeable drop)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
bめっきりmekkirinoticeablyस्पष्ट रूप सेનોંધપાત્ર રીતે

Why: めっきり describes a marked, noticeable change (here, a decrease); たっぷり describes an ample/abundant quantity and doesn't fit describing something dropping off.

HI: सुधारा गया सॉफ़्टवेयर पहले से काफ़ी इस्तेमाल में आसान है।
GU: સુધારેલું સોફ્ટવેર પહેલા કરતાં ઘણું વાપરવામાં સહેલું છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
aかなりkanariconsiderablyकाफ़ीઘણું
b次第にshidai nigradually (fits a process unfolding over time, not a static comparison)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Why: かなり directly modifies the degree of "easier to use" in a straightforward 前より comparison; 次第に describes something changing gradually over time, which doesn't fit this simple side-by-side comparison.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Today's whole lesson is near-synonyms for "a lot" and "more/further" — exactly what Q1–Q3, Q6, and Q7–Q8 above test. Compare them:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
めっきりmekkiri Noticeably (a marked change) Highlights that a change is striking/obvious, often for a decrease or a seasonal shift. "Her emails have noticeably dropped off."
じょじょに／次第にjojo ni / shidai ni Gradually A slow, steady process unfolding over time — not a single comparison. "The snow gradually melted."
ぐっとgutto Much more (a sudden jump) A single, marked jump in degree, often after one triggering event. "Adding salt made it taste a lot better."
一段とichidan to Even more, a notch further An additional step up from an already-existing degree. "It's become a lot colder still."
わりと／わりにwarito / warini Relatively (＝比較的) An objective, neutral evaluation against a general standard. "This is a relatively easy problem."
わりあいにwariai ni Relatively, unexpectedly Similar to わりに but often carries a note of surprise at the outcome. "I arrived earlier than I expected."
むしろmushiro Rather, if anything Directly compares two options and picks the less obvious one. "I'd rather have B than A."
余計にyokei ni All the more (unwanted) An increase that is a side-effect, usually negative/unwelcome. "That only made it more confusing."
Direction trap: いくらか ("somewhat") and よけいに ("all the more") both modify degree, but point opposite directions — いくらかましになった means the pain got a little *better*, while よけいにひどくなった would mean it got *worse*. Confusing these two in Q4 flips the sentence's meaning entirely, so always check whether the surrounding verb (ましになる vs 悪くなる/わからなくなる) implies improvement or worsening before picking one.
