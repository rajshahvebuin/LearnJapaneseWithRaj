Theme
意味がたくさんある動詞 — 切れる／切る・つく／つける・当たる／当てる (verbs with many meanings: "break/run out/cut," "stick/attach," "hit/apply")

Notes
第7週 まとめて覚えましょう② is a review/consolidation week. Days 1–3 each collect verbs/adjectives that carry many distinct senses rather than one. For every headword below, each row is a separate sense the book itself demonstrates through its own example collocation — they are kept side by side rather than flattened into a single gloss. Scene: a mother notices the milk is expired and asks her son to buy milk and soy sauce; the son, mid-study, gets visibly annoyed — illustrating the slang sense of キレる ("to snap/lose one's temper") alongside its literal "to cut/break" senses.
⊙ = 関連する語彙 (a related word/expression)
Scene

A mother holds up a milk carton while her son studies at his desk, getting a headache.

Mother: あ、この牛乳、賞味期限が切れてる。たかし、牛乳買ってきて。それからしょうゆも切れそうだから、ついでにしょうゆもお願い。それから…A, kono gyuunyuu, shoumikigen ga kireteru. Takashi, gyuunyuu katte kite. Sorekara shouyu mo kiresou dakara, tsuide ni shouyu mo onegai. Sorekara...
("Oh, this milk is past its expiration date. Takashi, go buy some milk. Also the soy sauce is about to run out, so please get soy sauce too while you're at it. And then...")

Son (thinking, holding his head): もう、勉強中なのに。Mou, benkyouchuu na noni.
("Ugh, and I'm right in the middle of studying.")

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Today's whole theme is words that carry many senses — the danger is flattening distinct meanings of the same headword into one English gloss, or mixing up the intransitive/transitive pair (～れる vs ～る／～せる) that each headword group is built from. Compare:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
電池が切れるdenchi ga kireru The battery dies (by itself) Intransitive — the subject changes state on its own. "The battery ran out."
電源を切るdengen o kiru To switch the power off (someone does it) Transitive — a person acts on the object. "He switched off the power."
見当がつくkentou ga tsuku To be able to guess (a guess comes to you) Intransitive — the estimate simply arises. "I have a rough idea."
見当をつけるkentou o tsukeru To make a guess (you actively estimate) Transitive — deliberately forming an estimate. "Let me make an educated guess."
答えが当たるkotae ga ataru The answer turns out right Describes the outcome of a guess already made. "My guess turned out correct."
見当がつくkentou ga tsuku To be able to form a guess at all Describes whether an estimate can be formed in the first place — before it is checked against reality. "I can't even guess."
Slang trap: キレる〈俗語〉("to snap/lose one's temper") looks identical to 切れる ("to break/run out") but is a separate colloquial usage applied to people's emotional state — it does not mean the person "ran out" of anything; it describes them suddenly losing control, as in 犯罪を犯す若者が増加している ("the number of young people who commit crimes out of sudden rage is increasing").
