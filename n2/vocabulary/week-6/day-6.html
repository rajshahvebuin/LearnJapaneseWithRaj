Notes
Third of Week 6's まとめ days on confusable words — this time the confusion is purely acoustic: every headword pairs two same-sounding words distinguished only by kanji. Opens with a man scolding someone for giving up on school too easily, directly using あきた／あきらめる／あきれる／見方／味方 in one breath.
Scene

A man lectures a younger person who has decided to quit studying.

Him: 勉強にあきたからと言って、簡単に進学をあきらめるなんて、聞いてあきれるよ。物事の見方を変えないと、誰も君の味方をしないよ。Benkyou ni akita kara to itte, kantan ni shingaku o akirameru nante, kiite akireru yo. Monogoto no mikata o kaenai to, dare mo kimi no mikata o shinai yo.
("Just because you got tired of studying, giving up on going to school that easily — I'm appalled to hear it. If you don't change how you look at things, nobody's going to be on your side.")

One sentence, four confusable sound-alikes: あきた (あきる, "got tired of"), あきらめる ("give up"), あきれる ("be appalled"), and 見方／味方 (mikata/mikata, "viewpoint" vs. "ally") — exactly today's lesson in miniature.

Q1

インターネットは情報社会に大きい変化を（a. もたれた　b. もたらした）。Intaanetto wa jouhou shakai ni ookii henka o (a. motareta / b. motarashita).

Full sentence: インターネットは情報社会に大きい変化をもたらした。Intaanetto wa jouhou shakai ni ookii henka o motarashita.
EN: The internet brought about a big change in the information society.
HI: इंटरनेट ने सूचना समाज में एक बड़ा बदलाव लाया।
GU: ઇન્ટરનેટે માહિતી સમાજમાં મોટું પરિવર્તન લાવ્યું.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
aもたれたmotaretaleaned against (fits a wall, not causing a change)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
bもたらしたmotarashitabrought aboutलायाલાવ્યું

Why: 変化をもたらす ("bring about change") is the fixed collocation for causing an effect; もたれる means "to lean against" something physical, an unrelated word that only sounds similar.

Q2

これは、（a. 人口　b. 人工）で作られた湖である。Kore wa, (a. jinkou / b. jinkou) de tsukurareta mizuumi de aru.

Full sentence: これは、人工で作られた湖である。Kore wa, jinkou de tsukurareta mizuumi de aru.
EN: This is an artificially made lake.
HI: यह एक कृत्रिम रूप से बनाई गई झील है।
GU: આ કૃત્રિમ રીતે બનાવેલું તળાવ છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a人口jinkoupopulation (a count of people — unrelated to how a lake was made)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
b人工jinkouartificial/man-madeकृत्रिमકૃત્રિમ

Why: 人工で作られた ("artificially made") describes something built by human effort rather than occurring naturally; 人口 ("population") is a completely different word that just happens to share the same pronunciation.

Q3

このあたりの駐車料金は（a. あきれる　b. あきる）ほど高い。Kono atari no chuusha ryoukin wa (a. akireru / b. akiru) hodo takai.

Full sentence: このあたりの駐車料金はあきれるほど高い。Kono atari no chuusha ryoukin wa akireru hodo takai.
EN: The parking fees around here are shockingly (appallingly) high.
HI: इस इलाक़े में पार्किंग शुल्क चौंकाने की हद तक ज़्यादा है।
GU: આ વિસ્તારમાં પાર્કિંગ ફી અચંબિત થાય એટલી વધારે છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
aあきれるakireruto be appalled/shockedचौंकाने की हद तकઅચંબિત થાય એટલું
bあきるakiruto be tired of (fits weariness from repetition, not a one-time shocking price)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Why: あきれるほど高い ("shockingly/appallingly high") uses あきれる for something extreme enough to provoke disbelief; あきる describes growing weary of something repetitive over time, which doesn't fit describing a price level.

Q5

死んだと思った猫は（a. 実は　b. 実に）隣の家で飼われていた。Shinda to omotta neko wa (a. jitsu wa / b. jitsu ni) tonari no ie de kawarete ita.

Full sentence: 死んだと思った猫は実は隣の家で飼われていた。Shinda to omotta neko wa jitsu wa tonari no ie de kawarete ita.
EN: The cat I thought had died was, in fact, being kept at the house next door.
HI: जिस बिल्ली को मैं मरी हुई समझ बैठा था, वह असल में पड़ोस के घर में पाली जा रही थी।
GU: જે બિલાડી મરી ગઈ છે એમ મેં માન્યું હતું, તે ખરેખર પડોશના ઘરમાં પાળવામાં આવી રહી હતી.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a実はjitsu wain fact/actually (reveals a surprising truth)असल मेंખરેખર
b実にjitsu nitruly/really (an intensifier for adjectives, not an opener for a revelation)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Why: 実は introduces a surprising fact that reveals the real situation ("actually, it turns out..."); 実に is an intensifying adverb that modifies an adjective (実にすばらしい, "truly wonderful"), not a way to introduce a revelation.

Full sentence: 事件の犯人は意外な人物だった。Jiken no hannin wa igai na jinbutsu datta.
EN: The culprit of the incident was an unexpected person.
HI: घटना का अपराधी एक अप्रत्याशित व्यक्ति निकला।
GU: ઘટનાનો ગુનેગાર એક અનપેક્ષિત વ્યક્તિ હતો.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
1意外なigai naunexpected (modifies 人物 directly)अप्रत्याशितઅનપેક્ષિત
2案外なangai na(unnatural na-adjective use here — 案外 pairs with と/に before an adjective/verb, not before a noun this way)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Today's whole point is acoustic confusion — same or near-identical pronunciation, different kanji and meaning. These are the highest-value pairs to drill for listening and reading alike:

Same soundKanji AKanji BTell them apart by
せめる責める semeru — to blame攻める semeru — to attack責める targets a person's fault; 攻める targets an opponent/position.
のる乗る noru — to go along with (advice)載る noru — to be published/listed乗る is about a person engaging (相談に乗る); 載る is about information appearing in print.
あたり辺り atari — vicinity/around当たり atari — per (unit)辺り marks a place; 当たり marks a rate/unit ("per person").
いがい以外 igai — other than, except意外 igai — unexpected以外 excludes an item from a set; 意外 describes surprise.
ふじん夫人 fujin — a specific man's wife/Mrs.婦人 fujin — women (general/formal)夫人 names one married woman; 婦人 is a category, as in 婦人服.
じんこう人口 jinkou — population人工 jinkou — artificial人口 counts people; 人工 describes something human-made.
みかた見方 mikata — viewpoint味方 mikata — ally/side見方 is how you see something; 味方 is whose side you're on.
とくちょう特徴 tokuchou — characteristic (neutral)特長 tokuchou — strong point (positive)特徴 can be good or bad; 特長 is always a point of superiority.
Book's own usage note: 「特徴」は良い点も悪い点も含むが、「特長」はほかと比べて優れている点を表す — a giraffe's long neck is a 特徴 (just a defining trait), but a new product's best feature over its competitors is a 特長. Confusing the two is one of this book's own flagged traps (see 実戦問題 Q21).
あきる family: あきる (飽きる, "tired of," gradual weariness) → あきらめる (諦める, "give up," the decision that can follow) → あきれる (呆れる, "appalled," a third party's shocked reaction to it) — today's scene dialogue uses all three in sequence, which is the easiest way to keep them straight.
