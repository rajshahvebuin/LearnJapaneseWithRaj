Q1

いい天気なので（a. 毛布　b. 分布）を洗った。よく乾いた。Ii tenki na node (a. moufu / b. bunpu) o aratta. Yoku kawaita.

Full sentence: いい天気なので毛布を洗った。よく乾いた。Ii tenki na node moufu o aratta. Yoku kawaita.
EN: Since the weather was nice, I washed the blanket. It dried well.
HI: मौसम अच्छा था इसलिए मैंने कंबल धोया। वह अच्छी तरह सूख गया।
GU: હવામાન સારું હતું એટલે મેં ધાબળો ધોયો. તે સારી રીતે સુકાઈ ગયો.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a毛布moufublanketकंबलધાબળો
b分布bunpudistribution (wrong sense)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Why: 毛布 ("blanket") is something you can wash and dry; 分布 means "distribution" (a statistical/geographic term) and cannot be washed.

2 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Several pairs today share a kanji or a reading and are worth comparing directly — exactly what today's quiz questions test:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
途中tochuu On the way, midway Used with a verb of motion — "on the way to/from somewhere." "On the way home," "partway through a movie."
中途chuuto Midway, unfinished Describes an abstract state of being incomplete, not physical travel. "An unfinished plan," "a mid-career change" (中途採用).
❶床yuka Floor (indoor) The plain, physical indoor floor surface. "Lay a carpet on the floor."
床の間toko no ma Alcove Same base kanji 床, but read とこ — a raised decorative alcove in a Japanese-style room. Displaying a flower arrangement or scroll.
床屋tokoya Barbershop Also read とこ — an unrelated everyday word that happens to share the same kanji. "Getting a haircut."
抜くnuku To pull out / remove (transitive) Someone actively removes something. "The dentist pulled the tooth."
抜けるnukeru To fall out / come loose (intransitive) Something falls out or goes missing by itself. "My hair is falling out" (抜け毛).
予防yobou Prevention (of illness) Standard collocation for diseases/health conditions. "Disease prevention," "cavity prevention."
防止boushi Prevention (of incidents) Standard collocation for accidents, crime, or incidents. "Accident prevention," "crime prevention."
防虫剤bouchuuzai Insect/moth repellent Repels insects away, used passively (e.g. in a drawer). Protecting stored clothing from moths.
殺虫剤sacchuuzai Insecticide Actively kills insects on contact. Spraying to kill bugs directly.
Exam trap: 床 alone carries two completely different kun-yomi depending on the compound: ゆか (plain "floor," as in 床にカーペットをしく) versus とこ (as in 床の間 "alcove" and 床屋 "barbershop," both flagged with ❶/note in the book). Always check the surrounding word, not just the kanji, before deciding the reading.
