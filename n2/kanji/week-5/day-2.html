Q1

スポーツには（a. 軟弱　b. 柔軟）体操は欠かせない。Supootsu ni wa (a. nanjaku / b. juunan) taisou wa kakasenai.

Full sentence: スポーツには柔軟体操は欠かせない。Supootsu ni wa juunan taisou wa kakasenai.
EN: Warm-up/stretching exercises are indispensable for sports.
HI: खेल के लिए वार्मअप व्यायाम अनिवार्य है।
GU: રમતગમત માટે વોર્મ-અપ કસરત અનિવાર્ય છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a軟弱nanjakuweak/feeble (wrong sense)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

2 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Several pairs today look alike or share a reading and are worth comparing directly — exactly what today's quiz questions test:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
柔らかいyawarakai (柔) Soft, tender Two different kanji, same reading やわらかい — 柔 leans toward flexibility/pliancy (judo, fabric). "A soft fabric," "flexible movement" — 柔軟.
❶軟らかいyawarakai (軟) Soft Same reading as 柔らかい, but 軟 leans toward physical softness of a substance/texture. "Soft ground," "tender meat" — physical texture.
香料kouryou Fragrance, scent ingredient Used in cosmetics/perfumery — something you smell. "This cosmetic contains no fragrance."
香辛料koushinryou Spice Used in cooking — something you taste, shares 香 but is a food ingredient. "This dish uses a lot of spices."
軟弱（な）nanjaku (na) Weak, feeble Negative — lacking strength or resolve. "A weak stance," "feeble-willed."
柔軟（な）juunan (na) Flexible, supple Positive/neutral — able to bend or adapt without breaking. "A flexible schedule," "supple muscles" — 柔軟体操.
溶けるtokeru To dissolve/melt (intransitive) Something dissolves by itself. "The ice melted."
溶かすtokasu To dissolve/melt (transitive) Someone actively dissolves something. "Dissolve the medicine in water" (an instruction).
一緒issho Together The real word — 緒 (14 strokes, ショ). "Let's go together."
一諸— (not a word) — 諸 ("various," as in 諸国) is a different, visually similar kanji — never combines with 一 this way. Never — a common distractor on reading tests.
Exam trap: 柔らかい and ❶軟らかい are true homophones (both やわらかい) written with different kanji — 柔 for flexible/supple things (judo, fabric, attitude) and 軟 for a substance's physical softness (ground, meat, water). The book flags 軟らかい with ❶ because test-takers often can't tell which kanji a given やわらかい sentence wants.
