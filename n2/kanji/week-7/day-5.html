QSentenceOptionsAnswer
①たくさんの（　）が空高く舞い上がる。Takusan no (___) ga sora takaku maiagaru.
Lots of [___] soar high into the sky.		a. 風船fuusen　b. 造船zousena — 風船fuusen (balloons)
②船便は遅いから（　）便でお願いします。Funabin wa osoi kara (___) bin de onegai shimasu.
Sea mail is slow, so please send it by [___] mail.		a. 航空koukuu　b. 空港kuukoua — 航空koukuu (air mail)
③（　）の気候は降水量が少ない。(___) no kikou wa kousuiryou ga sukunai.
A [___] climate has little rainfall.		a. 陸上rikujou　b. 大陸tairikub — 大陸tairiku (a continental climate)
④火事や事故などに備えて（　）保険に入る。Kaji ya jiko nado ni sonaete (___) hoken ni hairu.
To prepare for fires and accidents, I take out [___] insurance.		a. 損害songai　b. 公害kougaia — 損害songai (damage insurance)

Why: ① 風船 ("balloon") is the only thing that floats up into the sky here; 造船 is "shipbuilding," an unrelated activity. ② 航空便 ("air mail") is the fast alternative to slow 船便 ("sea mail"); 空港 is just "airport," a place, not a shipping method. ③ 大陸性気候 ("continental climate," dry with little rain) is the fixed term; 陸上 just means "on land" generically. ④ 損害保険 ("damage/casualty insurance") is the real product name for fire/accident coverage; 公害 means "pollution," an unrelated kind of harm.

Station notice board: delays & a suspended line

Realia caption, box 1: 混雑が予想されますので、あらかじめ帰りの切符をお買い求めください。(Konzatsu ga yosou saremasu node, arakajime kaeri no kippu o o-kaimotome kudasai. — Congestion is expected, so please purchase your return ticket in advance.)

練習 — 5日目 右 (p.117)

正しいほうに○をつけ、文の漢字にひらがなで読みを書きなさい。Circle the correct option and write the reading of the underlined kanji in hiragana. Answers confirmed from 別冊 p.13.

QSentenceOptionsAnswer
①現金（　）車が事故を起こした。Genkin (___) sha ga jiko o okoshita.
A cash-[___] vehicle got into an accident.		a. 郵送yuusou　b. 輸送yusoub — 輸送yusou (transport)
②昼休みで飲食店はどこも（　）している。Hiruyasumi de inshokuten wa doko mo (___) shite iru.
During lunch break, every restaurant is [___].		a. 混雑konzatsu　b. 混乱konrana — 混雑konzatsu (crowded)
③（　）と現実にはギャップがある。(___) to genjitsu ni wa gyappu ga aru.
There's a gap between [___] and reality.		a. 理想risou　b. 感想kansoua — 理想risou (ideal)
④人身事故でダイヤが（　）います。Jinshin jiko de daiya ga (___) imasu.
Due to a person-involved accident, the schedule is [___].		a. 乱れてmidarete　b. 混じってmajittea — 乱れてmidarete (in disarray)

Why: ① 現金輸送車 ("armored cash-transport vehicle") is the fixed term; 郵送 means "to mail/post something," not vehicle transport. ② 混雑 describes a place being crowded/congested; 混乱 is "chaos/disorder" — a stronger, more chaotic word than "restaurants are busy at lunch." ③ 理想と現実のギャップ ("gap between ideal and reality") is a set phrase; 感想 ("impressions") isn't something that has a "gap" with reality. ④ ダイヤが乱れる ("the schedule falls into disarray") is the fixed collocation for train delays; 混じる ("be mixed") doesn't apply to a timetable.

2 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Same-reading and shared-kanji traps from today's two scenes:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
損害songai Damage, loss (broad/legal) Broad term for harm/loss, often financial — used for insurance products. "Damage insurance (損害保険)."
損傷sonshou Damage, injury (to a specific object) Physical damage to a specific thing, like a door. "Flight cancelled due to door damage (ドア損傷)."
混雑konzatsu Congestion, crowding A place is crowded with people/traffic — orderly but busy. "The restaurant is crowded (混雑している)."
混乱konran Chaos, disorder A situation has broken down into disorder/confusion, not just crowded. "The evacuation caused chaos (混乱)."
理想risou An ideal A hoped-for perfect state, contrasted with reality. "Ideal vs. reality (理想と現実)."
感想kansou Impressions, thoughts A personal reaction after experiencing something. "Please share your thoughts (感想) on the book."
河kawa A river (kun-yomi, plain word) The everyday word for a river. General conversation about a river.
❶河川kasen A river (formal, on-yomi) The formal/written register used in official notices and reports. "River levels rising due to heavy rain (河川の増水)."
Reading trap: 欠航 (けっこう, "cancellation of a scheduled sailing/flight") is a pure homophone of 結構 (also けっこう, "splendid" / "no thanks") from Day 4 — completely unrelated meanings that only share sound. On a listening or reading section, use context (a transportation notice vs. a polite reply) to tell them apart, never spelling.
