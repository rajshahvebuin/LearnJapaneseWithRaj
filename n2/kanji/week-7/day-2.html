練習 (p.110) — choose the correct option

Q1

デパートで気に入った指輪を（a. 探す　b. 探る）。Depaato de ki ni itta yubiwa o (a. sagasu / b. saguru).

Full sentence: デパートで気に入った指輪を探す。Depaato de ki ni itta yubiwa o sagasu.
EN: I look for a ring I like at the department store.
HI: मैं डिपार्टमेंट स्टोर में अपनी पसंद की अंगूठी ढूँढता/ढूँढती हूँ।
GU: હું ડિપાર્ટમેન્ટ સ્ટોરમાં ગમતી વીંટી શોધું છું.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a探すsagasuto look for (a physical object)ढूँढनाશોધવું
b探るsaguruto probe/feel around for (figurative or by touch)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Why: 探す is the everyday verb for looking for something you want to find, like a ring in a shop; 探る is used for probing/groping for something (often figuratively, like feeling out someone's intentions, or feeling with your hands in the dark).

HI: आइए पूरे समुदाय के तौर पर बच्चों का पालन-पोषण करें।
GU: ચાલો સમગ્ર સમુદાય તરીકે બાળકોનો ઉછેર કરીએ.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a育ちsodachiintransitive stem "to grow" (wrong for an object like 子供たちを)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

HI: आइए सार्वजनिक स्थानों पर एक-दूसरे के लिए नियमों का पालन करें।
GU: ચાલો જાહેર સ્થળોએ એકબીજા માટે નિયમોનું પાલન કરીએ.

HI: पैदल यात्रियों को ज़ेब्रा क्रॉसिंग से सड़क पार करनी चाहिए।
GU: રાહદારીઓએ ઝેબ્રા ક્રોસિંગ પરથી રસ્તો ઓળંગવો જોઈએ.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati

OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a種類shuruitype, kindप्रकारપ્રકાર

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

This day is full of transitive/intransitive pairs and same-reading traps drawn straight from its own kanji list. Compare them:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
探すsagasu To look for Everyday verb for finding something you want (an object, a person, a job). "Looking for a lost cat," "job-hunting."
探るsaguru To probe, feel around for Same kanji 探, but for groping physically in the dark, or figuratively probing someone's intentions. "Feel around in your bag," "sound out the other side's intentions."
育つsodatsu To grow (intransitive) The subject itself grows — no direct object with を. "The children are growing up well."
育てるsodateru To raise, bring up (transitive) Someone raises something/someone else — takes a direct object with を. "The whole community should raise the children."
渡るwataru To cross over (intransitive) The subject itself moves across. "Pedestrians cross the crosswalk."
渡すwatasu To hand over (transitive) Someone gives an object to someone else. "Hand over the documents."
公演kouen A public performance A staged show — a play, concert, or dance. "The theater company's performance."
講演kouen A lecture, talk Same reading こうえん, but a spoken address, not a staged show. "A guest lecture at the university."
公園kouen A park A third same-reading こうえん word in this same cluster — a physical green space, unrelated to either performance or lecture. "Meet me at the park."
Exam trap: 公演・講演・公園 are all read こうえん/こうえん but mean three completely different things (performance / lecture / park) — this book's own 練習 Q8 is built entirely around telling 公演 and 講演 apart. Always check whether the sentence is about watching a show, listening to a talk, or naming a place before picking the kanji.
