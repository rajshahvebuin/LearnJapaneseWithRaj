JLPT N2 · Kanji · Week 6 — みる② (Miru ② — Look and See ②)

6日目 — どっち？ (Docchi? — Which One?)

Source
日本語総まとめ N2 漢字 (Nihongo Sou-Matome N2 Kanji) — printed p.102–103 (PDF index = printed page number for this scan)
Theme
13 kanji (#541–553), taught entirely through visual contrast pairs on real-life signage: low floor/high floor elevator buttons, mild/hot curry, cotton/wool labels, old/new museum wings (p.102); thick/thin cut meat, fine/thick pens, large/small-grain natto, vinyl/leather bags, an acidity/bitterness taste scale (p.103). This day is a built-in antonym/contrast drill — see §3 below.
Answer key
練習 (p.102) confirmed from [別冊] 解答・解説, 「6日目　左 (p.102)」, PDF index 171 (booklet's own p.11). 練習 (p.103) confirmed from 「6日目　右 (p.103)」, same booklet page.
Notes
"どっち？" literally means "which one?" — the whole day is built around pairs of near-opposite or easily-swapped words shown side by side on signs, tying directly into this week's closing コラム on look-alike kanji (see Day 7).
❶ = 特に注意してほしい読み — a reading requiring particular attention (usage caution)
⊙ = 特別な読み — an unusual/irregular reading not built from the kanji's listed on/kun readings
~ = this kanji attaches as a prefix/suffix to another word

2 Quiz / Exercise Section

練習 (p.102, 4 items) and 練習 (p.103, 4 items). Answers confirmed from the [別冊] answer booklet: 「6日目　左 (p.102)」and 「6日目　右 (p.103)」, both on PDF index 171 (booklet's own p.11).

練習 — p.102

Q1

バラを花束にすると一層（　）香りがする。Bara o hanataba ni suru to issō (___) kaori ga suru.

Full sentence: バラを花束にすると一層甘い香りがする。Bara o hanataba ni suru to issō amai kaori ga suru.
EN: When you make roses into a bouquet, they smell even sweeter.
HI: गुलाबों का गुलदस्ता बनाने पर उनकी ख़ुशबू और भी मीठी हो जाती है।
GU: ગુલાબનો ગુલદસ્તો બનાવવાથી તેની સુગંધ વધુ મીઠી બને છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a甘いamaisweet (a scent can be "sweet")मीठाમીઠું
b辛いkaraispicy hot — doesn't describe a flower's scent(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Why: 甘い香り ("a sweet scent") is a natural collocation for flowers; 辛い ("spicy hot") describes taste/heat, not fragrance.

Q2

肌が弱い人は、（　）のシャツを着るとよいでしょう。Hada ga yowai hito wa, (___) no shatsu o kiru to yoi deshō.

Full sentence: 肌が弱い人は、綿のシャツを着るとよいでしょう。Hada ga yowai hito wa, men no shatsu o kiru to yoi deshō.
EN: People with sensitive skin should wear cotton shirts.
HI: जिनकी त्वचा संवेदनशील है उन्हें सूती शर्ट पहननी चाहिए।
GU: જેમની ત્વચા સંવેદનશીલ હોય તેમણે સુતરાઉ શર્ટ પહેરવી જોઈએ.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a綿mencotton — gentle on sensitive skinकपास, सूतीસુતરાઉ
b毛kewool — can irritate sensitive skin (the label's contrast pair)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Why: 綿 (cotton) is the gentler, breathable fabric recommended for sensitive skin; 毛 (wool), the labeled contrast on the illustration's "毛100%" tag, can feel scratchy.

Q3

当店のスパゲティは（　）が2人前の量です。Tōten no supagetii wa (___) ga ni-nin-mae no ryō desu.

Full sentence: 当店のスパゲティは一皿が2人前の量です。Tōten no supagetii wa hito-zara ga ni-nin-mae no ryō desu.
EN: At our restaurant, one plate of spaghetti is a two-person portion.
HI: हमारे यहाँ एक प्लेट स्पेगेटी दो लोगों के लिए काफ़ी होती है।
GU: અમારે ત્યાં એક પ્લેટ સ્પગેટી બે વ્યક્તિ માટે પૂરતી હોય છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a一皿hito-zaraone plate (the right counter for a serving of pasta)एक प्लेटએક પ્લેટ
b一山hito-yamaone pile/heap — not a real serving counter here(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Why: 一皿 ("one plate") is the natural counter for a restaurant serving; 一山 ("one pile/heap") isn't used to describe a portion size on a menu.

Q4

今は田中ですが、（　）は林です。Ima wa Tanaka desu ga, (___) wa Hayashi desu.

Full sentence: 今は田中ですが、旧姓は林です。Ima wa Tanaka desu ga, kyūsei wa Hayashi desu.
EN: I'm "Tanaka" now, but my maiden name is "Hayashi."
HI: अभी मेरा नाम तनाका है, पर विवाह-पूर्व उपनाम हायाशी था।
GU: હાલમાં હું તનાકા છું, પણ લગ્ન પહેલાંની અટક હાયાશી હતી.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a前名zenmeinot a standard word for this meaning(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
b旧姓kyūseia maiden name (one's surname before marriage)विवाह-पूर्व उपनामલગ્ન પહેલાંની અટક

Why: 旧姓 ("former/maiden surname") is the fixed term for a pre-marriage last name; 前名 isn't a standard Japanese word for this.

練習 — p.103

Q5

「みそ汁、味が（　）から、今度からもう少し濃くして。」"Misoshiru, aji ga (___) kara, kondo kara mō sukoshi koku shite."

Full sentence: 「みそ汁、味が薄いから、今度からもう少し濃くして。」"Misoshiru, aji ga usui kara, kondo kara mō sukoshi koku shite."
EN: "This miso soup is bland (weak-flavored) — make it a bit stronger next time."
HI: "मिसो सूप का स्वाद हल्का है, अगली बार थोड़ा गाढ़ा बनाना।"
GU: "મિસો સૂપનો સ્વાદ પાતળો છે, હવે પછી થોડો ઘટ્ટ બનાવજે."
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a薄いusuiweak-flavored, bland (opposite of 濃い)हल्का (स्वाद में)પાતળો (સ્વાદમાં)
b厚いatsuithick (physical thickness) — wrong sense for taste(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Why: 味が薄い ("the flavor is weak/bland") is the fixed phrase, contrasted with 濃い (strong-flavored); 厚い describes physical thickness (a book, a slice), not taste intensity.

Q6

「（　）でお口の中がスッキリ、さわやか！　○○キャンディー」"(___) de o-kuchi no naka ga sukkiri, sawayaka! ○○ Kyandii"

Full sentence: 「一粒でお口の中がスッキリ、さわやか！　○○キャンディー」"Hitotsubu de o-kuchi no naka ga sukkiri, sawayaka! ○○ Kyandii"
EN: "One little piece and your mouth feels clean and refreshed! ○○ Candy"
HI: "बस एक दाना और मुँह एकदम ताज़ा-तरोताज़ा! ○○ कैंडी"
GU: "ફક્ત એક દાણો ને મોં એકદમ તાજું! ○○ કેન્ડી"
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a一層issōeven more/still more — not a countable object(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
b一粒hitotsubuone grain/piece (of candy)एक दानाએક દાણો

Why: A candy ad naturally counts pieces with 一粒 ("one piece/grain"); 一層 is an adverb meaning "even more," which can't be the subject of a slogan like this.

Q7

「教育制度の改革に（　）に取り組もう！」"Kyōiku seido no kaikaku ni (___) ni torikumō!"

Full sentence: 「教育制度の改革に積極的に取り組もう！」"Kyōiku seido no kaikaku ni sekkyokuteki ni torikumō!"
EN: "Let's tackle education-system reform proactively!"
HI: "शिक्षा व्यवस्था के सुधार में सक्रियता से जुट जाएँ!"
GU: "શિક્ષણ પ્રણાલીના સુધારામાં સક્રિય રીતે જોડાઈએ!"
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a消極的shōkyokutekipassive, negative — the opposite of what a rallying call needs(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
b積極的sekkyokutekiactive, proactive, positiveसक्रिय, सकारात्मकસક્રિય, હકારાત્મક

Why: 積極的に取り組む ("to tackle proactively") fits a call to action; 消極的 ("passive/negative") is its direct opposite and would contradict the exclamation mark's energy.

Q8

フランス（　）は1978年に起きた。Furansu (___) wa sen-kyūhyaku-nanajū-hachi-nen ni okita.

Full sentence: フランス革命は1978年に起きた。Furansu kakumei wa sen-kyūhyaku-nanajū-hachi-nen ni okita.
EN: The French Revolution happened in 1978. (the textbook's own practice sentence gives this year — the real French Revolution began in 1789; reproduced as printed, not fact-checked by the book)
HI: फ़्रांसीसी क्रांति सन 1978 में हुई थी। (पाठ्यपुस्तक के वाक्य में यही वर्ष दिया गया है)
GU: ફ્રેન્ચ ક્રાંતિ 1978માં થઈ હતી. (પાઠ્યપુસ્તકના વાક્યમાં આ જ વર્ષ આપેલું છે)
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a革命kakumeia revolution (a historic political upheaval)क्रांतिક્રાંતિ
b改革kaikakua reform — gradual change, not a historic upheaval(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Why: フランス革命 ("the French Revolution") is the fixed historical name; 改革 ("a reform") describes an incremental policy change, not a named historical event.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes — the whole day, in pairs

「どっち？」("Which one?") is built entirely around contrast pairs shown side by side on real signage — these are the actual pairs illustrated on p.102–103, not invented ones:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
高層kōsō The upper floors Elevator serves floors near the top of a tall building. "High-floor elevator."
低層teisō The lower floors Elevator serves floors near the ground. "Low-floor elevator."
甘口amakuchi The mild/sweet side Less spice, more sweetness (curry, sake). "Mild curry."
辛口karakuchi The hot/dry side More spice or a "dry" (less sweet) taste. "Hot curry," "dry sake."
大皿ōzara A big plate Larger serving dish, often shared. "A big platter for the table."
小皿kozara A small plate Individual-sized dish, e.g. for sauces. "A small side plate."
旧館kyūkan The older building The original/older wing of a facility. "Check in at the old wing."
新館shinkan The newer building The newly added wing of a facility (not taught this day, but shown on the sign as 旧館's counterpart). "The new wing has the pool."
厚切りatsugiri A thick slice Cut wide/deep — used for meat, bread. "Thick-cut bacon."
薄切りusugiri A thin slice Cut fine/shallow. "Thinly sliced ham."
積極的sekkyokuteki Active, proactive Eager to engage, take initiative. "Get involved proactively."
消極的shōkyokuteki Passive, negative Reluctant, holding back. "A passive/reluctant attitude."
Caution & special readings: ❶極, read ごく (not the on-yomi キョク seen in 北極/南極), means "very/extremely" as in 極上 (goku-jō, "top quality") — don't confuse it with the pole/extreme-point sense. Separately, ⊙木綿 is a true irregular reading — もめん, not もくめん as the individual kanji readings 木(モク/き) + 綿(メン) would suggest.
