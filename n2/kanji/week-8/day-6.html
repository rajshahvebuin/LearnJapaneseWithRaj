Q1

この電車は（a. 境界線　b. 環状線）なので乗り越してもまた戻ってきます。Kono densha wa (a. kyoukaisen / b. kanjousen) na node norikoshite mo mata modotte kimasu.

Full sentence: この電車は環状線なので乗り越してもまた戻ってきます。Kono densha wa kanjousen na node norikoshite mo mata modotte kimasu.
EN: Since this train runs on a loop line, even if you miss your stop, you'll come back around.
HI: यह ट्रेन एक चक्करदार लाइन पर चलती है, इसलिए स्टेशन छूट भी जाए तो वापस आ जाएगी।
GU: આ ટ્રેન વર્તુળાકાર માર્ગ પર ચાલે છે, એટલે સ્ટેશન છૂટી જાય તોપણ પાછી આવી જશે.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a境界線kyoukaisena boundary line (wrong sense — trains don't run on boundaries)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
b環状線kanjousena loop lineवृत्ताकार रेल लाइनવર્તુળાકાર રેલવે લાઇન

Why: 環状線 names a circular train line that loops back to its starting point (like Tokyo's Yamanote Line); 境界線 means a boundary/demarcation line, unrelated to train routes.

GU: સ્વયંસેવક પ્રવૃત્તિને કોઈ રાષ્ટ્રીય સીમા નથી.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati

b環境kankyouenvironment (wrong word entirely)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Why: 国境はない is a set idiom meaning "knows no national borders" (used for ideals like charity or volunteering); 環境 means "environment," an unrelated word that just happens to share the kanji 境.

Q3

GU: આ આ શહેરની વસ્તીના વધારા-ઘટાડાને દર્શાવતો ગ્રાફ છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a削減sakugena cut/reduction (one-directional, wrong for a graph showing both ways)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Why: 増減 covers both rises and falls, matching a population graph that goes up and down over time; 削減 only means a deliberate cut/reduction, a one-way and usually intentional action (like a budget cut), not a natural population trend.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Several pairs today share a kanji but point to very different institutions or meanings — exactly what today's 練習 tests:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
増減zougen Increase and decrease Covers movement in both directions — a net change over time. "A graph of population increase/decrease" (人口の増減).
削減sakugen A cut, reduction Shares 減 but is one-directional and usually deliberate. "Budget cuts" (予算の削減), intentional downsizing.
加減kagen Adjustment, degree/extent Also shares 減 but describes fine-tuning or a state's degree, not a simple increase/decrease. "How's the bath water temperature?" (湯かげん), adjusting seasoning.
独自dokuji Original, unique to Describes a distinctive trait, approach, or method belonging to a group. "Japan's own unique approach" (日本独自の方法).
独身dokushin Single, unmarried Same kanji 独, but only ever refers to marital status. "He is still single" (彼はまだ独身だ).
国境kokkyou National border The larger-scale border, read with rendaku (こっきょう). Crossing into another country, international affairs.
県境kenzakai Prefectural border Same 境, smaller administrative scale, and note the reading shift to ざかい. Domestic travel between prefectures.
政治seiji Politics The general activity/field of governance and public affairs. "He's interested in politics" (政治に関心がある).
政府seifu Government (the institution) Shares 政 but names the executive body itself, not the activity. "The government announced..." (政府が発表した).
Institution trap, set up for Day 7: This week's articles use three different titles for "the person in charge": 大臣 (daijin, a cabinet minister — heads a 省 ministry like 環境省), 長官 (choukan, a director-general — heads a 庁 agency like 気象庁 or 社会保険庁), and 総理大臣 (souri daijin, the Prime Minister — heads the government/政府 itself). Keep 省↔大臣 and 庁↔長官 paired in your mind — Day 7's 問題4 passage tests exactly this distinction.
