The sample casual email reads: 「浅野君　久しぶり。元気？　僕は相変わらず忙しいけど元気！　お互いに頑張ろう！　じゃあ、また！　ダン　追伸　皆さんにもよろしく。」 — note the casual 〜君 address, the plain-style です／ます-less sentences, and 追伸 (P.S.) for an afterthought.

2 Quiz / Exercise Section

Each half-page has its own 練習 (4 items). Answers confirmed from the [別冊] 解答・解説 booklet p.6.

練習 — p.64 (3日目 左)

Q1

久しぶりに会った友達はひげを（a. 伸びて　b. 伸ばして）いた。Hisashiburi ni atta tomodachi wa hige o (a. nobite / b. nobashite) ita.

EN: The friend I met after a long time had grown out his beard.
HI: बहुत दिनों बाद मिले दोस्त ने अपनी दाढ़ी बढ़ा रखी थी।
GU: ઘણા સમય પછી મળેલા મિત્રએ પોતાની દાઢી વધારી હતી.
OptionJapaneseEnglish
a伸びてnobitegrown (intransitive — wrong grammatical fit here)
b伸ばしてnobashitegrown out, let grow (transitive — deliberately)

Why: ひげを伸ばす treats the beard as an object the person deliberately grows out (transitive, 〜を伸ばす); 伸びる is intransitive ("[the beard] grows") and wouldn't take the object marker を this way.

Q2

この道路は（a. 追い越し　b. 引っ越し）禁止です。Kono douro wa (a. oikoshi / b. hikkoshi) kinshi desu.

EN: Overtaking is prohibited on this road.
HI: इस सड़क पर ओवरटेक करना मना है।
GU: આ રસ્તા પર ઓવરટેક કરવાની મનાઈ છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglish
a追い越しoikoshiovertaking
b引っ越しhikkoshimoving house (unrelated word)

Why: 追い越し禁止 is a standard road-sign phrase ("no overtaking"); 引っ越し means moving to a new home, unrelated to traffic.

Q3

この川は（a. 浅い　b. 細い）ので、子どもたちが遊ぶのにちょうどいい。Kono kawa wa (a. asai / b. hosoi) node, kodomotachi ga asobu no ni choudo ii.

EN: This river is shallow, so it's just right for children to play in.
HI: यह नदी उथली है, इसलिए बच्चों के खेलने के लिए बिल्कुल सही है।
GU: આ નદી છીછરી છે, તેથી બાળકોને રમવા માટે એકદમ યોગ્ય છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglish
a浅いasaishallow
b細いhosoinarrow/thin (wrong dimension)

Why: Safety for children playing in a river depends on depth, so 浅い ("shallow") is right; 細い describes width/thinness, not depth.

Q4

P.S.というのは（a. 追伸　b. 返信）のことです。P.S. to iu no wa (a. tsuishin / b. henshin) no koto desu.

EN: "P.S." refers to a postscript.
HI: "पी.एस." का मतलब है एक अनुलेख (जोड़ा गया अंश)।
GU: "P.S." એટલે પત્રના અંતે ઉમેરેલો ટૂંકો સંદેશ.
OptionJapaneseEnglish
a追伸tsuishina postscript
b返信henshina reply (wrong meaning)

Why: 追伸 is the exact Japanese equivalent of "P.S." (a note added after the main letter); 返信 means "a reply" to a message, a different concept entirely.

練習 — p.65 (3日目 右)

Q5

年の（a. 慣れ　b. 暮れ）にお世話になった人にお礼をする習慣がある。Toshi no (a. nare / b. kure) ni osewa ni natta hito ni orei o suru shuukan ga aru.

EN: There is a custom of thanking people who helped you at the end of the year.
HI: साल के अंत में उन लोगों को धन्यवाद देने की प्रथा है जिन्होंने आपकी मदद की।
GU: વર્ષના અંતે જે લોકોએ મદદ કરી હોય તેમનો આભાર માનવાનો રિવાજ છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglish
a慣れnaregetting used to (wrong word entirely)
b暮れkurethe end of the year

Why: 年の暮れ ("the end of the year") is the set phrase for this year-end thank-you custom (like お歳暮); 慣れ is an unrelated word meaning "getting accustomed to something."

Q6

（a. 健康　b. 生活）保険証を持ってきてください。(a. Kenkou / b. Seikatsu) hokenshou o motte kite kudasai.

EN: Please bring your health insurance card.
HI: कृपया अपना स्वास्थ्य बीमा कार्ड लेकर आएं।
GU: કૃપા કરી તમારું આરોગ્ય વીમા કાર્ડ લઈ આવો.
OptionJapaneseEnglish
a健康kenkouhealth
b生活seikatsulife/livelihood (not a real compound with 保険証)

Why: 健康保険証 ("health insurance card") is the one fixed term for this document; 生活保険証 is not a real word.

Q7

私はいつも食事の前に（a. お祈り　b. お祝い）をします。Watashi wa itsumo shokuji no mae ni (a. oinori / b. oiwai) o shimasu.

EN: I always say grace before meals.
HI: मैं हमेशा खाना खाने से पहले प्रार्थना करता/करती हूँ।
GU: હું હંમેશા ભોજન પહેલાં પ્રાર્થના કરું છું.
OptionJapaneseEnglish
aお祈りoinoria prayer, grace
bお祝いoiwaia congratulation (wrong occasion)

Why: Saying grace before a meal is お祈りをする ("to pray"); お祝い is for celebrating an event, not a mealtime ritual.

Q8

（a. 活発な　b. 健在な）議論をする。(a. Kappatsu na / b. Kenzai na) giron o suru.

EN: To have a lively discussion.
HI: एक जीवंत चर्चा करना।
GU: એક જીવંત ચર્ચા કરવી.
OptionJapaneseEnglish
a活発なkappatsu nalively, active
b健在なkenzai naalive and well (describes a person's state, not a discussion's energy)

Why: A lively, energetic discussion is 活発な議論; 健在 describes a person (or institution) still being alive/well-functioning, not the energy of a conversation.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

This day's kanji cluster around transitive/intransitive verb pairs and the 健 family of "healthy" words — both are classic N2 exam traps:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
伸びるnobiru To grow, stretch (intransitive) Something grows/extends on its own — no direct object. "Hair grows." / "Sales have grown."
伸ばすnobasu To extend, grow out, stretch (transitive) Someone deliberately makes something grow/extend — takes を + object. "He grew out his beard." / "Extend the deadline."
追い越すoikosu To overtake, pass Moving past something/someone that was ahead. Overtaking a car; road-sign prohibitions.
追い付くoitsuku To catch up Closing the gap to reach something/someone, without necessarily passing it. "I finally caught up to him."
浅いasai Shallow (physical depth) Literal, physical lack of depth. A shallow river, shallow sleep, shallow knowledge.
浅ましいasamashii Shameful, sordid Same kanji 浅, entirely different figurative meaning — describes despicable behavior, not depth. Describing greedy or contemptible conduct.
健康kenkou Health (the general noun) The everyday, neutral word for physical health/wellness. 健康保険証, 健康診断, general health topics.
健在kenzai Alive and well, still going strong Emphasizes continued existence/vitality of a person or thing, often after time has passed. "My grandfather is still alive and well at 90."
健やかsukoyaka Sound, wholesome growth A gentler, often child-oriented word for healthy development. "Wishing for a child's healthy growth."
健全kenzen Sound, wholesome (structural/moral) Applies to systems, finances, or moral conduct, not physical bodies. 健全な財政 ("sound finances"), 健全な精神 ("a sound mind").
Register contrast: Compare this day's two model letters side by side — the friend email uses casual 〜君, plain-form sentences, and 追伸 for an afterthought, while the teacher letter uses full です／ます, the humble-form closing 祈り申し上げます, and no casual address at all. Matching register to recipient is exactly what N2 reading/writing questions test.
