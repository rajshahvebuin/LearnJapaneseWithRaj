ビジネスメール① 「打ち合わせの日程調整」 (Uchiawase no nittei chousei — scheduling a meeting)

p.66 — kanji #312–317

福山　様Fukuyama-sama
お世話になっております。メール拝見しました。O-sewa ni natte orimasu. Meeru haiken shimashita.
打ち合わせの件ですが、ご希望の日時はあいにく先約があります。Uchiawase no ken desu ga, go-kibou no nichiji wa ainiku sen'yaku ga arimasu.
下記の日時でしたらお伺いできますが、ご都合いかがでしょうか。Kaki no nichiji deshitara o-ukagai dekimasu ga, go-tsugou ikaga deshou ka.
お返事いただければ幸いです。O-henji itadakereba saiwai desu.
取り急ぎ用件のみにて失礼いたします。Toriisogi youken nomi nite shitsurei itashimasu.

("Dear Fukuyama-san — Thank you for your email. I saw your message. Regarding the meeting, unfortunately I already have a prior engagement at your preferred time. I could meet at the date/time below — would that work for you? I'd be grateful for a reply. Please excuse this brief message covering only the essentials. — Daniel Reed")

ビジネスメール② 「退職のご挨拶」 (Taishoku no go-aisatsu — retirement notice)

p.67 — kanji #318–325

いつもお世話になっております。Itsumo o-sewa ni natte orimasu.
突然ですが、この度、諸般の事情により退職することとなりました。Totsuzen desu ga, kono tabi, shohan no jijou ni yori taishoku suru koto to narimashita.
つきましては後任の者をご紹介させていただきたく、ご挨拶に参りたいと存じます。Tsukimashite wa kounin no mono o go-shoukai sasete itadakitaku, go-aisatsu ni mairitai to zonjimasu.

Q1

「ちょっと（a. 拝見　b. お伺い）しますが、郵便局はどちらでしょうか。」"Chotto (a. haiken / b. o-ukagai) shimasu ga, yuubinkyoku wa dochira deshou ka."

Full sentence: 「ちょっとお伺いしますが、郵便局はどちらでしょうか。」"Chotto o-ukagai shimasu ga, yuubinkyoku wa dochira deshou ka."
EN: "Excuse me for asking, but where is the post office?"
HI: "थोड़ा पूछना चाहता/चाहती हूँ, डाकघर कहाँ है?"
GU: "થોડું પૂછવા માંગુ છું, પોસ્ટ ઑફિસ ક્યાં છે?"
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a拝見haikento look/see (humble) — wrong action(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
bお伺いo-ukagaito ask/visit (humble)पूछना/मिलने जाना (विनम्र)પૂછવું/મળવા જવું (નમ્ર)

Why: お伺いする is the humble form used when asking someone a question or visiting them; 拝見する is the humble form for "to look at" something the other person shows you — the wrong action for asking directions.

Full sentence: 「君には失望したよ」と部長に言われてしまった。"Kimi ni wa shitsubou shita yo" to buchou ni iwarete shimatta.
EN: I was told by my manager, "You've disappointed me."
HI: मैनेजर ने मुझसे कहा, "तुमने मुझे निराश किया।"
GU: મેનેજરે મને કહ્યું, "તેં મને નિરાશ કર્યો."
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a失業shitsugyouunemployment — wrong meaning(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
b失望shitsuboudisappointmentनिराशाનિરાશા

Why: 失望する means "to be disappointed in someone/something"; 失業する means "to become unemployed" — unrelated to disappointing a person.

GU: આ વિસ્તારમાં પણ વિકાસ વધી રહ્યો છે, અને પ્રકૃતિ ધીમે ધીમે ગુમાવાઈ રહી છે.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Business-email Japanese leans heavily on humble forms and near-synonyms — compare these pairs from today's kanji:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
拝見するhaiken suru To look/see (humble) Humbles the act of looking at something the other person shows or sends you. "I saw your email" (メール拝見しました).
伺うukagau To visit / to ask (humble) Humbles the act of going somewhere or asking a question. "I will come visit," "may I ask..."
幸いsaiwai Fortunately / good fortune An adverb/noun describing a lucky circumstance. "I would be grateful if..." (お返事いただければ幸いです).
幸せshiawase Happy A na-adjective describing a person's emotional state. Wishing someone happiness directly: お幸せに.
自然shizen Nature A noun naming the natural environment itself. "Nature is being lost," "the beauty of nature."
天然tennen Natural, unprocessed Describes a specific thing as naturally occurring (vs. artificial). 天然水 (natural water), 天然ガス (natural gas).
退職taishoku Retirement from a job Leaving one's employment (any age/reason). An employee resigns or retires from a company.
退学taigaku Withdrawal from school Leaving a school/course before completing it. A student drops out or is expelled.
❶ reading traps: Several kanji today carry a kun-yomi reading distinct from the on-yomi shown in most compounds: 拝む(おがむ, "to worship") sits apart from the はい readings in 拝見・拝借; 失う(うしなう, "to lose") sits apart from the しつ readings in 失礼・失望・失業; 情け(なさけ, "compassion") sits apart from the ジョウ readings in 事情・表情・友情; 退く(しりぞく, "to withdraw") sits apart from the タイ readings in 引退・退学・退院. Exam questions often test whether you can tell which reading a sentence calls for.
