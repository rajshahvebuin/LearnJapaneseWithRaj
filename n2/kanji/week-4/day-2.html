Source

Theme
14 kanji across two reply-writing scenarios: an RSVP reply card for a wedding invitation (◆出欠), and a hand-written thank-you note (◆礼状)

2 Quiz / Exercise Section

Each half-page has its own 練習 (4 items). Answers confirmed from the [別冊] 解答・解説 booklet p.6.

練習 — p.62 (2日目 左)

Q1

ゴミの出し方は地方（a. 自治体　b. 自治会）によって違う。Gomi no dashikata wa chihou (a. jichitai / b. jichikai) ni yotte chigau.

EN: How you put out the trash differs depending on the local government.
HI: कचरा निकालने का तरीक़ा स्थानीय स्वशासी निकाय के अनुसार अलग-अलग होता है।
GU: કચરો કાઢવાની રીત સ્થાનિક સ્વશાસિત સંસ્થા પ્રમાણે અલગ અલગ હોય છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglish
a自治体jichitaia self-governing body (a municipality)
b自治会jichikaia neighborhood association (too small a scope)

Why: 地方自治体 ("local government/municipality") is the fixed term for the administrative body that sets trash rules; 自治会 is a smaller neighborhood resident association, not the rule-making body.

Q2

短所、つまり（a. 利点　b. 欠点）はだれにでもあるだろう。Tansho, tsumari (a. riten / b. ketten) wa dare ni demo aru darou.

EN: Shortcomings — that is, flaws — everyone probably has some.
HI: कमियाँ यानी दोष — शायद हर किसी में होते हैं।
GU: ખામીઓ, એટલે કે દોષો — કદાચ દરેકમાં હોય છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglish
a利点ritenan advantage (opposite meaning)
b欠点kettena shortcoming, flaw

Why: 短所 ("a shortcoming") is being restated with つまり ("in other words"), so the blank must mean the same thing: 欠点; 利点 ("an advantage") is the opposite.

Q3

（a. 未婚　b. 再婚）というのはもう一度結婚することです。(a. Mikon / b. Saikon) to iu no wa mou ichido kekkon suru koto desu.

EN: "Remarriage" means getting married once again.
HI: "पुनर्विवाह" का मतलब है दोबारा शादी करना।
GU: "પુનર્લગ્ન" એટલે ફરીથી લગ્ન કરવા.
OptionJapaneseEnglish
a未婚mikonunmarried (never having married at all — wrong meaning)
b再婚saikonremarriage

Why: "Marrying once again" describes 再婚 (re- + marriage); 未婚 means never having married at all — the opposite situation. Note: 再婚 itself uses 再 (not one of today's headwords), but it's built from 婚, this day's own kanji.

Q4

結婚式の（a. 委任状　b. 招待状）に返事を書く。Kekkonshiki no (a. ininjou / b. shoutaijou) ni henji o kaku.

EN: To write a reply to a wedding invitation.
HI: शादी के निमंत्रण पत्र का उत्तर लिखना।
GU: લગ્નના આમંત્રણ પત્રનો જવાબ લખવો.
OptionJapaneseEnglish
a委任状ininjoua power of attorney (wrong document)
b招待状shoutaijouan invitation letter

Why: A wedding sends out 招待状 ("invitation letters") that guests reply to; 委任状 is a legal delegation document used to skip a meeting, unrelated to weddings.

練習 — p.63 (2日目 右)

Q5

梅雨明けの暑い時に、日本では（a. 暑中見舞い　b. 年賀状）を出す。Tsuyuake no atsui toki ni, nihon de wa (a. shochuu mimai / b. nengajou) o dasu.

EN: When it gets hot after the rainy season ends, people in Japan send midsummer greeting cards.
HI: बरसात के मौसम के बाद जब गर्मी बढ़ती है, तो जापान में गर्मी की शुभकामना कार्ड भेजे जाते हैं।
GU: ચોમાસુ પૂરું થયા પછી ગરમીના સમયે, જાપાનમાં ઉનાળાની શુભેચ્છા કાર્ડ મોકલવામાં આવે છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglish
a暑中見舞いshochuu mimaia midsummer greeting card
b年賀状nengajoua New Year's card (wrong season)

Why: 暑中見舞い is sent specifically during the hot midsummer period after the rainy season; 年賀状 is the New Year's equivalent, sent in winter.

Q6

5月5日は「こどもの日」、（a. 祝日　b. 平日）です。Go-gatsu itsuka wa "Kodomo no Hi", (a. shukujitsu / b. heijitsu) desu.

EN: May 5th is "Children's Day," a national holiday.
HI: 5 मई "बाल दिवस" है, एक राष्ट्रीय अवकाश।
GU: 5 મે "બાળ દિવસ" છે, એક રાષ્ટ્રીય રજા.
OptionJapaneseEnglish
a祝日shukujitsua national holiday
b平日heijitsua weekday (opposite meaning)

Why: Children's Day is a designated 祝日 (national holiday); 平日 means an ordinary weekday, the opposite of a holiday.

Q7

明日は北海道へ（a. 出張　b. 主張）だ。Ashita wa Hokkaidou e (a. shucchou / b. shuchou) da.

EN: Tomorrow I have a business trip to Hokkaido.
HI: कल मुझे होक्काइदो के लिए व्यावसायिक यात्रा पर जाना है।
GU: આવતીકાલે મારે હોકાઇડો માટે વ્યવસાયિક પ્રવાસ પર જવાનું છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglish
a出張shucchoua business trip
b主張shuchouan assertion, claim (unrelated homophone-like word)

Why: Traveling to Hokkaido for work is 出張 ("business trip"); 主張 means making a claim or assertion — a near-homophone with a totally different meaning.

Q8

りんごを送ってもらったので（a. お祝い　b. お礼）の手紙を出す。Ringo o okutte moratta node (a. oiwai / b. orei) no tegami o dasu.

EN: Since someone sent me apples, I'm sending a thank-you letter.
HI: क्योंकि किसी ने मुझे सेब भेजे, इसलिए मैं धन्यवाद पत्र भेज रहा/रही हूँ।
GU: કોઈએ મને સફરજન મોકલ્યાં હોવાથી, હું આભારનો પત્ર મોકલી રહ્યો/રહી છું.
OptionJapaneseEnglish
aお祝いoiwaia congratulation (wrong occasion — no event being celebrated)
bお礼oreithanks

Why: Receiving a gift calls for a thank-you letter (お礼の手紙); お祝い is for congratulating an event or achievement, and there's no such occasion here.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Reply-writing vocabulary today splits into several near-homophone or same-kanji pairs that the 練習 above directly tests:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
未婚mikon Unmarried (never married) Describes someone who has not yet married at all. "She is still unmarried."
再婚saikon Remarriage Marrying again after a previous marriage ended. "He remarried last year."
自治体jichitai A self-governing body (municipality/local government) An official administrative entity that makes rules and policy. "Trash rules differ by local government."
自治会jichikai A neighborhood association A smaller voluntary resident group, not a governing authority. "The neighborhood association organizes the festival."
欠席kesseki Absence (from an event) Describes not attending something specific. RSVP-ing "I will not attend."
欠点ketten A shortcoming, flaw A personal or structural weakness — unrelated to attendance despite sharing 欠. "Everyone has their flaws."
出張shucchou A business trip Travel for work purposes. "I'm going on a business trip to Hokkaido."
主張shuchou An assertion, claim A stated opinion or position — sounds similar but shares no kanji with 出張. "He asserted his innocence."
お祝いoiwai A congratulation (gift/message) Marks a happy event or achievement (wedding, birth, promotion). Congratulating a wedding or new job.
お礼orei Thanks (gift/message) Expresses gratitude for a favor or gift received — no event being celebrated. Thanking someone for a gift or kindness.
Etiquette note: On an RSVP card, don't just circle 「ご出席」or「ご欠席」— the polite convention is to also cross out the honorific ご/御 in front of whichever one does NOT apply, with a double line (=), since that ご/御 was addressed to you by the host and shouldn't be left on your own reply.
