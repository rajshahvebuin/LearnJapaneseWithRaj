2 Quiz / Exercise Section

Each half-page has its own 練習 (4 items, "choose the correct option and mark ○, then write the reading"). Answers confirmed from the [別冊] 解答・解説 booklet p.6.

練習 — p.60 (1日目 左)

Q1

自分の（a. 望み　b. 頼み）通りの大学に入学できた。Jibun no (a. nozomi / b. tanomi) doori no daigaku ni nyuugaku dekita.

EN: I was able to enter the university I wished for.
HI: मैं अपनी इच्छा के अनुसार विश्वविद्यालय में दाख़िला ले पाया/पाई।
GU: હું મારી ઇચ્છા મુજબની યુનિવર્સિટીમાં પ્રવેશ મેળવી શક્યો/શકી.
OptionJapaneseEnglish
a望みnozomia wish, hope
b頼みtanomia request (wrong sense here)

Why: 「〜望み通り」("just as one wished") is the fixed phrase; 頼み is a request made of someone else, not a personal wish.

Q2

来日する友人を空港の（a. 出発　b. 到着）ロビーで待つ。Rainichi suru yuujin o kuukou no (a. shuppatsu / b. touchaku) robii de matsu.

EN: I wait for my friend arriving in Japan at the airport's arrival lobby.
HI: मैं जापान आ रहे अपने दोस्त का इंतज़ार हवाई अड्डे के आगमन लॉबी में करता/करती हूँ।
GU: હું જાપાન આવી રહેલા મિત્રની રાહ એરપોર્ટના આગમન લોબીમાં જોઉં છું.
OptionJapaneseEnglish
a出発shuppatsudeparture (wrong direction)
b到着touchakuarrival

Why: A friend flying in lands and arrives (到着), so you wait at the 到着ロビー; 出発ロビー is for people leaving.

Q3

商品の配送を（a. 依頼　b. 願い）する。Shouhin no haisou o (a. irai / b. negai) suru.

EN: To request delivery of the goods.
HI: सामान की डिलीवरी के लिए अनुरोध करना।
GU: માલસામાનની ડિલિવરી માટે વિનંતી કરવી.
OptionJapaneseEnglish
a依頼iraia request, commission
b願いnegaia wish (doesn't take する this way)

Why: 依頼する is the standard verb for formally requesting a service (like delivery); 願い is a noun for a wish and isn't used this way with 〜を.

Q4

市民が駅前に立ち（a. 署名　b. 照明）を集める活動を始めた。Shimin ga ekimae ni tachi (a. shomei / b. shoumei) o atsumeru katsudou o hajimeta.

EN: Citizens started an activity of standing in front of the station to collect signatures.
HI: नागरिकों ने स्टेशन के सामने खड़े होकर हस्ताक्षर इकट्ठा करने की गतिविधि शुरू की।
GU: નાગરિકોએ સ્ટેશન સામે ઊભા રહી સહીઓ એકઠી કરવાની પ્રવૃત્તિ શરૂ કરી.
OptionJapaneseEnglish
a署名shomeia signature
b照明shoumeilighting/illumination (unrelated homophone-like word)

Why: 署名を集める ("collecting signatures") is a set phrase for petition drives; 照明 means lighting fixtures/illumination — a completely different word that just sounds similar.

練習 — p.61 (1日目 右)

Q5

日本では結婚すると夫の（a. 性　b. 姓）になる女の人が多い。Nihon de wa kekkon suru to otto no (a. sei / b. sei) ni naru onna no hito ga ooi.

EN: In Japan, many women take their husband's family name when they marry.
HI: जापान में, शादी करने पर कई महिलाएँ अपने पति का उपनाम अपना लेती हैं।
GU: જાપાનમાં, લગ્ન કરતી વખતે ઘણી સ્ત્રીઓ પતિની અટક અપનાવે છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglish
a性seigender, nature (wrong kanji)
b姓seifamily name

Why: 夫の姓になる ("take the husband's family name") uses 姓 (surname); 性, though also read せい, means gender/nature/disposition.

Q6

朝夕の（a. 通勤　b. 出勤）ラッシュ時は電車が満員になる。Asayuu no (a. tsuukin / b. shukkin) rasshu-ji wa densha ga man'in ni naru.

EN: During the morning and evening commuting rush, the trains become packed.
HI: सुबह-शाम के आवागमन के भीड़ के समय ट्रेनें खचाखच भर जाती हैं।
GU: સવાર-સાંજની અવરજવરની ભીડના સમયે ટ્રેનો ખીચોખીચ ભરાઈ જાય છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglish
a通勤tsuukincommuting to work
b出勤shukkinattending work (an arrival event, not the traffic word)

Why: 通勤ラッシュ ("commuter rush") is the fixed collocation for the crowded travel period; 出勤 refers to the act of showing up at work, not the traffic itself.

Q7

お客様に迷子のお知らせを（a. 申します　b. 申し上げます）。Okyakusama ni maigo no oshirase o (a. moushimasu / b. moushiagemasu).

EN: We would like to make an announcement to our customers about a lost child.
HI: हम ग्राहकों को एक खोए हुए बच्चे के बारे में सूचना देना चाहेंगे। (अति विनम्र)
GU: અમે ગ્રાહકોને એક ખોવાયેલા બાળક વિશે જાણ કરવા માંગીએ છીએ. (અતિ નમ્ર)
OptionJapaneseEnglish
a申しますmoushimasusay (plain humble — too casual for a public announcement)
b申し上げますmoushiagemasusay (very humble — appropriate for addressing customers)

Why: Addressing お客様 (customers) in a public announcement calls for the very-humble 申し上げる; plain 申す, while still humble, is a register below what's expected here.

Q8

パスポートの（a. 申請　b. 再生）をする。Pasupooto no (a. shinsei / b. saisei) o suru.

EN: To apply for a passport.
HI: पासपोर्ट के लिए आवेदन करना।
GU: પાસપોર્ટ માટે અરજી કરવી.
OptionJapaneseEnglish
a申請shinseian application
b再生saiseiplayback/regeneration (unrelated word)

Why: パスポートの申請 ("passport application") is the standard collocation; 再生 means playback or regeneration, and has nothing to do with official documents.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Today's kanji cluster around requests, dependence, and formal writing — several same-sounding or same-kanji pairs are worth separating clearly:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
姓sei Family name Same reading せい as 性, completely different kanji/meaning. "His surname/family name is..."
性sei Gender, nature, disposition An abstract trait or category, not a name at all. 性別 (gender), 性質 (disposition), 可能性 (possibility).
頼むtanomu To request, ask a favor of someone An active request you make of another person. "I asked him to do it."
頼るtayoru To depend/rely upon A passive reliance on someone/something for support, not a specific ask. "I rely on my parents."
頼もしいtanomoshii Reliable, dependable A positive trait describing someone worth depending on. Praising someone's dependability.
頼りないtayorinai Unreliable, powerless The negative opposite — someone/something not worth depending on. Criticizing a lack of dependability.
申すmousu To say (humble) Standard humble register — lowers the speaker. Everyday polite self-introduction: 田中と申します。
申し上げるmoushiageru To say (very humble) A deeper humble register — also raises the listener; used toward customers/superiors. Formal announcements, addressing customers or superiors.
通勤tsuukin Commuting to work The ongoing act/routine of traveling to work. 通勤ラッシュ ("commuter rush"), 通勤時間 ("commute time").
出勤shukkin Attendance at work The event of arriving/showing up at the workplace, not the travel itself. "He came to work today" / attendance records.
Reading trap: 頼 alone carries four different readings on this single page — たの-む (request), たの-もしい (reliable), たよ-る (depend on), and たよ-りない (unreliable) — all marked ❶ by the book as readings worth extra attention. Context (is it a verb of asking, or a description of trustworthiness?) is the only way to tell them apart in a reading-comprehension question.
