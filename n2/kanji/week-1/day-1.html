2 Quiz / Exercise Section

Each printed page (左 p.12, 右 p.13) has its own 練習 exercise. Answers confirmed from the [別冊] 解答・解説 booklet, PDF index 161, sections「1日目 左 (p.12)」and「1日目 右 (p.13)」.

練習 — p.12 (１日目 左)

Q1

「関係者以外立ち入り禁止」＝関係者でない人は（a. 入らなければならない　b. 入ってはいけない）"Kankeisha igai tachiiri kinshi" = kankeisha de nai hito wa (a. hairanakereba naranai / b. haitte wa ikenai)

EN: "No entry except for authorized personnel" = people who are not authorized personnel (a. must enter / b. must not enter).
HI: "अधिकृत व्यक्तियों के अलावा प्रवेश वर्जित" = जो अधिकृत व्यक्ति नहीं हैं उन्हें (a. अंदर जाना ही होगा / b. अंदर नहीं जाना चाहिए)।
GU: "અધિકૃત વ્યક્તિઓ સિવાય પ્રવેશ મનાઈ" = જે અધિકૃત વ્યક્તિ નથી તેમણે (a. અંદર જવું જ પડશે / b. અંદર ન જવું જોઈએ).
OptionEnglish
amust enter
bmust not enter

Why: 立ち入り禁止 = "entry prohibited" — the notice forbids entry to anyone not 関係者 (authorized personnel).

Q2

「お静かに」＝（a. 静かにしてください　b. 静かにしてはいけません）"O-shizuka ni" = (a. shizuka ni shite kudasai / b. shizuka ni shite wa ikemasen)

EN: "Please be quiet" = (a. please be quiet / b. you must not be quiet).
HI: "कृपया शांत रहें" = (a. कृपया शांत रहें / b. शांत रहना मना है)।
GU: "કૃપા કરી શાંત રહો" = (a. કૃપા કરી શાંત રહો / b. શાંત રહેવાની મનાઈ છે).
OptionEnglish
aplease be quiet
byou must not be quiet

Why: お静かに is a polite imperative — a direct request to be quiet, not a prohibition against it.

Q3

「(a. 危うい　b. 危ない) ですから、下がってください。」"(a. Ayaui / b. Abunai) desu kara, sagatte kudasai."

EN: "It's dangerous, so please step back."
HI: "यह ख़तरनाक है, इसलिए कृपया पीछे हट जाइए।"
GU: "તે ખતરનાક છે, તેથી કૃપા કરી પાછળ ખસો."
OptionEnglish
a危うい (ayaui) — dangerous/narrow, a caution reading, less common in spoken warnings
b危ない (abunai) — dangerous, the everyday spoken word

Why: 危ない is the standard everyday word for "dangerous" used in spoken warnings; 危うい is the same kanji's more literary/cautionary reading, marked ❶ in this book as one to recognize but not the default choice here.

練習 — p.13 (１日目 右)

Vocabulary note from the booklet: ゴミgomi = rubbish, garbage (कचरा / કચરો).

Q4

「スピード落とせ」＝スピードを（a. 上げろ　b. おそくしろ）"Supiido otose" = supiido o (a. agero / b. osoku shiro)

EN: "Reduce speed" = (a. increase / b. slow down) your speed.
HI: "गति कम करो" = अपनी गति को (a. बढ़ाओ / b. धीमा करो)।
GU: "ઝડપ ઘટાડો" = તમારી ઝડપ (a. વધારો / b. ધીમી કરો).
OptionEnglish
aincrease
bslow down

Why: 落とす here means "to lower/reduce," so 「スピード落とせ」is a command to reduce speed.

Q5

「遊泳禁止」＝（a. ここで遊ぶべきだ　b. ここで泳いではいけない）"Yuuei kinshi" = (a. koko de asobu beki da / b. koko de oyoide wa ikenai)

EN: "No swimming" = (a. you should play here / b. you must not swim here).
HI: "तैरना वर्जित" = (a. यहाँ खेलना चाहिए / b. यहाँ तैरना मना है)।
GU: "તરવાની મનાઈ" = (a. અહીં રમવું જોઈએ / b. અહીં તરવાની મનાઈ છે).
OptionEnglish
ayou should play here
byou must not swim here

Why: 遊泳 specifically means "swimming," not general play — 遊泳禁止 forbids swimming, not all recreation.

Q6

「落石注意」＝（a. 石が落ちてくるかもしれない　b. 石を落としてはいけない）"Rakuseki chuui" = (a. ishi ga ochite kuru kamoshirenai / b. ishi o otoshite wa ikenai)

EN: "Beware of falling rocks" = (a. rocks might fall / b. you must not drop rocks).
HI: "गिरते पत्थरों से सावधान" = (a. पत्थर गिर सकते हैं / b. पत्थर नहीं गिराने चाहिए)।
GU: "પડતા પથ્થરોથી સાવધાન" = (a. પથ્થર પડી શકે છે / b. પથ્થર ન પાડવા જોઈએ).
OptionEnglish
arocks might fall
byou must not drop rocks

Why: 落石 is a noun meaning "falling rocks" (a natural hazard); 注意 is a warning to be alert to that hazard, not an instruction directed at the reader's own actions.

Q7

「駐車禁止」＝（a. 駐車場がない　b. 車を止めてはいけない）"Chuusha kinshi" = (a. chuushajou ga nai / b. kuruma o tomete wa ikenai)

EN: "No parking" = (a. there is no parking lot / b. you must not park your car).
HI: "पार्किंग वर्जित" = (a. यहाँ पार्किंग स्थल नहीं है / b. गाड़ी खड़ी नहीं करनी चाहिए)।
GU: "પાર્કિંગ મનાઈ" = (a. અહીં પાર્કિંગ સ્થળ નથી / b. ગાડી ઊભી ન રાખવી જોઈએ).
OptionEnglish
athere is no parking lot
byou must not park your car

Why: 駐車禁止 is a direct prohibition on the act of parking, not a statement about the absence of a parking lot.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

This book teaches kanji by scene, but several pairs taught today share the same kanji or the same core meaning with a different nuance — worth comparing directly:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
危ないabunai Dangerous The everyday spoken word for "dangerous." Warning someone about immediate danger, e.g. shouting a warning.
❶危ういayaui Dangerous / narrow(ly avoided) Same kanji, a more literary/cautionary reading — the book flags it as a reading to recognize, not the default spoken choice. Written warnings, or describing a narrow escape ("he narrowly avoided...").
危険（な）kiken (na) Danger A na-adjective/noun naming a hazardous state or situation. "This area is dangerous" (危険な地域), signage.
❶険しいkewashii Steep Same kanji 険, but an i-adjective describing a steep/harsh physical form (a slope, a face). "A steep mountain path" (険しい山道).
落ちるochiru To fall (intransitive) The subject itself falls — no external agent. "A stone falls" (石が落ちる).
落とすotosu To drop (transitive) An agent causes something else to fall. "Drop your speed" (スピードを落とす), "he dropped his wallet."
遊泳yuuei Swimming (formal/notice register) The word this book's signage uses — appears in official warnings like 遊泳禁止. Official notices, rules, regulations.
水泳suiei Swimming (general/sport register) The everyday word for the activity/sport of swimming. "I go swimming as exercise," swim class, swim team.
Reading trap: Both ❶-marked readings today — 危うい (ayaui) and 険しい (kewashii) — reuse kanji whose everyday readings (危ない abunai, 危険 kiken) students learn first. On a 漢字読み question, do not default to the common reading automatically: check whether the sentence needs the i-adjective form (危うい/険しい) or the everyday noun/adjective (危ない/危険).
