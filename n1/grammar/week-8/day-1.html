Notes
This is the opening day of the final week (第8週). Watch two register traps today: はおろか needs a following clause that describes a bad/negative situation (it cannot close on a positive statement), and ならいざ知らず is 硬 (formal/written) — it explicitly disclaims knowledge of one case before contrasting it with the real point being made. Don't confuse the topic-shifting はさておき with the "no matter what" はどう(で)あれ — both can follow N は, but they do very different jobs (see §3).

1 Grammar Points

【1】 冗談はさておき (joudan wa sateoki)

neutral register
ReadingN + はさておき
Meaning (EN)"setting N aside / never mind N for now" — used to shift away from a topic
Meaning (HI)N को फ़िलहाल छोड़कर / N की बात छोड़ो
Meaning (GU)N ને હમણાં બાજુ પર મૂકીને / N ની વાત છોડો
Connection (接続)N + はさておき (N + wa sateoki)
RegisterNeutral — common in both speech and writing
Typical useUsed to change the topic (話題を変えるときに使う) — parking one subject to move to another, often before getting down to business.
Word typeFormationExample
NounN + はさておき冗談はさておきjoudan wa sateoki
Noun (topic/story)N + はさておきこの話はさておきkono hanashi wa sateoki
Verb／い-adj／な-adj(added, not in book; not a natural fit — はさておき attaches to a noun being shelved, not a verb or descriptive state)—
JapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
冗談はさておき、この件についての解決方法を考えましょう。Joudan wa sateoki, kono ken ni tsuite no kaiketsu houhou o kangaemashou. Joking aside, let's think about how to solve this matter. मज़ाक़ छोड़ो, इस मामले को सुलझाने के तरीक़े के बारे में सोचते हैं। મજાક છોડો, આ બાબતને ઉકેલવાની રીત વિશે વિચારીએ.
この話はさておき、教科書の80ページを見てください。Kono hanashi wa sateoki, kyoukasho no hachijuu peeji o mite kudasai. Setting this story aside, please look at page 80 of the textbook. यह बात फ़िलहाल छोड़िए, कृपया किताब के 80वें पेज को देखिए। આ વાત હમણાં છોડો, કૃપા કરી પુસ્તકના 80મા પાના પર જુઓ.

さておき comes from さておく ("to set/place aside"), so N はさておき literally means "putting N aside." It's a clean way to pivot a conversation — 冗談はさておき ("joking aside") and この話はさておき ("setting this story aside") are both extremely common openers before getting to the real point. No value judgment is implied about N itself; it's purely a topic-management phrase.

【2】 結果はどうあれ (kekka wa dou are)

neutral register
ReadingN + はどう(で)あれ
Meaning (EN)"no matter what N is/turns out to be, regardless of N"
Meaning (HI)N चाहे जो भी हो / N चाहे जैसा भी हो
Meaning (GU)N ભલે ગમે તે હોય
Connection (接続)N + はどう(で)あれ (N + wa dou (de) are)
RegisterNeutral — common in both speech and writing
Typical useDeclares that whatever value N takes, the following statement holds unconditionally; the same あれ (the classical imperative-like form of ある) also powers a small family of fixed idioms: いつであれ (=いつでも), だれであれ (=だれでも), どこであれ (=どこでも), どんな〜であれ (=どんな〜でも).
Word typeFormationExample
NounN + はどう(で)あれ結果はどう(で)あれkekka wa dou (de) are
Noun (with 何)理由 + は何であれ理由は何であれriyuu wa nan de are
Fixed idiom family(added, not in book) いつ／だれ／どこ／どんな〜 + であれいつであれ＝いつでもitsu de are = itsu demo
JapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
結果はどう(で)あれ、悔いはない。Kekka wa dou (de) are, kui wa nai. I have no regrets regardless of the result. नतीजा जो भी हो, कोई पछतावा नहीं है। પરિણામ ગમે તે હોય, કોઈ પસ્તાવો નથી.
理由は何であれ、教室での携帯電話の使用は認めません。Riyuu wa nan de are, kyoushitsu de no keitai denwa no shiyou wa mitomemasen. We won't allow the use of cell phones in the classroom for any reason. वजह चाहे जो भी हो, कक्षा में मोबाइल फ़ोन के इस्तेमाल की इजाज़त नहीं है। કારણ ગમે તે હોય, વર્ગમાં મોબાઇલ ફોનના ઉપયોગની મંજૂરી નથી.

あれ here is the classical imperative-like form of ある, so N はどうあれ literally reads "whatever N may be" — a formal-flavored but still everyday way of saying "regardless of." Don't confuse this with Point 1's はさておき: どうあれ makes an unconditional claim about an uncertain value (the result, the reason), while さておき simply shelves a topic without any "no matter what" meaning.

【3】 敬語はおろか (keigo wa oroka)

neutral register
ReadingN1 + はおろか + N2 + (も／まで／すら)
Meaning (EN)"let alone N1, even N2 (is/isn't) too" — escalates from a lesser fact to a more extreme one, in a bad situation
Meaning (HI)N1 की तो बात ही छोड़िए, N2 तक भी (नहीं)
Meaning (GU)N1 ની તો વાત જ છોડો, N2 પણ (નથી)
Connection (接続)N1はおろかN2も／まで／すら (N1 wa oroka N2 mo/made/sura)
RegisterNeutral, but the sentence that follows must describe an unfavorable situation (よくない状況を表す文が続く)
Typical useStates that not only is N1 true/lacking, an even more extreme N2 is too — always used to intensify a negative or troubling fact.
Word typeFormationExample
NounN1 + はおろか + N2 + も敬語はおろか日常会話もできないkeigo wa oroka nichijou kaiwa mo dekinai
NounN1 + はおろか + N2 + まで自分の会社はおろか住んでいる家まで失ったjibun no kaisha wa oroka sunde iru ie made ushinatta
Verb (nominalized)Vの + はおろか + Vの + すら歩くのはおろか立つことすらできないaruku no wa oroka tatsu koto sura dekinai
JapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
彼は、敬語はおろか日常会話もできない。Kare wa, keigo wa oroka nichijou kaiwa mo dekinai. He can't carry on a daily conversation, let alone properly use honorific expressions. वह रोज़मर्रा की बातचीत भी नहीं कर सकता, आदरसूचक भाषा की तो बात ही छोड़िए। તે રોજિંદી વાતચીત પણ કરી શકતો નથી, આદરસૂચક ભાષાની તો વાત જ છોડો.
彼は自分の会社はおろか住んでいる家まで失ってしまった。Kare wa jibun no kaisha wa oroka sunde iru ie made ushinatte shimatta. Not only did he lose his own company, he even lost the home he was living in. उसने अपनी कंपनी तो खोई ही, यहाँ तक कि जिस घर में रह रहा था वह भी खो दिया। તેણે પોતાની કંપની તો ગુમાવી જ, ત્યાં સુધી કે જે ઘરમાં રહેતો હતો તે પણ ગુમાવ્યું.
ケガをして、歩くのはおろか立つことすらできない。Kega o shite, aruku no wa oroka tatsu koto sura dekinai. Because of the injury, I can't even stand up, let alone walk. चोट लगने की वजह से, चलना तो दूर, खड़ा होना भी नहीं हो पाता। ઈજાના કારણે, ચાલવું તો દૂર, ઊભા રહેવું પણ શક્ય નથી.

はおろか always sets up a "not even the lesser thing, let alone anything more" logic, and the clause that follows must be negative or otherwise unfavorable — you can't use it to close on a happy statement. It behaves much like Point 4 of Week 7 Day 6's ぐらい／すら family in spirit (an extreme, almost surprising minimum), but here the emphasis comes from contrasting two nouns (N1 vs N2) rather than a single extreme case.

【4】 10年前ならいざ知らず (juu-nen mae nara iza shirazu)

硬 formal/written
ReadingN + なら／は + いざ知らず
Meaning (EN)"I don't know about N, but ~ / that may be true for N, but the real point is..."
Meaning (HI)N के बारे में तो पता नहीं, लेकिन...
Meaning (GU)N વિશે તો ખબર નથી, પણ...
Connection (接続)N + なら／は + いざ知らず (N + nara/wa + iza shirazu)
Register硬 — formal, appears in both careful speech and writing, with a literary flavor
Typical useExplicitly sets aside a different case (a past time, another place, a hypothetical) as unknown/irrelevant, then makes a firm statement about the case that actually matters now.
Word typeFormationExample
Noun (with なら)N + ならいざ知らず10年前ならいざ知らずjuu-nen mae nara iza shirazu
Noun (with は)N + はいざ知らず外国はいざ知らずgaikoku wa iza shirazu
Verb／い-adj／な-adj(added, not in book; not a natural fit — いざ知らず attaches to the noun/case being set aside, not to a verb or descriptive predicate directly)—
JapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
10年前ならいざ知らず、今時、携帯電話を持っていない人は珍しい。Juu-nen mae nara iza shirazu, imadoki, keitai denwa o motte inai hito wa mezurashii. I don't know about 10 years ago, but nowadays it's rare to find a person without a cell phone. 10 साल पहले की बात तो पता नहीं, लेकिन आजकल मोबाइल फ़ोन न रखने वाला व्यक्ति मिलना दुर्लभ है। 10 વર્ષ પહેલાંની વાત તો ખબર નથી, પણ આજકાલ મોબાઇલ ફોન ન ધરાવતી વ્યક્તિ મળવી દુર્લભ છે.
外国はいざ知らず、それは我が国では法律で禁止されている。Gaikoku wa iza shirazu, sore wa wagakuni de wa houritsu de kinshi sarete iru. I don't know about other countries, but it is prohibited by law in our country. विदेशों की तो जानकारी नहीं, लेकिन यह हमारे देश में क़ानूनन प्रतिबंधित है। વિદેશોની તો જાણકારી નથી, પણ આ અમારા દેશમાં કાયદેસર પ્રતિબંધિત છે.

いざ知らず literally means "I would not know" (a classical negative of 知る), so N ならいざ知らず reads "I wouldn't know about N, but..." — it politely disclaims a different case before pivoting to the real, current point. It's a close cousin of Point 2's どうあれ in that both dismiss a variable before making a firm claim, but いざ知らず names one specific alternative case to set aside (often the past, or somewhere else), while どうあれ covers every possible value unconditionally. See §3 for the direct contrast.

Q7

難しい問題は＿＿＿ ＿＿＿ ＿＿＿ ＿＿＿ ものから手をつけよう。Muzukashii mondai wa ___ mono kara te o tsukeyou.

Tiles: 1 解決 (kaiketsu)　2 すぐに (sugu ni)　3 さておき (sateoki)　4 できそうな (dekisou na)

3 さておき→2 すぐに→1 解決→4 できそうな

難しい問題はさておき、すぐに解決できそうなものから手をつけよう。Muzukashii mondai wa sateoki, sugu ni kaiketsu dekisou na mono kara te o tsukeyou.

EN: Let's set aside the difficult problems and start with the ones that seem like they can be solved quickly.
HI: मुश्किल समस्याओं को फ़िलहाल छोड़कर, जो जल्दी हल हो सकती लगें उनसे शुरू करते हैं।
GU: મુશ્કેલ સમસ્યાઓને હમણાં છોડીને, જે ઝડપથી ઉકેલાય તેવી લાગે તેનાથી શરૂઆત કરીએ.

Why: Built on Point 1's Nはさておき — shelving the hard problems for now, then picking the quickly-solvable ones first.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

All four of today's points can trail directly after N は, which makes them easy to mix up at a glance. Compare them side by side:

FormMeaningConnectionKey differenceUse when
NはさておきN wa sateoki Setting N aside (changing topic) N + はさておき Pure topic-management — no claim about "regardless of value" or comparing severity, just moving the conversation on. You want to shelve a subject to get to the real point (冗談はさておき).
Nはどう(で)あれN wa dou (de) are No matter what N is/turns out to be N + はどう(で)あれ Makes an unconditional claim that holds for every possible value of N — not about postponing a topic. You want to say an outcome doesn't change your stance (結果はどうあれ).
N1はおろかN2も／まで／すらN1 wa oroka N2 mo/made/sura Let alone N1, even N2 (in a bad situation) N1はおろかN2も Requires two things being compared and a negative/unfavorable clause — escalates from a lesser fact to a more extreme one. You want to emphasize just how bad something is by piling on a more extreme fact (敬語はおろか日常会話も).
Nならいざ知らずN nara iza shirazu (硬) I don't know about N, but... (unlike the real case) N + なら／は + いざ知らず Names one specific alternative case and disclaims knowledge of it, then pivots to a firm claim about the actual, current case. You want to distance a past/hypothetical case before making a point about the present (10年前ならいざ知らず).
Exam trap: はさておき (postpone a topic) and どうあれ ("no matter what") both look like plain "regardless of" phrases but answer different questions — さておき answers "what are we not talking about right now?" while どうあれ answers "what value could this variable take, and does it matter?" Separately, はおろか absolutely requires a negative/troubling follow-up clause (Q1 and Q4 above) — it cannot be used to praise something. Finally, いざ知らず is 硬 and always names a specific alternative case being set aside (the past, another country), unlike どうあれ's totally open "any value at all."
