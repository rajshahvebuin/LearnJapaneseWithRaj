Notes
Three of today's four points (禁じ得ない, 余儀なくされる, 嫌いがある) are marked 硬 (formal/written) in the book — all common in news reports and formal writing about unfortunate or forced situations. 落第する始末だ is the odd one out, register-neutral but always closing on a bad outcome. Watch the shape of each: を禁じ得ない and を余儀なくされる both take an object marked with を, but 禁じ得ない is the speaker's own uncontrollable feeling while 余儀なくされる is an externally forced action.

1 Grammar Points

【1】 同情を禁じ得ない (doujou o kinjienai)

硬 formal/written
ReadingN + を禁じ得ない
Meaning (EN)"cannot help but feel N / cannot suppress N" — an involuntary emotional reaction
Meaning (HI)N को रोक नहीं पाना / बिना N महसूस किए नहीं रह पाना
Meaning (GU)N ને રોકી ન શકવું / N અનુભવ્યા વગર રહી ન શકવું
Connection (接続)N + を禁じ得ない (N + o kinjienai)
Register硬 — formal, written, common in news reports and essays about others' hardships
Typical useAttaches to emotion-nouns like 同情 (sympathy), 涙 (tears), 笑い (laughter), 怒り (anger), 憤り (indignation) to say the feeling simply cannot be held back.
Word typeFormationExample
Noun (emotion)N + を禁じ得ない同情を禁じ得ないdoujou o kinjienai
Noun (emotion, past)N + を禁じ得なかった涙を禁じ得なかったnamida o kinjienakatta
Verb／い-adj／な-adj(added, not in book; not a natural fit — 禁じ得ない attaches to an emotion noun being suppressed, not a verb or descriptive state)—
JapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
彼の不幸な人生には、同情を禁じ得ない。Kare no fukou na jinsei ni wa, doujou o kinjienai. I can't help but feel sorry for his unfortunate life. उसकी दुर्भाग्यपूर्ण ज़िंदगी देखकर सहानुभूति महसूस किए बिना नहीं रहा जा सकता। તેની કમનસીબ જિંદગી જોઈને સહાનુભૂતિ અનુભવ્યા વગર રહી ન શકાય.
その葬式での家族の挨拶に、涙を禁じ得なかった。Sono soushiki de no kazoku no aisatsu ni, namida o kinjienakatta. I couldn't hold back tears at the family's address at that funeral. उस अंतिम संस्कार में परिवार के भाषण को सुनकर आँसू रोक नहीं पाया/पाई। તે અંતિમ સંસ્કારમાં પરિવારના ભાષણને સાંભળીને આંસુ રોકી ન શકાયા.

禁じる means "to forbid/suppress," and 得ない means "cannot manage to," so を禁じ得ない literally reads "cannot manage to suppress N" — the emotion wins out despite any attempt at composure. It's always an involuntary reaction to something moving or upsetting, and it appears almost exclusively in formal, written contexts (news articles, essays, speeches).

【2】 延期を余儀なくされた (enki o yoginakusareta)

硬 formal/written
ReadingN + を余儀なくされる
Meaning (EN)"be forced into N / have no choice but to undergo N" — an externally-imposed necessity
Meaning (HI)N करने पर मजबूर होना / N के अलावा कोई चारा न होना
Meaning (GU)N કરવા મજબૂર થવું / N સિવાય કોઈ છૂટકો ન હોવો
Connection (接続)N + を余儀なくされる (N + o yoginakusareru)
Register硬 — formal, written, common in news about disasters, cancellations, and forced circumstances
Typical useStates that outside circumstances (weather, disaster, illness) left no option but N — always passive in form (される), since the subject doesn't choose this outcome.
Word typeFormationExample
NounN + を余儀なくされる延期を余儀なくされたenki o yoginakusareta
Noun (phrase)N + を余儀なくされる避難所での生活を余儀なくされているhinanjo de no seikatsu o yoginakusarete iru
Verb／い-adj／な-adj(added, not in book; not a natural fit — 余儀なくされる attaches to a noun naming the forced outcome, not a verb or descriptive state)—
JapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
その試合は雨のため、延期を余儀なくされた。Sono shiai wa ame no tame, enki o yoginakusareta. Due to the rain, the game had to be postponed. बारिश की वजह से, वह मैच टालना ही पड़ा। વરસાદના કારણે, તે મેચ મુલતવી રાખવી જ પડી.
災害のため、多くの住民が避難所での生活を余儀なくされている。Saigai no tame, ooku no juumin ga hinanjo de no seikatsu o yoginakusarete iru. Because of the disaster, many residents have been forced to live in shelters. आपदा की वजह से, कई निवासियों को शरण-शिविरों में रहना पड़ रहा है। આપત્તિના કારણે, ઘણા રહેવાસીઓને આશ્રય-શિબિરોમાં રહેવું પડી રહ્યું છે.

余儀ない (よぎない) means "unavoidable," so N を余儀なくされる literally reads "is made to have no alternative to N" — a passive construction where circumstances, not the subject, force the outcome. Compare with Point 1: 禁じ得ない is the speaker's own emotion escaping their control, while 余儀なくされる is an external situation (weather, disaster) forcing an action on someone.

【3】 考えすぎる嫌いがある (kangaesugiru kirai ga aru)

硬 formal/written
ReadingV-る + 嫌いがある ／ N + の嫌いがある
Meaning (EN)"tends to ~ / has a tendency toward ~" — describing an unfavorable habitual tendency
Meaning (HI)...करने की प्रवृत्ति होना (नकारात्मक प्रवृत्ति)
Meaning (GU)...કરવાનું વલણ હોવું (નકારાત્મક વલણ)
Connection (接続)V-る + 嫌いがある (V-ru + kirai ga aru)；N + の嫌いがある (N + no kirai ga aru)
Register硬 — formal, written, common in evaluative/critical writing
Typical usePoints out an unfavorable tendency (よくない傾向を表す); most often appears as 「すぎる嫌いがある」— overdoing something — e.g. 長すぎる嫌いがある ("tends to be too long"), 〜に欠ける嫌いがある ("tends to lack ~").
Word typeFormationExample
Verb (dictionary form, often すぎる)V-る + 嫌いがある考えすぎる嫌いがあるkangaesugiru kirai ga aru
Verb (に欠ける)〜に欠ける + 嫌いがある協調性に欠ける嫌いがあるkyouchousei ni kakeru kirai ga aru
NounN + の嫌いがある(added, not in book) 頑固の嫌いがあるganko no kirai ga aru
JapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
彼は物事を少し考えすぎる嫌いがある。Kare wa monogoto o sukoshi kangaesugiru kirai ga aru. He tends to overthink things a little. वह चीज़ों के बारे में थोड़ा ज़्यादा ही सोचने की प्रवृत्ति रखता है। તે વસ્તુઓ વિશે થોડું વધારે પડતું વિચારવાનું વલણ ધરાવે છે.
この頃の若者は協調性に欠ける嫌いがある。Kono koro no wakamono wa kyouchousei ni kakeru kirai ga aru. Young people these days tend to lack cooperativeness. आजकल के युवाओं में सहयोग की भावना की कमी की प्रवृत्ति है। આજકાલના યુવાનોમાં સહકારની ભાવનાની ઉણપનું વલણ છે.

嫌い here is not the common word for "dislike" (きらい／嫌い) in its everyday sense, but a formal noun meaning "tendency" — always toward something negative or worth criticizing. It's most often seen following すぎる (overdoing something) or に欠ける (lacking something), rather than attaching directly to a plain neutral verb.

【4】 落第する始末だ (rakudai suru shimatsu da)

neutral register
ReadingV-る + 始末だ
Meaning (EN)"it has come to the point of ~ / it ended up in the sorry state of ~" — a bad final outcome after a chain of events
Meaning (HI)आख़िरकार ...जैसी बुरी स्थिति तक पहुँच जाना
Meaning (GU)છેવટે ...જેવી ખરાબ સ્થિતિ સુધી પહોંચી જવું
Connection (接続)V-る + 始末だ (V-ru + shimatsu da)；この／その／あの + 始末だ
RegisterNeutral, but always closes on a bad final state (最後によくない状況や結果になったことを表す)
Typical useSummarizes a bad outcome at the end of a chain of events, often with an exasperated or disappointed tone — frequently paired with どころか ("far from ~, it even...").
Word typeFormationExample
Verb (dictionary form)V-る + 始末だ落第する始末だrakudai suru shimatsu da
Verb (passive, past)V-られる + 始末だった止められる始末だったtomerareru shimatsu datta
Demonstrativeこの／その／あの + 始末だ(added, not in book) その始末だsono shimatsu da
JapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
息子には失望した。勉強しないどころか、落第する始末だ。Musuko ni wa shitsubou shita. Benkyou shinai dokoro ka, rakudai suru shimatsu da. I'm so disappointed with my son. Not only does he not study, he's ended up failing a course. बेटे से निराशा हुई। पढ़ाई न करना तो दूर, फ़ेल तक हो गया। દીકરાથી નિરાશા થઈ. ભણવાનું તો દૂર, નાપાસ પણ થઈ ગયો.
昔はお金がなくて、電気や水道も止められる始末だった。Mukashi wa okane ga nakute, denki ya suidou mo tomerareru shimatsu datta. In the past I had so little money that even the electricity and water ended up being cut off. पहले पैसे इतने कम थे कि बिजली और पानी तक कट जाते थे। પહેલાં પૈસા એટલા ઓછા હતા કે વીજળી અને પાણી પણ કપાઈ જતાં.

始末 (しまつ) means "the outcome/upshot of a matter," almost always a regrettable one here. 落第する始末だ reads like the last, worst link in a chain: things got progressively worse until they ended in failure. It often teams up with どころか (as in the first example) to contrast a lesser expectation with the actual, worse outcome.

Q6

田中さんは＿＿＿ ＿＿＿ ＿＿＿ ＿＿＿ あると指摘された。Tanaka-san wa ___ aru to shiteki sareta.

Tiles: 1 に欠ける (ni kakeru)　2 慎重すぎて (shinchou sugite)　3 積極性 (sekkyokusei)　4 きらいが (kirai ga)

EN: Tanaka-san was pointed out as tending to be overly cautious and lacking in initiative.
HI: तनाका जी को यह कहकर बताया गया कि वे ज़रूरत से ज़्यादा सतर्क रहते हैं और पहलक़दमी की कमी रखते हैं।

Q7

彼は、＿＿＿ ＿＿＿ ＿＿＿ ＿＿＿ のか疑問である。Kare wa, ___ no ka gimon de aru.

EN: He came in late, and on top of that it got to the point where he started saying he wanted to leave early — it makes me question whether he's motivated at all.
HI: वह देर से आया, और ऊपर से जल्दी निकलने की बात करने लगा — पता नहीं उसमें काम करने की इच्छा है भी या नहीं।
GU: તે મોડો આવ્યો, અને ઉપરથી વહેલા નીકળવાની વાત કરવા લાગ્યો — ખબર નથી તેનામાં કામ કરવાની ઇચ્છા છે કે નહીં.

Why: Built on Point 4's V る始末で — being late and then asking to leave early is presented as the final, exasperating low point that raises doubts about his motivation.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

All four points describe something going wrong or being unavoidable, but each does a distinct job. Compare them side by side:

FormMeaningConnectionKey differenceUse when
Nを禁じ得ないN o kinjienai (硬) Cannot suppress N (an emotion) N + を禁じ得ない The speaker's own uncontrollable internal feeling (sympathy, tears, anger) escapes despite trying to hold it in. Describing your own overwhelmed emotional reaction (同情を禁じ得ない).
Nを余儀なくされるN o yoginakusareru (硬) Be forced into N (an action) N + を余儀なくされる An external circumstance (weather, disaster) forces an action on the subject — passive, not an internal feeling. Reporting that outside forces left no alternative (延期を余儀なくされた).
V/Nの嫌いがある~kirai ga aru (硬) Tends to ~ (an ongoing negative habit) V-る／N-の + 嫌いがある Describes a recurring, characteristic tendency — not a single event, and not forced by outside circumstances. Critiquing someone's (or something's) habitual shortcoming (考えすぎる嫌いがある).
V る始末だ~shimatsu da It came to the point of ~ (a bad final outcome) V-る + 始末だ Marks the last, worst link in a specific chain of events — a one-time conclusion, not a general tendency. Summing up how a situation deteriorated to a regrettable end (落第する始末だ).
Exam trap: を禁じ得ない and を余儀なくされる both take を and both sound "forced," but one is about a feeling the speaker cannot suppress (internal), and the other is about an action circumstances force on someone (external) — see Q1 vs Q4. Separately, 嫌いがある (a recurring, general tendency) and 始末だ (one specific bad outcome at the end of a chain) are easy to conflate since both describe negative situations, but 嫌いがある never marks a single completed event the way 始末だ does — see Q2 vs Q3 above.
