Points 1 and 3 are each labeled "（一）" in the source, meaning this book plans a "（二）" continuation later — not included in this day's spread, flagged transparently. All four patterns list two examples to build a broader claim, but the tone ranges from neutral (といい) to complaining (だの) to critical (が〜なら).

1 Grammar Points

【1】 〜だの〜だの （一）

neutral–colloquial register
Reading~da no ~da no
Meaning (EN)"~ and ~ and so on" — lists examples, usually with a nagging or complaining tone
Meaning (HI)...है, ...है, और भी बहुत कुछ (अक्सर शिकायती लहज़े में)
Meaning (GU)...છે, ...છે, અને ઘણું બધું (ઘણી વાર ફરિયાદના સ્વરમાં)
Connection (接続)[aだのbだの] V／A（plain） + だの; na／naだった + だの; N／Nだった + だの　◆不満を言うときよく使う
RegisterNeutral, colloquial
Typical useRattling off a list of complaints or minor examples, implying there are even more unlisted.
JapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
僕の毎月の小遣いは、雑誌だのDVDだので消えていく。Boku no maitsuki no kozukai wa, zasshi da no DVD da no de kiete iku. My monthly allowance disappears quickly on magazines, DVDs and so on. मेरा महीने का ख़र्चा पत्रिकाओं, डीवीडी जैसी चीज़ों में उड़ जाता है। મારું મહિનાનું ખર્ચ મેગેઝિન, ડીવીડી જેવી ચીજોમાં ઉડી જાય છે.
彼は部屋が狭いだの、食事がまずいだのといつも文句を言っている。Kare wa heya ga semai da no, shokuji ga mazui da no to itsumo monku o itte iru. He is always complaining about how small his room is, how terrible the food is, or something else. वह हमेशा शिकायत करता है — कमरा छोटा है, खाना बेकार है, वगैरह-वगैरह। તે હંમેશા ફરિયાદ કરે છે — રૂમ નાનો છે, ખાવાનું ખરાબ છે, વગેરે વગેરે.
彼は、風邪を引いただの、頭が痛いだのと言って、よく授業を休む。Kare wa, kaze o hiita da no, atama ga itai da no to itte, yoku jugyou o yasumu. He often skips classes saying he has a cold, a headache, and so on. वह ज़ुकाम है या सिर दर्द है, कहकर अक्सर क्लास छोड़ देता है। તેને શરદી છે કે માથું દુખે છે, કહીને તે વારંવાર ક્લાસ છોડી દે છે.

Labeled "（一）" in the book — presumably a "（二）" installment appears in a later week, not covered on this page. だの always carries a slightly whiny or exasperated tone, which is why it fits so naturally after 文句を言っている ("complaining").

【2】 〜といい〜といい

neutral register
Reading~to ii ~to ii
Meaning (EN)"both ~ and ~ (taking any aspect, the same conclusion holds)" — praises or criticizes by pointing to multiple aspects that all confirm the same judgment
Meaning (HI)चाहे...देखें या...देखें, दोनों में एक ही बात लागू होती है
Meaning (GU)ભલે...જુઓ કે...જુઓ, બંનેમાં એક જ વાત લાગુ પડે છે
Connection (接続)N1といいN2といい
RegisterNeutral
Typical useBacking up an overall judgment (good or bad) by citing two supporting aspects.
JapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
デザインといい、色といい、すごく気に入った靴があったんだけれど、サイズがなかった。Dezain to ii, iro to ii, sugoku ki ni itta kutsu ga atta n dakeredo, saizu ga nakatta. I liked the design and colour of the shoes but they didn't have ones in my size. डिज़ाइन भी अच्छा, रंग भी अच्छा — जूता बहुत पसंद आया, पर मेरे साइज़ में नहीं था। ડિઝાઈન પણ સારી, રંગ પણ સારો — બુટ ખૂબ ગમ્યા, પણ મારા સાઈઝમાં નહોતા.
運動といい勉強といい、僕は何をやってもダメだ。Undou to ii benkyou to ii, boku wa nani o yatte mo dame da. I'm not good at sports or study, or anything. खेल भी नहीं, पढ़ाई भी नहीं — मैं जो भी करता हूँ, बेकार ही होता है। રમત પણ નહીં, ભણવાનું પણ નહીં — હું જે પણ કરું, નકામું જ થાય છે.

といい carries no built-in positive or negative charge by itself — the two examples given always share whatever tone the rest of the sentence sets (both good, as in Example 1, or both bad, as in Example 2).

【3】 N1が／N1もN1なら、N2もN2だ （一）

neutral–critical register
ReadingN1 ga/mo N1 nara, N2 mo N2 da
Meaning (EN)"if N1 is like that, then N2 is just as bad" — criticizes two related parties together, implying both share the same fault
Meaning (HI)अगर N1 वैसा है, तो N2 भी वैसा ही है (दोनों बराबर के दोषी)
Meaning (GU)જો N1 એવો છે, તો N2 પણ એવો જ છે (બંને સરખા દોષિત)
Connection (接続)N1が／N1も + N1なら、N2もN2だ
RegisterNeutral, but always carries a critical/disapproving tone
Typical useBlaming two associated people/things together (often "the kid and the parents," "the food and the service").
JapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
子どもが子どもなら、親も親だ。Kodomo ga kodomo nara, oya mo oya da. That kid is terrible and so are his parents. बच्चा भी वैसा ही है, और माँ-बाप भी वैसे ही हैं। બાળક પણ એવું જ છે, અને માતાપિતા પણ એવા જ છે.
あのレストランは、味も味なら、サービスもサービスだ。Ano resutoran wa, aji mo aji nara, saabisu mo saabisu da. That restaurant's food is terrible, and so is its service. उस रेस्तराँ का स्वाद भी बेकार, और सेवा भी बेकार। તે રેસ્ટોરન્ટનો સ્વાદ પણ ખરાબ, અને સેવા પણ ખરાબ.

Labeled "（一）" in the book, same as Point 1. The repetition of the exact same noun (子どもが子ども, 味も味) is what signals "this thing is bad in a way typical/expected of itself" — a distinctively Japanese rhetorical shrug that doesn't translate literally into English.

【4】 〜といわず〜といわず

neutral register
Reading~to iwazu ~to iwazu
Meaning (EN)"not just ~ or ~ — literally everywhere/everyone/every time" — emphasizes that something applies universally, without exception across the named categories
Meaning (HI)चाहे...हो या...हो — हर जगह/हर किसी पर (बिना किसी अपवाद के)
Meaning (GU)ભલે...હોય કે...હોય — બધે જ/બધા પર (કોઈ અપવાદ વગર)
Connection (接続)N1といわずN2といわず
RegisterNeutral
Typical useNaming two obvious categories to emphasize that something extends well beyond them, to everything/everyone.
JapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
日本人は、子どもといわず、大人といわず、マンガをよく読む。Nihonjin wa, kodomo to iwazu, otona to iwazu, manga o yoku yomu. Japanese kids and adults both love comics. जापानी लोग, बच्चे हों या बड़े, मंगा बहुत पढ़ते हैं। જાપાનીઓ, બાળકો હોય કે મોટા, મંગા ખૂબ વાંચે છે.
私は、牛肉といわず、豚肉といわず、肉は食べません。Watashi wa, gyuuniku to iwazu, butaniku to iwazu, niku wa tabemasen. I don't eat beef, pork, or any other meat. मैं बीफ़ हो या पोर्क, कोई भी मांस नहीं खाता/खाती। હું બીફ હોય કે પોર્ક, કોઈ પણ માંસ ખાતો/ખાતી નથી.
最近の若者は、食事中といわず、テレビを見ている間といわず、いつでも携帯電話を手にしている。Saikin no wakamono wa, shokujichuu to iwazu, terebi o mite iru aida to iwazu, itsudemo keitai denwa o te ni shite iru. Young people these days always keep their cell phones in their hands even when they are eating, watching TV, and doing other things. आजकल के युवा, खाना खाते वक़्त हो या टीवी देखते वक़्त, हर समय मोबाइल हाथ में रखते हैं। આજકાલના યુવાનો, ખાતી વખતે હોય કે ટીવી જોતી વખતે, દરેક સમયે મોબાઈલ હાથમાં રાખે છે.

といわず literally means "without saying/distinguishing" — the two named categories are explicitly the ones you might expect to matter, but the pattern insists the statement applies beyond them, to everything.

Q1

彼女はまだ若いけれど、（　）申し分のない女性だ。Kanojo wa mada wakai keredo, (___) moushibun no nai josei da.

Full sentence: 彼女はまだ若いけれど、経験といい能力といい申し分のない女性だ。Kanojo wa mada wakai keredo, keiken to ii nouryoku to ii moushibun no nai josei da.
EN: She's still young, but she's a flawless woman in terms of both experience and ability.
HI: वह अभी भी जवान है, लेकिन अनुभव और क्षमता दोनों में बेहतरीन महिला है।
GU: તે હજુ યુવાન છે, પણ અનુભવ અને ક્ષમતા બંનેમાં ઉત્તમ મહિલા છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a経験といい能力といいkeiken to ii nouryoku to iiboth experience and abilityअनुभव और क्षमता दोनोंઅનુભવ અને ક્ષમતા બંને
b経験だの能力だのkeiken da no nouryoku da noexperience, ability, whatever (wrong tone — this is a compliment, not a complaint)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

GU: બિલાડીએ ખંજવાળ્યું — ફક્ત ચહેરા પર જ નહીં, હાથ પર પણ (બધે જ).

練習Ⅱ — sentence ordering (並べ替え)

Q6

姉がデザインが＿＿＿ ＿＿＿ ＿＿＿ ＿＿＿ バッグをずっと使っている。Ane ga dezain ga ___ baggu o zutto tsukatte iru.

Tiles: 1 くれた (kureta)　2 色が気に入らないだの (iro ga ki ni iranai da no)　3 古いだの (furui da no)　4 といって (to itte)

3 古いだの→2 色が気に入らないだの→4 といって→1 くれた

姉がデザインが古いだの色が気に入らないだのといってくれたバッグをずっと使っている。Ane ga dezain ga furui da no iro ga ki ni iranai da no to itte kureta baggu o zutto tsukatte iru.

EN: I've been using, all this time, the bag that my older sister gave me while complaining that its design is old, its color isn't to her liking, and so on.
HI: बहन ने डिज़ाइन पुराना है, रंग पसंद नहीं है वगैरह कहते हुए जो बैग दिया, उसे मैं अभी तक इस्तेमाल कर रहा/रही हूँ।
GU: બહેને ડિઝાઈન જૂની છે, રંગ ગમતો નથી વગેરે કહીને જે બેગ આપી, તે હું હજુ પણ વાપરું છું.

Why: Built on Point 1's だのだの — the sister's list of complaints (design, color) modifies the bag she nevertheless gave away.

Q7

彼は学校の＿＿＿ ＿＿＿ ＿＿＿ ＿＿＿ から女の子に全然もてない。Kare wa gakkou no ___ kara onna no ko ni zenzen motenai.

Tiles: 1 しない (shinai)　2 成績といい (seiseki to ii)　3 パッと (patto)　4 容姿といい (yousi to ii)

2 成績といい→4 容姿といい→3 パッと→1 しない

彼は学校の成績といい容姿といいパッとしないから女の子に全然もてない。Kare wa gakkou no seiseki to ii yousi to ii patto shinai kara onna no ko ni zenzen motenai.

EN: Both his school grades and his looks are unremarkable, so he's not at all popular with girls.
HI: उसके स्कूल के अंक भी फीके, और शक्ल भी साधारण — इसलिए लड़कियों में उसकी कोई पूछ नहीं है।
GU: તેના શાળાના માર્ક્સ પણ ફિક્કા, અને દેખાવ પણ સામાન્ય — એટલે છોકરીઓમાં તેની કોઈ પૂછ નથી.

Why: Built on Point 2's といいといい — grades and looks are cited together to support the overall negative judgment (パッとしない, "unremarkable").

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

All four patterns list two examples to support a broader claim, but the tone and structure differ sharply:

FormMeaningConnectionKey differenceUse when
〜だの〜だの~da no ~da no ~ and ~ and so on V／A（plain）／na／N（+だった） + だの Complaining/nagging tone; lists grievances. Venting a string of complaints.
〜といい〜といい~to ii ~to ii Both ~ and ~ (same conclusion) N1といいN2といい Tone-neutral; two aspects both confirm whatever judgment follows. Supporting praise or criticism with two examples.
N1もN1なら、N2もN2だN1 mo N1 nara... If N1 is like that, N2 is just as bad N1が／N1もN1なら、N2もN2だ Always critical; blames two related parties by repeating the same noun. "The kid and the parents are both bad."
〜といわず〜といわず~to iwazu ~to iwazu Not just ~ or ~ — everywhere/always N1といわずN2といわず Emphasizes the statement extends beyond the two named categories to everything. "Not just X or Y — literally all of it."
Exam trap: といい and といわず both use two named nouns and look superficially similar, but といい just supports a single overall judgment (design and color, both liked), while といわず insists the claim goes beyond the two named items to everything (face and hands — scratched everywhere, not just those two spots). If the sentence implies "and more besides these two," it's といわず; if it's simply citing two supporting examples, it's といい.
