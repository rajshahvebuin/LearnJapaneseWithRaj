Notes
This day trains JLPT 問題 "文章の文法": a short essay with five numbered blanks, each with four options. The N2 set is labelled N2-level and the N1 set N1 as in the book. The book's passages are not reproduced here. For each passage you get a short passage summary written by us (not the book's text), and for each blank only the sentence (or clause) that contains it. Blanks test three things: connectives (しかし, 逆に, そうすれば, だが), sentence-final stance (ものだ, わけではない, とは限らない) and earlier grammar (にとって, にかかわらず, 一方で). Note: in N1 問題1 blank 3, options 1 and 3 are printed identically (困る) — reproduced as printed.

1 Grammar Points

【1】 How a 文章の文法 question works

from the book's format

Instruction (every 問題): 次の文章を読んで、［1］から［5］の中に入る最もよいものを、1・2・3・4から一つ選びなさい。 (Tsugi no bunshou o yonde, ichi kara go no naka ni hairu mottomo yoi mono o, ichi・ni・san・yon kara hitotsu erabinasai.) — Read the passage and choose the best option (1–4) for each of the blanks 1–5. (HI: लेख पढ़कर 1 से 5 तक की हर ख़ाली जगह के लिए 1–4 में से सबसे सही विकल्प चुनिए। GU: લેખ વાંચીને 1 થી 5 સુધીની દરેક ખાલી જગ્યા માટે 1–4 માંથી સૌથી યોગ્ય વિકલ્પ પસંદ કરો.)

Strategy (added, not in book): unlike a single-sentence item, the right option often depends on the sentence before or after the blank. Before choosing, ask: (1) does the blank start a sentence (→ a connective: contrast, result, condition)? (2) does it end one (→ a stance ending: ものだ, わけではない, とは限らない)? (3) whose point of view is it (→ にとって, voice: 受身／使役)? Then check that your choice keeps the writer's argument consistent.

N2レベル 問題1 — encouraging others vs encouraging yourself (p.136–137)

Passage summary (not the book's text):

EN: The writer notes that giving advice to others is easy while knowing yourself is hard, and cites a poem saying that encouraging others is easy but encouraging yourself is tough. A friend who had lost his job saw upbeat slogans on the wall of a job-counselling office and felt angry, because they were empty words from an uninvolved outsider. The writer concludes that encouragement rarely works as intended and can even backfire.

HI: लेखक कहता है कि दूसरों को सलाह देना आसान है पर ख़ुद को समझना मुश्किल, और एक कविता का हवाला देता है कि दूसरों का हौसला बढ़ाना आसान है, अपना मुश्किल। नौकरी खो चुके एक दोस्त को रोज़गार-सलाह केंद्र की दीवार पर लगे प्रेरक नारे देखकर ग़ुस्सा आया, क्योंकि वे किसी बाहरी व्यक्ति के खोखले शब्द लगे। निष्कर्ष: हौसला बढ़ाना सोचे मुताबिक़ कम ही काम करता है, उल्टा नुक़सान भी कर सकता है।

GU: લેખક કહે છે કે બીજાને સલાહ આપવી સહેલી છે પણ પોતાને ઓળખવું અઘરું, અને એક કવિતા ટાંકે છે કે બીજાને હિંમત આપવી સહેલી છે, પોતાને અઘરી. નોકરી ગુમાવનાર એક મિત્રને રોજગાર-સલાહ કેન્દ્રની દીવાલ પરનાં પ્રેરક સૂત્રો જોઈને ગુસ્સો આવ્યો, કારણ કે તે કોઈ બહારની વ્યક્તિના પોકળ શબ્દો લાગ્યા. નિષ્કર્ષ: હિંમત આપવી ધાર્યા પ્રમાણે ભાગ્યે જ કામ કરે છે, ઊલટું નુકસાન પણ કરી શકે.

N2レベル 問題2 — children who "snap" (キレる) (p.137–138)

Passage summary (not the book's text):

EN: The writer reports that more elementary-school children now "snap" (キレる) at the slightest provocation. An expert explains that problems should be settled by putting feelings into words, but today's children cannot express themselves well — and the same lack of expressive skill makes some adults explosive too. The writer suggests this skill normally grows through childhood conversation and play, time that games and phones have eaten into.

HI: लेखक बताता है कि छोटी-छोटी बातों पर "भड़क उठने" (キレる) वाले प्राइमरी स्कूल के बच्चे बढ़ रहे हैं। एक विशेषज्ञ के अनुसार मसले भावनाएँ शब्दों में कहकर सुलझने चाहिए, पर आज के बच्चे ख़ुद को ठीक से व्यक्त नहीं कर पाते — यही कमी कुछ बड़ों को भी ग़ुस्सैल बनाती है। लेखक का मत है कि यह क्षमता बचपन की बातचीत और खेल से आती है, जिसका समय गेम और मोबाइल ने छीन लिया है।

GU: લેખક જણાવે છે કે નાની નાની વાતમાં "ભડકી ઊઠતાં" (キレる) પ્રાથમિક શાળાનાં બાળકો વધી રહ્યાં છે. એક નિષ્ણાત મુજબ પ્રશ્નો લાગણીઓ શબ્દોમાં કહીને ઉકેલાવા જોઈએ, પણ આજનાં બાળકો પોતાને બરાબર વ્યક્ત કરી શકતાં નથી — આ જ ઊણપ કેટલાક મોટાઓને પણ ગુસ્સાવાળા બનાવે છે. લેખકના મતે આ ક્ષમતા બાળપણની વાતચીત અને રમતમાંથી આવે છે, જેનો સમય ગેમ અને મોબાઇલે છીનવી લીધો છે.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

The option sets in these passages pit near-synonyms against each other. The pairs below are the ones the 30 blanks actually test:

FormMeaningConnectionKey differenceUse when
しかし／だが vs だから／そこでshikashi / da ga vs dakara / soko de but vs so sentence-initial Contrast vs consequence — decide whether the new sentence goes against or follows from the previous one. N2-2 [3], N1-3 [5].
そうすれば vs そうしてもsou sureba / sou shite mo if you do that vs even if you do that sentence-initial Result of advice vs concession. N2-3 [4].
逆に vs 特に／別に／仮にgyaku ni / toku ni / betsu ni / kari ni on the contrary vs especially / (not) particularly / supposing adverbs Only 逆に reverses the expected result. N2-1 [5].
〜にとって vs 〜によって／について／に対して~ni totte / ~ni yotte / ~ni tsuite / ~ni taishite for (viewpoint) vs by / about / toward N + に〜 にとって introduces whose judgement it is (大切だ, 〜に過ぎない). N2-1 [3], N1-2 [1].
〜にかかわらず vs 〜にもかかわらず~ni kakawarazu / ~ni mo kakawarazu regardless of vs despite AかBか／V V-ない + にかかわらず ／ plain + にもかかわらず にかかわらず takes a pair or range; にもかかわらず takes one fact. N1-3 [2].
〜一方で vs 〜上に／〜以上~ippou de / ~ue ni / ~ijou while vs on top of / since plain + 一方で／上に／以上 Contrast vs addition vs firm reason. N1-3 [1].
〜とは限らない vs 〜はずだ／〜ものである~to wa kagiranai / ~hazu da / ~mono de aru not necessarily vs should / by nature plain + とは限らない Partial negation, often with すべて／必ずしも. N1-1 [1].
〜わけではない vs 〜ものでもない~wake de wa nai / ~mono demo nai it's not that vs (usually 〜ないものでもない) it's not impossible plain + わけではない わけではない denies a natural assumption; pairs with 決して／別に. N1-2 [5].
困らせる vs 困る／困らせられるkomaraseru / komaru / komaraserareru cause trouble vs be troubled / be made troubled N を 困らせる Only the causative takes a を-object of the person troubled. N1-1 [2].

Exam trap 1 — read one sentence beyond the blank: many blanks (N2-2 [2], N2-3 [3], N1-2 [3], N1-3 [3]) are decided by what comes after them — the examples or explanation that follow. Don't choose from the blank's own sentence alone.
Exam trap 2 — voice and viewpoint: passive vs active vs causative options (注意されただけ, 困らせる, 受け入れられずに) look almost identical. Mark the subject of the clause and who does what to whom before picking.
Exam trap 3 — the writer's stance: sentence endings (ものだ, とは限らない, わけではない) must agree with the passage's overall argument. If the passage concludes "language is not logical", an ending that asserts "everything can be explained" is wrong however natural it sounds.
