1 Question Type: ポイント理解 — Point Comprehension

Key idea

質問や選択肢から話を予測しよう！Shitsumon ya sentakushi kara hanashi o yosoku shiyou! — "Predict what the talk will be about from the question and the options!"

This type gives you a real advantage the other types don't: you hear the question before the dialogue starts, and you get a pause to read the four printed options before the audio continues. Use that pause. The dialogue will contain plenty of information that overlaps with the options — your job isn't to understand everything, it's to catch the one specific fact the question actually asks for.

  1. ① 聞説明和提問 — Listen to the setup and the question.
  2. ② 浏览选项，抓住重点 — Use the pause to skim the four printed options and predict what to listen for.
  3. ③ 聞対話 — Listen to the dialogue.
  4. ④ 再聞一遍提問 — Hear the question repeated.
  5. ⑤ 選択答案 — Choose your answer.

In the actual exam, nothing is said after the question is repeated the second time — that silence is your cue to mark an answer immediately.

2 Worked Example — 例をやってみよう

例 CD1, Track 28 (question + options) → Track 29 (dialogue)

男の人が、新しいテレビの開発について話しています。新しいテレビを開発する上で、今後の課題は何ですか。Otoko no hito ga, atarashii terebi no kaihatsu ni tsuite hanashite imasu. Atarashii terebi o kaihatsu suru ue de, kongo no kadai wa nan desu ka.

EN: A man is talking about developing a new TV. In developing a new TV, what is the challenge going forward?   HI: एक आदमी नए टीवी के विकास के बारे में बात कर रहा है। नया टीवी विकसित करने में, आगे की चुनौती क्या है?   GU: એક માણસ નવા ટીવીના વિકાસ વિશે વાત કરી રહ્યો છે. નવું ટીવી વિકસાવવામાં, આગળનો પડકાર શું છે?

男: 薄型テレビは、各社の努力の結果、現在では薄さも軽さも限界に近いくらいになり、質も良くなり、また販売価格も以前に比べてかなり下がりました。テレビ本来の機能としても、もうこれ以上開発の余地がないところまで来ています。これから、各社が競争に勝つためには、付加価値をどうつけるかについて考えていくことが重要だと思います。つまり、これからは、映像を見ること以外の部分に力を入れていかないといけないでしょう。Usugata terebi wa, kakusha no doryoku no kekka, genzai de wa ususa mo karusa mo genkai ni chikai kurai ni nari, shitsu mo yoku nari, mata hanbai kakaku mo izen ni kurabete kanari sagarimashita. Terebi honrai no kinou toshite mo, mou kore ijou kaihatsu no yochi ga nai tokoro made kite imasu. Kore kara, kakusha ga kyousou ni katsu tame ni wa, fukakachi o dou tsukeru ka ni tsuite kangaete iku koto ga juuyou da to omoimasu. Tsumari, kore kara wa, eizou o miru koto igai no bubun ni chikara o irete ikanai to ikenai deshou.

EN translation: "Thanks to every manufacturer's efforts, flat-screen TVs have now become about as thin and light as they can get, picture quality has improved, and prices have dropped a lot compared to before. Even in terms of the TV's core function, we've basically run out of room to develop further. Going forward, I think what matters for companies to win the competition is how they add extra value — in other words, they'll need to focus on things beyond just the picture itself."

#OptionMeaning
1もっと軽く薄くすることMotto karuku usuku suru kotoMaking it even lighter and thinner — explicitly called "near the limit" already, not the open challenge
2価格を見直すこことKakaku o minaosu kotoRevisiting the price — prices are said to have already dropped a lot; not framed as the future challenge
3映像の質を上げることEizou no shitsu o ageru kotoRaising picture quality — also said to have already improved; and he says the future is about things besides the picture
4別の価値をつけることBetsu no kachi o tsukeru kotoAdding a different kind of value — matches his closing point about 付加価値 (added value) beyond picture-watching

Why: Options 1–3 all describe things the speaker says are already essentially finished (thinness, price, picture quality). The question specifically asks about the future challenge (今後の課題), which he states directly in his closing sentence: adding value beyond the picture itself. That is 付加価値 (added value) — option 4.

Glossary: 開発の余地はない kaihatsu no yochi wa nai — "no room left for development"; 付加価値 fukakachi — "added value."

3 Listening Practice — れんしゅう (answers confirmed p.90)

2 items from p.29. First listen to the question, then read the printed options during the pause, then listen to the dialogue and choose 1〜4. Play the referenced CD track alongside this page, or read the script/options below as a pure recall drill.

1番 CD1, Track 30

男の人と女の人が話しています。女の人がメガネにした理由は何ですか。Otoko no hito to onna no hito ga hanashite imasu. Onna no hito ga megane ni shita riyuu wa nan desu ka.

EN: A man and a woman are talking. Why did the woman switch to glasses?   HI: एक पुरुष और एक महिला बात कर रहे हैं। महिला ने चश्मा क्यों पहना?   GU: એક પુરુષ અને એક સ્ત્રી વાત કરી રહ્યા છે. સ્ત્રીએ ચશ્મા કેમ પહેર્યા?

男: あれ、メガネ？コンタクトに変えたんじゃなかったの？Are, megane? Kontakuto ni kaeta n ja nakatta no?

女: うん、でもね、実はもう3回もなくしちゃったのよね。取り外すときに、どこかへ行っちゃって…。Un, demo ne, jitsu wa mou san-kai mo nakushichatta no yo ne. Torihazusu toki ni, dokoka e icchatte...

男: おっちょこちょいだな。すぐ探せばいいじゃないか。Occhokochoi da na. Sugu sagaseba ii ja nai ka.

女: だって、見えないんだから探すのも大変なのよ。使い捨てのソフトなら、2、3枚なくしても、そんなにかかんないんだけど、私のはハードだから、けっこうかかっちゃって…。だからもうやめたの。Datte, mienai n dakara sagasu no mo taihen na no yo. Tsukaisute no sofuto nara, ni-san mai nakushite mo, sonna ni kakannai n dakedo, watashi no wa haado dakara, kekkou kakacchatte... Dakara mou yameta no.

男: まあ、似合ってるからいいんじゃない？最近、女の子のメガネ、流行ってるみたいだし。Maa, niatteru kara ii n ja nai? Saikin, onna no ko no megane, hayatteru mitai da shi.

女: そういうわけじゃないんだけどね。でも、これ、作ったばかりなのに、目に合ってないみたいで疲れるの…メガネ屋さんに行かないと…。Sou iu wake ja nai n dakedo ne. Demo, kore, tsukutta bakari na noni, me ni atte nai mitai de tsukareru no... Megane-ya-san ni ikanai to...

#OptionMeaning
1コンタクトにして高くついたからKontakuto ni shite takaku tsuita karaBecause switching to contacts turned out expensive — she lost 3 pairs of hard (not disposable) lenses, each costly to replace
2コンタクトが目に合わなかったからKontakuto ga me ni awanakatta karaBecause the contacts didn't fit her eyes — a trap: it's her new glasses that don't feel right, not the old contacts
3メガネが流行っているからMegane ga hayatte iru karaBecause glasses are trendy — the man suggests this, but she explicitly denies it ("そういうわけじゃない")
4メガネのほうが取り外しが楽だからMegane no hou ga torihazushi ga raku dakaraBecause glasses are easier to take on and off — never mentioned in the dialogue

Why: Her actual complaint is cost: she kept losing her hard contact lenses, and unlike cheap disposables, replacing hard lenses is expensive — that's why she gave up on contacts (option 1). The dialogue mentions "not fitting" and "trendy," but both apply to something else (her new glasses; the man's guess, which she denies), making 2 and 3 classic same-word traps.

2番 CD1, Track 31

テレビでアナウンサーが、男の人にインタビューをしています。男の人は、新しい商品の開発について、何を一番重視したと言っていますか。Terebi de anaunsaa ga, otoko no hito ni intabyuu o shite imasu. Otoko no hito wa, atarashii shouhin no kaihatsu ni tsuite, nani o ichiban juushi shita to itte imasu ka.

EN: A TV announcer is interviewing a man. What does he say he emphasized most in developing the new product?   HI: टीवी पर एक एंकर एक आदमी का साक्षात्कार ले रहा है। नए उत्पाद के विकास में उसने सबसे ज़्यादा किस पर ज़ोर दिया?   GU: ટીવી પર એક એન્કર એક માણસનો ઇન્ટરવ્યુ લઈ રહ્યો છે. નવા ઉત્પાદનના વિકાસમાં તેણે સૌથી વધુ શેના પર ભાર મૂક્યો?

女: 今日は、新発売の「どこでもカレーライス」を開発された山田さんにお越しいただいております。山田さん、この商品の特長をお聞かせください。Kyou wa, shinhatsubai no "Doko demo Karee Raisu" o kaihatsu sareta Yamada-san ni okoshi itadaite orimasu. Yamada-san, kono shouhin no tokuchou o okikase kudasai.

男: えー、いくつかありますが、まずはカレーとライスを、量に応じて別々に組み合わせられることです。また、カレーの辛さを各自調節できるように、スパイスも別添えで付けています。それから、冷たい状態で食べてもおいしいということです。Ee, ikutsuka arimasu ga, mazu wa karee to raisu o, ryou ni oujite betsubetsu ni kumiawaserareru koto desu. Mata, karee no karasa o kakuji chousetsu dekiru you ni, supaisu mo betsuzoe de tsukete imasu. Sore kara, tsumetai joutai de tabete mo oishii to iu koto desu.

女: 量が調整できるのは、ダイエットしている女性にとっても大変うれしいですねえ。辛さも人それぞれ好みがありますから、自分で調整できるというのもいいですね。でもー…カレーは温かくないと、おいしくないのではないでしょうか…。Ryou ga chousei dekiru no wa, daietto shite iru josei ni totte mo taihen ureshii desu nee. Karasa mo hito sorezore konomi ga arimasu kara, jibun de chousei dekiru to iu no mo ii desu ne. Demo... karee wa atatakakunai to, oishikunai no de wa nai deshou ka...

男: そこがこのカレーの最大の特長なんです。特殊な製法で、冷たくてもおいしさが損なわれないカレーなんです。もちろん温めてもおいしいのですが、夏の暑い日に、冷たく冷やしたこのカレーを、ぜひ召し上がっていただきたいと思います。Soko ga kono karee no saidai no tokuchou nan desu. Tokushu na seihou de, tsumetakute mo oishisa ga sokonawarenai karee nan desu. Mochiron atatamete mo oishii no desu ga, natsu no atsui hi ni, tsumetaku hiyashita kono karee o, zehi meshiagatte itadakitai to omoimasu.

#OptionMeaning
1カレーとライスを別々に組み合わせられることKaree to raisu o betsubetsu ni kumiawaserareru kotoBeing able to combine curry and rice separately — mentioned first, but not what he calls the "biggest" feature
2カレーの辛さを自由に調節できることKaree no karasa o jiyuu ni chousetsu dekiru kotoBeing able to freely adjust the curry's spiciness — also mentioned, also not the one he singles out
3ダイエットしている人のことを考えていることDaietto shite iru hito no koto o kangaete iru kotoBeing considerate of people on a diet — this is the interviewer's own comment, not the man's stated priority
4冷たいままで食べてもおいしいことTsumetai mama de tabete mo oishii kotoThat it's delicious even eaten cold — he directly calls this "このカレーの最大の特長" (this curry's biggest feature)

Why: He lists three features, but only pins down one as the biggest: after the interviewer questions whether cold curry can taste good, he explicitly says "そこがこのカレーの最大の特長なんです" — that point (staying delicious cold) is the one he most emphasizes. The other features are real, but he never ranks them above this one.

4 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

ポイント理解 questions are built from a small, recognizable set of question stems. Memorizing their shape (from the book's own 「注意しよう」 box) lets you know exactly what to listen for before the dialogue even starts:

Question stemWhat it's really askingSeen in
何が一番／最も／特に　重要だ・大変だ・不満だと言っていますか。 Which single thing is called "most important / hardest / most dissatisfying" — expect several candidates mentioned, only one explicitly ranked highest. Both items above
何を一番重視したと言っていますか。
何を最優先すべきだと言っていますか。		 "What did they emphasize most?" / "What should be given top priority?" — listen for a speaker's own ranking, not the listener's guess. 2番
今後の課題は何ですか。 "What's the challenge going forward?" — distinguish already-solved problems from the one still open. Worked example
〜した理由は何だと言っていますか。
どこに問題があると言っていますか。		 "What reason do they give for ~?" / "Where do they say the problem is?" — the real reason is often buried after a "だって" or "実は." 1番
Exam trap: Every wrong option in this lesson was a real detail from the dialogue — just not the detail the question stem asked for. Reading the question stem carefully (最も? 一番? 今後の?) before the dialogue starts is half the battle.
