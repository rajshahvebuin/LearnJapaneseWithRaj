1 Question Type: 統合理解② — Two-Question Integrated Comprehension

Key idea

必要な情報だけ聞き取ろう！Hitsuyou na jouhou dake kikitorou! — "Only try to catch the information you actually need!"

This is the hardest variant of 統合理解: first you hear a single-speaker explanation of four things (four course options, four hospital rooms), then a two-person conversation reacting to that explanation, then two separate questions, each with its own 1〜4 options — "which one does person A choose?" and "which one does person B choose?" You cannot re-listen, so note-taking during the four-item explanation is essential; trying to hold 4 options × several features each in your head alone is exactly how this type is designed to defeat you.

  1. ① 聞説明 — Listen to the setup.
  2. ② 先聞一段対話 — First, listen to a single-speaker explanation of four items — take notes.
  3. ③ 再聞両個人的対話 — Then listen to two people discussing/deciding between those items.
  4. ④ 聞提問 — Listen to the two separate questions.
  5. ⑤ 選択答案 — Answer each question from its own four options.

Jot a one-line note per item as the four-way explanation plays (e.g. course name + one key feature) — that's usually enough to tell them apart once the conversation starts referring back to them by name only.

2 Worked Example — 例をやってみよう

例 (説明) CD1, Track 38

夫婦がパソコンスクールの受付の人の話を聞いています。Fuufu ga pasokon sukuuru no uketsuke no hito no hanashi o kiite imasu.

EN: A married couple is listening to the receptionist at a computer school explain the courses. Take a one-line note per course as you listen.   HI: एक पति-पत्नी कंप्यूटर स्कूल के रिसेप्शनिस्ट की बात सुन रहे हैं। हर कोर्स के लिए एक पंक्ति नोट बनाएं।   GU: એક પતિ-પત્ની કમ્પ્યુટર સ્કૂલના રિસેપ્શનિસ્ટની વાત સાંભળી રહ્યા છે. દરેક કોર્સ માટે એક લીટીની નોંધ બનાવો.

女: パソコンを全く使ったことのない人のための「いろはコース」ですが、ここでは、インターネットを使えるようになることが目標です。「わかばコース」は、メールやインターネットをやったことがある人向けで、簡単なワープロと表計算のソフトを使えるようにするのが目的です。「みのりコース」は、基本的なワープロ機能を使える人が対象で、より高度なワープロや表計算のソフトの使い方をマスターします。その上には、イラストや音楽の製作などを覚える「マスターコース」などもあります。「体験コース」ですが、現在進行中のコースを見学兼体験していただくというもので、3回までは無料でいつでも参加が可能です。じっくり自分にあったコースをお選びいただけます。なお、各コースの受講開始日は、すべて来週からとなっています。Pasokon o mattaku tsukatta koto no nai hito no tame no "Iroha Koosu" desu ga, koko dewa, intaanetto o tsukaeru you ni naru koto ga mokuhyou desu. "Wakaba Koosu" wa, meeru ya intaanetto o yatta koto ga aru hito muke de, kantan na waapuro to hyoukeisan no sofuto o tsukaeru you ni suru no ga mokuteki desu. "Minori Koosu" wa, kihonteki na waapuro kinou o tsukaeru hito ga taishou de, yori koudo na waapuro ya hyoukeisan no sofuto no tsukaikata o masutaa shimasu. Sono ue ni wa, irasuto ya ongaku no seisaku nado o oboeru "Masutaa Koosu" nado mo arimasu. "Taiken Koosu" desu ga, genzai shinkouchuu no koosu o kengaku ken taiken shite itadaku to iu mono de, san-kai made wa muryou de itsu demo sanka ga kanou desu. Jikkuri jibun ni atta koosu o oerabi itadakemasu. Nao, kaku koosu no jukou kaishibi wa, subete raishuu kara to natte imasu.

EN translation: "The 'Iroha Course' is for people who've never used a computer at all — the goal is just to become able to use the internet. The 'Wakaba Course' is for those who've already done email/internet, aiming to get comfortable with basic word-processing and spreadsheet software. The 'Minori Course' is for those who can already use basic word-processor functions, and has them master more advanced word-processing/spreadsheet skills. Above that there's also a 'Master Course' covering things like illustration and music production. The 'Trial Course' lets you sit in on and try a currently running course, free, up to 3 times, any time. Please take your time picking the course that suits you — note that all courses start next week."

③ 二人の対話を聞いてください。Futari no taiwa o kiite kudasai.

CD1, Track 39

男: もう来週から始まっちゃうんだ…体験してる暇はないね。Mou raishuu kara hajimacchau n da... Taiken shiteru hima wa nai ne.

女: あなたは一番上のコースでしょ。Anata wa ichiban ue no koosu desho.

男: いや、僕は基本的なことしかできないからねえ…まずワープロと表計算のソフトを使いこなしてからだね。Iya, boku wa kihonteki na koto shika dekinai kara nee... Mazu waapuro to hyoukeisan no sofuto o tsukaikonashite kara da ne.

女: ふーん、私は、やっぱり体験してから決めようかな。Fuun, watashi wa, yappari taiken shite kara kimeyou ka na.

男: お前、インターネットもろくに使えないし、携帯のメールは得意でも、パソコンでの文字入力はほとんどやったことないだろ。すぐに一から教えてもらったほうがいいよ。Omae, intaanetto mo roku ni tsukaenai shi, keitai no meeru wa tokui demo, pasokon de no moji nyuuryoku wa hotondo yatta koto nai daro. Sugu ni ichi kara oshiete moratta hou ga ii yo.

女: うーん…そうねー…迷ってる暇はないかも。それにくやしいけど、あなたの言う通りだし、私はこのコースね。Uun... sou nee... Mayotteru hima wa nai kamo. Sore ni kuyashii kedo, anata no iu toori da shi, watashi wa kono koosu ne.

EN translation: He says there's no time to try a course first since it starts next week. She assumes he'll take the top course; he says no — he can only do basic things and first needs to really master word-processing and spreadsheet software. She says she'll decide after trying one out, but he points out she can barely use the internet or type on a PC (even if she's good with phone email), and should just be taught from scratch right away. She concedes there's no time to hesitate and, grudgingly agreeing he's right, picks "this course."

質問1　男の人はどのコースを受けますか。Shitsumon 1: Otoko no hito wa dono koosu o ukemasu ka.

質問2　女の人はどのコースを受けますか。Shitsumon 2: Onna no hito wa dono koosu o ukemasu ka.

#Option質問1 (man)質問2 (woman)
1体験コースTaiken Koosu——
2いろはコースIroha Koosu—✅ correct
3わかばコースWakaba Koosu——
4みのりコースMinori Koosu✅ correct—

Why: The man says he already handles "basic things" and now needs to master (使いこなす) word-processing/spreadsheet software — that's exactly the level the みのりコース targets (already-basic users aiming for advanced mastery), not わかば (which starts from email/internet users learning basic use). The woman, meanwhile, is told point-blank that she can't really use the internet or type on a PC and should be "taught from scratch" (一から) — that phrase maps directly onto いろはコース's total-beginner goal ("インターネットを使えるようになる").

4 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

The two-question 統合理解② format has its own special failure mode: getting the right facts but assigning them to the wrong person. Watch for these:

TrapWhat confuses learnersHow to catch it
Swapped attribution Both items above have a "she thinks she's more advanced/should pick X" moment that gets corrected by the other speaker — if you stop listening early, you'll assign the wrong course/room to the wrong person. Track whose final statement settles it, not the first guess floated in the conversation.
Similar-sounding options Four named items (courses, rooms) sharing overlapping features (all have a fridge; all are "advanced" to some degree) are easy to blur together from memory alone. A one-line note per item during the initial 4-way explanation is far more reliable than trying to reconstruct it after the fact.
Cost vs. comfort trade-off Speakers often weigh a cheaper/shared option against a pricier/private one, and change their mind mid-conversation (「でも」「まあ」「やっぱり」). Whatever opinion comes right before the reversal marker (でも, まあ, やっぱり) is usually overridden by what follows it.
Exam trap: Because two questions share one recording, a mistake on "who said what" early in the dialogue can cost you both answers, not just one. Treat the two questions as two separate tracking tasks from the very first line.
