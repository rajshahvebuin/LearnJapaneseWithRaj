1 Question Type: 即時応答 — Immediate Response

Key idea

最初の文と同じ言葉を使っている選択肢に惑わされないようにしよう！Saisho no bun to onaji kotoba o tsukatte iru sentakushi ni madowasarenai you ni shiyou! — "Don't be fooled by an answer choice that just recycles words from the opening line!"

This is the shortest and fastest-paced question type on N1 聴解: no printed text at all on the question sheet — just a one-line utterance (a greeting, question, request, opinion, thanks, or apology), followed immediately by three spoken replies. You mark the best one before the next item starts. There is no time to think in translation; you must recognize the function of the opening line (is it a request? a complaint? an apology?) and judge which reply actually answers that function.

  1. ① 聞く (kiku) — Listen to the short opening line.
  2. ② 選ぶ (erabu) — Immediately choose the best of the three spoken responses (1・2・3).

Opening lines are almost always one of: あいさつ (a greeting), 質問 (a question), お願い/依頼文 (a request), 許可を求める (asking permission), 意見や感想 (an opinion/impression), or お礼・謝罪 (thanks/apology).

2 Worked Example — 例をやってみよう

例 CD1, Track 20

雨でお足もとの悪い中をお越しいただき、申し訳ありません。Ame de oashimoto no warui naka o okoshi itadaki, moushiwake arimasen.

EN: "I'm terribly sorry to have had you come all this way in the rain, with the footing so bad."   HI: बारिश में इतनी खराब जगह पर आने के लिए मुझे बहुत खेद है।   GU: વરસાદમાં આટલી ખરાબ જગ્યાએ આવવા બદલ મને ખૂબ દુઃખ છે.

#ReplyMeaning
1いえいえ、おかげさまで、足はよくなりましたよ。Iie iie, okagesama de, ashi wa yoku narimashita yo."No no, thankfully, my leg has gotten better." (recycles 足 "foot/leg" from the trap word 足もと — wrong context)
2いえいえ、とても楽しみにしていたんですよ。Iie iie, totemo tanoshimi ni shiteitan desu yo."No no, I was really looking forward to this." — a genuine reply to being thanked for making the trip
3いえいえ、雨ばかりか雷も鳴っていましたよ。Iie iie, ame bakari ka kaminari mo natte imashita yo."No no, it wasn't just rain, there was thunder too." (recycles 雨 "rain" from the opening line — wrong context)

Why: The opening line is a host apologizing to a guest for making them travel in bad weather — this calls for a reassuring reply, not a factual comment. Choices 1 and 3 each reuse a literal word from the prompt (足, 雨) to sound plausible, but neither actually functions as a reply to the apology. Only 2 responds the way a guest naturally would.

3 Listening Practice — れんしゅう (answers confirmed p.86)

4 items from p.25. The question sheet has nothing printed on it — first listen to the opening line, then to the three spoken replies, and choose the best one (1〜3). Play the referenced CD track alongside this page, or read the script/options below as a pure recall drill.

1番 CD1, Track 21

あんなに毎晩遅くまで勉強していたのにねえ…。Anna ni maiban osoku made benkyou shite ita noni nee...

EN: "And after all that studying late every night, too..." (said with regret, implying something went wrong)   HI: हर रात इतनी देर तक पढ़ाई करने के बावजूद भी...(अफ़सोस के साथ)   GU: દરરોજ રાત્રે આટલું મોડું અભ્યાસ કરવા છતાં પણ...(અફસોસ સાથે)

#ReplyMeaning
1遅かったということが原因でしょう。Osokatta to iu koto ga gen'in deshou."Being late was probably the cause." (misreads noni as being about a train/appointment)
2なんとか合格できました。Nantoka goukaku dekimashita."I somehow managed to pass." (assumes the opposite outcome)
3本当に…残念でしたねえ…。Hontou ni... zannen deshita nee..."That really was... too bad, wasn't it..." — sympathizing with a failure

Why: The 〜のに ending signals frustration/regret about an unfair outcome — despite all that studying, something bad happened (almost certainly failing an exam). Only option 3 offers the matching sympathetic reaction; 1 and 2 both assume a different, more positive story.

2番 CD1, Track 22

ごめん、出がけに宅配便が来て…一本遅いのになっちゃって。Gomen, degake ni takuhaibin ga kite... ippon osoi no ni nacchatte.

EN: "Sorry, a delivery arrived just as I was heading out... so I ended up on a later train."   HI: माफ़ करना, निकलते समय डिलीवरी आ गई... इसलिए एक ट्रेन लेट हो गई।   GU: માફ કરજો, નીકળતી વખતે ડિલિવરી આવી ગઈ... એટલે એક ટ્રેન મોડી થઈ ગઈ.

#ReplyMeaning
1大丈夫、私も今来たところだから。Daijoubu, watashi mo ima kita tokoro dakara."It's fine, I only just got here myself." — a natural reassurance to someone apologizing for being late
2事故でもあったのかなあ。Jiko demo atta no ka naa."I wonder if there was an accident?" (wrong direction — speaker already explained the reason)
3荷物はあとで配達してもらえるよ。Nimotsu wa ato de haitatsu shite moraeru yo."You can have the package delivered later." (misreads 宅配便 as the listener's own problem to solve)

Why: The speaker apologizes for lateness and gives the reason. The correct reply reassures them that it's fine — 2 and 3 both latch onto a keyword (事故, 荷物) instead of responding to the actual social function of the line, which is an apology needing reassurance.

3番 CD1, Track 23

絶対に来るって言ってたのに、あいつ、いったい何考えてんだろう。Zettai ni kuru tte itteta noni, aitsu, ittai nani kangaeten darou.

EN: "He said he'd definitely come, so what on earth is he thinking?" (annoyed/worried he hasn't shown up)   HI: उसने कहा था कि वह ज़रूर आएगा, तो आखिर वह क्या सोच रहा है?   GU: તેણે કહ્યું હતું કે તે ચોક્કસ આવશે, તો તે છેવટે શું વિચારે છે?

#ReplyMeaning
1何かあったんじゃないかなあ。Nanika atta n ja nai ka naa."Maybe something came up?" — a sympathetic guess at why he hasn't arrived
2考えてもいいアイデア浮かばないと思うよ。Kangaete mo ii aidea ukabanai to omou yo."I don't think a good idea will come to you even if you think about it." (misreads 考えてんだろう literally as "thinking of an idea")
3予定に入れてなかったんじゃない？Yotei ni irete nakatta n ja nai?"Maybe he just never put it on his schedule?" (contradicts the premise — he explicitly said he'd come)

Why: 何考えてんだろう here is a rhetorical complaint ("what's he playing at?"), not a literal question about an idea — option 2 falls for that literal misreading. Option 3 ignores that he had already promised to come. Only 1 responds to the speaker's actual worry.

4番 CD1, Track 24

あのう、これ、つまらないものですが、どうぞお納めください。Anou, kore, tsumaranai mono desu ga, douzo osame kudasai.

EN: "Um, this is nothing much, but please accept it." (a set gift-giving phrase)   HI: उम, यह कोई खास चीज़ नहीं है, पर कृपया स्वीकार करें। (उपहार देते समय बोला जाने वाला विनम्र वाक्य)   GU: ઉમ, આ કંઈ ખાસ નથી, પણ કૃપા કરીને સ્વીકારો. (ભેટ આપતી વખતે બોલાતું નમ્ર વાક્ય)

#ReplyMeaning
1そんなことありませんよ。Sonna koto arimasen yo."That's not true at all." (a reply to being complimented or self-deprecated about, not to being given a gift)
2ああ、どうもご丁寧に、恐縮です。Aa, doumo go-teinei ni, kyoushuku desu."Oh, how very kind of you, I'm much obliged." — the standard polite way to accept a gift
3いただきものなので、ご遠慮なく。Itadakimono na node, go-enryo naku."It's something I was given myself, so don't be shy about it." (wrongly reframes the item as a regift being offered onward)

Why: 「つまらないものですが、お納めください」 is a fixed, humble gift-giving phrase — the polite reply accepts the gift graciously (2). 1 mistakes it for a compliment being deflected, and 3 misreads the situation as if the listener were re-gifting something.

4 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

即時応答 traps rarely come from vocabulary difficulty — they come from four families of set expressions and grammar nuance that the book's own 「注意しよう」 box flags. Learn to recognize the family, not just the words:

Pattern familyExampleWhat it actually signals
Request patterns
irai-bun		 「〜たいんですが…」「〜してくれない？」「〜してくれるとうれしいんだけど…」 These all function as a request even though none contains 「お願い」 — the correct reply must grant, decline, or negotiate the request itself, not comment on the wording.
Feeling/emotion expressions 「どうせ…でしょう」（諦めている）「どういうつもりだ。まったく！」（呆れている・怒っている）「…のに、どうして…」（残念）「そんなこと、よく言うね」（呆れ・不満） Each hides a specific emotion (resignation, anger, disappointment, exasperation) — the reply must match that emotion, not the literal words.
Intonation flips meaning 「これじゃない↘」(＝これではない) vs「これじゃない↗」(＝これだと思う)「できたじゃない！」（＝できてよかったね）「そんなところ、行くんじゃない！」（＝行ってはいけない） The exact same string of words can mean opposite things depending on rising vs. falling pitch — 聴解 only gives you the audio, so intonation IS the grammar here.
Past-tense confirmation 「今日の会議、3時からだったっけ？」「明日の出勤、早かったんじゃない？」 A past-tense ending on a question about something not yet finished usually means "wasn't it...?" / "remind me, was it...?" — a memory-check, not a report about the past.
Exam trap: On every item in this lesson, at least one wrong choice recycled a literal word from the opening line (足, 雨, 考える, つまらない) to sound relevant. Train yourself to judge replies by function (does this actually respond to a request/apology/complaint?), never by keyword overlap.
