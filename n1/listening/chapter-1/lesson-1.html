1 Sound Patterns to Watch For

Key idea

似ている音や縮約形に注意しよう！Niteiru oto ya shukuyakukei ni chuui shiyou! — "Watch out for similar-sounding words and contracted forms!"

Before you can tackle full listening questions, this book trains your ear on two low-level traps that quietly change a word's meaning: (1) whether a small っ (doubled consonant) is really there, and whether a vowel is long or short; (2) fast, casual speech that contracts grammar endings — both are extremely common on the actual N1 聴解 audio.

1a. 「はま（浜）」「はば（幅）」「はっぱ（葉っぱ）」「はんぱ（半端）」— minimal pairs

When memorizing a word, always check whether it has a small 「っ・ッ」, a long vowel (う), a small 「ゃ・ゅ・ょ」, or a 「ん・ン」 — each one is a separate word.

1b. 「見てらんない」「聞きゃしない」— casual contractions

Native speakers compress these endings constantly in spoken Japanese — recognizing the contracted form and mentally "expanding" it back to its dictionary form is the core skill this lesson builds.

2 Listening Practice — れんしゅう

3 exercises from p.13, each drilling one of the traps above. Play the referenced CD track alongside this page (see note in the header), or use these as pure read-and-recall drills if you don't have the audio to hand.

1番 CD1, Track 2

小さい「っ（ッ）」があれば＿に書いてください。Chiisai "tsu (ッ)" ga areba ___ ni kaite kudasai.

EN: If there's a small っ, write it in the blank.   HI: अगर छोटा 「っ」 हो तो उसे रिक्त स्थान में भरें।   GU: જો નાનો 「っ」 હોય તો ખાલી જગ્યામાં ભરો.

例題 reidai (example): ま＿ち → マ＿チ   =   まち (町, "town") → マッチ (match) machi → macchi

#PuzzleAnswerMeaning
①く＿せ＿つくっせつ（屈折）kussetsurefraction, bending
②バ＿ス＿ケ＿トバスケットbasukettobasket (trap — no っ at all!)
③じ＿か＿んじっかん（実感）jikkana real, concrete feeling
④や＿り＿ぱ＿な＿しやりっぱなしyarippanashileaving something half-done, not following through
⑤カ＿プ＿ルカップルkappurucouple
⑥ほ＿そ＿くほっそく（発足）hossokuinauguration, launch (of a group/organization)
⑦ピ＿ク＿ニ＿クピクニックpikunikkupicnic (trap — no っ at all!)
⑧し＿た＿か＿ぶ＿りしったかぶり（知ったかぶり）shittakaburipretending to know something you don't

Why: A small っ adds one full extra beat (mora) and doubles the next consonant — miss it and くっせつ becomes the meaningless くせつ. But the reverse trap is just as common: loanwords like バスケット and ピクニック sound like they might double a consonant but don't, so writing バッスケット or ピックニック would be wrong. Always check both directions.

2番 CD1, Track 3

書き方にも注意して、聞こえたものを選んでください。Kakikata ni mo chuui shite, kikoeta mono o erande kudasai.

EN: Pay attention to the spelling too, and choose what you actually hear.   HI: लिखने के तरीके पर भी ध्यान दें और जो सुनाई दे वही चुनें।   GU: લખવાની રીત પર પણ ધ્યાન આપો અને જે સંભળાય તે જ પસંદ કરો.

例題 reidai: しゅるいがおおい（種類が多い）   shurui ga ooi — "there are many kinds/types"

#ChoicesCorrectMeaning
①（おうべい／おおべい）の（マーカー／メーカー）おうべいのメーカーoubei no meekaaa Western (European/American) manufacturer
②（とけい／とうけい）の（データ／レート）とうけいのデータtoukei no deetastatistical data
③（おうはば／おおはば）な（へんこ／へんこう）おおはばなへんこうoohaba na henkoua large-scale/major change
④（どりょく／どうりょく）（もくひょう／もくひゅう）どりょくもくひょうdoryoku mokuhyouan effort goal/target
⑤（どそうかい／どうそうかい）の（あんないじょう／あんねいじゅう）どうそうかいのあんないじょうdousoukai no annaijouan alumni-reunion invitation letter

Why: Every pair here differs only by vowel length (お vs おう, と vs とう, おうvs おお) or by which katakana loanword actually fits the meaning (メーカー "maker/manufacturer" vs マーカー "marker pen"; データ "data" vs レート "rate"). A single missed long-vowel beat turns 統計 (statistics, toukei) into a meaningless とけい-sounding word — the same beat that separates とけい (時計, "clock").

3番 CD1, Track 4

何と言いましたか。＿に一つずつひらがなを書いて、（）にもとの形を書いてください。Nan to iimashita ka. ___ ni hitotsu zutsu hiragana o kaite, ( ) ni moto no katachi o kaite kudasai.

EN: What did they say? Write the sounds in the blanks, then the dictionary form in parentheses.   HI: क्या कहा गया? रिक्त स्थान में ध्वनियाँ और कोष्ठक में मूल रूप लिखें।   GU: શું કહ્યું? ખાલી જગ્યામાં અવાજો અને કૌંસમાં મૂળ સ્વરૂપ લખો.

例題 reidai: これ、冷蔵庫にいれとくね。（いれておく）   Kore, reizouko ni iretoku ne. (irete oku) — "I'll put this in the fridge (for now), OK?"

#Heard (contracted)Original formMeaning
① ちょっとさがしゃ、すぐ見つかるのにね。Chotto sagasha, sugu mitsukaru noni ne. （さがせば）sagaseba "If you'd just look for it a bit, you'd find it right away."
② 誰もこんなもの、ぬすみゃしないだろう。Dare mo konna mono, nusumya shinai darou. （ぬすみは）nusumi wa "No one would steal a thing like this."
③ 彼は僕の言うことなんか、ききゃしないよ。Kare wa boku no iu koto nanka, kikya shinai yo. （ききは）kiki wa "He doesn't listen to a word I say."
④ わざわざ送ってくんなくたって、そのうち取りに行くよ。Wazawaza okutte kunnakutatte, sono uchi tori ni iku yo. （くれなくても）kurenakute mo "You don't have to bother sending it — I'll come pick it up eventually."

Why: Each item chains two of section 1b's patterns: せば→しゃ (①), a masu-stem + は→ゃ (②③, ぬすみは→ぬすみゃ, ききは→ききゃ), and でも→たって stacked on top of くれない's own colloquial shrinkage to くんない (④). Recognize the ending shape first, then mentally expand it — trying to guess the meaning before decoding the grammar is what trips most learners up here.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

This lesson's whole point is ear-training against three specific trap types — treat this table as the day's confusion-pairs summary:

Trap typeWhat confuses learnersHow to catch itSeen in
Geminate っ An extra silent beat that doubles the next consonant is easy to miss or hallucinate Listen for a short pause/held consonant; don't assume loanwords always have one (バスケット, ピクニック don't) 1番
Long vs. short vowel おう/とう/おお sound almost identical to お/と/お at natural speed Long vowels add a full extra mora — train your ear on minimal pairs like とけい (clock) vs とうけい (statistics) 2番
Casual contractions ば/は/でも/ては endings compress into ゃ/たって/ちゃ, hiding the underlying grammar Learn the contraction table (1b) as fixed patterns, then decode by pattern-matching, not by guessing 3番
Exam trap: N1 聴解 rarely tests this chapter's material directly as its own question — it's foundational ear-training so that later chapters' actual question types (即時応答, 課題理解, etc. — Chapter 2) don't trip you up on a mishearing before you even get to the real listening skill being tested.
