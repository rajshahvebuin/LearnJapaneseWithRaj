1 Keigo Patterns to Watch For

Key idea

敬語の再確認！ Keigo no saikakunin! — "Reconfirming keigo!"

敬語 (keigo) doesn't just make a sentence politer — it can completely change who is doing the action. This lesson trains you to instantly tell whether a polite verb form belongs to the speaker (humble, 謙譲語 — lowers themselves) or is directed at the listener (respectful, 尊敬語 — raises the other person), and to recognize a set of fixed keigo phrases whose surface meaning has nothing to do with what they actually communicate.

3a. 確認しよう — 話し手 (speaker/humble) vs. 聞き手 (listener/respectful)

Left column = what the speaker says about their own action (humbling themselves); right column = what's said to the listener about the listener's action (elevating them). Mixing these up flips who's doing what.

3b. よく聞く敬語 — fixed keigo phrases you'll hear constantly

These don't map onto a speaker/listener pair — they're standalone set phrases. Learn the whole phrase as a block; decoding it word-by-word gives the wrong meaning almost every time.

JapanesePlain meaning
恐れ入ります／恐縮ですosoreirimasu / kyoushuku desu（＝ありがとう・すみませんarigatou / sumimasen）"thank you" / "sorry to trouble you" — despite 恐れ literally meaning "fear"

（田中が）承りました(Tanaka ga) uketamawarimashita（＝（田中が）聞きました・受けました(Tanaka ga) kikimashita / uketamashita）"Tanaka has taken care of it" — the book's own gloss: 田中接受了您的预约 ("Tanaka has accepted/received your request")

2 Listening Practice — れんしゅう

2 exercises from p.17, testing whether you can convert keigo back to its plain-form meaning on the fly. Play the referenced CD track alongside this page (see note in the header), or use these as pure read-and-recall drills if you don't have the audio to hand.

1番 CD1, Track 7

内容の正しいほうを選んでください。Naiyou no tadashii hou o erande kudasai.

EN: Choose the option that correctly matches what you heard.   HI: जो सुना उससे सही मेल खाने वाला विकल्प चुनें।   GU: જે સાંભળ્યું તેની સાથે સાચી રીતે મેળ ખાતો વિકલ્પ પસંદ કરો.

例題 reidai (example): 1 田中さんが帰る。2 田中さんが来た。   correct = ②田中さんが来た Tanaka-san ga kaeru / Tanaka-san ga kita

#Option 1Option 2Correct
①私は鈴木です。Watashi wa Suzuki desu.鈴木さんです。Suzuki-san desu.2
②先生は知っている。Sensei wa shitte iru.先生だと思う。Sensei da to omou.1
③千円ください。Sen'en kudasai.千円のおつりです。Sen'en no otsuri desu.1
④どうかしましたか。Dou ka shimashita ka.どうしたらいいですか。Dou shitara ii desu ka.1
⑤問題については知らない。Mondai ni tsuite wa shiranai.問題ないと思う。Mondai nai to omou.2

Why: ① 鈴木さんとおっしゃいます ("goes by the name Suzuki") is 尊敬語 describing someone else named Suzuki, not the speaker introducing themselves. ② 先生はご存知です is the respectful form of 知っている, so "the teacher knows" (option 1) — not "thinks." ③ 千円頂戴します literally "I will humbly receive 1000 yen" — i.e. asking for 1000 yen, not handing back change. ④ どうかなさいましたか is simply the polite form of どうかしましたか ("what's wrong/happened"), not "what should I do." ⑤ 問題はないかと存じます ("I believe there's no problem") uses 3a's ～と存じます pattern for "I think," giving option 2.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Keigo mix-ups on the N1 聴解 fall into two clusters — treat this table as the lesson's confusion-pairs summary:

Trap typeWhat confuses learnersHow to catch itSeen in
謙譲語 vs. 尊敬語 Both sound equally "polite," so it's easy to lose track of whether the speaker or the listener is the one acting 謙譲語 (拝見します・～と申します・お電話させて頂きます) always lowers the speaker's own action; 尊敬語 (ご覧ください・おっしゃる・お電話頂けますか) always raises the listener's action 3a, 1番
Set keigo phrases Literal word-by-word translation gives the wrong meaning (恐れ入ります ≠ "I am afraid"; 承りました ≠ just "I heard") Memorize the whole phrase as a block tied to its function (thanks/apology, confirmation, recommendation) rather than decoding each kanji 3b, 2番
Exam trap: Business/formal-setting 会話 items (店員・受付・電話応対 scenes) on N1 聴解 lean heavily on 3b's fixed phrases — if a line sounds oddly formal or old-fashioned, it's very likely one of these sets, and the "obvious" literal reading is usually the wrong answer.
