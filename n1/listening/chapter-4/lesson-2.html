1 Vocabulary List — 省略形や使い方に注意しよう

省略形や使い方に注意しよう！Shouryakukei ya tsukaikata ni chuui shiyou! — "Watch out for abbreviated forms and usage!" This lesson covers three traps: words natives clip down to a short form, katakana words that don't mean what their English root suggests, and words Japan invented itself that only sound like English (和製英語, wasei-eigo).

2 Listening Practice — れんしゅう

4 exercises from p.55, drilling the words above in context. Play the referenced CD track alongside this page (see note in the header), or use these as pure read-and-recall drills if you don't have the audio to hand. Answers confirmed from p.124–125.

1番 CD2, Track 14

彼、スタートが遅れたのに、そのハンデにも関わらず、がんばっていますよね。Kare, sutaato ga okureta noni, sono hande ni mo kakawarazu, ganbatte imasu yo ne.

EN: "Even though he got a late start, he's doing great despite that handicap, isn't he."   HI: उसकी शुरुआत देर से हुई फिर भी वह उस बाधा के बावजूद बहुत मेहनत कर रहा है, है ना।   GU: તેની શરૂઆત મોડી થઈ છતાં તે એ અડચણ છતાં ખૂબ મહેનત કરી રહ્યો છે, ખરું ને.

#ResponseEnglish
1本当に、こんなはずではなかったのに…。Hontou ni, konna hazu de wa nakatta noni…"Really, it wasn't supposed to be like this…" (implies disappointment, contradicts the praise)
2ええ、普通なら、とっくにあきらめてるところでしょうね。Ee, futsuu nara, tokku ni akirameteru tokoro deshou ne."Yeah, normally he'd have given up long ago."
3彼は、そんなタイプではありませんから、大丈夫でしょう。Kare wa, sonna taipu de wa arimasen kara, daijoubu deshou."He's not that type, so he'll be fine." (answers a worry that wasn't expressed)

Why: The remark praises him for persevering despite a handicap; the natural continuation agrees and amplifies the praise (option 2: "most people would've quit already"). 1 and 3 both assume a negative framing that isn't in the original line.

2番 CD2, Track 15

田中さんの様子、どうだった？落ち込んでた？Tanaka-san no yousu, dou datta? Ochikondeta?

EN: "How did Tanaka seem? Was he down?"   HI: तनाका कैसे दिख रहे थे? उदास थे क्या?   GU: તનાકા કેવા લાગતા હતા? નિરાશ હતા?

#ResponseEnglish
1ううん、いつもよりテンション高かったよ。Uun, itsumo yori tenshon takakatta yo."No, he was more energetic than usual."
2うん、ワンパターンだったから。Un, wanpataan datta kara."Yeah, because it was the same old pattern." (doesn't answer "was he down?")
3いや、マンネリな感じがしたよ。Iya, manneri na kanji ga shita yo."No, it felt like he was in a rut." (mismatched — マンネリ describes a routine, not a mood)

Why: The question directly asks whether Tanaka seemed 落ち込んでた (down); only option 1 answers that yes/no question about mood, and does so by reporting the opposite (テンションが高い, "in high spirits"). 2 and 3 describe routines, not emotional states.

3番 CD2, Track 16

男の人と女の人が話しています。女の人は、レストランの何を一番重視すると言っていますか。Otoko no hito to onna no hito ga hanashite imasu. Onna no hito wa, resutoran no nani o ichiban juushi suru to itte imasu ka.

EN: A man and woman are talking. What does the woman say she values most in a restaurant?   HI: एक पुरुष और महिला बात कर रहे हैं। महिला रेस्तरां में सबसे ज़्यादा किस चीज़ को महत्व देती है?   GU: એક પુરુષ અને સ્ત્રી વાત કરે છે. સ્ત્રી રેસ્ટોરન્ટમાં સૌથી વધુ શેને મહત્વ આપે છે?

男：昨日行ったレストラン、おいしかったと思わない？バイキングで安かったし。Otoko: Kinou itta resutoran, oishikatta to omowanai? Baikingu de yasukatta shi.
女：バイキングであの値段はありえないとは思うけど…うーん…でも、なんか雰囲気がねえ。アットホームと言えばそうなんだけど、なんかねえ…。Onna: Baikingu de ano nedan wa arienai to wa omou kedo… uun… demo, nanka fun'iki ga nee. Attohoomu to ieba sou nan da kedo, nanka nee…
男：君は雰囲気、雰囲気ってうるさいよねえ。俺は、おいしくて雰囲気がよくても、量が少なかったら許せないなあ。Otoko: Kimi wa fun'iki, fun'iki tte urusai yo nee. Ore wa, oishikute fun'iki ga yokutemo, ryou ga sukunakattara yurusenai naa.
女：雰囲気は重要よ。でもそれは二の次で、やっぱりなんと言っても、おいしくなくっちゃ。それに、あと、安ければ言うことないね。あなたは量さえ多ければ、味なんてどうだっていいんじゃないの？Onna: Fun'iki wa juuyou yo. Demo sore wa ninoji de, yappari nanto itte mo, oishiku nakucha. Sore ni, ato, yasukereba iu koto nai ne. Anata wa ryou sae ookereba, aji nante dou datte ii n ja nai no?
男：そんなことない、まずいのは嫌に決まってるよ。でもね、とにかくお代わり自由っていうのは惹かれるねえ。Otoko: Sonna koto nai, mazui no wa iya ni kimatteru yo. Demo ne, tonikaku okawari jiyuu tte iu no wa hikareru nee.

EN translation: M: "Wasn't the restaurant we went to yesterday good? It was a buffet and cheap too." W: "I do think that price for a buffet is unbelievable… but somehow the atmosphere… I mean it was homey I guess, but somehow…" M: "You're always going on about atmosphere. Even if it's tasty with good atmosphere, I can't forgive it if the portions are small." W: "Atmosphere is important, but that's secondary — no matter what, it has to be tasty. And if it's cheap, no complaints. Don't you think as long as the portion is big, you don't care about taste?" M: "That's not true, of course I wouldn't like bad food. But free refills are appealing, you know."

#OptionEnglish
1雰囲気Fun'ikiAtmosphere (she calls this 二の次 — secondary)
2量RyouQuantity/portion size (that's the man's priority, not hers)
3値段NedanPrice ("nothing to complain about" if cheap — a bonus, not her top value)
4味AjiTaste

Why: The woman explicitly ranks her priorities: 雰囲気は重要 but 二の次 (secondary); 「やっぱりなんと言っても、おいしくなくっちゃ」("no matter what, it has to be tasty") is her real bottom line, with price merely a bonus. She contrasts herself with the man, for whom 量 (quantity) matters more than taste — testing whether you correctly assign each opinion to the right speaker.

4番 CD2, Track 17

男の人がファックスでDMを送る場合について話しています。Otoko no hito ga fakkusu de DM o okuru baai ni tsuite hanashite imasu.

男：一般消費者を対象とした業種、例えば、デパートやスーパーにファックスでDMを送信する場合ですが、週末や給料日後などは忙しい場合が多く、大きな反応は望めないので、避けたほうが賢明です。また、一般企業などに送信する場合、土日は社員が不在であったり、週明けの月曜日は忙しいなどの状況を考えて送りましょう。また、送信する時間帯ですが、相手の業種を考えて、仕事が集中すると思われる時間は避け、比較的ゆっくりした時間を狙うなどの工夫をすることが必要です。一般消費者へ送信する場合は、深夜の送信では、安眠妨害だとクレームが相次ぐ場合がありますので、昼過ぎに実施するようにしましょう。Otoko: Ippan shouhisha o taishou to shita gyoushu, tatoeba, depaato ya suupaa ni fakkusu de DM o soushin suru baai desu ga, shuumatsu ya kyuuryoubi go nado wa isogashii baai ga ooku, ookina hannou wa nozomenai node, saketa hou ga kenmei desu. Mata, ippan kigyou nado ni soushin suru baai, dojitsu wa shain ga fuzai de attari, shuuake no getsuyoubi wa isogashii nado no joukyou o kangaete okurimashou. Mata, soushin suru jikantai desu ga, aite no gyoushu o kangaete, shigoto ga shuuchuu suru to omowareru jikan wa sake, hikakuteki yukkuri shita jikan o nerau nado no kufuu o suru koto ga hitsuyou desu. Ippan shouhisha e soushin suru baai wa, shin'ya no soushin de wa, anmin bougai da to kureemu ga aitsugu baai ga arimasu node, hirusugi ni jisshi suru you ni shimashou.

EN translation: "For industries targeting general consumers — e.g. sending fax DMs to department stores or supermarkets — weekends and right after payday tend to be busy, so a big response can't be expected; it's wise to avoid those times. For general companies, remember staff may be out on weekends, or busy on the Monday after. As for time of day, avoid the recipient's busiest hours and aim for relatively quiet times. When sending to general consumers, late-night sends risk complaints about disturbed sleep, so send in the early afternoon instead."

男の人は、ファックスでDMを送る場合の何について話していますか。Otoko no hito wa, fakkusu de DM o okuru baai no nani ni tsuite hanashite imasu ka. — "What is the man talking about regarding sending DMs by fax?"

#OptionEnglish
1タイミングTaiminguTiming
2反応Han'nouReaction (mentioned once, not the overall topic)
3対象TaishouTarget audience (named but not the focus)
4クレームKureemuComplaints (only one supporting detail near the end)

Why: Every point in the talk is really about when to send: avoiding weekends/payday, avoiding Monday mornings, avoiding busy hours, and sending consumers' DMs in the early afternoon rather than at night — all timing advice. The other three nouns each appear once as a supporting detail, not the organizing theme.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

This lesson is built entirely around near-miss traps — words that sound alike, or sound like English but aren't. Treat this as the day's confusion-pairs summary:

Trap typeWhat confuses learnersHow to catch itSeen in
ブレイク vs ブレークbureiku / bureeku Near-identical katakana spellings for two unrelated meanings ("a break" vs. "to break out/hit big") Check context: コーヒーブレイク (rest) vs. 大ブレークした曲 (a hit song) — the accompanying noun disambiguates Vocabulary 1b
クレームkureemu Learners assume it means "a claim" (as in insurance/ownership) In Japanese it always means "a complaint" — クレームをつける = to complain, never "to file a claim" Vocabulary 1b, Exercise 4番
テンションtenshon Learners assume it means "tension" (stress/anxiety) In casual Japanese, テンションが高い means "excited/pumped up" — the opposite emotional register from English "tense" Vocabulary 1b, Exercise 2番
Wasei-eigo (バイキング, ペーパードライバー, etc.) These look like real English but were coined in Japan and don't exist (with that meaning) outside it Learn them as vocabulary items in their own right, not as English loanwords you can already guess Vocabulary 1c
Exam trap: N1 聴解 loves burying the real topic under several named-but-secondary nouns (Exercise 4番: 反応, 対象, クレーム are all mentioned, but only タイミング organizes the whole talk). When a 概要理解 question asks "what is this about," look for the word that every sentence relates back to — not just any word that appears in the audio.
