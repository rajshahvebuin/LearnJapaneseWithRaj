1 Strategy & Key Words — conclusion, request, rule, cause

The book's instruction (p.124) + tips for this set
  • Nothing is printed. Hear the talk first, then the question and options; choose 1–4. Mazu hanashi o kiite kudasai. Sorekara shitsumon to sentakushi o kiite, ichi kara yon no naka kara, mottomo yoi mono o hitotsu erande kudasai.
    HI: कुछ छपा नहीं होता। पहले बात सुनें, फिर प्रश्न और विकल्प; 1–4 में से चुनें। GU: કંઈ છપાયેલું નથી. પહેલાં વાત સાંભળો, પછી પ્રશ્ન અને વિકલ્પ; 1–4 માંથી પસંદ કરો.
  • Each talk type has a predictable "point" (added, not in book): a commentary ends with its claim (〜ではないでしょうか); a voicemail ends with what the caller wants you to do (電話くれる？); an announcement is a list of rules, so for a "which is different" question check each option against a rule; a life story gives the cause just before the turn (それまでは…, でも…). Keep a one-line note for each. … de wa nai deshou ka / denwa kureru? / chigau mono wa dore desu ka / kikkake
    HI: हर तरह की बात की "मुख्य बात" अनुमानित होती है: टिप्पणी अंत में दावा करती है; वॉइसमेल में अंत में अनुरोध; घोषणा नियमों की सूची है, तो "कौन-सा अलग है" में हर विकल्प को नियम से मिलाएँ; जीवन-कथा में कारण मोड़ से ठीक पहले आता है। GU: દરેક પ્રકારની વાતનો "મુખ્ય મુદ્દો" અનુમાનિત હોય છે: ટિપ્પણી અંતે દાવો કરે છે; વૉઇસમેલમાં અંતે વિનંતી; જાહેરાત નિયમોની યાદી છે, તો "કયું અલગ છે" માં દરેક વિકલ્પ નિયમ સાથે મેળવો; જીવનકથામાં કારણ વળાંક પહેલાં આવે છે.

2 Every question — 4番〜7番

女の人の最も言いたいことは何ですか。Terebi de onna no hito ga hanashite imasu. / Onna no hito no mottomo iitai koto wa nan desu ka.

EN: A woman is speaking on TV. / What does she most want to say?
HI: टीवी पर एक औरत बोल रही है। / वह सबसे ज़्यादा क्या कहना चाहती है?
GU: ટીવી પર એક સ્ત્રી બોલે છે. / તે સૌથી વધુ શું કહેવા માગે છે?
Talk summary (not the book's text)

EN: A reporter in Yubari, Hokkaido — a city about ¥40 billion in the red and effectively bankrupt — explains that from next year it will raise taxes and start charging for rubbish collection. It will also send out snow-ploughs only after 15 cm of snow instead of 10. Only 5 cm, but the real impact is serious: wind piles snow into drifts up to 60 cm, and residents may be unable even to get to hospital. Answer 1 (book 正解, p.140). Why: the book glosses 上司と衝突しちゃいまして as "things stopped going well with my boss" — option 1 is that paraphrase. (added, not in book) 2 takes 衝突 ("collision") literally; his health problems were from stress, not an injury. 3 is advice that kept him at the bank, the opposite of the trigger. 4 confuses who quit: it was he who quit, after his wife said 辞めてもいいよ.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Where this unit's wrong options come from (added, not in book):

辞めてもいいよ (wife says) vs 妻が辞めたyamete mo ii yo / tsuma ga yameta "you can quit" vs the wife quit The speaker of a line is not its subject. HI: किसने कहा ≠ किसने किया · GU: કોણે કહ્યું ≠ કોણે કર્યું 7番
Exam trap: 6番 asks for the option that is different (違うもの). Because nothing is printed, it is easy to forget the "not" by the time the options are read. Write a small ✕ on your sheet as soon as you hear 違う, then tick each option you can match to a rule; the one left unticked is the answer. (added, not in book)
