1 Reading Strategy

Key idea

これまで学んだ読解のコツを総動員しよう！Kore made mananda dokkai no kotsu o soudouin shiyou!

Today's 実戦問題 (test day) doesn't introduce a new strategy — it's a graded, timed test mixing all six of this week's reading tactics. Quick recap before you start:

  • 1日目: Find the exact wording (or a close synonym) in the passage that answers the question directly.
  • 2日目: Locate the passage's conclusion and work out its shape — conclusion-first, conclusion-last, or conclusion/example/confirmation.
  • 3日目: For an underlined phrase, substitute each option into its place and check which one reads naturally in context.
  • 4日目: When a passage explains something through a metaphor, remember the real topic is the thing being illustrated, not the comparison itself.
  • 5日目: Strip a long sentence's modifiers away to see its bare subject-and-verb skeleton before answering.
  • 6日目: If a sentence looks incomplete, reconstruct the missing words from a nearby (usually preceding) sentence.

Q1

Passage (1)

油を塗った布に水を一滴注1たらす注2と、水は玉になって布の上にのっかります注3。液体には、なるべく表面積を小さくしようとする表面張力があるからです。Abura o nutta nuno ni mizu o itteki tarasu to, mizu wa tama ni natte nuno no ue ni nokkarimasu. Ekitai ni wa, narubeku hyoumenseki o chiisaku shiyou to suru hyoumen chouryoku ga aru kara desu.

ところが、これに洗剤をたらすと、玉が崩れて布の表面がぬれてしまいます。このように、表面張力をなくす働きをするものを界面活性剤というのです。Tokoro ga, kore ni senzai o tarasu to, tama ga kuzurete nuno no hyoumen ga nurete shimaimasu. Kono you ni, hyoumen chouryoku o nakusu hataraki o suru mono o kaimen kassei zai to iu no desu.

洗濯するためには、まず洗うものをよくぬらして、繊維のすみずみ注4まで洗剤をしみこませなくてはなりません。そのとき、洗剤の界面活性力が必要とされるわけです。Sentaku suru tame ni wa, mazu arau mono o yoku nurashite, sen'i no sumizumi made senzai o shimikomasenakute wa narimasen. Sono toki, senzai no kaimen kassei-ryoku ga hitsuyou to sareru wake desu.

（竹内均『頭にやさしい雑学読本 ちょっと意外ないい話』同文書院）(Takeuchi Hitoshi, "Atama ni Yasashii Zatsugaku Dokuhon: Chotto Igai na Ii Hanashi," Doubun Shoin)　·　注1 一滴：液体のひとしずく　注2 たらす：液体などを落とす　注3 のっかる：乗る　注4 すみずみ：すみからすみまで全部

1　ここで言う「界面活性剤」とは何か。1: Koko de iu "kaimen kassei zai" to wa nani ka.

EN: If you drip a single drop of water onto oiled cloth, the water beads up and sits on top of the cloth. That's because liquids have surface tension, which acts to minimize surface area as much as possible. However, if you drip detergent onto it, the bead collapses and the cloth's surface gets wet. Something that acts to eliminate surface tension like this is called a surfactant. To do laundry, you first have to thoroughly wet what you're washing, letting detergent soak into every nook and cranny of the fibers — and that's exactly when the detergent's surfactant power is needed.

Passage (2)

Ganrai, jouhouryou ga fueru ni tsurete taisetsu ni natte kuru no wa, hitsuyou na jouhou o seikaku ni hayaku aite ni tsutaeru to iu koto de aru. 情報量が増えるから、確率的にいっても、そういうことが生じるのはしかたがないという言訳はおかしい。あいまいさをなくす努力をしないで、情報化などというのはナンセンスだからである。Jouhouryou ga fueru kara, kakuritsu-teki ni itte mo, sou iu koto ga shoujiru no wa shikata ga nai to iu iiwake wa okashii. Aimaisa o nakusu doryoku o shinaide, jouhouka nado to iu no wa nansensu da kara de aru.

（菊池誠『学び心、遊び心』三笠書房）(Kikuchi Makoto, "Manabigokoro, Asobigokoro," Mikasa Shobou)

2　下線の部分はどういう意味か。全文を読んで考えなさい。2: Kasen no bubun wa dou iu imi ka. Zenbun o yonde kangaenasai.

EN: The phrase "information society" is heard more and more, and extremely difficult debates about it get exchanged, yet at a considerably lower level, I keep noticing phenomena that don't fit the idea of "information-ization" at all. Originally, as the volume of information grows, what matters most is conveying necessary information accurately and quickly — something that's obvious without any difficult theorizing at all. Even so, in our daily lives, we're often left feeling annoyed or struggling with something simply because it's ambiguous. It's a poor excuse to say that, statistically, this is just bound to happen because there's more information now — because it would be nonsense to call something "information-ization" without even trying to eliminate ambiguity.

Why: The underlined phrase points at a mismatch between sophisticated talk about "information society" and the mundane ambiguity people actually deal with daily. Option 4 restates exactly the excuse the author explicitly rejects as "おかしい" (a poor excuse) — a clear trap.
