1 Reading Strategy

Key idea

質問の言葉をそのまま文章の中に探そう！Shitsumon no kotoba o sono mama bunshou no naka ni sagasou!

Week 3 opens with the week's core idea: the answer to almost every question is sitting right there in the passage — you just have to find it. This book's diagram (its explanatory text is in Chinese in this edition) shows the process:

  • 1. Look at the question itself. A question like 「○○とは何か」(What is ○○?), 「△△とは何のことか」(What does △△ refer to?) or 「××はどんな場合に起こるか」(When does ×× happen?) tells you exactly which word or phrase to go hunting for in the passage.
    HI: प्रश्न में मौजूद ○○, △△ या ×× जैसे शब्द ही बताते हैं कि पाठ में क्या ढूँढना है। GU: પ્રશ્નમાં રહેલા ○○, △△ કે ×× જેવા શબ્દો જ જણાવે છે કે લખાણમાં શું શોધવાનું છે.
  • 2. Scan the passage for that exact phrase. Definitions are almost always signalled by set patterns: 「…を○○という」(...is called ○○), 「△△とは…のことである」(△△ means ...), 「××は…ときに起こる」(×× occurs when...). Find the sentence built on one of these patterns and the answer is usually sitting right inside it.
    HI: परिभाषाएँ अक्सर तय पैटर्न में लिखी होती हैं — जैसे "...को ○○ कहा जाता है" — उसी वाक्य में जवाब मिल जाता है। GU: વ્યાખ્યાઓ ઘણી વાર નિશ્ચિત ઢાંચામાં લખાયેલી હોય છે — જેમ કે "...ને ○○ કહેવાય છે" — એ જ વાક્યમાં જવાબ મળી જાય છે.
  • 3. If the exact words aren't there, look for a synonym. Sometimes the passage doesn't repeat the question's exact wording — it uses an equivalent phrase instead. As the book's mascot cats put it: "そんな言葉はどこにもないよ" (that exact wording isn't anywhere) / "ないときは、同じような意味の言葉を探すんだ" (when it's not there, go looking for words with a similar meaning).
    HI: कभी-कभी वही शब्द नहीं मिलते — तब वैसे ही अर्थ वाले दूसरे शब्द ढूँढें। GU: ક્યારેક એ જ શબ્દો ન મળે — ત્યારે એવા જ અર્થવાળા બીજા શબ્દો શોધો.

Q1

Passage

得意な芸を「十八番」とか「おはこ」と言うが、この語源は歌舞伎にあるらしい。ある歌舞伎役者が自分の得意な芸『歌舞伎十八番』の台本を「箱」に入れて大事にしまっていたからだという*。Tokui na gei o "juuhachiban" toka "ohako" to iu ga, kono gogen wa kabuki ni aru rashii. Aru kabuki yakusha ga jibun no tokui na gei "Kabuki Juuhachiban" no daihon o "hako" ni irete daiji ni shimatte ita kara da to iu.

問い　得意な芸のことを何と言うか。Toi: Tokui na gei no koto o nan to iu ka.

* footnote translation: "It is said that a Kabuki actor used to store the script of his most popular performance 'Kabuki No.18' in his box."

EN: A person's specialty/signature skill is called "juuhachiban" or "ohako" — the term's origin apparently lies in kabuki. It's said that a certain kabuki actor kept the script for his own signature performance, "Kabuki Juuhachiban," carefully stored in a "box" (hako).
HI: किसी की सबसे निपुण कला को "जूहाचिबन" या "ओहाको" कहा जाता है — इस शब्द की उत्पत्ति काबुकी नाटक से मानी जाती है। कहा जाता है कि एक काबुकी अभिनेता अपनी सबसे प्रसिद्ध प्रस्तुति "काबुकी जूहाचिबन" की पटकथा को एक "बक्से (हाको)" में सहेज कर रखता था।
GU: કોઈની સૌથી નિપુણ કલાને "જૂહાચિબાન" અથવા "ઓહાકો" કહેવામાં આવે છે — આ શબ્દની ઉત્પત્તિ કાબુકી નાટકમાંથી મનાય છે. કહેવાય છે કે એક કાબુકી અભિનેતા પોતાની સૌથી પ્રખ્યાત રજૂઆત "કાબુકી જૂહાચિબાન"ની સ્ક્રિપ્ટને એક "બોક્સ (હાકો)"માં સાચવીને રાખતો હતો.

Option 2 names the specific kabuki play and the box mentioned only in the origin-story explanation that follows — it's the trivia behind the word, not the everyday term the question is actually asking about.

Q2

Passage

アユの成魚はコケを食べるのでえさでは釣れない。そこで、友釣りという日本独自の方法が生まれた。生きたおとり注のアユを使っておびき寄せ、仕掛けておいた針で引っかける**、というものだ。「友釣り」と書くと、アユがおとりを友達だと思って近づいてくるかのようにも思えるが、自分の縄張を荒らす者を追い払おうと近づいてくるのである***。Ayu no seigyo wa koke o taberu no de esa de wa tsurenai. Soko de, tomozuri to iu Nihon dokuji no houhou ga umareta. Ikita otori (chuu) no ayu o tsukatte obikiyose, shikakete oita hari de hikkakeru, to iu mono da. "Tomozuri" to kaku to, ayu ga otori o tomodachi da to omotte chikazuite kuru ka no you ni mo omoeru ga, jibun no nawabari o arasu mono o oiharaou to chikazuite kuru no de aru.

注 おとり：何かを捕まえるとき、誘うために使うもの Chuu: otori: nanika o tsukamaeru toki, sasou tame ni tsukau mono — "decoy: something used to lure [a target] when trying to catch something"　·　** footnote: "they lure ayu (river fish) by placing a live ayu on a fish hook"　·　*** footnote: "they come closer to chase away fish which invade their territory"

問い　「友釣り」とは、どういう釣り方か。Toi: "Tomozuri" to wa, dou iu tsurikata ka.

EN: Adult ayu (sweetfish) eat moss/algae, so they can't be caught with bait. That's how the uniquely Japanese method called "tomozuri" (friend-fishing) came about: using a live decoy ayu to lure another one close, then hooking it with a pre-set hook. Written as "friend-fishing," it might seem to suggest the ayu approaches the decoy because it thinks it's a friend — but in fact it approaches trying to drive away an intruder into its own territory.
HI: वयस्क आयु (एक मछली) काई खाती है, इसलिए उसे चारे से नहीं पकड़ा जा सकता। इसी वजह से जापान की खास मछली पकड़ने की विधि "तोमोज़ुरी" (मित्र-मछली पकड़ना) बनी: एक जीवित आयु को प्रलोभन के रूप में इस्तेमाल करके दूसरी आयु को पास बुलाया जाता है, फिर पहले से लगे काँटे से उसे पकड़ लिया जाता है। "मित्र-मछली पकड़ना" लिखने से लगता है कि आयु चारे को दोस्त समझकर पास आती है — लेकिन असल में वह अपने क्षेत्र में घुसपैठ करने वाले को भगाने आती है।
GU: પુખ્ત આયુ (એક માછલી) શેવાળ ખાય છે, એટલે તેને ચારાથી પકડી શકાતી નથી. એટલે જ જાપાનની આગવી માછલી પકડવાની પદ્ધતિ "તોમોઝુરી" (મિત્ર-માછીમારી) અસ્તિત્વમાં આવી: જીવતી આયુને પ્રલોભન તરીકે વાપરીને બીજી આયુને નજીક બોલાવવામાં આવે છે, પછી પહેલેથી ગોઠવેલા હૂકથી તેને પકડી લેવામાં આવે છે. "મિત્ર-માછીમારી" લખવાથી લાગે કે આયુ પ્રલોભનને મિત્ર માનીને નજીક આવે છે — પણ ખરેખર તે પોતાના વિસ્તારમાં ઘૂસનારને ભગાડવા નજીક આવે છે.

Q3

Passage

ゴロ寝ということばがある。そこから来たテレ寝という新語もある。要するに、サラリーマンが日曜日に、畳の上にごろりと寝そべり、片ひじついてテレビなど見ている図である*。金のない日本人の平均的安楽の図、といえよう。この場合も、仰向いていては天井が見えるだけで、やはりテレ寝にはなるまい。テレビ視聴という最低限の対社会的姿勢のためには、せめて片ひじつくくらいの努力は要るのである。Gorone to iu kotoba ga aru. Soko kara kita terene to iu shingo mo aru. You suru ni, sarariiman ga nichiyoubi ni, tatami no ue ni gorori to nesobari, kata hiji tsuite terebi nado mite iru zu de aru. Kane no nai nihonjin no heikinteki anraku no zu, to ieyou. Kono baai mo, aomuite ite wa tenjou ga mieru dake de, yahari terene ni wa naru mai. Terebi shichou to iu saiteigen no tai-shakaiteki shisei no tame ni wa, semete kata hiji tsuku kurai no doryoku wa iru no de aru.

(多田道太郎『しぐさの日本文化』角川書店) (Tada Michitarou, "Shigusa no Nihon Bunka," Kadokawa Shoten)　·　* footnote: "it is a description of a businessman lying down on tatami resting his head on one elbow and watching TV"

問い　「ゴロ寝」とは何か。Toi: "Gorone" to wa nani ka.

EN: There's a word "gorone" (sprawling out lazily). From it comes a new coinage, "terene" (TV-sprawling). In short, it's the picture of a salaryman on a Sunday, flopped down on the tatami, propped up on one elbow, watching TV or the like — call it the average relaxation scene of a Japanese person without much money. Here too, if you just lie flat on your back, all you'd see is the ceiling — that still wouldn't count as "terene." Even the bare minimum of social decency involved in watching TV takes at least the effort of propping yourself up on one elbow.
HI: "गोरोने" (आलस से पसर कर लेटना) नाम का एक शब्द है। इससे बना एक नया शब्द है "तेरेने" (टीवी देखते हुए पसरना)। संक्षेप में, यह एक नौकरीपेशा व्यक्ति की तस्वीर है जो रविवार को तातामी पर लेट कर, एक कोहनी टिकाकर टीवी वगैरह देख रहा है — इसे बिना पैसे वाले औसत जापानी व्यक्ति के आराम की तस्वीर भी कह सकते हैं। यहाँ भी, अगर कोई सीधा पीठ के बल लेट जाए तो सिर्फ छत ही दिखेगी, और उसे "तेरेने" नहीं कहा जाएगा। टीवी देखने की न्यूनतम सामाजिक शालीनता के लिए भी कम से कम एक कोहनी टिकाने जितनी मेहनत तो चाहिए ही।
GU: "ગોરોને" (આળસથી પસારાઈને સૂવું) નામનો એક શબ્દ છે. તેમાંથી બનેલો નવો શબ્દ છે "તેરેને" (ટીવી જોતાં પસારાવું). ટૂંકમાં, આ એક નોકરિયાત વ્યક્તિનું ચિત્ર છે જે રવિવારે તાતામી પર સૂઈને, એક કોણી ટેકવીને ટીવી વગેરે જુએ છે — તેને પૈસા વગરના સામાન્ય જાપાની વ્યક્તિના આરામના ચિત્ર તરીકે પણ કહી શકાય. અહીં પણ, જો કોઈ સીધું પીઠ પર સૂઈ જાય તો ફક્ત છત જ દેખાશે, અને તેને "તેરેને" નહીં કહેવાય. ટીવી જોવાના ન્યૂનતમ સામાજિક શિષ્ટાચાર માટે પણ ઓછામાં ઓછી એક કોણી ટેકવવા જેટલી મહેનત તો જોઈએ જ.

Why (reasoned, not from an official key): Unlike the other questions today, the book never spells out a one-line "ゴロ寝とは…である" definition — it only fully defines the derived word 「テレ寝」via the 要するに sentence. Reasoning it out: 「そこから来たテレ寝」("terene," which came FROM gorone) tells us gorone is the broader, plainer concept, and terene narrows it down specifically to "sprawling while watching TV." The next line confirms this — lying flat on your back doesn't count as terene specifically because you can't watch TV that way ("この場合も…やはりテレ寝にはなるまい"). Stripping away that TV-watching-posture requirement (which is what makes it specifically terene) leaves the shared core both words describe: sprawling out and watching something. Option 3 captures that core. Option 1 is circular/backwards, option 2 wrongly treats the illustrative example (a salaryman, on a Sunday) as the definition itself, and option 4 is just one physical detail, not a definition.

Passage

駿河湾や富山湾のように、海底の地形がけわしくて、深海が海岸にせまっている海では、深海魚が岸近く、しかも海面近くでとれる。少なくとも、一年のうちのある季節には、海の底から湧いてくるみたいに、毎晩のように、深海魚の群れが海の表面に上がってくる。深海魚というのは、一般には「海の深いところに棲んでいる魚」だと思われている。それも間違いではないが、少し言葉が足りない。あるいは、少し誤解されている。「深い海に棲む魚」を深海魚というのであって、棲んでいる深さ範囲はさまざま、と考えた方が正しい**。Surugawan ya Toyamawan no you ni, kaitei no chikei ga kewashikute, shinkai ga kaigan ni sematte iru umi de wa, shinkaigyo ga kishi chikaku, shikamo kaimen chikaku de toreru. Sukunaku tomo, ichinen no uchi no aru kisetsu ni wa, umi no soko kara waite kuru mitai ni, maiban no you ni, shinkaigyo no mure ga umi no hyoumen ni agatte kuru. Shinkaigyo to iu no wa, ippanteki ni wa "umi no fukai tokoro ni sunde iru sakana" da to omowarete iru. Sore mo machigai de wa nai ga, sukoshi kotoba ga tarinai. Aruiwa, sukoshi gokai sarete iru. "Fukai umi ni sumu sakana" o shinkaigyo to iu no de atte, sunde iru fukasa han'i wa samazama, to kangaeta hou ga tadashii.

(鈴木克美『魚は夢を見ているか』丸善) (Suzuki Katsumi, "Sakana wa Yume o Mite Iru ka," Maruzen)　·　** footnote: "It is correct to think that deep-sea fish are 'fish living in the deep sea' and to realize that the depths at which they live vary."

問い　筆者の考えでは、「深海魚」とはどういう魚のことか。Toi: Hissha no kangae de wa, "shinkaigyo" to wa dou iu sakana no koto ka.

EN: In seas like Suruga Bay or Toyama Bay, where the seafloor terrain is steep and the deep sea presses close to shore, deep-sea fish can be caught near the coast, even near the surface. At least during certain seasons, deep-sea fish gather in schools and rise to the surface almost every night, as if welling up from the ocean floor. "Deep-sea fish" is generally thought to mean "fish that live in the deep parts of the sea." That's not wrong exactly, but it's a bit imprecise — or rather, slightly misunderstood. It's more accurate to think of "deep-sea fish" as meaning "fish that live in a deep sea," understanding that the range of depths they actually inhabit varies widely.
