1 Reading Strategy

Key idea

たとえ話が伝えたい本当の意味を考えよう！Tatoebanashi ga tsutaetai hontou no imi o kangaeyou!

Day 3 was about paraphrasing a literal underlined phrase; Day 4 tackles the same "what are they really saying?" question when the passage answers it through a metaphor or analogy instead. This book's sentence-pattern boxes (explained in Chinese in this edition) show the two shapes an analogy usually takes:

  • 1. ○○は<analogy>[に似ている／のようなものだ／みたいだ／に対応する]。 "○○ resembles/is like/corresponds to <analogy>." The real topic (○○) comes first, and the analogy is offered to help you picture it.
    HI: "○○, <तुलना> जैसा है / जैसी चीज़ है / से मेल खाता है" — असली विषय पहले आता है, तुलना उसे समझाने के लिए दी जाती है। GU: "○○, <સરખામણી> જેવું છે / જેવી વસ્તુ છે / સાથે મેળ ખાય છે" — ખરો વિષય પહેલાં આવે છે, સરખામણી તેને સમજાવવા અપાય છે.
  • 2. <analogy>は…です。これは○○[にも当てはまる／にも言える／も同じだ]。 "<Analogy> is... This also applies to/can be said of/is the same for ○○." Here the analogy is explained first, and only afterward is it explicitly mapped onto the real topic (○○).
    HI: "<तुलना> ...है। यह ○○ पर भी लागू होता है / ○○ के लिए भी वैसा ही है" — पहले तुलना समझाई जाती है, फिर असली विषय पर लागू की जाती है। GU: "<સરખામણી> ...છે. આ ○○ પર પણ લાગુ પડે છે / ○○ માટે પણ એવું જ છે" — પહેલાં સરખામણી સમજાવાય છે, પછી ખરા વિષય પર લાગુ કરાય છે.
  • 3. Don't mistake the vehicle for the topic. When a passage explains something through a metaphor, the actual point being made is about the real subject — not about the thing used for comparison. The book's own cat-mascot example: 「ぼくの気持ちは今日の天気みたいだ……」("My feelings are like today's weather...") — the sentence is about the speaker's mood, using weather only as the illustration, not the other way around.
    HI: जब कोई अंश किसी बात को उपमा के ज़रिए समझाए, तो असली मुद्दा उस विषय के बारे में होता है जिसे समझाया जा रहा है — तुलना की गई वस्तु के बारे में नहीं। GU: જ્યારે કોઈ ફકરો કોઈ વાતને ઉપમા દ્વારા સમજાવે, ત્યારે ખરો મુદ્દો એ વિષય વિશે હોય છે જેને સમજાવાઈ રહ્યો છે — સરખામણી માટે વપરાયેલી વસ્તુ વિશે નહીં.

Q1

Passage

人生は一箱のマッチに似ている。重大に扱うのはばかばかしい。重大に扱わなければ危険である。Jinsei wa hitohako no matchi ni nite iru. Juudai ni atsukau no wa bakabakashii. Juudai ni atsukawanakereba kiken de aru.

（芥川龍之介『侏儒の言葉』）(Akutagawa Ryuunosuke, "Shuju no Kotoba")

問い　筆者の言いたいことはどちらか。Toi: Hissha no iitai koto wa dochira ka.

EN: Life is like a box of matches. It's absurd to treat it as a grave, weighty matter. But if you don't treat it seriously at all, it's dangerous.
HI: जीवन एक माचिस की डिब्बी जैसा है। इसे बहुत गंभीरता से लेना हास्यास्पद है। पर अगर इसे बिल्कुल भी गंभीरता से न लिया जाए, तो यह ख़तरनाक है।
GU: જીવન એક માચીસની ડબ્બી જેવું છે. તેને ખૂબ ગંભીરતાથી લેવું હાસ્યાસ્પદ છે. પણ જો તેને બિલકુલ ગંભીરતાથી ન લેવાય, તો તે ખતરનાક છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
1人生は大したものではないが、軽く見るととんでもないことになる。Jinsei wa taishita mono de wa nai ga, karuku miru to tondemonai koto ni naru.Life isn't a big deal, but if you take it lightly, terrible things happen.जीवन कोई बड़ी बात नहीं है, पर इसे हल्के में लेने पर भयंकर परिणाम हो सकते हैं।જીવન કંઈ મોટી વાત નથી, પણ તેને હલકામાં લેવાથી ભયંકર પરિણામ આવી શકે.
2マッチは人生と同じように危険なので、取り扱いに注意すべきだ。Matchi wa jinsei to onaji you ni kiken na node, toriatsukai ni chuui subeki da.Matches are as dangerous as life, so you should handle them with care.माचिस जीवन जितनी ही ख़तरनाक है, इसलिए इसे संभालने में सावधानी बरतनी चाहिए।માચીસ જીવન જેટલી જ ખતરનાક છે, એટલે તેને સંભાળવામાં સાવચેતી રાખવી જોઈએ.

Why: The metaphor "life is like a box of matches" is Pattern 1 (○○は<analogy>に似ている) — life (○○) is the real topic, matches are just the illustration. The point: treating life as a grand, weighty thing is absurd/silly (life isn't a big deal), but treating it too carelessly is dangerous (be careless and terrible things happen) — matching option 1 exactly. Option 2 mistakes the vehicle (matches) for the topic, turning the sentence into literal fire-safety advice, which misses the point entirely.

Q2

Passage

(laughter) (omitted) "Citric acid is a vitalizer. When you want more energy, or you're really exhausted, it's good to eat fruit that's rich in citric acid."

(હાસ્ય) (છોડેલો ભાગ) "સાઇટ્રિક એસિડ એક ઊર્જાવર્ધક છે. જ્યારે વધુ ઊર્જા જોઈતી હોય, અથવા ખૂબ થાક લાગ્યો હોય, ત્યારે સાઇટ્રિક એસિડથી ભરપૂર ફળ ખાવું સારું રહે છે."
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
1漫画家の仕事はとても疲れるので、クエン酸たっぷりの果物を食べたほうがいい。Mangaka no shigoto wa totemo tsukareru node, kuen san tappuri no kudamono o tabeta hou ga ii.A manga artist's job is very tiring, so it's good to eat fruit rich in citric acid.मांगा कलाकार का काम बहुत थका देने वाला होता है, इसलिए साइट्रिक एसिड वाले फल खाना अच्छा है।મંગા કલાકારનું કામ ખૂબ થકવનારું છે, એટલે સાઇટ્રિક એસિડવાળા ફળ ખાવા સારા છે.
2編集者には、漫画家の仕事をすみやかに進行させるという重要な役割がある。Henshuusha ni wa, mangaka no shigoto o sumiyaka ni shinkou saseru to iu juuyou na yakuwari ga aru.Editors have the important role of speeding along a manga artist's work.संपादक की एक महत्वपूर्ण भूमिका होती है — मांगा कलाकार के काम को तेज़ी से आगे बढ़ाना।સંપાદકની એક મહત્વની ભૂમિકા છે — મંગા કલાકારના કામને ઝડપથી આગળ વધારવું.
3クエン酸はエネルギー代謝を進行させる活力剤である。Kuen san wa enerugii taisha o shinkou saseru katsuryokuzai de aru.Citric acid is a vitalizer that promotes energy metabolism.साइट्रिक एसिड एक ऊर्जावर्धक है जो ऊर्जा उपापचय को आगे बढ़ाता है।સાઇટ્રિક એસિડ એક ઊર્જાવર્ધક છે જે ઊર્જા ચયાપચયને આગળ વધારે છે.

Why: The analogy here is Pattern 2 in reverse order of emphasis — "citric acid is like an editor who makes a lazy manga artist work" — but the real topic being explained the whole time is citric acid itself, not editors or manga artists. Options 1 and 2 both mistake the vehicle of the metaphor (manga artists, editors) for the actual subject. Option 3 correctly identifies that the passage is really about what citric acid does: it's a vitalizer that speeds up energy metabolism (the editor comparison was only there to make that function easy to picture).

Q3

Passage

コンピュータには記憶というものがある。記憶は、RAMというLSI注に記憶されており、電源を切ると忘れてしまう。人間が死ぬと、記憶も消滅してしまうことに対応するだろう。コンピュータの記憶を維持するには、つねに電気をいれておく必要がある。これは人間が食物をとって生きていることに対応している。またディスクという磁気記憶装置があり、これに記録されたことは電源を落としても、忘れられない。これなどは、人間の書く本にたとえることができるであろう。Konpyuuta ni wa kioku to iu mono ga aru. Kioku wa, RAM to iu LSI ni kioku sarete ori, dengen o kiru to wasurete shimau. Ningen ga shinu to, kioku mo shoumetsu shite shimau koto ni taiou suru darou. Konpyuuta no kioku o iji suru ni wa, tsuneni denki o irete oku hitsuyou ga aru. Kore wa ningen ga shokumotsu o totte ikite iru koto ni taiou shite iru. Mata disuku to iu jiki kioku souchi ga ari, kore ni kiroku sareta koto wa dengen o otoshite mo, wasurerarenai. Kore nado wa, ningen no kaku hon ni tatoeru koto ga dekiru de arou.

（松田卓也・二間瀬敏史『時間の本質をさぐる』講談社）(Matsuda Takuya, Nimase Toshifumi, "Jikan no Honshitsu o Saguru," Koudansha)　·　注 LSI: Large scale integration、大規模（高密度）集積回路 Chuu: LSI: daikibo (koumitsudo) shuuseki kairo — "LSI: large-scale (high-density) integrated circuit"

問い　この文章の内容と合うのはどちらか。Toi: Kono bunshou no naiyou to au no wa dochira ka.

EN: Computers have something called memory. Memory is stored in an LSI chip called RAM, and it's forgotten when the power is turned off — probably corresponding to how, when a human dies, their memory also disappears. To maintain a computer's memory, power has to be kept on constantly — corresponding to how humans stay alive by eating food. There's also a magnetic storage device called a disk; what's recorded on it isn't forgotten even after the power is switched off. This could perhaps be compared to a book that a human writes.
HI: कंप्यूटर में मेमोरी नाम की एक चीज़ होती है। मेमोरी, RAM नामक LSI चिप में संचित होती है, और बिजली बंद करने पर भुला दी जाती है — शायद यह इससे मेल खाता है कि इंसान के मरने पर उसकी याददाश्त भी मिट जाती है। कंप्यूटर की मेमोरी बनाए रखने के लिए, बिजली लगातार चालू रखनी पड़ती है — यह इंसान के भोजन करके जीवित रहने से मेल खाता है। डिस्क नाम का एक चुंबकीय भंडारण यंत्र भी होता है; उस पर दर्ज चीज़ बिजली बंद करने पर भी नहीं भूलती। शायद इसकी तुलना इंसान द्वारा लिखी गई किताब से की जा सकती है।
GU: કમ્પ્યુટરમાં મેમરી નામની એક વસ્તુ હોય છે. મેમરી, RAM નામની LSI ચિપમાં સંગ્રહાય છે, અને વીજળી બંધ કરવાથી ભુલાઈ જાય છે — કદાચ આ એ વાત સાથે મેળ ખાય છે કે માણસ મરે ત્યારે તેની યાદશક્તિ પણ ભૂંસાઈ જાય છે. કમ્પ્યુટરની મેમરી જાળવવા માટે, વીજળી સતત ચાલુ રાખવી પડે છે — આ માણસ ખોરાક લઈને જીવતો રહે છે તેની સાથે મેળ ખાય છે. ડિસ્ક નામનું એક ચુંબકીય સંગ્રહ ઉપકરણ પણ હોય છે; તેના પર નોંધાયેલી વસ્તુ વીજળી બંધ કરવા છતાં ભુલાતી નથી. કદાચ આની સરખામણી માણસ દ્વારા લખાયેલા પુસ્તક સાથે કરી શકાય.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
1人間は死ぬと何も残らないが、コンピュータは記憶を保存できる。Ningen wa shinu to nani mo nokoranai ga, konpyuuta wa kioku o hozon dekiru.When a human dies nothing remains, but a computer can preserve memory.इंसान के मरने पर कुछ नहीं बचता, पर कंप्यूटर मेमोरी सहेज सकता है।માણસ મરે ત્યારે કંઈ બચતું નથી, પણ કમ્પ્યુટર મેમરી સાચવી શકે છે.
2コンピュータも人間と似たようなやり方で記憶を維持したり残したりしている。Konpyuuta mo ningen to nita you na yarikata de kioku o iji shitari nokoshitari shite iru.Computers also maintain and preserve memory in ways similar to humans.कंप्यूटर भी इंसानों जैसे तरीक़ों से मेमोरी बनाए रखता और सहेजता है।કમ્પ્યુટર પણ માણસો જેવી રીતોથી મેમરી જાળવે છે અને સાચવે છે.

Why: This passage is Pattern 1 run three times in a row: RAM forgetting on power-off is compared to human memory disappearing at death; keeping the power on is compared to humans eating to stay alive; the permanent disk is compared to a book a human writes. Every comparison points the same direction — computer memory processes work in ways parallel to human memory, not opposite to it — matching option 2. Option 1 is a one-sided distortion: it ignores the disk/book comparison, which shows computers (like humans, via books) also have a way of making things permanent.

問い　（　　）には、ある文が続く。それはどれか、全文を読んで考えなさい。Toi: (___) ni wa, aru bun ga tsuzuku. Sore wa dore ka, zenbun o yonde kangaenasai.

EN: An attitude of scoring your real-life husband against the standard of an "ideal husband" is, I think, quite far removed from the real, unmanageable messiness of actual men and women — and it works to damage the relationship. Trying to grasp your husband through analysis and verification is a foolish thing to do. If you watch a movie determined to criticize it and find its flaws, most movies end up feeling flat and unlikeable — but if you watch it throwing yourself into it emotionally, you can get swept up in its fictional world, and the film's good qualities come into view. I think that's even more true when it comes to human relationships. ( ___ )

Why: The analogy runs: watching a movie critically (looking for flaws) makes it feel flat and unlikeable, while watching with emotional immersion lets its good qualities come through. The author then says "それが、人間の関係だったら、もっとそうだろう" — if that were true of human relationships, it'd be even more so. The missing sentence has to extend that same logic to relationships specifically. Option 3 does exactly that: showing warmth/goodwill (the relationship equivalent of "immersing yourself") causes the other person to open up in turn (the relationship equivalent of "the film's good qualities coming into view"). Option 1 is an unrelated tangent about couples fading into background presence. Option 2 just restates the movie point itself without extending it to relationships as promised. Option 4 contradicts the passage's overall warm, encouraging tone.
