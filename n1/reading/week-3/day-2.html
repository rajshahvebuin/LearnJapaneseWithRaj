1 Reading Strategy

Key idea

結論の位置を見極めよう！Ketsuron no ichi o mikiwameyou!

Once you can find the conclusion (結論) of a short passage, most questions answer themselves. This book's diagram (explained in Chinese in this edition) lays out three shapes a passage can take:

  • Pattern I — Conclusion → Example/explanation. The passage opens by stating its point, then spends the rest of the passage backing it up with examples or explanation.
    HI: पहले निष्कर्ष (मुख्य बात) बताई जाती है, फिर उदाहरण या स्पष्टीकरण दिया जाता है। GU: પહેલાં નિષ્કર્ષ (મુખ્ય વાત) જણાવાય છે, પછી ઉદાહરણ કે સ્પષ્ટતા આપવામાં આવે છે.
  • Pattern II — Example/explanation → Conclusion. The passage builds up examples or explanation first, and only states its actual point at the end.
    HI: पहले उदाहरण या स्पष्टीकरण दिया जाता है, और मुख्य बात (निष्कर्ष) सबसे अंत में आती है। GU: પહેલાં ઉદાહરણ કે સ્પષ્ટતા આપવામાં આવે છે, અને મુખ્ય વાત (નિષ્કર્ષ) સૌથી છેલ્લે આવે છે.
  • Pattern III — Conclusion → Example/explanation → Confirmation. The passage states its point, backs it up with an example, then restates/re-emphasizes the same conclusion once more at the end.
    HI: पहले निष्कर्ष, फिर उदाहरण, और अंत में उसी निष्कर्ष को दोबारा ज़ोर देकर दोहराया जाता है। GU: પહેલાં નિષ્કર્ષ, પછી ઉદાહરણ, અને છેલ્લે એ જ નિષ્કર્ષને ફરી ભાર દઈને દોહરાવવામાં આવે છે.
  • Watch for the signal words. In patterns II and III, the conclusion is very often introduced by words like 要するに (in short), つまり (in other words) or 結局 (in the end/ultimately) — these words are your flag that the real point is arriving now.
    HI: "要するに", "つまり", "結局" जैसे शब्द अक्सर असली निष्कर्ष से ठीक पहले आते हैं। GU: "要するに", "つまり", "結局" જેવા શબ્દો ઘણી વાર ખરા નિષ્કર્ષ પહેલાં જ આવે છે.

A word of caution from the book's mascot cats: 「結論が書いてある場所がわかっても、書いてある内容がわかんないんだよね……」— "Even once you know WHERE the conclusion is written, you still have to actually understand WHAT it says." Finding the sentence is only step one.

Q1

Passage

雨が降れば創作に没頭していたし、晴れれば自転車でどこまでも気の向くまま出かけていった。要するに、予定というものとは無縁の生活*だったのだ。Ame ga fureba sousaku ni bottou shite ita shi, hareba jitensha de dokomade mo ki no muku mama dekakete itta. You suru ni, yotei to iu mono to wa muen no seikatsu datta no da.

問い　筆者はどんな生活をしていたか。Toi: Hissha wa donna seikatsu o shite ita ka.

* footnote translation: "my life was free and without schedules"

EN: When it rained, I'd throw myself into creative work; when it was sunny, I'd head out on my bicycle wherever the mood took me. In short, it was a life with no connection to anything like a schedule.
HI: बारिश होती तो मैं रचनात्मक काम में डूब जाता; धूप निकलती तो साइकिल पर जहाँ मन करे वहाँ निकल जाता। संक्षेप में कहें तो, यह किसी भी तय कार्यक्रम से बिल्कुल अनजान जिंदगी थी।
GU: વરસાદ પડે તો હું સર્જનાત્મક કામમાં ડૂબી જતો; તડકો હોય તો સાયકલ પર જ્યાં મન થાય ત્યાં નીકળી પડતો. ટૂંકમાં, આ કોઈપણ નિશ્ચિત કાર્યક્રમ સાથે કંઈ સંબંધ ન હોય તેવું જીવન હતું.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
1時間に追われる生活Jikan ni owareru seikatsuA life pressed for timeसमय की कमी से जूझती जिंदगीસમયની અછતથી ઝઝૂમતું જીવન
2自由気ままな生活Jiyuu kimama na seikatsuA free and unrestrained lifeस्वतंत्र और बेफ़िक्र जिंदगीસ્વતંત્ર અને બેફિકર જીવન

Why: This is Pattern II — two vivid examples (rainy-day writing, sunny-day cycling) followed by the actual conclusion, flagged by 要するに: 予定というものとは無縁の生活 ("a life with no connection to schedules"). A life with no schedule is a free, unrestrained life — option 2. Option 1 is the literal opposite.

Q2

Passage

「いい人」の定義は一様ではない。「おとなしいいい人」が人を殺し、「いい人なんだけど」と交際相手にふられたりする。いい人とひと言で言っても、人当たりのよい無責任な人もいればまじめな被害妄想注の家もいる**。"Ii hito" no teigi wa ichiyou de wa nai. "Otonashii ii hito" ga hito o koroshi, "ii hito na n da kedo" to kousai aite ni furaretari suru. Ii hito to hitokoto de itte mo, hitoatari no yoi musekinin na hito mo ireba majime na higai mousou no ke mo iru.

注 被害妄想：自分が他人から害を加えられていると思いこむこと Chuu: Higai mousou: jibun ga tanin kara gai o kuwaerarete iru to omoikomu koto — "persecutory delusion: convincing oneself that one is being harmed by others"　·　** footnote: "there are charming but irresponsible people, and honest but paranoid people"

問い　筆者の言いたいことはどういうことか。Toi: Hissha no iitai koto wa dou iu koto ka.

EN: The definition of a "good person" isn't uniform. A "mild-mannered good person" might kill someone; someone might get dumped by their partner with the line "you're a good person, but...". Even summed up in a single phrase, "good person" can mean a personable-but-irresponsible type just as easily as an earnest type with persecutory delusions.
HI: "अच्छे इंसान" की परिभाषा एक जैसी नहीं होती। कोई "सीधा-सादा अच्छा इंसान" किसी की हत्या कर सकता है; किसी को उसका साथी "तुम अच्छे हो, लेकिन..." कहकर छोड़ भी सकता है। "अच्छा इंसान" कहने भर से भी इसका मतलब मिलनसार पर गैर-जिम्मेदार व्यक्ति भी हो सकता है, और ईमानदार पर पीड़ा-भ्रम से ग्रस्त व्यक्ति भी।
GU: "સારા માણસ"ની વ્યાખ્યા એકસરખી નથી હોતી. કોઈ "સીધો-સાદો સારો માણસ" કોઈની હત્યા કરી શકે છે; કોઈને તેનો સાથી "તું સારો છે, પણ..." કહીને છોડી પણ શકે છે. "સારો માણસ" કહેવા માત્રથી પણ તેનો અર્થ મિલનસાર પણ બેજવાબદાર વ્યક્તિ પણ થઈ શકે, અને પ્રામાણિક પણ પીડિત-ભ્રમથી પીડાતી વ્યક્તિ પણ.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
1「いい人」の定義は難しい。"Ii hito" no teigi wa muzukashii.The definition of "good person" is difficult."अच्छे इंसान" की परिभाषा कठिन है।"સારા માણસ"ની વ્યાખ્યા મુશ્કેલ છે.
2「いい人」は本当はいい人ではない。"Ii hito" wa hontou wa ii hito de wa nai.A "good person" isn't really a good person."अच्छा इंसान" असल में अच्छा इंसान नहीं होता।"સારો માણસ" ખરેખર સારો માણસ નથી હોતો.
3「いい人」とは二面性を持つ人のことだ。"Ii hito" to wa nimensei o motsu hito no koto da.A "good person" is someone with two sides/a duality."अच्छा इंसान" वह है जिसके दो रूप होते हैं।"સારો માણસ" એટલે એવી વ્યક્તિ જેના બે સ્વરૂપ હોય.

Why: Pattern I — the conclusion opens the passage: 「いい人」の定義は一様ではない ("the definition of 'good person' isn't uniform/consistent"). Everything after it (the murderer, the dumped partner, the two contrasting types) is supporting example, not a second point. Option 1 restates that opening conclusion (a definition that varies this much is, in practice, a hard one to pin down). Option 3 ("duality") over-interprets the examples into a fancier claim the text never actually makes — the passage lists a *range* of types, not a single person with two faces. Option 2 is too extreme and contradicted by the earnest, non-malicious "被害妄想" type mentioned.

Q3

Passage

A　多くの人がこの作品に共感を覚えるのは、人間の普遍性注1を見事に描写注2しているからであろう。Ooku no hito ga kono sakuhin ni kyoukan o oboeru no wa, ningen no fuhensei o migoto ni byousha shite iru kara de arou.

B　この主人公のように殺人を犯すという行為については、だれもが自分にはありえないことだと思う。Kono shujinkou no you ni satsujin o okasu to iu koui ni tsuite wa, dare mo ga jibun ni wa arienai koto da to omou.

C　しかし、その行為に至った彼女の嫉妬について、それが理解できるだけではなく、実際に同じような気持ちを抱いたことがあるという人は多いだろう。Shikashi, sono koui ni itatta kanojo no shitto ni tsuite, sore ga rikai dekiru dake de wa naku, jissai ni onaji you na kimochi o idaita koto ga aru to iu hito wa ooi darou.

注1 普遍性：すべてのものにあてはまる可能性 Chuu 1: Fuhensei: subete no mono ni atehamaru kanousei — "universality: the possibility of applying to everything"　·　注2 描写：表現すること Chuu 2: Byousha: hyougen suru koto — "depiction: to express"

問い　この文章の結論はどの部分か。Toi: Kono bunshou no ketsuron wa dono bubun ka.

EN: A: The reason many people feel empathy for this work is probably that it vividly depicts the universality of human nature. B: As for the act of committing murder like this protagonist, everyone thinks it's something that could never happen to them. C: But as for the jealousy that led her to that act, many people would say they not only understand it, but have actually felt something similar themselves.
HI: A: बहुत से लोग इस रचना से जुड़ाव महसूस करते हैं, इसकी वजह शायद यह है कि इसमें मानवता की सार्वभौमिकता को बख़ूबी दर्शाया गया है। B: इस नायिका की तरह हत्या करने के कृत्य के बारे में, हर कोई सोचता है कि यह उसके साथ कभी नहीं हो सकता। C: लेकिन उसे उस कृत्य तक ले जाने वाली ईर्ष्या के बारे में, कई लोग न सिर्फ इसे समझ सकते हैं, बल्कि खुद भी कभी वैसी ही भावना महसूस कर चुके होंगे।
GU: A: ઘણા લોકો આ કૃતિ સાથે જોડાણ અનુભવે છે, તેનું કારણ કદાચ એ છે કે તેમાં માનવતાની સાર્વત્રિકતાને સુંદર રીતે દર્શાવવામાં આવી છે. B: આ નાયિકાની જેમ હત્યા કરવાના કૃત્ય વિશે, દરેક વ્યક્તિ વિચારે છે કે આ તેની સાથે ક્યારેય ન બની શકે. C: પણ તેને એ કૃત્ય સુધી લઈ ગયેલી ઈર્ષ્યા વિશે, ઘણા લોકો ન ફક્ત તેને સમજી શકે છે, પણ પોતે પણ ક્યારેક એવી જ લાગણી અનુભવી ચૂક્યા હશે.

Why: Pattern I — A states the general claim (people empathize with this work because it captures something universal about being human); B and C then explain exactly *what* that universal thing is: not the murder itself (B: nobody thinks they'd do that) but the jealousy behind it (C: many people have felt that). A is the conclusion; B and C are the supporting explanation that unpacks it.

Q4

Passage

8 9 6 7 3 5 4 4 2 0 1 8 7 2 8 3 0 1

このような数字の羅列注1を一瞬で覚え、しかも一生それを記憶していられる人がいる。また、学習したことのない外国語で書かれた詩を読むことができ、時間がたってからも、その詩を暗唱注2できるという女性もいる。彼女が言うには、心の中でそのページが見えるらしい。その証拠に、彼女はその詩を後ろから、つまり、最後の文字から逆に読むことさえできるという*。Kono you na suuji no raretsu o isshun de oboe, shikamo isshou sore o kioku shite irareru hito ga iru. Mata, gakushuu shita koto no nai gaikokugo de kakareta shi o yomu koto ga deki, jikan ga tatte kara mo, sono shi o anshou dekiru to iu josei mo iru. Kanojo ga iu ni wa, kokoro no naka de sono peeji ga mieru rashii. Sono shouko ni, kanojo wa sono shi o ushiro kara, tsumari, saigo no moji kara gyaku ni yomu koto sae dekiru to iu.

信じられないような話だが、5,000人に1人の割合でこのような人がいるという。彼らは物を見てそれを永久に覚えていられる、いわゆる写真のような記憶力を持っているのである。驚くばかりである。Shinjirarenai you na hanashi da ga, gosen-nin ni hitori no wariai de kono you na hito ga iru to iu. Karera wa mono o mite sore o eikyuu ni oboete irareru, iwayuru shashin no you na kiokuryoku o motte iru no de aru. Odoroku bakari de aru.

注1 羅列：並んでいるもの Chuu 1: Raretsu: narande iru mono — "a sequence: things lined up in a row"　·　注2 暗唱：見ないで言うこと Chuu 2: Anshou: minaide iu koto — "recitation from memory: saying it without looking"　·　* footnote: "To prove it, she can recite the poem from the end, that is to say, even backwards from the last letter."

問い　この文章でいちばん言いたいことはどういうことか。Toi: Kono bunshou de ichiban iitai koto wa dou iu koto ka.

EN: There are people who can memorize a string of numbers like this instantly and remember it for the rest of their lives. There's also said to be a woman who can read a poem written in a foreign language she's never studied, and recite it from memory even after time has passed. According to her, she can apparently see the page in her mind — as proof, she can even read the poem backwards, starting from the very last character. It sounds unbelievable, but apparently about 1 in 5,000 people are like this. They have what's called photographic memory — they can look at something and remember it forever. It's nothing short of astonishing.
HI: ऐसे अंकों की एक शृंखला को पल भर में याद कर लेने वाले और उसे जीवन भर याद रख सकने वाले लोग होते हैं। ऐसी भी एक महिला बताई जाती है, जो कभी न सीखी हुई विदेशी भाषा में लिखी कविता पढ़ सकती है, और समय बीत जाने के बाद भी उसे कंठस्थ सुना सकती है। उसके अनुसार, उसे मन में वह पन्ना दिखाई देता है — इसके सबूत के तौर पर, वह उस कविता को उल्टा भी पढ़ सकती है, यानी आख़िरी अक्षर से शुरू करके। यह बात अविश्वसनीय लगती है, पर कहा जाता है कि लगभग हर 5,000 में से 1 व्यक्ति ऐसा होता है। उनके पास तथाकथित फोटोग्राफिक याददाश्त होती है — वे किसी चीज़ को देखकर उसे हमेशा के लिए याद रख सकते हैं। यह सचमुच चकित कर देने वाली बात है।
