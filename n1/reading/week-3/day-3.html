1 Reading Strategy

Key idea

下線部を選択肢に当てはめて読んでみよう！Kasenbu o sentakushi ni atehamete yonde miyou!

Today's questions ask what an underlined phrase actually means. The book's own worked example (explained in Chinese in this edition) is a warning against answering too quickly:

  • 1. Substitute, don't just guess. When asked what an underlined phrase means, don't judge it from the words alone — mentally plug each answer option into that same spot in the sentence, and read the whole thing back.
    HI: रेखांकित हिस्से का मतलब पूछे जाने पर, सिर्फ शब्दों से अंदाज़ा न लगाएँ — हर विकल्प को उसी जगह पर रखकर पूरा वाक्य दोबारा पढ़ें। GU: લીટી દોરેલા ભાગનો અર્થ પુછાય ત્યારે, ફક્ત શબ્દો પરથી અંદાજ ન લગાવો — દરેક વિકલ્પને એ જ જગ્યાએ મૂકીને આખું વાક્ય ફરી વાંચો.
  • 2. Check the fit, not just the sound. See whether the substituted sentence reads naturally and stays logically consistent with the surrounding context — an option that sounds fine in isolation but clashes with the context is wrong.
    HI: देखें कि विकल्प डालने पर वाक्य स्वाभाविक लगे और संदर्भ से मेल खाए — अकेले सही लगने वाला पर संदर्भ से टकराने वाला विकल्प ग़लत है। GU: જુઓ કે વિકલ્પ મૂક્યા પછી વાક્ય સ્વાભાવિક લાગે અને સંદર્ભ સાથે મેળ ખાય — એકલો સાચો લાગતો પણ સંદર્ભ સાથે અથડાતો વિકલ્પ ખોટો છે.
  • 3. The book's own trap example. 「世界は無限に広がっています。窓を閉ざしてはいけません。」("The world stretches out infinitely. You mustn't shut the window.") Read only literally, one cat naively answers "it means open your house's windows!" — the other cat snaps back, 「まったく、何もわかってないのね」("Honestly, you understand nothing at all"). The "window" here is figurative — it stands for one's mind and curiosity toward the wider world, not an actual window.
    HI: "खिड़की" यहाँ प्रतीकात्मक है — असली खिड़की नहीं, बल्कि व्यापक दुनिया के प्रति मन/जिज्ञासा का प्रतीक है। GU: "બારી" અહીં પ્રતીકાત્મક છે — ખરી બારી નહીં, પણ વ્યાપક દુનિયા પ્રત્યેના મન/જિજ્ઞાસાનું પ્રતીક છે.

Q1

Passage

一つの側面にとらわれずに注、多方面から眺めて初めて、物事の全体がわかるものである*。Hitotsu no sokumen ni torawarezu ni, tahoumen kara nagamete hajimete, monogoto no zentai ga wakaru mono de aru.

注 とらわれずに：影響を受けて心が支配されることがないように Chuu: Torawarezu ni: eikyou o ukete kokoro ga shihai sareru koto ga nai you ni — "without being trapped by: so as not to have one's mind controlled by [a single thing]"　·　* footnote: "You can understand the entire picture only when you look at something from not one, but all, sides."

問い　この文と近い意味を表す文はどれか。Toi: Kono bun to chikai imi o arawasu bun wa dore ka.

EN: Only by not being confined to a single facet, and looking at something from many angles, can you understand the whole of it for the first time.
HI: किसी एक पहलू में बंधे बिना, कई दिशाओं से देखने पर ही आप किसी चीज़ को पूरी तरह समझ सकते हैं।
GU: કોઈ એક પાસામાં બંધાયા વગર, અનેક દિશાઓથી જોવાથી જ તમે કોઈ વસ્તુને સંપૂર્ણ રીતે સમજી શકો છો.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
1一つの側面からでも全体がわかるときがある。Hitotsu no sokumen kara demo zentai ga wakaru toki ga aru.There are times you can understand the whole even from a single facet.कभी-कभी एक ही पहलू से भी पूरी बात समझ में आ जाती है।ક્યારેક એક જ પાસાથી પણ આખી વાત સમજાઈ જાય છે.
2物事はいろいろな方向から見ないと全体はわからない。Monogoto wa iroiro na houkou kara minai to zentai wa wakaranai.Unless you look at things from various directions, you can't understand the whole.चीज़ों को अलग-अलग दिशाओं से न देखा जाए तो पूरी बात समझ में नहीं आती।વસ્તુઓને જુદી જુદી દિશાઓથી ન જોવાય તો આખી વાત સમજાતી નથી.

Why: Substituting option 2 into the passage's place produces the exact same logical claim in negated form: "only from many angles → you understand the whole" restates as "unless from various directions → you can't understand the whole." The footnote confirms this reading directly. Option 1 flatly contradicts the passage, which explicitly rules out a single facet being sufficient.

Q2

Passage

物理的な時間注はもちろん一定であるが、人間が感じる時間は一定ではない**。一瞬の出来事が永遠のものとなったり、長い期間であっても「あっ」という間の出来事になったりするのである。Butsuriteki na jikan wa mochiron ittei de aru ga, ningen ga kanjiru jikan wa ittei de wa nai. Isshun no dekigoto ga eien no mono to nattari, nagai kikan de atte mo "a" to iu ma no dekigoto ni nattari suru no de aru.

注 物理的な時間：時計が刻む時間 Chuu: Butsuriteki na jikan: tokei ga kizamu jikan — "physical time: the time a clock ticks off"　·　** footnote: "Physical time is fixed but the human perceptions of time vary."

問い　一瞬の出来事が永遠のものとなったりとあるが、どのような意味か。Toi: Isshun no dekigoto ga eien no mono to nattari to aru ga, dono you na imi ka.

EN: Physical time (the time a clock measures) is of course constant, but the time humans perceive is not. A momentary event can turn into something felt as eternal, and conversely, even a long stretch of time can become an event that feels like it passed in the blink of an eye.
HI: भौतिक समय (घड़ी से मापा जाने वाला समय) निश्चित रूप से स्थिर होता है, लेकिन मनुष्य जो समय महसूस करता है वह स्थिर नहीं होता। एक क्षण भर की घटना अनंतकाल जैसी महसूस हो सकती है, और इसके उलट, एक लंबी अवधि भी पलक झपकते बीत जाने जैसी लग सकती है।
GU: ભૌતિક સમય (ઘડિયાળથી માપાતો સમય) ચોક્કસપણે સ્થિર હોય છે, પણ મનુષ્ય જે સમય અનુભવે છે તે સ્થિર નથી હોતો. એક ક્ષણની ઘટના અનંતકાળ જેવી અનુભવાઈ શકે છે, અને તેનાથી ઊલટું, એક લાંબો ગાળો પણ પલક ઝબકતાં વીતી ગયો હોય એવો લાગી શકે છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
1短い出来事でも、その後ずっと忘れないことであったりMijikai dekigoto demo, sono ato zutto wasurenai koto de attariEven a brief event might mean never forgetting it afterwardछोटी घटना का मतलब यह भी हो सकता है कि उसे बाद में कभी न भूलनाટૂંકી ઘટનાનો અર્થ એ પણ થઈ શકે કે તેને પછી ક્યારેય ન ભૂલવું
2出来事が短く感じられても、実は永久に続いて起こる出来事であったりDekigoto ga mijikaku kanjirarete mo, jitsu wa eikyuu ni tsuzuite okoru dekigoto de attariEven if an event feels brief, it might actually be one that goes on happening foreverभले ही कोई घटना छोटी महसूस हो, पर असल में वह हमेशा चलती रहने वाली घटना हो सकती हैભલે કોઈ ઘટના ટૂંકી લાગે, પણ ખરેખર તે કાયમ ચાલતી રહેતી ઘટના હોઈ શકે

Why (reasoned, more inference than the others): The sentence describes subjective time distortion in two mirrored directions: a brief real event can be *felt*, in the moment, as if it lasted forever, while a long stretch can conversely feel instantaneous. Option 1 shifts the topic to long-term memory ("never forgetting it later") — a different idea the passage never raises, since this sentence is entirely about felt *duration*, not memory retention. Option 2, despite its slightly literal phrasing, is the closer match to "a momentary event becoming something felt as eternal" — the direct mirror of the passage's other example ("a long period becoming an instant"). No footnote pins this one down precisely, so treat this as the best available reasoning rather than a certainty.

Q3

Passage

ホスピスとは、癌やエイズ患者の症状のコントロールやケア注1を主体とするが、延命措置注2や苦痛を伴う治療を行わず、患者が最期の時を迎えるまで少しでも快適に生きられるように*、痛みを取り除く治療のみを行い、その結果として安らかで自然な死を迎えられることを目的とした施設である。Hosupisu to wa, gan ya eizu kanja no shoujou no kontorooru ya kea no shutai to suru ga, enmei sochi ya kutsuu o tomonau chiryou o okonawazu, kanja ga saigo no toki o mukaeru made sukoshi demo kaiteki ni ikirareru you ni, itami o torinozoku chiryou nomi o okonai, sono kekka to shite yasuraka de shizen na shi o mukaerareru koto o mokuteki to shita shisetsu de aru.

注1 ケア：介護、世話 Chuu 1: Kea: kaigo, sewa — "care: nursing, looking after"　·　注2 延命措置：命を延ばす方法をとること Chuu 2: Enmei sochi: inochi o nobasu houhou o toru koto — "life-prolonging measures: taking measures to extend life"　·　* footnote: "in order for the patients to comfortably spend the last moments of their lives"

問い　安らかで自然な死とは、ここではどういう死か。Toi: Yasuraka de shizen na shi to wa, koko de wa dou iu shi ka.

EN: A hospice is a facility centered on controlling symptoms and providing care for cancer and AIDS patients; rather than performing life-prolonging measures or painful treatments, its purpose is for patients — so they can live as comfortably as possible until their final moments — to receive only pain-relief treatment, so that as a result they can meet a peaceful, natural death.
HI: हॉस्पिस एक ऐसी सुविधा है जो कैंसर और एड्स रोगियों के लक्षणों पर नियंत्रण और देखभाल को केंद्र में रखती है; जीवन बढ़ाने वाले उपाय या दर्दनाक उपचार करने के बजाय, इसका उद्देश्य यह है कि मरीज़ अपने अंतिम क्षणों तक जितना हो सके आराम से जी सकें, इसके लिए केवल दर्द दूर करने वाला उपचार दिया जाए, ताकि परिणामस्वरूप उन्हें एक शांत, स्वाभाविक मृत्यु मिल सके।

Q4

Passage

近年、不登校の生徒が増えているそうだ。病気以外で年間30日以上欠席した生徒は、中学生で100人に2、3人いるらしい。学校に行きたくないと思っている生徒、つまり潜在不登校児はその10倍もいるという。ところが、不登校児について真剣に対応している教師は、現場ではまだまだ少ない。教育学者や心理学者も具体的な解決策を明示するに至っていない。しかし、彼らにとって本当に必要なのは、親の意識の改革だという。過度の期待をやめ、束縛注から解いてやると、彼らは自然と学校に戻っていくのである**。Kinnen, futoukou no seito ga fuete iru sou da. Byouki igai de nenkan sanjuu-nichi ijou kesseki shita seito wa, chuugakusei de hyaku-nin ni ni, san-nin iru rashii. Gakkou ni ikitakunai to omotte iru seito, tsumari senzai futoukouji wa sono juu-bai mo iru to iu. Tokoro ga, futoukouji ni tsuite shinken ni taiou shite iru kyoushi wa, genba de wa mada mada sukunai. Kyouikugakusha ya shinrigakusha mo gutaiteki na kaiketsusaku o meiji suru ni itotte inai. Shikashi, karera ni totte hontou ni hitsuyou na no wa, oya no ishiki no kaikaku da to iu. Kado no kitai o yame, sokubaku kara toite yaru to, karera wa shizen to gakkou ni modotte iku no de aru.

注 束縛：人を自由にさせないこと Chuu: Sokubaku: hito o jiyuu ni sasenai koto — "restraint: not letting someone be free"　·　** footnote: "They will eventually return to school if you relieve them from excessive expectations and restraints."

問い　親の意識の改革とは、ここではどのような意味か。Toi: Oya no ishiki no kaikaku to wa, koko de wa dono you na imi ka.

EN: In recent years, the number of students refusing to attend school is said to be rising. About 2 or 3 out of every 100 middle-schoolers miss 30 or more days a year for reasons other than illness. Students who simply don't want to go to school — "latent" school-refusers — are said to number ten times that. Yet teachers who seriously address school refusal are still few on the ground, and education scholars and psychologists have yet to offer any concrete solution.
