1 Reading Strategy

Key idea

只看事情的内容（用件）！Zhǐ kàn shìqíng de nèiróng (yòngjiàn)!

"Just look at the actual business point (用件)!" — Formal Japanese letters and notices (お知らせ・案内) all follow the same fixed skeleton. Once you recognise the skeleton, you can skip most of it and jump straight to the facts:

  • 1. Opening words + season greeting are pure decoration. 拝啓 (Dear Sir/Madam) or 前略 (omitting the preamble), followed by a stock seasonal greeting and a line asking after the reader's health — none of this carries exam-relevant content. Skip it.
    HI: पत्र की शुरुआत के औपचारिक शब्द और मौसम का अभिवादन केवल सजावट हैं — इन्हें छोड़ दें। GU: પત્રની શરૂઆતના ઔપચારિક શબ્દો અને ઋતુ-અભિવાદન ફક્ત શણગાર છે — તેમને છોડી દો.
  • 2. The real content starts at the 本文 (body). It's almost always signalled by さて (well now), ところで (by the way) or 早速ですが (to get right to it) — that's the doorway into the actual 用件 (the matter at hand).
    HI: असली विषय-वस्तु 本文 (मुख्य भाग) से शुरू होती है, जो अक्सर "さて", "ところで" या "早速ですが" जैसे शब्दों से पहचानी जाती है। GU: ખરી વિષયવસ્તુ 本文 (મુખ્ય ભાગ)થી શરૂ થાય છે, જે ઘણી વાર "さて", "ところで" કે "早速ですが" જેવા શબ્દોથી ઓળખાય છે.
  • 3. Closing greeting + closing words are decoration too — but check for a 記～以上 block. お願い/お詫び/感謝 phrases and 敬具・草々 at the end can be skipped just like the opening. But if the letter has a itemised 記 (details) section ending in 以上, that block lists the hard facts — dates, places, amounts — in bullet form. Always check the question against that block first.
    HI: अंत की औपचारिकताएँ भी छोड़ी जा सकती हैं, लेकिन यदि पत्र में 「記」से 「以上」तक का सूचीबद्ध भाग है, तो वहाँ असली तथ्य (तारीख़, स्थान, राशि) लिखे होते हैं — पहले वहीं देखें। GU: અંતની ઔપચારિકતાઓ પણ છોડી શકાય, પણ જો પત્રમાં 「記」થી 「以上」સુધીનો યાદી-વિભાગ હોય, તો ત્યાં જ સાચી હકીકતો (તારીખ, સ્થળ, રકમ) લખેલી હોય છે — પહેલાં ત્યાં જ જુઓ.

The book's cartoon cat sums it up: 「そこは読み飛ばしていいところよ！」"Soko wa yomi tobashite ii tokoro yo!" — "That part is fine to skim right past!" (pointing at the opening greeting).

Q1

Notice

事務所移転のお知らせJimusho iten no oshirase

拝啓　新緑の候　ますますご清栄のこととお慶び申し上げます。平素は格別のご高配を賜り厚くお礼申し上げます*。Haikei, shinryoku no kou, masumasu go-seiei no koto to o-yorokobi moushiagemasu. Heiso wa kakubetsu no go-kouhai o tamawari atsuku o-rei moushiagemasu.

さて、このたび業務拡大に伴い、下記に事務所を移転する運びとなりました。新事務所での営業は来る5月20日（月）より開始いたします。Sate, kono tabi gyoumu kakudai ni tomonai, kaki ni jimusho o iten suru hakobi to narimashita. Shin-jimusho de no eigyou wa kitaru go-gatsu hatsuka (getsu) yori kaishi itashimasu.

まずは、ご通知かたがたご挨拶申し上げます。Mazu wa, go-tsuuchi katagata go-aisatsu moushiagemasu.

敬具Keigu

平成XX年5月吉日Heisei XX-nen go-gatsu kichijitsu

新住所　〒989-xxxx　みどり市寺下2丁目2-1-XShin-juusho: Yuubin bangou 989-xxxx, Midori-shi Terashita 2-choume 2-1-X

株式会社グリーン企画Kabushiki-gaisha Guriin Kikaku

* footnote translation: "I hope this letter finds you well in this nice spring season. I would like to thank you for your continued support."

Full notice (gist): 業務拡大に伴い事務所を移転し、新事務所での営業は5月20日（月）から開始する。新住所はみどり市寺下2丁目2-1-X。Gyoumu kakudai ni tomonai jimusho o iten shi, shin-jimusho de no eigyou wa go-gatsu hatsuka (getsu) kara kaishi suru. Shin-juusho wa Midori-shi Terashita 2-choume 2-1-X.
EN: Dear Sir/Madam. Due to business expansion, our office is being relocated, and business at the new office will begin on Monday, May 20. The new address is 2-1-X Terashita 2-chome, Midori City. This letter serves as both notice and greeting.
HI: प्रिय महोदय/महोदया। व्यापार विस्तार के कारण हमारा कार्यालय स्थानांतरित हो रहा है, और नए कार्यालय में कामकाज सोमवार, 20 मई से शुरू होगा। नया पता है मिदोरी शहर, तेरशिता 2-चोमे 2-1-X। यह पत्र सूचना और अभिवादन दोनों के रूप में है।
GU: પ્રિય મહોદય/મહોદયા. વ્યાપાર વિસ્તરણને કારણે અમારી ઓફિસ સ્થળાંતરિત થઈ રહી છે, અને નવી ઓફિસમાં કામકાજ સોમવાર, 20 મેથી શરૂ થશે. નવું સરનામું છે મિદોરી શહેર, તેરાશિતા 2-ચોમે 2-1-X. આ પત્ર સૂચના અને અભિવાદન બંને તરીકે છે.

Why: Applying strategy #3 — skip the greeting, read the 記-style facts — the notice states exactly two hard facts: the new address (新住所) and the date business resumes there (5月20日より開始). That's option 1. Option 2 invents an "old office's last business day," which is never mentioned. Option 3 invents a "change of business days" — the notice never says operating days/hours changed, only that the location moved. Option 4 invents a "construction schedule," which also never appears. All three wrong options mix one true detail with one fabricated detail — always check each half of an option against the text separately.
