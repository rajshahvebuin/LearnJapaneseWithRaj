1 Reading Strategy

Key idea

先看问题！①Xiān kàn wèntí! (1)

"Look at the question first! (1)" A list/table question (event schedules, product listings, etc.) usually has far more information than any single question needs. Reading the whole table first wastes time — read the question first, then hunt only for what it asks about.

  • 1. Read the question before the table. Once you know exactly what's being asked (a date range, a location type, a minimum value), scan only the columns relevant to that condition.
    HI: तालिका पढ़ने से पहले प्रश्न पढ़ें। जब पता हो कि क्या पूछा जा रहा है (तारीख़ की सीमा, जगह का प्रकार, न्यूनतम संख्या), तो सिर्फ़ उससे जुड़े स्तंभ देखें। GU: ટેબલ વાંચતાં પહેલાં પ્રશ્ન વાંચો. જ્યારે ખબર હોય કે શું પુછાઈ રહ્યું છે (તારીખની મર્યાદા, સ્થળનો પ્રકાર, ન્યૂનતમ સંખ્યા), ત્યારે ફક્ત તેની સાથે જોડાયેલ કૉલમ જુઓ.
  • 2. Skip anything the question doesn't touch. If a question never mentions a certain column (say, contact phone numbers), that column is irrelevant to solving it — don't waste time reading it carefully.
    HI: जो जानकारी प्रश्न में नहीं आती, उसे पूरी तरह छोड़ दें — जैसे फ़ोन नंबर वाला स्तंभ, अगर प्रश्न में उसका ज़िक्र ही नहीं है। GU: જે માહિતી પ્રશ્નમાં નથી આવતી, તેને સંપૂર્ણપણે છોડી દો — જેમ કે ફોન નંબરવાળી કૉલમ, જો પ્રશ્નમાં તેનો ઉલ્લેખ જ ન હોય.

The book's cats model this: one is amazed reading every word (「わー！すごい！！」"Waa! Sugoi!!" — "Wow, amazing!!"), while the other says 「自分に必要な情報だけ探すんだ！」"Jibun ni hitsuyou na jouhou dake sagasu nda!" — "Just look for the information you actually need!" The book adds: ★碰到列表题时，问题中没有涉及的，统统跳过不用看。"Pèngdào lièbiǎo tí shí, wèntí zhōng méiyǒu shèjí de, tǒngtǒng tiàoguò bùyòng kàn." — "With list-type questions, anything the question doesn't touch on — skip it all, no need to read it."

問1　お盆休み（8/13〜8/15）の間に開催される花火大会のうち、打ち上げ数が5000発以上ある花火大会はいくつあるか。Toi 1: O-bon yasumi (8/13-8/15) no aida ni kaisai sareru hanabi taikai no uchi, uchiage-suu ga 5000-patsu ijou aru hanabi taikai wa ikutsu aru ka.

EN: Among the fireworks festivals held during the Obon holidays (8/13–8/15), how many have 5,000 or more fireworks launched?
HI: ओबोन छुट्टियों (8/13–8/15) के दौरान होने वाले आतिशबाज़ी उत्सवों में से, कितनों में 5000 या उससे ज़्यादा पटाखे छोड़े जाते हैं?
GU: ઓબોન રજાઓ (8/13–8/15) દરમિયાન થતા ફટાકડા ઉત્સવોમાંથી, કેટલામાં 5000 કે તેથી વધુ ફટાકડા છોડાય છે?
OptionJapaneseEnglish

22つfutatsu2

Why: Within 8/13–8/15: a (8/13, 16,000), f (8/15, 5,000), g (8/14, 8,000), and j (8/15, 4,000). Of these, a, f, and g all reach ≥5,000 (f is exactly 5,000, which counts as "5000発以上"); j falls short at 4,000. That's 3 festivals — option 3.

問2　海の近くで開かれる花火大会のうち、最も大きい花火大会はどれか。Toi 2: Umi no chikaku de hirakareru hanabi taikai no uchi, mottomo ookii hanabi taikai wa dore ka.

EN: Among the fireworks festivals held near the sea, which is the largest?
HI: समुद्र के पास होने वाले आतिशबाज़ी उत्सवों में से, सबसे बड़ा कौन-सा है?
GU: સમુદ્ર નજીક થતા ફટાકડા ઉત્સવોમાંથી, સૌથી મોટો કયો છે?
OptionJapaneseEnglish
1bb
2cc
3gg
4ii

Why: "Near the sea" (海) rules out b, whose venue is a 河川敷 (riverbed) — a river, not the sea. Among the true sea-adjacent venues — c (漁港, fishing port, 3,000), g (海岸, beach, 8,000), and i (海浜公園, seaside park, 18,000) — the "largest" by 打ち上げ数 (fireworks count) is i, with 18,000. This is strategy #2: the question never asks about dates or exact locations, only "near the sea" + "biggest," so those two columns are all that matter.

問1　週末に体を動かしたい36歳の会社員に合っている募集／催しはいくつあるか。Toi 1: Shuumatsu ni karada o ugokashitai sanjuu-roku-sai no kaishain ni atte iru boshuu/moyoshi wa ikutsu aru ka.

EN: How many of these recruiting/event listings suit a 36-year-old office worker who wants to get physical exercise on weekends?
HI: इनमें से कितने विज्ञापन/आयोजन एक 36 वर्षीय कर्मचारी के लिए उपयुक्त हैं, जो सप्ताहांत में शारीरिक व्यायाम करना चाहता है?
GU: આમાંથી કેટલી જાહેરાતો/કાર્યક્રમો એક 36 વર્ષના કર્મચારી માટે યોગ્ય છે, જે સપ્તાહાંતે શારીરિક કસરત કરવા માંગે છે?
OptionJapaneseEnglish
11つhitotsu1
22つfutatsu2
33つmittsu3
44つyottsu4

Why: Three conditions must all be met: weekend, physical exercise, no age barrier for a 36-year-old. a (dance) is every Tuesday — a weekday, out. b (cooking class) is Saturday, but its content is dietary guidance and cooking practice, not exercise. c (exercise seminar) is Sunday and clearly physical, but restricted to 40歳以上 (40 and over) — a 36-year-old doesn't qualify. e/f/g/h are all spectator events, not exercise for the attendee (g's mikoshi is explicitly for children, 子どもみこし). Only d, table tennis on Saturdays, 18歳以上 with no upper limit, satisfies all three conditions — just 1.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

No vocabulary confusion cluster today — instead, compare which condition each question hinges on:

QuestionStrategy usedDeciding conditionTrap to avoid
List 1, Q1 Filter by date range, then by value Only rows within 8/13–8/15 count; among those, ≥5,000 fireworks j falls within the date range but its count (4,000) misses the ≥5,000 cutoff — don't just count everything in the date range.
List 1, Q2 Filter by venue type, then by value "海の近く" excludes river/lake venues even if they sound aquatic b's 河川敷 (riverbed) is not the sea — a classic near-miss meant to catch readers skimming for "water."
List 2, Q1 Stack three filters together Weekend AND exercise AND no age restriction, all three at once c looks perfect (Sunday, physical) until the 40歳以上 age line rules out a 36-year-old — read every fine-print condition, not just the headline.
List 2, Q2 Match theme, not just "Japanese-sounding" Only g names specifically traditional Japanese practices (mikoshi, taiko, yukata) An art exhibition (h) or flower garden (e) isn't automatically "Japanese culture" just because it's in Japan.
