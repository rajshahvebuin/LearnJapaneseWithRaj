問題1 (Mondai 1) — p.42

Q1

Passage (1)

他人という日本語は不思議な言葉である。それは文字通りには、他の人ということであるが、しかし実際に自分以外の他の人を意味するためには他者という新しい言葉がつくられていて、それとは違う特殊な意味合いが他人という言葉には含まれている。（土居健郎『「甘え」の構造』弘文堂）Tanin to iu nihongo wa fushigi na kotoba de aru. Sore wa mojidoori ni wa, ta no hito to iu koto de aru ga, shikashi jissai ni jibun igai no ta no hito o imisuru tame ni wa tasha to iu atarashii kotoba ga tsukurarete ite, sore to wa chigau tokushu na imiai ga tanin to iu kotoba ni wa fukumarete iru.

問い　それとは何のことか。Toi: Sore to wa nan no koto ka.

Full sentence: 他者とは違う特殊な意味合いが他人という言葉には含まれている。Tasha to wa chigau tokushu na imiai ga tanin to iu kotoba ni wa fukumarete iru.
EN: The Japanese word 他人 (tanin, "another person") is a curious word. Literally it just means "another person," but a new word, 他者 (tasha, "the Other"), had to be coined to mean "a person other than oneself" in the plain, literal sense — and 他人 carries within it a special nuance different from that.
HI: जापानी शब्द 他人 (तानिन, "दूसरा व्यक्ति") एक अजीब शब्द है। शब्दशः इसका मतलब सिर्फ़ "दूसरा व्यक्ति" है, पर सिर्फ़ "अपने अलावा कोई और" कहने के लिए एक नया शब्द 他者 (तशा) गढ़ना पड़ा — और 他人 में उससे अलग एक ख़ास अर्थ-छाया छिपी है।
GU: જાપાની શબ્દ 他人 (તાનિન, "બીજી વ્યક્તિ") એક વિચિત્ર શબ્દ છે. શબ્દશઃ તેનો અર્થ ફક્ત "બીજી વ્યક્તિ" છે, પણ ફક્ત "પોતાના સિવાય કોઈ બીજું" કહેવા માટે એક નવો શબ્દ 他者 (તશા) ઘડવો પડ્યો — અને 他人 માં તેનાથી અલગ એક ખાસ અર્થચ્છાયા છુપાયેલી છે.

Why: Immediately-preceding-clause reference — それ sits right after 「他者という新しい言葉がつくられていて」, so それ = 他者. The whole sentence's logic depends on it: 他人 is said to carry a nuance different from それ, so それ cannot be 他人 itself (option 1, which would be circular), and it names the specific coined word 他者, not the abstract idea of "a new word" (option 2) or 自分 (option 3), which never appears as an antecedent candidate here.

Q2

Passage (2)

ナイアシンは昔ニコチン酸とも呼ばれたことがあります。ニコチン酸といってもたばこのニコチンと直接関係があるわけではありません。ニコチンの分解物から見付けられた酸の一つなので、この名前が付けられました。ニコチン酸には、少し余分なものが付いてしかも同じ生理活性をもっているニコチン酸アミドという化合物があります。この二つを総称する時にナイアシンと呼んでいます。普通の時にはナイアシンでもニコチン酸でも同じだと考えてよいでしょう。（五十嵐脩『ビタミン』丸善）Naiashin wa mukashi nikochin-san to mo yobareta koto ga arimasu. Nikochin-san to itte mo tabako no nikochin to chokusetsu kankei ga aru wake de wa arimasen. Nikochin no bunkaibutsu kara mitsukerareta san no hitotsu na node, kono namae ga tsukeraremashita. Nikochin-san ni wa, sukoshi yobun na mono ga tsuite shikamo onaji seiri kassei o motte iru nikochin-san amido to iu kagoubutsu ga arimasu. Kono futatsu o soushou suru toki ni naiashin to yonde imasu. Futsuu no toki ni wa naiashin demo nikochin-san demo onaji da to kangaete ii deshou.

問い　この二つとは何のことか。Toi: Kono futatsu to wa nan no koto ka.

Full sentence: ニコチン酸とニコチン酸アミドの二つを総称する時にナイアシンと呼んでいます。Nikochin-san to nikochin-san amido no futatsu o soushou suru toki ni naiashin to yonde imasu.
EN: Niacin used to also be called "nicotinic acid." But nicotinic acid isn't directly related to the nicotine in tobacco — it was named this way simply because it was one of the acids found among nicotine's breakdown products. Nicotinic acid also has a related compound, nicotinamide, which has a bit extra attached but the same physiological activity. When referring to these two together, they're called niacin. Ordinarily, it's fine to think of niacin and nicotinic acid as the same thing.
HI: नायसिन को पहले "निकोटिनिक एसिड" भी कहा जाता था। पर निकोटिनिक एसिड का तंबाकू की निकोटिन से सीधा कोई संबंध नहीं है — यह नाम सिर्फ़ इसलिए पड़ा क्योंकि यह निकोटिन के टूटने से बने एसिड में से एक था। निकोटिनिक एसिड का एक और यौगिक है, निकोटिनिक एसिड एमाइड, जिसमें थोड़ा अतिरिक्त हिस्सा जुड़ा है पर शारीरिक सक्रियता वही है। इन दोनों को मिलाकर कहते समय नायसिन कहा जाता है। सामान्यतः नायसिन और निकोटिनिक एसिड को एक ही चीज़ मान लेना ठीक है।
GU: નાયાસિનને પહેલાં "નિકોટિનિક એસિડ" પણ કહેવાતું. પણ નિકોટિનિક એસિડનો તમાકુની નિકોટિન સાથે સીધો કોઈ સંબંધ નથી — આ નામ ફક્ત એટલા માટે પડ્યું કારણ કે તે નિકોટિનના તૂટવાથી બનેલા એસિડમાંનું એક હતું. નિકોટિનિક એસિડનું એક બીજું સંયોજન છે, નિકોટિનિક એસિડ એમાઈડ, જેમાં થોડો વધારાનો ભાગ જોડાયેલો છે પણ શારીરિક સક્રિયતા એ જ છે. આ બંનેને ભેગા કરીને કહેતી વખતે નાયાસિન કહેવાય છે. સામાન્ય રીતે નાયાસિન અને નિકોટિનિક એસિડને એક જ વસ્તુ માની લેવું ઠીક છે.

Why: Immediately-preceding-clause reference — この二つ sits right after the sentence naming both ニコチン酸 and ニコチン酸アミド side by side as having "the same physiological activity." Those are the two things jointly nicknamed "niacin," matching option 3. ナイアシン (option 1) is the umbrella term for the pair, not one of the two things being paired; nicotine itself (option 2) is only mentioned as unrelated background.

問題2 (Mondai 2) — p.43–45

Q3

Passage (1)

この国は自然が味方してくれたからね。今でも国土の7割から8割が森林でしょう。何千年もその森の中で生きてきたから日本民族の遺伝子の中に、すっかり森林文化が定着した。私はこれが実は日本人の弱点と思うのです。森には動物がいるし、実もなる。飢えに苦しむこともなく穏やかに生きてこられた。ただ目の前にあるいばら注1を折ったり、手でかき分けたりして進んできた。器用にはなった。が、必然的に、いかに対応するかということだけが①自分の仕事になってしまった。目の前にある枝を上手に折る、道を作る。そんな仕事だけに明け暮れるHOW文化がすべてになったんです。（中略）その致命的な注2欠陥は、遠くが見渡せないこと。木の葉や枝があるから遠い所を見渡す文化が育たなかった。欧米や中国の文化は砂漠文化です。そこではオアシスを見つけなければ生きられない過酷な注3状況で生き抜いてきた。必死に現状に耐えて遠くを見なければ死に至る。自分で方向を見定め、自分の足でそこにたどり着く。なぜこっちへ進むべきなのか、本当にこれでいいのか、命がけで自分に問いかけながら生きるのが砂漠のWHY文化なんです。②そういう視点で日本民族を見ていると、政治でも経済でも、そして外交でも、目の前のことでどうしよう、どう対応しようということばっかり考える。なぜこうなったのかを考える遺伝子がない。それを自覚しないとね。（中坊公平「仕事の現場に神宿る〈中坊公平が語る仕事・1〉」2003年3月30日　朝日新聞社）Kono kuni wa shizen ga mikata shite kureta kara ne. Ima demo kokudo no nana-wari kara hachi-wari ga shinrin deshou. Nan-zen-nen mo sono mori no naka de ikite kita kara nihon minzoku no idenshi no naka ni, sukkari shinrin bunka ga teichaku shita. Watashi wa kore ga jitsu wa nihonjin no jakuten to omou no desu. Mori ni wa doubutsu ga iru shi, mi mo naru. Ue ni kurushimu koto mo naku odayaka ni ikite korareta. Tada me no mae ni aru ibara o ottari, te de kakiwaketari shite susunde kita. Kiyou ni wa natta. Ga, hitsuzenteki ni, ika ni taiou suru ka to iu koto dake ga jibun no shigoto ni natte shimatta. Me no mae ni aru eda o jouzu ni oru, michi o tsukuru. Sonna shigoto dake ni akekureru HOW bunka ga subete ni natta n desu. (Chuuryaku) Sono chimeiteki na kekkan wa, tooku ga miwatasenai koto. Ki no ha ya eda ga aru kara tooi tokoro o miwatasu bunka ga sodatanakatta. Oubei ya Chuugoku no bunka wa sabaku bunka desu. Soko de wa oashisu o mitsukenakereba ikirarenai kakoku na joukyou de ikinuite kita. Hisshi ni genjou ni taete tooku o minakereba shi ni itaru. Jibun de houkou o misadame, jibun no ashi de soko ni tadoritsuku. Naze kocchi e susumu beki na no ka, hontou ni kore de ii no ka, inochigake de jibun ni toikakenagara ikiru no ga sabaku no WHY bunka nan desu. Sou iu shiten de nihon minzoku o mite iru to, seiji demo keizai demo, soshite gaikou demo, me no mae no koto de dou shiyou, dou taiou shiyou to iu koto bakkari kangaeru. Naze kou natta no ka o kangaeru idenshi ga nai. Sore o jikaku shinai to ne.

注1 いばら：触ると痛いトゲのある植物 — "a plant with painful thorns"　·　注2 致命的な：死に至るような、重大な — "fatal, critically serious"　·　注3 過酷な：厳しすぎる — "excessively harsh"

問い　①自分とはだれを指すか。Toi: ①Jibun to wa dare o sasu ka.

Full sentence: 日本民族は、いかに対応するかということだけが自分たちの仕事になってしまった。Nihon minzoku wa, ika ni taiou suru ka to iu koto dake ga jibuntachi no shigoto ni natte shimatta.
EN: This country had nature on its side — even now, 70–80% of the land is forest. Having lived in that forest for thousands of years, forest culture became completely fixed in the Japanese people's genes. I actually think this is a weak point of the Japanese. In the forest there are animals, and fruit grows; people could live gently, without suffering hunger. They simply pushed forward, breaking through the thorny bushes in front of them, pushing branches aside by hand. They became skillful — but inevitably, all that ever became "their own job" was figuring out how to respond to what was right in front of them. Skillfully snapping a branch, cutting a path — a "HOW culture" absorbed entirely in that kind of task became everything. Its fatal flaw is not being able to see far into the distance — with all those leaves and branches around, a culture of looking far ahead never developed. Western and Chinese culture, by contrast, is a desert culture. There, people survived in a harsh environment where you die if you don't find an oasis. You must endure the present and look far ahead, or you die. You determine your own direction and reach it on your own feet. Living while constantly, desperately asking yourself "why should I go this way, is this really right?" — that's the desert's "WHY culture." Looking at the Japanese people through that lens, in politics, economics, and diplomacy alike, all they ever think about is what to do and how to respond to what's right in front of them.
