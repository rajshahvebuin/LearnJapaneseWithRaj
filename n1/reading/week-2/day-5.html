1 Reading Strategy

Key idea

指示語が複数出てきたら、ひとつずつ順番に何を指すか確認する。Shijigo ga fukusuu dete kitara, hitotsu zutsu junban ni nani o sasu ka kakunin suru.

When これ/それ appears more than once in a short stretch of text, don't assume they all point to the same thing. This book shows two patterns, translated here:

  • 1. Pattern A — the referent changes each time. 「○○です。これは△△です。これが××です。」 — here the first これ points to ○○, but the second これ has already shifted to point to △△. Each これ builds on the one before it, like a chain.
    HI: पैटर्न A में हर "これ" पिछले वाले से आगे बढ़कर एक नई चीज़ की ओर इशारा करता है — जैसे एक ज़ंजीर। GU: પેટર્ન Aમાં દરેક "これ" પાછલા કરતાં આગળ વધીને નવી વસ્તુ તરફ ઈશારો કરે છે — જાણે એક સાંકળ.
  • 2. Pattern B — the referent stays fixed. Same surface pattern, 「○○です。これは△△です。これが××です。」, but here every これ still points back to the same ○○ the whole way through — nothing has shifted.
    HI: पैटर्न B में दिखने में वही वाक्य ढांचा होता है, पर हर "これ" शुरू से आख़िर तक एक ही चीज़ की ओर इशारा करता रहता है। GU: પેટર્ન Bમાં દેખાવમાં એ જ વાક્ય માળખું હોય છે, પણ દરેક "これ" શરૂઆતથી અંત સુધી એક જ વસ્તુ તરફ ઈશારો કરતું રહે છે.

Q1

Passage

友人がまたやって来た。彼は金に困るとやってくる。嘘をついて私をだましては金を巻き上げて注いくのだ。今日も彼の話が始まった。それはまたいつもの嘘だった。それが私にわからないはずがなかったが、彼もそれを承知で芝居を続けるしかなかったのだ*。Yuujin ga mata yatte kita. Kare wa kane ni komaru to yatte kuru. Uso o tsuite watashi o damashite wa kane o makiagete iku no da. Kyou mo kare no hanashi ga hajimatta. Sore wa mata itsumo no uso datta. Sore ga watashi ni wakaranai hazu ga nakatta ga, kare mo sore o shouchi de shibai o tsuzukeru shika nakatta no da.

注 金を巻き上げる：swindle money out of　·　* footnote: "he had no choice but to continue deceiving me"

問い　それは、何を指しているか。（彼もそれを承知で、のそれ）Toi: Sore wa, nani o sashite iru ka. (Kare mo sore o shouchi de, no sore)

Full sentence: 彼も、彼の話が嘘だと私が知っていることを承知で、芝居を続けるしかなかった。Kare mo, kare no hanashi ga uso da to watashi ga shitte iru koto o shouchi de, shibai o tsuzukeru shika nakatta.
EN: My friend showed up again. He always comes when he's short on money — lying to me, deceiving me, and swindling money out of me. Today his story started up again too. It was, once again, the usual lie. There was no way I couldn't have seen through it — but knowing that I knew, he too had no choice but to keep up the act.
HI: मेरा दोस्त फिर आ गया। जब भी उसे पैसों की तंगी होती है, वह आ जाता है — झूठ बोलकर, मुझे धोखा देकर पैसे ऐंठता है। आज भी उसकी वही पुरानी कहानी शुरू हुई। यह जानना नामुमकिन नहीं था कि यह झूठ है — पर यह जानते हुए भी कि मुझे उसका झूठ पता है, उसे भी नाटक जारी रखने के अलावा कोई चारा नहीं था।
GU: મારો મિત્ર ફરી આવ્યો. જ્યારે પણ તેને પૈસાની તંગી હોય, તે આવે છે — જૂઠું બોલીને, મને છેતરીને પૈસા પડાવે છે. આજે પણ તેની એ જ જૂની વાર્તા શરૂ થઈ. એ સમજવું અશક્ય નહોતું કે આ જૂઠ છે — પણ મને તેનું જૂઠ ખબર છે એ જાણવા છતાં, તેને પણ નાટક ચાલુ રાખવા સિવાય કોઈ ઉપાય નહોતો.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
1彼の話kare no hanashihis storyउसकी कहानीતેની વાર્તા
2彼の話が嘘だということkare no hanashi ga uso da to iu kotothe fact that his story is a lieयह बात कि उसकी कहानी झूठ हैએ વાત કે તેની વાર્તા જૂઠ છે
3彼の話が嘘だと私が知っていることkare no hanashi ga uso da to watashi ga shitte iru kotothe fact that I know his story is a lieयह बात कि मुझे पता है उसकी कहानी झूठ हैએ વાત કે મને ખબર છે તેની વાર્તા જૂઠ છે

Why: Pattern A — the referent shifts each time これ/それ repeats. First それ ("それはまたいつもの嘘だった") = 彼の話 (his story). The second それ ("それが私にわからないはずがなかった") has already moved on to = 彼の話が嘘だということ (the fact that it's a lie). The third それ ("彼もそれを承知で") shifts once more: 承知 ("aware of") must describe him being aware of something about me — namely that I know it's a lie. That's exactly why, per the footnote, he "had no choice but to continue deceiving me": he's performing the act because he knows that I'm onto him.

Q2

Passage

もう管理社会の時代が来ている、と考えるぼくが正しいか、それとも、まだその時代が来ていない、と考える人間の方が正しいか、そのことを問題にするのはやめましょう。むしろまだとか、もうとかいって争うような時代が来たと分かればいいのですし、それで充分だと思うのです。Mou kanri shakai no jidai ga kite iru, to kangaeru boku ga tadashii ka, sore tomo, mada sono jidai ga kite inai, to kangaeru ningen no hou ga tadashii ka, sono koto o mondai ni suru no wa yamemashou. Mushiro mada toka, mou toka itte arasou you na jidai ga kita to wakareba ii no desu shi, sore de juubun da to omou no desu.

問い　それの内容は何か。Toi: Sore no naiyou wa nani ka.

Full sentence: まだとか、もうとかいって争うような時代が来たとわかれば、それで充分だと思う。Mada toka, mou toka itte arasou you na jidai ga kita to wakareba, sore de juubun da to omou.
EN: Let's stop debating whether I'm right that the age of the managed society has already arrived, or whether those who think it hasn't yet are right. It's enough just to recognize that an age has come where people argue over "not yet" versus "already" — that, I think, is sufficient.
HI: यह बहस छोड़ दें कि मैं सही हूँ कि नियंत्रित समाज का दौर आ चुका है, या जो लोग सोचते हैं कि अभी नहीं आया, वे सही हैं। बस इतना समझ लेना काफ़ी है कि एक ऐसा दौर आ गया है जहाँ लोग "अभी नहीं" बनाम "अब आ चुका" पर बहस करते हैं — मुझे यही काफ़ी लगता है।
GU: આ ચર્ચા છોડી દઈએ કે હું સાચો છું કે નિયંત્રિત સમાજનો યુગ આવી ચૂક્યો છે, કે જેઓ માને છે કે હજી નથી આવ્યો તેઓ સાચા છે. બસ એટલું સમજી લેવું પૂરતું છે કે એવો યુગ આવી ગયો છે જ્યાં લોકો "હજી નહીં" વિરુદ્ધ "હવે આવી ગયું" તેની ચર્ચા કરે છે — મને એટલું જ પૂરતું લાગે છે.

Don't over-widen それ to cover the entire opening debate about 管理社会 (options 1–3) — the author explicitly says to stop arguing about that; それ refers only to the narrower, calmer realization proposed as an alternative.

Q3

Passage

喜怒哀楽や心配や嘆き等々のもろもろの感情は、日夜あらわれてくるが、どうしてそうなるのかはわからない。しかし、朝晩にそうした心の変化があってわれわれは生きているのであり、それらを除いては自分も存在しない。それこそが真実に近いようだが、なにがそうさせているのかはわからない。（中略）心のはたらきがあるからにはそうさせている主体があるはずだと一般には考えられる*が、じつはそのようなものは存在しない、と（荘子は）いいたいのであろう。（蜂屋邦夫『老荘を読む』講談社）Kidoairaku ya shinpai ya nageki toutou no moromoro no kanjou wa, nichiya arawarete kuru ga, doushite sou naru no ka wa wakaranai. Shikashi, asaban ni sou shita kokoro no henka ga atte wareware wa ikite iru no de ari, sorera o nozoite wa jibun mo sonzai shinai. Sore koso ga shinjitsu ni chikai you da ga, nani ga sou sasete iru no ka wa wakaranai. (Chuuryaku) Kokoro no hataraki ga aru kara ni wa sou sasete iru shutai ga aru hazu da to ippan ni wa kangaerareru ga, jitsu wa sono you na mono wa sonzai shinai, to (Soushi wa) iitai no de arou.

* footnote: "it is believed that because we experience such feelings, we must each have a sense of self"

問い　そのようなものとは何を指しているか。Toi: Sono you na mono to wa nani o sashite iru ka.

Full sentence: 心のはたらきがあるからにはそうさせている主体があるはずだと一般には考えられるが、そのようなもの（＝主体）はじつは存在しない。Kokoro no hataraki ga aru kara ni wa sou sasete iru shutai ga aru hazu da to ippan ni wa kangaerareru ga, sono you na mono (= shutai) wa jitsu wa sonzai shinai.
EN: All sorts of feelings — joy, anger, sorrow, pleasure, worry, grief — appear day and night, but why they arise is unknown. Still, it's these very changes of heart, morning and night, that keep us alive, and without them we wouldn't exist either — that does seem close to the truth, yet what causes them to happen remains unknown. Since the mind clearly works this way, it's commonly assumed there must be some subject/agent making it happen — but in fact, Zhuangzi seems to want to say, no such thing actually exists.
HI: हर्ष, क्रोध, शोक, आनंद, चिंता, विलाप जैसी अनेक भावनाएँ दिन-रात उभरती रहती हैं, पर क्यों उभरती हैं यह पता नहीं। फिर भी, सुबह-शाम होने वाले इन्हीं मन के बदलावों की वजह से हम जीवित हैं, और इनके बिना हमारा अस्तित्व भी नहीं होता — यह सच के क़रीब तो लगता है, पर इन्हें कौन उत्पन्न करता है यह अज्ञात है। चूँकि मन इस तरह काम करता है, तो आम तौर पर माना जाता है कि इसे कराने वाला कोई कर्ता ज़रूर होगा — पर असल में, झुआंगज़ी शायद यही कहना चाहते हैं कि ऐसा कोई कर्ता वजूद में नहीं है।
GU: હર્ષ, ક્રોધ, શોક, આનંદ, ચિંતા, વિલાપ જેવી અનેક લાગણીઓ રાત-દિવસ ઉદભવતી રહે છે, પણ શા માટે ઉદભવે છે તે ખબર નથી. છતાં, સવાર-સાંજ થતા આ મનના ફેરફારોને કારણે જ આપણે જીવિત છીએ, અને તેમના વગર આપણું અસ્તિત્વ પણ ન હોય — આ સાચાની નજીક લાગે છે, પણ તેમને કોણ ઉત્પન્ન કરે છે તે અજ્ઞાત છે. મન આ રીતે કામ કરે છે તેથી, સામાન્ય રીતે માનવામાં આવે છે કે તેને કરાવનાર કોઈ કર્તા હોવો જ જોઈએ — પણ ખરેખર, ઝુઆંગઝી કદાચ એમ કહેવા માંગે છે કે એવો કોઈ કર્તા અસ્તિત્વમાં નથી.

Q4

Passage

あたりまえの会話の大切なこと、それがどれほど心の支えになっていたか、私は父の死後、急速にそのことを感じた。父との会話に、父の言葉に、私は深い飢餓状態注1に落ちこんでいった**。そしてそこからぬけ出すことは、父に代わる誰かとの会話が必要であった。父と親しかった人と会えば、それはすぐその場で満たされることは充分承知していても、用事もないのに、気易く漠然と注2自分のためにだけ人を訪問する勇気は、私にはなかった。（室生朝子『父　室生犀星』三笠書房）Atarimae no kaiwa no taisetsu na koto, sore ga dore hodo kokoro no sasae ni natte ita ka, watashi wa chichi no shigo, kyuusoku ni sono koto o kanjita. Chichi to no kaiwa ni, chichi no kotoba ni, watashi wa fukai kigajoutai ni ochikonde itta. Soshite soko kara nukedasu koto wa, chichi ni kawaru dareka to no kaiwa ga hitsuyou de atta. Chichi to shitashikatta hito to aeba, sore wa sugu sono ba de mitasareru koto wa juubun shouchi shite ite mo, youji mo nai noni, kiyasuku bakuzen to jibun no tame ni dake hito o houmon suru yuuki wa, watashi ni wa nakatta.

注1 飢餓状態：飢えた状態、ここでは精神的な状態について言っている — "a state of starvation; here it refers to a mental/emotional state"　·　注2 気易く：気軽に — "casually, lightly"　·　** footnote: "I started craving conversations with my father and to hear my father's words."

問い　それとは、何のことか。Toi: Sore to wa, nan no koto ka.

Full sentence: 父に代わる誰かとの会話の必要性は、父と親しかった人と会えばすぐその場で満たされる。Chichi ni kawaru dareka to no kaiwa no hitsuyousei wa, chichi to shitashikatta hito to aeba sugu sono ba de mitasareru.
EN: How important ordinary conversation was, and how much it had been a support for my heart — I felt that keenly and suddenly after my father died. Starved of conversation with my father, of my father's words, I sank into a deep state of craving. Escaping from that required conversation with someone to take my father's place. I knew perfectly well that meeting someone who had been close to my father would satisfy that need on the spot — yet even with no particular errand, I couldn't summon the nerve to casually, vaguely visit someone just for my own sake.
