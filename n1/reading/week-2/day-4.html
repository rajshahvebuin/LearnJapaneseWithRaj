1 Reading Strategy

Key idea

指示語が指す内容は、ほとんどいつもすぐ前にある！Shijigo ga sasu naiyou wa, hotondo itsumo sugu mae ni aru!

Whatever これ/それ/この/その points to is almost always the sentence or clause immediately before it. This book gives two things to check, translated here:

  • 1. Look at the clause right before it. Example from the book: 「メールが届く。そのたびに……。」 — "An email arrives. Each time that happens..." そのたびに ("each time") points straight back to メールが届く.
    HI: उदाहरण: "मेल आता है। हर बार जब यह होता है..." — "उस बार" ठीक पहले वाले वाक्य की ओर इशारा करता है। GU: ઉદાહરણ: "મેલ આવે છે. દર વખતે જ્યારે આવું થાય..." — "તે વખતે" બરાબર પહેલાંના વાક્ય તરફ ઈશારો કરે છે.
  • 2. Don't grab a label word — find what's inside it. Example: 「夫が倒れたという知らせでした。そのことは……。」 — "It was news that my husband had collapsed. That thing..." Here そのこと means "the husband collapsing," not the label word 知らせ ("news") itself — even though 知らせ sits closer on the page. As the book's mascot puts it: "'right before it' always means the part that actually carries content, not just a label for it."
    HI: उदाहरण: "यह ख़बर थी कि पति गिर पड़े। वह बात..." — यहाँ "वह बात" का मतलब है "पति का गिरना," न कि "ख़बर" शब्द ख़ुद, भले ही "ख़बर" पास में हो। GU: ઉદાહરણ: "એ સમાચાર હતા કે પતિ પડી ગયા. તે વાત..." — અહીં "તે વાત" નો અર્થ છે "પતિનું પડવું," નહીં કે "સમાચાર" શબ્દ પોતે, ભલે "સમાચાર" નજીક હોય.

Q1

Passage

私はリハビリ注のつらさに何度もくじけそうになった*。そのたびに、「お兄ちゃん、一緒に頑張ろうね。」と声をかけてくれた幼い少年の笑顔に励まされて、歩行訓練の痛みに耐えたのです。Watashi wa rihabiri no tsurasa ni nando mo kujikesou ni natta. Sono tabi ni, "Oniichan, issho ni ganbarou ne." to koe o kakete kureta osanai shounen no egao ni hagemasarete, hokou kunren no itami ni taeta no desu.

注 リハビリ：リハビリテーション（治療の終わった体を社会復帰のために訓練すること） Chuu: rihabiri: rihabiriteeshon (chiryou no owatta karada o shakai fukki no tame ni kunren suru koto) — "rehabilitation: training the body after treatment so it can return to normal life"　·　* footnote: "I often felt discouraged because my rehabilitation was so difficult."

問い　そのたびにの内容はどちらか。Toi: Sono tabi ni no naiyou wa dochira ka.

Full sentence: リハビリのつらさに負けそうになるたびに、少年の笑顔に励まされた。Rihabiri no tsurasa ni makesou ni naru tabi ni, shounen no egao ni hagemasareta.
EN: I nearly lost heart from the pain of rehabilitation many times over. Each time that happened, I was encouraged by the smile of a young boy who'd say to me, "Come on, big brother, let's do our best together" — and that's how I endured the pain of walking training.
HI: पुनर्वास की कठिनाई से मैं कई बार हिम्मत हारने वाला था। हर बार ऐसा होने पर, एक छोटे लड़के की मुस्कान से हौसला मिलता था जो कहता, "चलो भैया, साथ में मेहनत करते हैं," और इसी तरह मैंने चलने के अभ्यास का दर्द सहा।

Q2

Passage

その役者注さんが舞台に出ているときに、悲しい電話が入りました。母親が亡くなったという知らせでした。日ごろからその母親は「私が死んでも舞台中の息子には知らせるな**」と言っていたので、関係者はそのことを知らせないことにしたそうです。Sono yakusha-san ga butai ni dete iru toki ni, kanashii denwa ga hairimashita. Hahaoya ga nakunatta to iu shirase deshita. Higoro kara sono hahaoya wa "Watashi ga shinde mo butai-chuu no musuko ni wa shirasu na" to itte ita node, kankeisha wa sono koto o shirasenai koto ni shita sou desu.

注 役者：俳優のこと Chuu: yakusha: haiyuu no koto — "yakusha: means an actor"　·　** footnote: "When I die, don't tell my son until he has finished performing."

問い　そのことは、何を指すか。Toi: Sono koto wa, nani o sasu ka.

Full sentence: 関係者は、その役者さんの母親が亡くなったことを知らせないことにした。Kankeisha wa, sono yakusha-san no hahaoya ga nakunatta koto o shirasenai koto ni shita.
EN: While that actor was performing on stage, sad news came in by phone: his mother had passed away. Since she had always said, "Even if I die, don't tell my son until he's finished performing," the staff apparently decided not to tell him that.
HI: जब वह अभिनेता मंच पर प्रदर्शन कर रहा था, तभी दुखद ख़बर आई: उसकी माँ का निधन हो गया था। चूँकि वह हमेशा कहती थीं, "मेरे मरने पर भी, बेटे को प्रदर्शन ख़त्म होने तक मत बताना," इसलिए संबंधित लोगों ने उसे यह बात न बताने का फ़ैसला किया।
GU: જ્યારે તે અભિનેતા મંચ પર પ્રદર્શન કરી રહ્યો હતો, ત્યારે જ દુઃખદ સમાચાર આવ્યા: તેની માતાનું અવસાન થયું હતું. તે હંમેશાં કહેતાં, "હું મરી જાઉં તોપણ, દીકરાને પ્રદર્શન પૂરું ન થાય ત્યાં સુધી ન કહેશો," એટલે સંબંધિત લોકોએ તેને આ વાત ન કહેવાનું નક્કી કર્યું.

Why: This mirrors the strategy box's own example exactly (知らせでした。そのことは…): そのこと refers to the actual event's content — 母親が亡くなったこと (that the mother died) — not the label word 知らせ, and not the mother's separately-quoted instruction either. That instruction explains why the staff kept it secret; そのこと is naming what they kept secret, which is the death itself.

Q3

Passage

母が死んでから、五、六年の間は、この状態で暮らしていた。おやじにはしかられる。兄とはけんかをする。清注1には菓子をもらう、ときどきほめられる。別に望みもない、これでたくさんだと思っていた。ほかの子供もいちがいに注2こんなものだろうと思っていた。ただ、清が何かにつけて、あなたはおかわいそうだ、不幸せだとむやみに注3言うものだから、それじゃかわいそうで不幸せなんだろうと思った。そのほかに苦になることは少しもなかった。ただおやじがこづかいをくれないには閉口した*。（夏目漱石『坊っちゃん』）Haha ga shinde kara, go, roku-nen no aida wa, kono joutai de kurashite ita. Oyaji ni wa shikarareru. Ani to wa kenka o suru. Kiyo ni wa kashi o morau, tokidoki homerareru. Betsu ni nozomi mo nai, kore de takusan da to omotte ita. Hoka no kodomo mo ichigai ni konna mono darou to omotte ita. Tada, Kiyo ga nani ka ni tsukete, anata wa okawaisou da, fushiawase da to muyami ni iu mono da kara, sore ja kawaisou de fushiawase nan darou to omotta. Sono hoka ni ku ni naru koto wa sukoshi mo nakatta. Tada oyaji ga kozukai o kurenai ni wa heikou shita.

注1 清：女の名前 — "Kiyo: a woman's name"　·　注2 いちがいに：みんな — "uniformly, all the same"　·　注3 むやみに：excessively　·　* footnote: "I found it difficult that my father did not give me any pocket money."

問い　そのほかにの「その」が指す内容はどれか。Toi: Sono hoka ni no "sono" ga sasu naiyou wa dore ka.

Full sentence: 清が、あなたはかわいそうだ、と言うことのほかに苦になることは少しもなかった。Kiyo ga, anata wa kawaisou da, to iu koto no hoka ni ku ni naru koto wa sukoshi mo nakatta.
EN: For the five or six years after my mother died, I lived in this same state — scolded by my father, fighting with my brother, getting sweets and the occasional compliment from Kiyo. I had no particular wish, and thought this was plenty. I figured every other child was more or less the same. It was only that Kiyo kept insisting, over anything at all, that I was pitiable and unhappy — so I came to think, well then, I must be pitiable and unhappy. Other than that, nothing troubled me in the least. Only my father not giving me any pocket money was hard to put up with.

GU: માતાના મૃત્યુ પછી પાંચ-છ વર્ષ સુધી હું આ જ સ્થિતિમાં રહ્યો — પિતાથી ઠપકો ખાતો, ભાઈ સાથે ઝઘડતો, ક્યોથી મીઠાઈ મેળવતો અને ક્યારેક વખાણ પણ. મારી કોઈ ખાસ ઇચ્છા નહોતી, મને લાગતું આટલું જ પૂરતું છે. વિચારતો કે બીજા બાળકો પણ લગભગ આવા જ હશે. બસ, ક્યો દરેક વાતે વગર કારણે કહેતી કે તું બિચારો છે, દુઃખી છે — તો હું પણ વિચારવા લાગ્યો કે કદાચ હું ખરેખર બિચારો અને દુઃખી છું. આ સિવાય મને કોઈ તકલીફ નહોતી. બસ પિતા ખિસ્સા-ખર્ચી ન આપે તે મને ખૂંચતું.

Q4

Passage

人間は生まれてから沈黙の期間が約一年間あります。その間に回りの人たちの話すことばをことばとして認識し始めます。そして、その後、物事とことばの対応からことばを次第に身につけ、次に、状況に合わせて自分から決まり文句や二語文を言い始めます。一度ある場面で、模倣でなく自発的に口にした表現を子どもたちは忘れることなくいつでも引き出して使います。ことばを出すときには、常に状況があり、その状況を即座に判断し、瞬時に何を話すかを頭の中で考えているのです**。この過程が大事なのです。一度言ったことばを忘れないのは、思考を通してそれを自分の口から使うことによって、自分のことばとして定着していくからです。（アカデミー・オブ・ランゲージ・アーツ『キッズピア入会しおり』（改））Ningen wa umarete kara chinmoku no kikan ga yaku ichi-nenkan arimasu. Sono aida ni mawari no hitotachi no hanasu kotoba o kotoba to shite ninshiki shihajimemasu. Soshite, sono ato, monogoto to kotoba no taiou kara kotoba o shidai ni mi ni tsuke, tsugi ni, joukyou ni awasete jibun kara kimari monku ya nigobun o iihajimemasu. Ichido aru bamen de, mohou de naku jihatsuteki ni kuchi ni shita hyougen o kodomotachi wa wasureru koto naku itsudemo hikidashite tsukaimasu. Kotoba o dasu toki ni wa, tsune ni joukyou ga ari, sono joukyou o sokuza ni handan shi, shunji ni nani o hanasu ka o atama no naka de kangaete iru no desu. Kono katei ga daiji na no desu. Ichido itta kotoba o wasurenai no wa, shikou o tooshite sore o jibun no kuchi kara tsukau koto ni yotte, jibun no kotoba to shite teichaku shite iku kara desu.

** footnote: "To come up with words, we always evaluate the situation, and instantly think about what to say next."

問い　この過程を表しているものはどれか。Toi: Kono katei o arawashite iru mono wa dore ka.

Full sentence: 状況の認識 → 思考 → 自分のことば、という過程が大事なのです。Joukyou no ninshiki → shikou → jibun no kotoba, to iu katei ga daiji na no desu.
EN: A person has a roughly one-year silent period after birth. During that time, they begin recognizing the words spoken by people around them as language. After that, they gradually acquire words from how things correspond to words, and then start producing set phrases and two-word sentences on their own, matching the situation. Once a child spontaneously (not by imitation) says an expression in some scene, they never forget it and can pull it out and use it any time. Whenever producing words, there's always a situation, and the child instantly judges that situation, and in a split second is thinking in their head what to say. This process is what matters. The reason a once-spoken word is never forgotten is that, by thinking it through and actually using it from one's own mouth, it becomes fixed as one's own vocabulary.
