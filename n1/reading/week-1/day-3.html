1 Reading Strategy

Key idea

Today's danger word is ない — it stacks, hides inside idioms, and can even flip its own meaning. This book (in this bilingual JP/Chinese edition) lists three things to watch for, translated here:

  • 1. 「ない」＋「ない」＝肯定の意味になる。"Nai" + "nai" = koutei no imi ni naru. — A double negative becomes a positive. "分からなくはない" doesn't mean "I don't understand" — it means "it's not that I don't understand," i.e. "I do understand, at least somewhat."
    HI: दो नकारात्मक शब्द मिलकर सकारात्मक अर्थ बनाते हैं — "समझ नहीं आता नहीं" का मतलब है "कुछ तो समझ आता ही है।" GU: બે નકારાત્મક શબ્દો ભેગા મળીને હકારાત્મક અર્થ બનાવે છે — "સમજાતું નથી નથી" નો અર્થ છે "થોડું તો સમજાય જ છે."
  • 2. 「〜ではないか。」＝私は〜だと思う。"~ dewa nai ka." = Watashi wa ~ da to omou. — "Isn't it ~?" is not a real question — it just means "I think it is ~," stated more forcefully.
    HI: "क्या यह ~ नहीं है?" कोई असली सवाल नहीं — इसका मतलब है "मुझे लगता है यह ~ है," बस ज़्यादा ज़ोर देकर कहा गया। GU: "શું આ ~ નથી?" કોઈ ખરો સવાલ નથી — તેનો અર્થ છે "મને લાગે છે આ ~ છે," ફક્ત વધુ ભાર દઈને કહેલું.
  • 3. "ない"を含む慣用句型を覚える。"Nai" o fukumu kanyou kukei o oboeru. — Memorize idiomatic patterns built on ない: 〜ほど…ものはない (superlative: "nothing is more ~ than this"), 〜ないではいられない (can't help but ~), 〜わけにはいかない (can't possibly ~), 〜かねない (might well ~), 〜ないことはない (it's not that it's never ~ / it does happen sometimes), 〜までもない (no need to even ~), 〜くてたまらない (unbearably ~), 〜てはおけない (can't just leave it as is), 〜でならない (can't help feeling ~), 〜ほかない (have no choice but to ~), 〜ないかなあ (I wish ~ would happen) — and more.
    HI: "ない" पर बने मुहावरेदार पैटर्न याद रखें — जैसे "सबसे ज़्यादा", "बिना किए रह नहीं सकता", "नहीं कर सकता", "हो सकता है", "कभी-कभी होता ही है", "करने की ज़रूरत नहीं", "असहनीय रूप से", "ऐसे नहीं छोड़ सकता", "महसूस किए बिना नहीं रह सकता", "करने के अलावा कोई चारा नहीं", "काश ऐसा हो"। GU: "ない" પર બનેલા રૂઢિપ્રયોગ યાદ રાખો — જેમ કે "સૌથી વધુ", "કર્યા વગર રહી ન શકાય", "ન કરી શકાય", "થઈ શકે છે", "ક્યારેક થાય જ છે", "કરવાની જરૂર નથી", "અસહ્ય રીતે", "એમ છોડી ન શકાય", "અનુભવ્યા વગર રહી ન શકાય", "કરવા સિવાય કોઈ ઈલાજ નથી", "કાશ આવું થાય".

Q1

Passage

その仕事ほどおもしろくない仕事はないのではないかと、私は思った。Sono shigoto hodo omoshirokunai shigoto wa nai no de wa nai ka to, watashi wa omotta.

Implied meaning: その仕事はとてもつまらない仕事だと私は思った。Sono shigoto wa totemo tsumaranai shigoto da to watashi wa omotta.
EN: I thought there was probably no job as uninteresting as that one. (i.e., I thought it was the single most boring job there is.)
HI: मुझे लगा कि शायद उस काम जितना उबाऊ कोई और काम नहीं है। (यानी: मुझे वह काम सबसे उबाऊ लगा।)
GU: મને લાગ્યું કે કદાચ તે કામ જેટલું કંટાળાજનક બીજું કોઈ કામ નથી. (એટલે કે: મને તે કામ સૌથી કંટાળાજનક લાગ્યું.)
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
1おもしろいと思った。Omoshiroi to omotta.Thought it was interesting.दिलचस्प लगा।રસપ્રદ લાગ્યું.
2つまらないと思った。Tsumaranai to omotta.Thought it was boring.उबाऊ/बेकार लगा।કંટાળાજનક લાગ્યું.

Why: Two layers stack here. First, "AほどXない…はない" is the superlative idiom from strategy #3: "there is no job less interesting (おもしろくない) than that job" = "that job is the most boring one." Second, this whole claim is wrapped in "〜のではないか" (strategy #2), a rhetorical question that just restates "私はそう思う" — I do think this. So the sentence simply means: "I thought that job was extremely boring." Option 1 mistakes the wrapped negative-superlative for a positive statement.

Q2

Passage

「まあ、ひどい。だれがこんなことを……。」ポチ(注1)は墨で眼鏡をかかれたひょうきんな(注2)顔でしっぽを振っていた*。「叔父さんに決まってるじゃない。」僕は笑った。母の弟はいたずら好きだ。あの人ならやりかねない。「まったく、子どもじゃあるまいし……。」母も笑っていた。"Maa, hidoi. Dare ga konna koto o......" Pochi wa sumi de megane o kakareta hyoukin na kao de shippo o futte ita. "Ojisan ni kimatteru janai." Boku wa waratta. Haha no otouto wa itazura-zuki da. Ano hito nara yarikanenai. "Mattaku, kodomo ja arumai shi......" Haha mo waratte ita.

注1 ポチ：犬の名前 Chuu 1: Pochi: inu no namae — "Pochi: a dog's name"　·　注2 ひょうきんな：人を笑わせるような、おもしろい Chuu 2: hyoukin na: hito o warawaseru you na, omoshiroi — "comical: the kind that makes people laugh"　·　* footnote: "Pochi was wagging his tail and looked funny because of the black circles that had been drawn around his eyes."

Full passage meaning: 今回のいたずらは叔父がやったに違いないと、僕も母も思っている。Konkai no itazura wa oji ga yatta ni chigainai to, boku mo haha mo omotte iru.
EN: "Oh no, how awful. Who could have done this……" Pochi was wagging his tail with a comical face, ink circles drawn around his eyes. "It's obviously Uncle." I laughed. Mother's younger brother loves pranks — he'd well do such a thing. "Honestly, he's not exactly a child……" Mother was laughing too.
HI: "अरे, कितना बुरा है। यह किसने किया……।" पोची अपनी दुम हिला रहा था, उसकी आँखों के चारों ओर स्याही से गोले बने हुए थे और चेहरा मज़ाकिया लग रहा था। "यह तो पक्का चाचा ने किया है।" मैं हँस पड़ा। माँ का छोटा भाई शरारत का शौकीन है — वह ऐसा कर ही सकता है। "सच में, वह कोई बच्चा तो है नहीं……।" माँ भी हँस रही थी।
GU: "અરે, કેટલું ખરાબ. આ કોણે કર્યું……." પોચી પોતાની પૂંછડી હલાવી રહ્યો હતો, તેની આંખોની આસપાસ શાહીથી ગોળા દોરેલા હતા અને ચહેરો રમૂજી લાગતો હતો. "આ તો ચોક્કસ કાકાએ કર્યું છે." હું હસી પડ્યો. મમ્મીનો નાનો ભાઈ મસ્તીખોર છે — તે આવું કરી શકે એમ છે. "ખરેખર, એ કંઈ બાળક તો નથી……." મમ્મી પણ હસી રહી હતી.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
1その子どもがまた今度いたずらをやったら許さない、と母は言った。Sono kodomo ga mata kondo itazura o yattara yurusanai, to haha wa itta.Mother said she wouldn't forgive the child if he pranked again.माँ ने कहा अगर वह बच्चा फिर शरारत करेगा तो माफ़ नहीं करेगी।મમ્મીએ કહ્યું જો બાળક ફરી મસ્તી કરે તો માફ નહીં કરે.
2今回のいたずらは、叔父さんがやったのではない、と僕は思った。Konkai no itazura wa, ojisan ga yatta no de wa nai, to boku wa omotta.I thought this prank wasn't done by Uncle.मुझे लगा इस बार की शरारत चाचा ने नहीं की।મને લાગ્યું આ વખતની મસ્તી કાકાએ નથી કરી.
3叔父はよくいたずらをするので、今回も叔父がやったと僕は思った。Oji wa yoku itazura o suru node, konkai mo oji ga yatta to boku wa omotta.Since Uncle often pranks, I thought this time was him too.चाचा अक्सर शरारत करता है, इसलिए मुझे लगा इस बार भी उसी ने किया।કાકા વારંવાર મસ્તી કરે છે, એટલે મને લાગ્યું આ વખતે પણ તેણે જ કર્યું.

Why: "叔父さんに決まってるじゃない" (a "〜じゃない" rhetorical, cousin of strategy #2's ではないか) means "it's obviously Uncle, isn't it!" — a confident assertion, not doubt. "やりかねない" (strategy #3 idiom) means "he might well do such a thing," describing his known mischievous streak. "子どもじゃあるまいし" underscores this is exactly the kind of thing this grown man does. All signs point to the narrator and mother both believing it was the uncle — option 3. Option 2 misreads the confident "〜に決まってるじゃない" as if it denied the uncle's involvement.

Q3

Passage

こうした授業状況のなかでは、教師はつねに「ダメ教師」というラベルをはられることに戦々恐々(注1)とならざるをえない*。親たちからも、同僚からも、さらには子どもたちからも「ダメ教師」というラベルをはられたときには、授業が崩壊する(注2)どころか、辞職という事態に追いこまれるほかないというのが今日の現実であるといっても過言ではない。Kou shita jugyou joukyou no naka de wa, kyoushi wa tsune ni "dame kyoushi" to iu raberu o hararareru koto ni sensen-kyoukyou to naraza-ru o enai. Oyatachi kara mo, douryou kara mo, sara ni wa kodomotachi kara mo "dame kyoushi" to iu raberu o hararareta toki ni wa, jugyou ga houkai suru dokoro ka, jishoku to iu jitai ni oikomareru hoka nai to iu no ga konnichi no genjitsu de aru to itte mo kagon de wa nai. (竹内常一「日本の学校のゆくえ「偏差値教育はどうなるか」」太郎次郎社)

注1 戦々恐々：恐れる様子 Chuu 1: sensen-kyoukyou: osoreru yousu — "trembling with fear: a state of being afraid"　·　注2 崩壊する：壊れる Chuu 2: houkai suru: kowareru — "to collapse: to break down"　·　* footnote: "naturally, teachers have become terrified of being labeled as a 'Failed Teacher'"

Full sentence: 「ダメ教師」のラベルをはられることを教師が恐れるのは当然であり、実際に辞職に追い込まれることさえある。"Dame kyoushi" no raberu o hararareru koto o kyoushi ga osoreru no wa touzen de ari, jissai ni jishoku ni oikomareru koto sae aru.
EN: Amid this state of classes today, teachers cannot help but constantly tremble with fear of being labeled a "Failed Teacher." When that label is stuck on them — by parents, colleagues, or even the children themselves — it's no exaggeration to say today's reality is that, far from just having their class collapse, they're driven all the way to resignation.
HI: आज के कक्षा हालात में, शिक्षक हमेशा "नाकाबिल शिक्षक" का ठप्पा लगने के डर से काँपते रहने पर मजबूर हैं। जब माता-पिता, सहकर्मी, यहाँ तक कि बच्चों से भी यह ठप्पा लग जाता है, तो सिर्फ़ कक्षा बिगड़ना ही नहीं, बल्कि इस्तीफ़ा देने तक की नौबत आ जाना आज की सच्चाई है — यह कहना ज़रा भी अतिशयोक्ति नहीं।
GU: આજની વર્ગખંડ સ્થિતિમાં, શિક્ષકો હંમેશા "નાલાયક શિક્ષક" નું લેબલ લાગવાના ડરથી ધ્રૂજતા રહેવા મજબૂર છે. જ્યારે માતાપિતા, સહકર્મીઓ, છેક બાળકો તરફથી પણ આ લેબલ લાગે છે, ત્યારે ફક્ત વર્ગ પડી ભાંગવો જ નહીં, પણ રાજીનામા સુધીની નોબત આવવી એ આજની વાસ્તવિકતા છે — એમ કહેવું જરાય અતિશયોક્તિ નથી.

1現状況では教師が「ダメ教師」のラベルをはられることを恐れるのも仕方がない。Genjoukyou de wa kyoushi ga "dame kyoushi" no raberu o hararareru koto o osoreru no mo shikata ga nai.Given the current situation, it can't be helped that teachers fear being labeled "Failed Teacher."मौजूदा हालात में शिक्षकों का यह ठप्पा लगने से डरना लाज़मी है।વર્તમાન સ્થિતિમાં શિક્ષકોનું આ લેબલ લાગવાનો ડર રાખવો સ્વાભાવિક છે.
2「ダメ教師」のラベルをはられたからといって授業が崩壊するようなことはない。"Dame kyoushi" no raberu o hararareta kara to itte jugyou ga houkai suru you na koto wa nai.Being labeled "Failed Teacher" doesn't mean the class actually collapses.ठप्पा लग जाने से कक्षा बिगड़ ही जाए, ऐसा नहीं है।લેબલ લાગવાથી વર્ગ ખરેખર પડી ભાંગે એવું નથી.
3「ダメ教師」のラベルをはられて辞職に追い込まれることもある。"Dame kyoushi" no raberu o harararete jishoku ni oikomareru koto mo aru.Being labeled can even drive a teacher to resign.ठप्पा लगने से इस्तीफ़ा देने तक की नौबत भी आ सकती है।લેબલ લાગવાથી રાજીનામા સુધીની નોબત પણ આવી શકે છે.

Why: This asks for the option that does NOT match. "ならざるをえない" (strategy #3: cannot help but ~) confirms option 1 — teachers' fear is unavoidable given the situation, so option 1 matches. "どころか" (far from X, actually Y) means class collapse is the lesser problem — resignation is worse and does happen, so option 3 matches directly. But option 2 claims classes never actually collapse, directly contradicting "崩壊するどころか" (which implies collapse is a real, lesser possibility on the way to the worse outcome of forced resignation) — so option 2 is the one that does NOT match the passage.

Q4

Passage

新しいことをやるには勇気がいることは言うまでもないが、手をつける前から、自分なんかにできるわけがないとあきらめる態度ほどじれったいものはない**。やってみなければ何も始まらない。特に最近の若い人は教育制度の影響か、早くからもう自分の将来はこのくらいのものだと見切りをつけて、それ以上は望まないというような考えの人が少なからずいるのは残念でならない。それに比べ、「年寄りの冷や水」(注1)なんて陰口にもめげる(注2)ことなく、苦労もいとわず(注3)、体力や記憶力の衰えと闘っても何かやってみたくてしかたないというシルバー世代(注4)の元気なこと！Atarashii koto o yaru ni wa yuuki ga iru koto wa iu made mo nai ga, te o tsukeru mae kara, jibun nanka ni dekiru wake ga nai to akirameru taido hodo jirettai mono wa nai. Yatte minakereba nani mo hajimaranai. Toku ni saikin no wakai hito wa kyouiku seido no eikyou ka, hayaku kara mou jibun no shourai wa kono kurai no mono da to mikiri o tsukete, sore ijou wa nozomanai to iu you na kangae no hito ga sukunakarazu iru no wa zannen de naranai. Sore ni kurabe, "toshiyori no hiyamizu" nante kageguchi ni mo mageru koto naku, kurou mo itowazu, tairyoku ya kiokuryoku no otoroe to tatakatte mo nanika yatte mitakute shikata nai to iu shirubaa sedai no genki na koto! (加藤秀俊『加藤秀俊著作集6』中央公論社)

注1 年寄りの冷や水：年寄りが自分の体力や年齢を考えないで無理をすること Chuu 1: toshiyori no hiyamizu: toshiyori ga jibun no tairyoku ya nenrei o kangaenai de muri o suru koto — "an old person overexerting without regard for their own strength/age"　·　注2 めげる：弱気になる Chuu 2: mageru: yowaki ni naru — "to lose heart"　·　注3 苦労もいとわず：つらいこと、苦しいことを避けないで Chuu 3: kurou mo itowazu: tsurai koto, kurushii koto o sakenai de — "not avoiding hardship or difficulty"　·　注4 シルバー世代：高齢の人々 Chuu 4: shirubaa sedai: koureino hitobito — "silver generation: elderly people"　·　** footnote: "it is very frustrating to see people giving up on things even before they start"

Full sentence: 自分の将来に見切りをつけて、それ以上を望まない若者が少なからずいることが残念だ。Jibun no shourai ni mikiri o tsukete, sore ijou o nozomanai wakamono ga sukunakarazu iru koto ga zannen da.
EN: It goes without saying that new things take courage, but nothing is more frustrating than the attitude of giving up before even trying, telling yourself "there's no way I could do it." Nothing begins without trying. Perhaps due to the education system, quite a few young people these days write off their own futures early, deciding "this is about as far as I'll get" and hoping for nothing more — and that's deeply regrettable. Compare that to the "silver generation," undiscouraged by gossip about "old folks overexerting themselves," never avoiding hardship, fighting physical and mental decline just because they can't help wanting to try something — how full of life they are!

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

No vocabulary cluster today — instead, compare which ない-construction unlocks each question:

Questionない-pattern usedWhat it actually meansTrap to avoid
Q1 〜ほど…ものはない (superlative) + 〜のではないか (rhetorical, = "I think") "Nothing is less interesting than this" = "this is extremely boring" Don't mistake the stacked negatives for cancelling out into something positive — this isn't the "nai + nai" case.
Q2 〜に決まってるじゃない (confident assertion) + 〜かねない (might well do) Both point to strong likelihood the uncle did it, not doubt じゃない here isn't a real negation — it's emphasis, like ではないか.
Q3 ならざるをえない (can't help but) + どころか (far from X, actually worse) Fear is unavoidable; being labeled can escalate all the way to forced resignation どころか signals the named thing (class collapse) is the milder outcome, not something that never happens.
Q4 〜ほどじれったいものはない (superlative) + 〜でならない (can't help feeling) Giving up before trying is framed as the single most frustrating, regrettable attitude Don't let a plausible-sounding option about the elderly (3) or about young people (4) distract from what the passage is actually praising and lamenting.
