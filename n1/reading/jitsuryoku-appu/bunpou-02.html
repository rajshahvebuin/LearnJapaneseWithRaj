1 Strategy & Key Words

Key idea

つぎの文章を読んで、［1］から［5］の中に入る最もよいものを、1・2・3・4から一つえらびなさい。Tsugi no bunshou o yonde, [ichi] kara [go] no naka ni hairu mottomo yoi mono o, ichi・ni・san・yon kara hitotsu erabinasai.

EN: Read the passage and choose the best option (1–4) for each of blanks 1–5.   HI: पाठ पढ़ें और रिक्त स्थान 1–5 में से हर एक के लिए 1–4 में से सबसे अच्छा विकल्प चुनें।   GU: લખાણ વાંચો અને ખાલી જગ્યા 1–5માંથી દરેક માટે 1–4માંથી સૌથી સારો વિકલ્પ પસંદ કરો.

  • A news-style text with headings. This passage is an explanatory article in two parts (the law is introduced → the trouble after it came into force). Note which part a blank is in: blanks 1–3 describe the law, blanks 4–5 the reaction to it. (added, not in book)
    HI: यह दो हिस्सों वाला समाचार-जैसा लेख है (क़ानून का परिचय → लागू होने के बाद की परेशानी)। देखें रिक्त स्थान किस हिस्से में है: 1–3 क़ानून के बारे में, 4–5 उसकी प्रतिक्रिया के बारे में। GU: આ બે ભાગવાળો સમાચાર જેવો લેખ છે (કાયદાનો પરિચય → અમલ પછીની મુશ્કેલી). જુઓ ખાલી જગ્યા કયા ભાગમાં છે: 1–3 કાયદા વિશે, 4–5 તેની પ્રતિક્રિયા વિશે.
  • Connector blanks: does the next sentence add, change topic, or concede? In blank 1, the sentence after the blank piles another problem on top of the first one, so an "and what's more" word fits; ところで would change the subject. (added, not in book; drawn from the 解説)
    HI: संयोजक वाले रिक्त स्थान में देखें कि अगला वाक्य बात जोड़ता है, विषय बदलता है या मान लेता है। रिक्त 1 के बाद पहली समस्या पर एक और समस्या जुड़ती है, इसलिए "ऊपर से" वाला शब्द ठीक है; ところで विषय बदल देता। GU: જોડાણ શબ્દવાળી ખાલી જગ્યામાં જુઓ કે પછીનું વાક્ય વાત ઉમેરે છે, વિષય બદલે છે કે સ્વીકારે છે. ખાલી જગ્યા 1 પછી પહેલી સમસ્યા પર બીજી સમસ્યા ઉમેરાય છે, તેથી "ઉપરાંત" વાળો શબ્દ બેસે; ところで વિષય બદલી નાખે.
  • Who does the action? Check the subject marker. When the options are 施行する / 施行された / 施行させた…, look at what is marked with が just before the blank: a law "is put into force" (passive), it does not do it itself. (added, not in book)
    HI: विकल्प कर्तृवाच्य/कर्मवाच्य/प्रेरणार्थक हों तो रिक्त से ठीक पहले が वाला कर्ता देखें: क़ानून "लागू किया जाता है" (कर्मवाच्य), वह ख़ुद कुछ लागू नहीं करता। GU: વિકલ્પો કર્તરિ/કર્મણિ/પ્રેરક હોય તો ખાલી જગ્યા પહેલાંનો が વાળો કર્તા જુઓ: કાયદો "અમલમાં મુકાય છે" (કર્મણિ), તે જાતે કંઈ અમલમાં મૂકતો નથી.
  • Content blanks inside a list: use the other list items. Blank 3 is item ④ of a list of rules; the right option must be consistent with rules ①–③ already given. (added, not in book; drawn from the 解説)
    HI: सूची के अंदर के रिक्त स्थान में बाक़ी बिंदु देखें। रिक्त 3 नियमों की सूची का ④ है; सही विकल्प ①–③ से मेल खाना चाहिए। GU: યાદીની અંદરની ખાલી જગ્યામાં બાકીના મુદ્દા જુઓ. ખાલી જગ્યા 3 નિયમોની યાદીનો ④ છે; સાચો વિકલ્પ ①–③ સાથે મેળ ખાવો જોઈએ.

2 Reading Exercise — 文章の文法 ②

One passage (printed p.22–23), five blanks, options on p.23. Read the passage in your copy of the book and answer first. Then use the summary, the blank sentences and the explanations to check yourself. The passage is summarised, not transcribed.

Passage

Passage — from 創作集団にほんご編著『中上級のにほんご』「今月の話題」 (p.22–23)

Passage summary (not the book's text): An article in two parts. First: as companies came to store huge amounts of secret and personal data, that data became easy to copy and misuse, so in April 2005 Japan's Personal Information Protection Law came into force; the article lists its main rules (tell people why their data is collected, no using or selling it without consent, keep it safe, disclose or correct it when asked). Second: schools, hospitals and companies reacted very nervously — some schools stopped making class lists, some firms imposed strict checks — and a year on, complaints that daily life had become inconvenient are surfacing, especially at schools (children cannot phone friends, no emergency contact chains, local events for children have stopped). Why 3 (answer line p.23; 解説 p.24–25): 1, 4 ×: the book quotes the passage's examples — primary schools stopped making class and graduate lists, and some companies imposed strict checks — so companies and schools both took great care; neither can be the careless one. 2 × and 3 ○: the book quotes 「個人情報やプライバシーなどというものには、無頓着だった」. Only a group that was careless before and is overreacting now fits: the Japanese, compared with Western countries — and the article is about a Japanese law. (The book prints option 4 as "b 学校の現場 ／ b 企業"; the first "b" is evidently a misprint for "a".)
