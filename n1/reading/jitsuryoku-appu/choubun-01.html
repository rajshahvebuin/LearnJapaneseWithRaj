Passage

Passage — from 藤原正彦『国家の品格』(新潮社) (p.84–85)

Passage summary (not the book's text): The writer grants that the efficiency globalism brings is good in a way, then follows the logic to its end: the most efficient world would teach every newborn only English, so that in a few decades nobody would need to struggle with foreign languages and everything would run smoothly. The writer says that if the world came to that, it would be better for the Earth to be blown up along with humanity, because it would no longer be a planet worth living on. Efficiency may be fine, but the cultures, traditions and sensibilities that grew in each country, people and region are worth far more — economics is "only economics". Using the image of flowers, the writer says the world must never be turned into a single field of tulips: each region has its own flowers, and that variety is what makes the Earth beautiful, so we must not standardise everything because we are dazzled by efficiency. In that sense the 21st century should be an age of localism, in which people respect and nurture one another's traditions and cultures; each country's universal values are its core, and for Japan these are its fine sensibility and the culture it has produced.

Why 1 (answer line p.85; 解説 p.88): 1 ○: the book links 「効率・能率に幻惑されて、画一化を進めては絶対にいけないのです」 with 「ローカリズムの中核を成すのが、それぞれの国の持っているこうした普遍的価値です」 — "that sense" is "do not make each country's valuable things uniform". 2 ×: 文中にない — not in the text. 3 ×: the book cites the writer's view that such a world would be better blown up, since it would no longer be worth living on — he is not crediting globalism. 4 ×: the passage says 「効率・能率に幻惑されて、画一化を進めては絶対にいけない」 — standardising is exactly what must not be done.

問4

この文章で筆者が言いたいことは何か。Kono bunshou de hissha ga iitai koto wa nani ka.

EN: What does the writer want to say in this passage?   HI: इस पाठ में लेखक क्या कहना चाहता है?   GU: આ લખાણમાં લેખક શું કહેવા માગે છે?

Key phrases quoted in the 解説 (both already given above)

「文化や伝統や情緒などは、そんな能率・効率よりも遥かに価値が高い」・「各国家に生まれた伝統、文化、文学、情緒、形などを、世界中の人々が互いに尊重しあい」"Bunka ya dentou ya joucho nado wa, sonna nouritsu・kouritsu yori mo haruka ni kachi ga takai" ・ "Kakukokka ni umareta dentou, bunka, bungaku, joucho, katachi nado o, sekaijuu no hitobito ga tagai ni sonchou shiai"

EN: "Culture, tradition and sensibility are worth far more than such efficiency" · "people all over the world mutually respecting the traditions, culture, literature, sensibility and forms born in each nation".   HI: "संस्कृति, परंपरा और संवेदना ऐसी दक्षता से कहीं ज़्यादा मूल्यवान हैं" · "हर राष्ट्र में जन्मी परंपरा, संस्कृति, साहित्य, संवेदना और रूपों का दुनिया भर के लोग आपस में सम्मान करें"।   GU: "સંસ્કૃતિ, પરંપરા અને સંવેદના આવી કાર્યક્ષમતા કરતાં ક્યાંય વધુ મૂલ્યવાન છે" · "દરેક રાષ્ટ્રમાં જન્મેલી પરંપરા, સંસ્કૃતિ, સાહિત્ય, સંવેદના અને સ્વરૂપોનો દુનિયાભરના લોકો એકબીજાનો આદર કરે".

OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
1文化や伝統などを世界全体として統一することが重要である。そのことが結局はローカリズムの時代の中核となるものである。Bunka ya dentou nado o sekai zentai to shite touitsu suru koto ga juuyou de aru. Sono koto ga kekkyoku wa roukarizumu no jidai no chuukaku to naru mono de aru.It is important to unify culture and tradition across the whole world. That, in the end, becomes the core of the age of localism.संस्कृति और परंपरा को पूरी दुनिया में एक करना ज़रूरी है। यही अंततः स्थानीयतावाद के युग का मूल बनेगा।સંસ્કૃતિ અને પરંપરાને આખી દુનિયામાં એક કરવી જરૂરી છે. એ જ અંતે સ્થાનિકતાવાદના યુગનું મૂળ બનશે.
2二十一世紀はそれぞれの国が持つ普遍的価値を互いに尊重しあう時代である。その文化や伝統は効率性よりも価値がある。Nijuuisseiki wa sorezore no kuni ga motsu fuhenteki kachi o tagai ni sonchou shiau jidai de aru. Sono bunka ya dentou wa kouritsusei yori mo kachi ga aru.The 21st century is an age in which countries mutually respect the universal values each one has. Those cultures and traditions are worth more than efficiency.21वीं सदी ऐसा युग है जिसमें हर देश के सार्वभौमिक मूल्यों का आपस में सम्मान हो। वह संस्कृति और परंपरा दक्षता से ज़्यादा मूल्यवान है।21મી સદી એવો યુગ છે જેમાં દરેક દેશનાં સાર્વત્રિક મૂલ્યોનો એકબીજા આદર કરે. એ સંસ્કૃતિ અને પરંપરા કાર્યક્ષમતા કરતાં વધુ મૂલ્યવાન છે.
3世界中の皆が英語で意思の疎通ができるようになると、効率的な世界になる。そして、それが文化や伝統を守る上でも大切なこととなる。Sekaijuu no mina ga eigo de ishi no sotsuu ga dekiru you ni naru to, kouritsuteki na sekai ni naru. Soshite, sore ga bunka ya dentou o mamoru ue de mo taisetsu na koto to naru.If everyone in the world can communicate in English, the world becomes efficient — and that is also important for protecting culture and tradition.अगर दुनिया में सब अंग्रेज़ी में बात कर सकें तो दुनिया कुशल बनेगी, और यह संस्कृति-परंपरा बचाने के लिए भी ज़रूरी होगा।જો દુનિયામાં બધા અંગ્રેજીમાં વાત કરી શકે તો દુનિયા કાર્યક્ષમ બનશે, અને એ સંસ્કૃતિ-પરંપરા બચાવવા માટે પણ જરૂરી બનશે.
4グローバリズムにおける効率性は素晴らしいことだ。画一性を進めるためには、文化や伝統の価値も見直さねばならない。Guroobarizumu ni okeru kouritsusei wa subarashii koto da. Kakuitsusei o susumeru tame ni wa, bunka ya dentou no kachi mo minaosaneba naranai.Efficiency under globalism is wonderful. To advance uniformity, we must also reconsider the value of culture and tradition.वैश्वीकरण की दक्षता शानदार है। एकरूपता बढ़ाने के लिए संस्कृति-परंपरा के मूल्य पर भी फिर से सोचना होगा।વૈશ્વિકીકરણની કાર્યક્ષમતા સરસ છે. એકરૂપતા વધારવા સંસ્કૃતિ-પરંપરાના મૂલ્ય પર પણ ફરી વિચારવું પડશે.

Why 2 (answer line p.86; 解説 p.88): 2 ○: the book quotes 「文化や伝統や情緒などは、そんな能率・効率よりも遥かに価値が高い」 and 「各国家に生まれた伝統、文化、文学、情緒、形などを、世界中の人々が互いに尊重しあい」 — both halves of option 2. 1 ×: the passage says 「効率・能率に幻惑されて、画一化を進めては絶対にいけない」 — unifying culture is the opposite of the writer's view. 3 ×: of the all-English world, the writer says 「そんな世界になったなら、人間もろとも地球など爆発してなくなった方がよい」. 4 ×: the passage says culture is 「能率・効率よりも遥かに価値が高い」, so efficiency is not the writer's priority.
