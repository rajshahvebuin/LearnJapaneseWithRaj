1 Reading Strategy

Key idea

今週の技を、時間制限の中で全部使う。Konshuu no waza o, jikan seigen no naka de zenbu tsukau.

This is a capstone test, not a new lesson — it's meant to combine everything from Days 1–6 under real exam pressure:

  • 1. Apply Day 1's two-pass method to each passage separately. Don't try to hold both passages in your head at once — finish 問題1's skim-then-answer cycle completely, then start fresh on 問題2. The 20-minute limit (10 questions across two long passages) means roughly 2 minutes per question, so don't over-invest in any single one.

問題1 (Mondai 1)

Passage

Passage

以前、企業の部課長クラスを対象としたセミナーの後の雑談で、上司の指示命令を部下がやり過ごしてしまうこともあるのでは？と水を向けて注1みたことがあります。その途端、ある大企業の部長からお叱りを受けました。いわく①「組織の中にあって、上司から出された命令や指示をやり過ごしてしまうなどということはあってはならないことである」。しかし後になって、もっと話を聞きたいという人がやってきました。そして驚いたことに、やり過ごしのできない部下は無能であるとまで言い切る人さえ現れたのです。（中略）Izen, kigyou no bukachou kurasu o taishou to shita seminaa no ato no zatsudan de, joushi no shiji meirei o buka ga yarisugoshite shimau koto mo aru no de wa? to mizu o mukete mita koto ga arimasu. Sono totan, aru dai-kigyou no buchou kara oshikari o ukemashita. Iwaku ① "Soshiki no naka ni atte, joushi kara dasareta meirei ya shiji o yarisugoshite shimau nado to iu koto wa atte wa naranai koto de aru." Shikashi ato ni natte, motto hanashi o kikitai to iu hito ga yattekimashita. Soshite odoroita koto ni, yarisugoshi no dekinai buka wa munou de aru to made iikiru hito sae arawareta no desu. (Chuuryaku)

実際の企業を調査すると、「やり過ごし」には重要な機能のあることがわかってきました。たとえば、業務量が非常に多く、忙しい職場では、上司の指示命令のすべてに応えることはもともと不可能なので、部下が自ら優先順位をつけ、上司の指示命令を上手にやり過ごしながら、時間と労力を節約して業務をこなしていくことをむしろ期待されているといいます。それができない部下は、言われたことをやるだけで、自分の仕事を管理する能力がないと低い評価しか得られないいそうですから、②世の中厳しいものです。Jissai no kigyou o chousa suru to, "yarisugoshi" ni wa juuyou na kinou no aru koto ga wakatte kimashita. Tatoeba, gyoumuryou ga hijou ni ooku, isogashii shokuba de wa, joushi no shiji meirei no subete ni kotaeru koto wa motomoto fukanou na node, buka ga mizukara yuusen jun'i o tsuke, joushi no shiji meirei o jouzu ni yarisugoshinagara, jikan to rouryoku o setsuyaku shite gyoumu o konashite iku koto o mushiro kitai sarete iru to iimasu. Sore ga dekinai buka wa, iwareta koto o yaru dake de, jibun no shigoto o kanri suru nouryoku ga nai to hikui hyouka shika erarenai sou desu kara, ② yo no naka kibishii mono desu.

あるいは、管理者の人事異動が頻繁で、実際の業務知識に乏しく経験も浅い管理者がよく巡って来るような職場では、その業務に長年従事し、職人としての知識をもつ部下にとっては、反論するのもばかばかしい指示が時としてなされることもあります。しかも悪いことに、面と向かって上司の指示がいかにナンセンスなものであるかを部下が立証したとしても、それを受け入れる度量の広さを上司が持ち合わせていないことも多く、③そんな場合、職場の人間関係はぎくしゃくするだけで終ることになります。「殿様が白といったらカラスも白いんだ」とわめいた上司もいるそうです。これをバカ殿状況と呼んだ人がいましたが、こうした状況下では、的はずれな指示は部下のやり過ごしによって濾過注2され、上司に恥をかかせずに、正当な指示に対する業務だけがラインに流れることになるといいます。Arui wa, kanrisha no jinji idou ga hinpan de, jissai no gyoumu chishiki ni toboshiku keiken mo asai kanrisha ga yoku megutte kuru you na shokuba de wa, sono gyoumu ni naganen juuji shi, shokunin to shite no chishiki o motsu buka ni totte wa, hanron suru no mo bakabakashii shiji ga toki toshite nasareru koto mo arimasu. Shikamo warui koto ni, men to mukatte joushi no shiji ga ika ni nansensu na mono de aru ka o buka ga risshou shita to shite mo, sore o ukeireru doryou no hirosa o joushi ga mochiawasete inai koto mo ooku, ③ sonna baai, shokuba no ningen kankei wa gikushaku suru dake de owaru koto ni narimasu. "Tonosama ga shiro to ittara karasu mo shiroi nda" to wameita joushi mo iru sou desu. Kore o baka-dono joukyou to yonda hito ga imashita ga, kou shita joukyou-ka de wa, matohazure na shiji wa buka no yarisugoshi ni yotte roka sare, joushi ni haji o kakasezu ni, seitou na shiji ni taisuru gyoumu dake ga rain ni nagareru koto ni naru to iimasu.

つまり組織の中における「やり過ごし」には、仕事の過大負荷や上司の低信頼性に対処して、組織的な破綻注3を回避するという注目すべき機能もあるのです。Tsumari soshiki no naka ni okeru "yarisugoshi" ni wa, shigoto no kadai fuka ya joushi no tei-shinraisei ni taisho shite, soshikiteki na hatan o kaihi suru to iu chuumoku subeki kinou mo aru no desu.

注1 水をむける：相手の関心を引くような問い方をする Mizu o mukeru: aite no kanshin o hiku you na toi-kata o suru — "to ask in a way that draws out someone's interest/reaction"　·　注2 濾過：液体などから布や紙などを使っていらないものを取り除くこと Roka: ekitai nado kara nuno ya kami nado o tsukatte iranai mono o torinozoku koto — "filtration: removing unwanted matter from a liquid using cloth or paper"　·　注3 破綻：物事がだめになること Hatan: monogoto ga dame ni naru koto — "breakdown: things falling apart/failing"　·　出典: 高橋伸夫「モデル-ジャンケンを通して見る意思決定の戦略」『知の技法』東京大学出版会 (Takahashi Nobuo, "Moderu - Janken o Tooshite Miru Ishi Kettei no Senryaku," Chi no Gihou, Toukyou Daigaku Shuppankai)

EN: Some time ago, in casual conversation after a seminar for section-chief and department-head level employees, I asked whether subordinates sometimes let a boss's orders slide. A department head from a large company immediately scolded me: "Within an organization, a subordinate must never let a superior's order or instruction slide." But afterward, people came wanting to hear more — and, surprisingly, some even declared that a subordinate who can't let things slide is incompetent. Investigating real companies revealed that "letting things slide" (yarisugoshi) serves an important function. In busy workplaces with a heavy workload, responding to every single order is simply impossible, so subordinates are actually expected to set their own priorities and skillfully let some orders slide to save time and effort. A subordinate who can't do this — who only does exactly what's said — ends up rated poorly as lacking the ability to manage their own work. So it's a harsh world. Or, in workplaces where managers rotate frequently and lack real knowledge or experience, subordinates with long experience and craftsman-level knowledge sometimes receive instructions so absurd that arguing back feels pointless. Worse, even when a subordinate proves to the boss's face how nonsensical an order is, many bosses lack the generosity to accept it — and in such cases, workplace relationships just get strained. Some bosses reportedly even shout things like "If the lord says it's white, the crow is white too!" Someone called this the "foolish lord" situation; under it, off-target orders get filtered out by subordinates letting them slide, sparing the boss embarrassment, so only work matching legitimate instructions actually flows down the line. In short, "letting things slide" within an organization also has a notable function: coping with excessive workload and low trust in a boss, and thereby avoiding organizational breakdown.
HI: कुछ समय पहले, कंपनी के विभाग-प्रमुख स्तर के लोगों के सेमिनार के बाद अनौपचारिक बातचीत में, मैंने पूछा कि क्या अधीनस्थ कभी-कभी बॉस के आदेशों को टाल देते हैं। एक बड़ी कंपनी के विभाग-प्रमुख ने फ़ौरन मुझे डाँट दिया: "किसी संस्था में, अधीनस्थ का ऊपर से आए आदेश को टाल देना कभी नहीं होना चाहिए।" पर बाद में, कुछ लोग और सुनने आए — और हैरानी की बात, कुछ ने तो कह दिया कि जो अधीनस्थ आदेश टाल नहीं सकता, वह अयोग्य है। असली कंपनियों की पड़ताल से पता चला कि "टालना" का एक ज़रूरी काम होता है। ज़्यादा काम वाली व्यस्त जगहों पर हर आदेश का पालन करना नामुमकिन होता है, इसलिए अधीनस्थों से ख़ुद प्राथमिकता तय कर, कुशलता से कुछ आदेश टालकर समय-मेहनत बचाने की उम्मीद ही की जाती है। जो ऐसा नहीं कर पाता — जो सिर्फ़ कहा गया वही करता है — उसे काम संभालने की क्षमता न होने का दोषी मानकर कम अंक मिलते हैं। तो दुनिया सख़्त जगह है। या फिर, जहाँ मैनेजर बार-बार बदलते और असली जानकारी-अनुभव कम रखते हैं, वहाँ लंबे अनुभव वाले अधीनस्थों को कभी-कभी इतने बेतुके आदेश मिलते हैं कि विरोध करना बेकार लगता है। बुरी बात यह कि, अधीनस्थ सामने साबित भी कर दे कि आदेश बेतुका है, फिर भी कई बॉस में उसे स्वीकारने की उदारता नहीं होती — और तब बस रिश्ते बिगड़ते हैं। कुछ बॉस तो "राजा कहे सफ़ेद तो कौवा भी सफ़ेद" जैसा चिल्ला उठते हैं। किसी ने इसे "मूर्ख राजा" स्थिति कहा; ऐसे में, बेतुके आदेश अधीनस्थों की टालमटोल से छन जाते हैं, बॉस की बेइज़्ज़ती नहीं होती, और सही आदेशों वाला काम ही आगे बढ़ता है। यानी संस्था में "टालना" का एक ध्यान देने लायक काम भी है: ज़्यादा बोझ और बॉस पर कम भरोसे से निपटकर, संस्था को टूटने से बचाना।
GU: થોડા સમય પહેલાં, કંપનીના વિભાગ-વડા સ્તરના લોકોના સેમિનાર પછીની અનૌપચારિક વાતચીતમાં, મેં પૂછ્યું કે શું ગૌણ કર્મચારીઓ ક્યારેક બોસના આદેશો ટાળે છે. એક મોટી કંપનીના વિભાગ-વડાએ તરત મને ઠપકો આપ્યો: "કોઈ સંસ્થામાં, ગૌણ કર્મચારીનું ઉપરથી આવેલો આદેશ ટાળવો ક્યારેય ન થવો જોઈએ." પણ પછી, કેટલાક લોકો વધુ સાંભળવા આવ્યા — અને નવાઈની વાત, કેટલાકે તો કહ્યું કે જે આદેશ ટાળી ન શકે, તે અયોગ્ય છે. ખરી કંપનીઓની તપાસથી ખબર પડી કે "ટાળવા"નું એક મહત્વનું કામ હોય છે. વધુ કામવાળી વ્યસ્ત જગ્યાઓએ દરેક આદેશનું પાલન અશક્ય હોય છે, એટલે ગૌણ કર્મચારીઓ પાસેથી જાતે અગ્રતા નક્કી કરી, કુશળતાથી અમુક આદેશો ટાળી સમય-મહેનત બચાવવાની અપેક્ષા જ રખાય છે. જે આવું ન કરી શકે — જે ફક્ત કહેવાયેલું જ કરે — તેને કામ સંભાળવાની ક્ષમતા ન હોવાનો દોષી ગણી ઓછા ગુણ મળે છે. તો દુનિયા કડક જગ્યા છે. અથવા, જ્યાં મેનેજર વારંવાર બદલાય અને ખરી જાણકારી-અનુભવ ઓછો ધરાવે, ત્યાં લાંબા અનુભવવાળા ગૌણ કર્મચારીઓને ક્યારેક એટલા બેહૂદા આદેશો મળે છે કે વિરોધ કરવો નકામો લાગે છે. ખરાબ વાત એ કે, ગૌણ કર્મચારી સામે સાબિત પણ કરી દે કે આદેશ બેહૂદો છે, છતાં ઘણા બોસમાં તેને સ્વીકારવાની ઉદારતા હોતી નથી — અને ત્યારે ફક્ત સંબંધો બગડે છે. અમુક બોસ તો "રાજા કહે સફેદ તો કાગડો પણ સફેદ" જેવું બરાડી ઊઠે છે. કોઈએ આને "મૂર્ખ રાજા" સ્થિતિ કહી; આવામાં, બેહૂદા આદેશો ગૌણ કર્મચારીઓની ટાળમટોળથી ગળાઈ જાય છે, બોસની બેઇજ્જતી થતી નથી, અને યોગ્ય આદેશોવાળું કામ જ આગળ વધે છે. એટલે કે સંસ્થામાં "ટાળવા"નું એક ધ્યાન આપવા લાયક કામ પણ છે: વધુ બોજ અને બોસ પરના ઓછા ભરોસાનો સામનો કરી, સંસ્થાને તૂટવાથી બચાવવી.

1We should make an effort to resolve economic friction.आर्थिक टकराव सुलझाने की कोशिश करनी चाहिए।આર્થિક ઘર્ષણ ઉકેલવાનો પ્રયત્ન કરવો જોઈએ.
2We should give more support to foreign cultural projects.विदेशी सांस्कृतिक परियोजनाओं की और मदद करनी चाहिए।વિદેશી સાંસ્કૃતિક પરિયોજનાઓને વધુ મદદ કરવી જોઈએ.
3We should accept more Asian music students.एशियाई संगीत छात्रों को और स्वीकारना चाहिए।એશિયન સંગીત વિદ્યાર્થીઓને વધુ સ્વીકારવા જોઈએ.
4We should study abroad in Europe more and become more international.यूरोप में और पढ़ने जाकर ज़्यादा अंतरराष्ट्रीय बनना चाहिए।યુરોપમાં વધુ ભણવા જઈને વધુ આંતરરાષ્ટ્રીય બનવું જોઈએ.

Why: The essay's title itself, 「国際社会への恩返し」("Repaying International Society"), and its closing line — 日本が受けた恩恵に見合った分は返したいものと…いつも思っている — both point to the same overarching thesis: Japan should support foreign cultural projects more generously, in return for the benefits it has received (option 2). The Concertgebouw donation shortfall and the Southeast Asian student example (option 3) are both supporting illustrations of this same broader point, not the main point on their own.

QuestionStrategy usedWhat the question testsTrap to avoid
1 いわく attributes a quote to the person named just before it Who is the speaker of a quoted opinion Don't default to "the author" for a quoted opinion the author is actually reacting to.
2 Read the passage's own functional definition of a term やり過ごし = deliberate, skillful deprioritizing, not forgetting or laziness The everyday sense of "letting something slide" can suggest carelessness — the passage frames it as a deliberate skill instead.
3 Read the clause immediately before から ("because") The stated cause of "the world being harsh" Option 4 reverses cause and effect — the passage says skillful time-saving IS rewarded, not penalized.
4 Trace そんな場合 to the clause right before it The specific trigger for damaged workplace relationships Several nearby facts (1–3) are all true, but only one is what そんな場合 grammatically refers to.
5 Find the essay's own concluding つまり sentence The essay's actual thesis, not its supporting details Vivid supporting details (the "foolish lord" story) are memorable but aren't the main point being argued.
6 Read an exclamation (なんと) against the numbers surrounding it What specifically the exclamation is surprised about Don't assume every strong reaction is about difficulty or timing — check what's actually being contrasted.
7 Trace それ to the full preceding clause, not a fragment of it それ covers the whole "cooperate only for self-promotion" behavior Options 2–4 are each part of the picture, but none alone is the full thing the author objects to.
8 Read でなければ as continuing the immediately preceding sentence The specific condition being negated Nearby ideas about trade friction and receiving benefits are easy to substitute for the actual preceding clause.
9 Read なおさら as reinforcing an argument already being made, not introducing a new one "All the more reason" points to the essay's ongoing call to support the Netherlands Don't read なおさら as pointing backward to a separate claim about receiving benefits.
