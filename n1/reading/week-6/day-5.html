1 Reading Strategy

Key idea

論説文は「主張」と「たとえ」を分けて読む。Ronsetsubun wa "shuchou" to "tatoe" o wakete yomu.

論説文 (formal argumentative essays) are more structured than personal essays. Two habits help here:

  • 1. Separate the writer's actual claim from the analogy used to illustrate it. This essay's real subject is the Japanese language becoming shared international property — but it explains that claim through an extended analogy to 柔道 (judo) becoming a world sport. Don't answer a question about Japanese by quoting the judo sentence, and vice versa — map the analogy point-by-point instead.
    HI: लेखक के असली दावे को उसे समझाने वाले उदाहरण/उपमा से अलग करके पढ़ें। यहाँ असली विषय जापानी भाषा है, जूडो सिर्फ़ समझाने के लिए दी गई उपमा है — दोनों को जोड़कर, बिंदु-दर-बिंदु मिलाएँ। GU: લેખકના ખરા દાવાને તેને સમજાવતા ઉદાહરણ/ઉપમાથી અલગ કરીને વાંચો. અહીં ખરો વિષય જાપાની ભાષા છે, જુડો ફક્ત સમજાવવા માટેની ઉપમા છે — બંનેને જોડીને, મુદ્દા-દર-મુદ્દા મેળવો.
  • 2. A rhetorical それはそれでよいのではないか ("and wouldn't that be fine?") signals the author's own stance, not a genuine question. Just like in essays, 論説文 uses this pattern to state an opinion gently — treat it as the writer saying "I think that's fine."
    HI: 「それはそれでよいのではないか」जैसा वाक्य असल में सवाल नहीं, लेखक की अपनी राय है — इसे "मुझे लगता है यह ठीक है" जैसा समझें। GU: 「それはそれでよいのではないか」જેવું વાક્ય ખરેખર પ્રશ્ન નથી, લેખકનો પોતાનો અભિપ્રાય છે — તેને "મને લાગે છે આ બરાબર છે" જેવું સમજો.

Passage

外国人に対して自国語の普及を推進するということは、一歩まちがうと自国文化のおしつけになりかねないし、文化侵略という非難をうけかねない。戦前の外国における日本語の普及には、そのようなきらいがあった*。①現在の状況はとてもそんなことではない。積極的な普及どころか、世界の要望のまえに受け身でたたされて、その需要に応じきれないというのが実情である。Gaikokujin ni taishite jikokugo no fukyuu o suishin suru to iu koto wa, ippo machigau to jikoku bunka no oshitsuke ni narikanenai shi, bunka shinryaku to iu hinan o ukekanenai. Senzen no gaikoku ni okeru nihongo no fukyuu ni wa, sono you na kirai ga atta. ① Genzai no joukyou wa totemo sonna koto de wa nai. Sekkyokuteki na fukyuu dokoro ka, sekai no youbou no mae ni ukemi de tatasarete, sono juyou ni oujikirenai to iu no ga jitsujou de aru.

世界のなかの日本語人口が増加すると、日本語はいずれ②ひとりあるきをはじめるだろう。外国人どうしのコミュニケーションに日本語がつかわれるようになるのである。日本人は日本語を自分たちの所有物であり、私有財産だとおもってきた。日本語をじょうずにあやつる外国人に対しては、なにか自分たちの聖域をおかされたような気もちをいだくひとがおおいのではないか。世界において日本語の使用人口が増大すると、そのような私有物感覚では対応できなくなるだろう。日本語は世界人類の共有財産の一部となるのである。日本人は、日本語を人類の共有にゆだねる注1べくさしだしたのである。ちょうど、柔道が世界的スポーツになったようなものである。柔道の起源は日本に発するが、いまや世界人類の共有するスポーツであって、日本人の独占物ではない。Sekai no naka no nihongo jinkou ga zouka suru to, nihongo wa izure ② hitori aruki o hajimeru darou. Gaikokujin doushi no komyunikeeshon ni nihongo ga tsukawareru you ni naru no de aru. Nihonjin wa nihongo o jibuntachi no shoyuubutsu de ari, shiyuu zaisan da to omotte kita. Nihongo o jouzu ni ayatsuru gaikokujin ni taishite wa, nanika jibuntachi no seiiki o okasareta you na kimochi o idaku hito ga ooi no de wa nai ka. Sekai ni oite nihongo no shiyou jinkou ga zoudai suru to, sono you na shiyuubutsu kankaku de wa taiou dekinaku naru darou. Nihongo wa sekai jinrui no kyouyuu zaisan no ichibu to naru no de aru. Nihonjin wa, nihongo o jinrui no kyouyuu ni yudaneru beku sashidashita no de aru. Choudo, juudou ga sekaiteki supootsu ni natta you na mono de aru. Juudou no kigen wa nihon ni hassuru ga, ima ya sekai jinrui no kyouyuu suru supootsu de atte, nihonjin no dokusenbutsu de wa nai.

日本国民の私有物から世界人類の共有財産にうつるとともに、そこではたぶんある程度の改変作用がおこるだろう**。柔道が国際化するとともに、体重別の制度が導入されたように、日本語も国際化とともに、なんらかのルールの変革がおこる可能性がある。たとえば、敬語が現状のままでおこなわれるかどうかには疑問がある。それはそれでよいのではないか。Nihon kokumin no shiyuubutsu kara sekai jinrui no kyouyuu zaisan ni utsuru to tomo ni, soko de wa tabun aru teido no kaihen sayou ga okoru darou. Juudou ga kokusaika suru to tomo ni, taijuubetsu no seido ga dounyuu sareta you ni, nihongo mo kokusaika to tomo ni, nanraka no ruuru no henkaku ga okoru kanousei ga aru. Tatoeba, keigo ga genjou no mama de okonawareru ka dou ka ni wa gimon ga aru. Sore wa sore de yoi no de wa nai ka.

国際化とともに、日本人には聞きなれない奇妙な表現が外国人の日本語のなかにはあらわれてきてもふしぎではない。ときには、かなり聞きぐるしいこともでてくるであろう。わたしはそれを「おぞましい注2日本語」といっている。日本人は、そのような「おぞましい日本語」をきくことにたえなければならないのである。わたしたちのつかう英語は、おそらくはイギリス人には「おぞましい英語」であろう。イギリス人は、世界でおこなわれている「おぞましい英語」にたえて、それを寛容にみとめているのである。言語の国際化とはそういうものであろう。Kokusaika to tomo ni, nihonjin ni wa kikinarenai kimyou na hyougen ga gaikokujin no nihongo no naka ni wa arawarete kite mo fushigi de wa nai. Toki ni wa, kanari kikigurushii koto mo dete kuru de arou. Watashi wa sore o "ozomashii nihongo" to itte iru. Nihonjin wa, sono you na "ozomashii nihongo" o kiku koto ni taenakereba naranai no de aru. Watashitachi no tsukau eigo wa, osoraku wa igirisujin ni wa "ozomashii eigo" de arou. Igirisujin wa, sekai de okonawarete iru "ozomashii eigo" ni taete, sore o kanyou ni mitomete iru no de aru. Gengo no kokusaika to wa sou iu mono de arou.

現在、国際語とかんがえられている言語は、いずれも③こういう試練注3をうけて発達してきたものであろう。英語なども国際化とともに、かなりかわってきたものらしい。外国人の手による改変とともに、イギリス人自身が外国人にわかりやすいものにしようと努力して、つくりかえたところがすくなくないようである。日本語もその過程をへるのではないか。Genzai, kokusaigo to kangaerarete iru gengo wa, izure mo ③ kou iu shiren o ukete hattatsu shite kita mono de arou. Eigo nado mo kokusaika to tomo ni, kanari kawatte kita mono rashii. Gaikokujin no te ni yoru kaihen to tomo ni, igirisujin jishin ga gaikokujin ni wakariyasui mono ni shiyou to doryoku shite, tsukurikaeta tokoro ga sukunakunai you de aru. Nihongo mo sono katei o heru no de wa nai ka.

注1 ゆだねる：まかせる Yudaneru: makaseru — "to entrust, leave up to"　·　注2 おぞましい：とてもいやな感じがする Ozomashii: totemo iya na kanji ga suru — "repulsive: gives a very unpleasant feeling"　·　注3 試練：能力を試される苦しみ Shiren: nouryoku o tamesareru kurushimi — "trial: hardship in which one's ability is put to the test"　·　* footnote: "To encourage foreigners to learn the language of your country could lead to a forceful introduction of your culture or to a cultural invasion. The spread of the Japanese language in foreign countries before World War II had this tendency."　** footnote: "As Japanese culture and traditions become international treasures shared by many, changes will no doubt occur."　·　出典: 梅棹忠夫『実戦・世界言語紀行』岩波書店 (Umesao Tadao, "Jissen Sekai Gengo Kikou," Iwanami Shoten)

EN: Actively promoting the spread of one's own language to foreigners can, if handled even slightly wrong, turn into forcing one's own culture on others and risks being criticized as cultural invasion. Japan's prewar spread of Japanese abroad had exactly that tendency. The present situation is nothing like that. Far from actively promoting it, Japan stands passively before the world's demand, unable to fully meet that need — that's the reality. As the world's Japanese-speaking population grows, Japanese will eventually start walking on its own, coming to be used for communication between foreigners themselves. The Japanese have long thought of Japanese as their own private property. Toward foreigners who handle Japanese skillfully, many Japanese probably feel as if some sacred territory of theirs has been violated. But as the number of Japanese-speakers worldwide grows, that sense of private ownership won't work anymore. Japanese will become part of humanity's shared property — the Japanese have, in effect, offered up their language to be entrusted to humanity's shared use, just as judo became a worldwide sport. Judo originated in Japan, but it's now a sport shared by all humanity, not something the Japanese monopolize. As it moves from being Japan's private property to humanity's shared property, some degree of transformation will likely occur. Just as judo's internationalization brought a weight-class system, Japanese may see some rule changes too as it internationalizes — for instance, it's doubtful whether honorific speech will stay exactly as it is now, and that would be perfectly fine. With internationalization, it wouldn't be strange for unfamiliar, odd expressions to appear in foreigners' Japanese; sometimes it may sound fairly harsh to the ear. I call this "cringe-worthy Japanese." The Japanese will have to bear hearing that kind of "cringe-worthy Japanese." The English we speak is probably, to the British, "cringe-worthy English." The British endure the "cringe-worthy English" spoken around the world and tolerantly accept it. That is what the internationalization of a language is. Any language currently considered international has surely developed by going through exactly this kind of trial. English, too, seems to have changed considerably as it internationalized — alongside changes made by foreigners, the British themselves have made no small effort to remake their language into something easier for foreigners to understand. Won't Japanese go through that same process too?

कभी-कभी यह सुनने में काफ़ी कठोर भी लग सकता है। मैं इसे "कर्णकटु जापानी" कहता हूँ। जापानियों को ऐसी "कर्णकटु जापानी" सुनना सहना होगा। हम जापानी जो अंग्रेज़ी बोलते हैं, वह शायद अंग्रेज़ों को "कर्णकटु अंग्रेज़ी" ही लगती होगी। अंग्रेज़ दुनियाभर में बोली जाने वाली "कर्णकटु अंग्रेज़ी" को सहते हैं और उदारता से स्वीकारते हैं। भाषा का अंतरराष्ट्रीयकरण यही होता है। आज जिन भाषाओं को अंतरराष्ट्रीय भाषा माना जाता है, वे सभी शायद इसी तरह की परीक्षा से गुज़रकर विकसित हुई हैं। अंग्रेज़ी भी अंतरराष्ट्रीय होने के साथ काफ़ी बदल गई लगती है — विदेशियों द्वारा किए बदलावों के साथ-साथ, अंग्रेज़ों ने ख़ुद भी अपनी भाषा को विदेशियों के लिए आसान बनाने की कोशिश करते हुए उसमें काफ़ी बदलाव किए हैं। क्या जापानी भाषा भी उसी प्रक्रिया से नहीं गुज़रेगी?

4聖域をおかされぬよう日本語に対する意識を強くすることseiiki o okasarenu you nihongo ni taisuru ishiki o tsuyoku suru kotoStrengthening one's guard over Japanese so its "sanctuary" isn't violatedजापानी भाषा को "पवित्र क्षेत्र" मानकर उसकी और सख़्ती से रक्षा करनाજાપાનીને "પવિત્ર ક્ષેત્ર" ગણી તેની વધુ કડક રક્ષા કરવી

Why: ③こういう試練 refers back to the whole preceding paragraph, whose point is: 日本人は、そのような「おぞましい日本語」をきくことにたえなければならない…イギリス人は、世界でおこなわれている「おぞましい英語」にたえて、それを寛容にみとめているのである — enduring the foreignized version of one's own language and tolerantly accepting it. That's option 2. Option 4 is actually the opposite instinct (guarding the language jealously) that the essay argues against; options 1 and 3 introduce activities (research, studying the British) that this paragraph doesn't describe.

問４　日本語についての筆者の考えは次のうちどれか。Toi 4: Nihongo ni tsuite no hissha no kangae wa tsugi no uchi dore ka.

1日本語が聞きぐるしい奇妙な言語となっていくのは好ましい傾向である。Nihongo ga kikigurushii kimyou na gengo to natte iku no wa konomashii keikou de aru.It's desirable for Japanese to become a harsh, strange-sounding language.जापानी का अजीब, कर्णकटु भाषा बनना अच्छा रुझान है।જાપાનીનું વિચિત્ર, કર્કશ ભાષા બનવું સારું વલણ છે.

Why: The essay's overall argument, capped by its final rhetorical question 日本語もその過程をへるのではないか ("won't Japanese go through that same process too?"), is that internationalization inevitably brings change/distortion — as it did for judo and for English — and that this is fine (それはそれでよいのではないか).
