1 Reading Strategy

Key idea

「〜ではないでしょうか」は疑問文ではなく、筆者の意見だ。"~ de wa nai deshou ka" wa gimonbun de wa naku, hissha no iken da.

Essays (エッセイ) argue a personal opinion, often indirectly. Two things to watch for in this genre:

  • 1. Rhetorical questions are statements in disguise. A line ending in 「〜ではないでしょうか」("isn't it the case that...?") or 「〜と思ってはいないでしょうか」("don't you think that...?") is not really asking the reader anything — it's the author's own claim, phrased more gently. Read it as if it ended in 「〜のです」 or 「〜と思います」.
    HI: 「〜ではないでしょうか」से खत्म होने वाला वाक्य असल में सवाल नहीं, बल्कि लेखक की अपनी राय है — इसे 「〜है」 जैसा मानकर पढ़ें। GU: 「〜ではないでしょうか」થી પૂરું થતું વાક્ય ખરેખર પ્રશ્ન નથી, પણ લેખકનો પોતાનો અભિપ્રાય છે — તેને 「〜છે」 જેવું ગણીને વાંચો.
  • 2. Track the essay's turn ("if you flip your viewpoint..."). This essay pivots halfway through with これはちょっと見方を変えてみればわかることで ("you'll understand this if you just change your viewpoint") — everything after that turn (being loved by someone you dislike) is a mirror held up to everything before it (loving your own child/husband). Questions often hinge on matching up the two halves.
    HI: यह निबंध बीच में नज़रिया बदलने का मोड़ लेता है — मोड़ के बाद की बात मोड़ से पहले की बात का आईना है। दोनों हिस्सों को जोड़कर पढ़ें। GU: આ નિબંધ વચ્ચે દૃષ્ટિકોણ બદલવાનો વળાંક લે છે — વળાંક પછીની વાત વળાંક પહેલાંની વાતનું અરીસા જેવું પ્રતિબિંબ છે. બંને ભાગોને જોડીને વાંચો.

It doesn't occur to her that, in the other person's position, she might have done the same thing. Doesn't a certain logic start working inside you — that because you love them, anything is forgivable? Don't you believe "for everyone's sake" excuses everything? That kind of reasoning — the kind a nineteenth-century woman might use — is something I think new women should throw away. 問１　①同じようなこととは、どんなことか。Toi 1: ① Onaji you na koto to wa, donna koto ka.

OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati

Why: The essay opens: a woman criticizing someone falls into thinking she's right, without considering 自分も相手のような立場にあったら①同じようなことをしたかもしれない — "she too, in the other person's position, might have done ①the same kind of thing." "The same kind of thing" as what the person being criticized did — i.e. 非難されるようなこと, the very thing that triggered the criticism (option 1). Options 2–4 all describe the critic's own mindset, not the action being mirrored back onto her.

Why: 息が詰まる literally means "one's breath gets blocked" — a set expression for feeling suffocated or stifled, i.e. trapped with no freedom, which the essay uses negatively (it directly parallels 子供を窒息させている, "suffocating the child," a few lines earlier). Options 2 and 3 wrongly read it as a positive, emotional reaction; option 1 makes it about physical illness rather than a feeling of being restricted. Only option 4 captures the idiom's real sense of unwanted confinement.

Why: This is the essay's "flip the viewpoint" turn (strategy #2 above): being pushed at by a man you dislike, purely because he "likes you," is the mirror image of a mother imposing smothering love on her child in the name of loving them. Option 1/2 miss the essay's critical stance entirely, and option 3 misidentifies the problem as "illusion" rather than the imposition it actually is.
