1 Reading Strategy

Key idea

小説は「事実」ではなく「人物の気持ち」を追う。Shousetsu wa "jijitsu" de wa naku "jinbutsu no kimochi" o ou.

The book doesn't print a new tip box for fiction — but reading a 小説 (short story/novel excerpt) does need a different focus from reading an essay, so here's how to adapt Day 1's method to this genre:

  • 1. Track who is speaking. Fiction jumps between dialogue and narration without always naming the speaker — use Day 1's "box the characters, note who says 「 」" trick constantly here. In this story there are only two people (患者 "the patient" and 医者 "the doctor"), so misreading who says a line completely flips the meaning.
    HI: कौन बोल रहा है यह लगातार ट्रैक करें। कहानी में सिर्फ़ दो किरदार हैं (मरीज़ और डॉक्टर), इसलिए बोलने वाले को ग़लत समझना पूरा मतलब बदल देता है। GU: કોણ બોલે છે તે સતત ટ્રેક કરો. વાર્તામાં ફક્ત બે પાત્રો છે (દર્દી અને ડૉક્ટર), એટલે બોલનારને ખોટું સમજવાથી આખો અર્થ બદલાઈ જાય છે.
  • 2. Underlined ①〜④ phrases are almost always referents or key actions. When a word or clause is underlined and numbered, the question is nearly always "what does this point to?" or "what does this actually mean here?" — read the sentence immediately before and after it, exactly as Day 1's arrow-marking technique suggests.
    HI: रेखांकित ①〜④ हिस्से लगभग हमेशा किसी चीज़ की ओर इशारा करते हैं या मुख्य घटना होते हैं — ठीक पहले और बाद का वाक्य पढ़ें। GU: રેખાંકિત ①〜④ ભાગો લગભગ હંમેશા કોઈ વસ્તુ તરફ ઈશારો કરે છે અથવા મુખ્ય ઘટના હોય છે — બરાબર પહેલાં અને પછીનું વાક્ય વાંચો.
  • 3. A physical gesture (like "tilting one's head") signals an emotion. Japanese fiction often shows feeling through action instead of stating it directly — 首をひねる ("tilting/twisting the head") is a set gesture for puzzlement or doubt, not literal confusion about direction.
    HI: "सिर हिलाना/मोड़ना" जैसा भाव-भंगिमा किसी भावना का संकेत है — यह हैरानी/शक जताने का एक तयशुदा तरीका है, सिर्फ़ शारीरिक हरकत नहीं। GU: "માથું નમાવવું/મરોડવું" જેવો હાવભાવ કોઈ લાગણીનો સંકેત છે — તે મૂંઝવણ/શંકા બતાવવાની એક ઠરેલી રીત છે, ફક્ત શારીરિક હલનચલન નથી.

「昨夜、悪魔がやってきました」と、患者は医者にいった。「癌で死にかけているわたしに、地獄へ落ちてもいいのなら、もっと生き続けさせてやるという取り引きを①申し出たのです*。もちろん、わたしは喜んでこの申し出を受けましたよ。わたしはまだ生き続けなきゃならんのです。仕事がありますのでね」"Sakuya, akuma ga yattekimashita" to, kanja wa isha ni itta. "Gan de shinikakete iru watashi ni, jigoku e ochite mo ii no nara, motto ikitsuzukesasete yaru to iu torihiki o ① moushideta no desu. Mochiron, watashi wa yorokonde kono moushide o ukemashita yo. Watashi wa mada ikitsuzukenakya naran no desu. Shigoto ga arimasu node ne."

そこは癌研究所の患者病棟の一室だった。医者は、この患者のことばに、あまり驚かなかった。死期の迫った患者の妄想だろうと判断したのである**。なにしろこの患者は、ただ胃癌であるというだけではなくその癌が食道や肝臓へも移っていて、②死ぬのは時間の問題だと思われていたからである。Soko wa gan kenkyuujo no kanja byoutou no isshitsu datta. Isha wa, kono kanja no kotoba ni, amari odorokanakatta. Shiki no sematta kanja no mousou darou to handan shita no de aru. Nanishiro kono kanja wa, tada igan de aru to iu dake de wa naku sono gan ga shokudou ya kanzou e mo utsutte ite, ② shinu no wa jikan no mondai da to omowarete ita kara de aru.

ところがそれから一カ月が経ち、二カ月経っても、患者は死ななかった。それどころではない。入院当初は半死半生だったこの患者はますます血色がよくなり、元気になってきたのだ。しかも癌はどんどん彼の全身に拡がり、彼の内臓の器官すべてに移り、今や人間のからだの中に癌があるというよりは、癌が人間の形をしているといった方が早いというような状態になった。医者は診断のしようがなく、これにはただ、あきれるばかりだった。Tokoro ga sore kara ikkagetsu ga tachi, nikagetsu tatte mo, kanja wa shinanakatta. Sore dokoro de wa nai. Nyuuin toushou wa hanshi hanshou datta kono kanja wa masumasu kesshoku ga yoku nari, genki ni natte kita no da. Shikamo gan wa dondon kare no zenshin ni hirogari, kare no naizou no kikan subete ni utsuri, ima ya ningen no karada no naka ni gan ga aru to iu yori wa, gan ga ningen no katachi o shite iru to itta hou ga hayai to iu you na joutai ni natta. Isha wa shindan no shiyou ga naku, kore ni wa tada, akireru bakari datta.

ついに癌が、患者の全身を占領した日、完全に元気をとり戻した患者は、医者に退院の許可を求めた。「まあ、元気なんだから退院したっていいんですがね」③医者は首をひねりながら答えた。「しかし不思議だなあ。あなたは本当ならもうとっくに死んでなきゃ、いけないんですがねえ」Tsui ni gan ga, kanja no zenshin o senryou shita hi, kanzen ni genki o torimodoshita kanja wa, isha ni taiin no kyoka o motometa. "Maa, genki nan dakara taiin shitatte ii ndesu ga ne" ③ isha wa kubi o hinerinagara kotaeta. "Shikashi fushigi da naa. Anata wa hontou nara mou tokku ni shindenakya, ikenai ndesu ga nee."

「わたしは、それほど不思議とは思いませんね」患者は健康そうに朗らかな笑いを見せ、医者にいった。「ほら、昔バンパイヤ（吸血鬼）というのがいたでしょう。あの連中は、④血を吸いとられることによって永遠の生命を得たのです。わたしも、全身を癌に侵されたため、完全に癌と一体になり、もしかすると、これで永遠の生命を得たのかもしれないのです」"Watashi wa, sore hodo fushigi to wa omoimasen ne" kanja wa kenkousou ni hogaraka na warai o mise, isha ni itta. "Hora, mukashi banpaiya (kyuuketsuki) to iu no ga ita deshou. Ano renchuu wa, ④ chi o suitorareru koto ni yotte eien no seimei o eta no desu. Watashi mo, zenshin o gan ni okasareta tame, kanzen ni gan to ittai ni nari, moshika suru to, kore de eien no seimei o eta no kamoshirenai no desu."

啞然としている注医者に向かって、患者はさらにいった。「ところで、ながい間病人用の食事だったもので、腹が猛烈に減っています。いかがでしょう。この病院の患者の体内から切除した肉腫を——つまりその、癌を、少しゆずっていただけないでしょうか」Azen to shite iru isha ni mukatte, kanja wa sara ni itta. "Tokoro de, nagai aida byounin-you no shokuji datta mono de, hara ga mouretsu ni hette imasu. Ikaga deshou. Kono byouin no kanja no tainai kara setsujo shita nikushu o -- tsumari sono, gan o, sukoshi yuzutte itadakenai deshou ka."

The doctor wasn't very surprised — he judged it to be the delusion of a dying patient, since this patient's cancer had already spread beyond his stomach to his esophagus and liver, and death was thought to be only a matter of time. But a month passed, then two, and the patient didn't die — far from it. This patient, half-dead on admission, kept looking healthier, his color and energy returning. The cancer kept spreading through his whole body until it reached every organ, to the point that it made more sense to say the cancer had taken human shape than that there was cancer inside a human body. The doctor had no way left to diagnose it and could only stare in blank amazement. Finally, on the day the cancer occupied his entire body, the now fully-recovered patient asked to be discharged. "Well, you're healthy, so I suppose you can be discharged," the doctor answered, tilting his head. "But it's strange — by rights you should have died long ago." "I don't find it that strange," the patient said with a healthy, cheerful laugh. "You know how vampires used to exist? They gained eternal life by having their blood sucked out. As for me, my whole body was taken over by cancer until I became one with it — maybe that's how I gained eternal life too." Facing the stunned doctor, the patient went on: "By the way, after so long eating nothing but hospital food, I'm ravenous. Would you mind — could you spare me a little of the tumor tissue removed from this hospital's patients? That is, some cancer?"

Why: The footnote (*) glosses the sentence as "I was offered a deal" — i.e. the deal was offered to the patient, not by him. Re-reading the grammar: 悪魔がやってきました…という取り引きを申し出た — the devil is the one who came and made the proposal; the patient only "gladly accepted" (受けました) it afterward. Options 1 and 4 both describe the patient, who is the receiver of the offer, not its source; option 2 (the doctor) isn't part of this exchange at all.

Why: The idiom 時間の問題 ("a matter of time") is a set phrase meaning something is inevitable and imminent — here, given the cancer had already spread to the esophagus and liver, the doctors expected death very soon, not at some vague future point. Options 1, 3 and 4 all misread 時間の問題 as being literally about the *duration* or *uncertainty* of time, when the idiom actually signals near-certain, near-term inevitability.

Q3

Why: 首をひねる ("tilting one's head") is the set gesture for puzzlement, and the doctor's very next line spells out exactly what puzzles him: 「しかし不思議だなあ。あなたは本当ならもうとっくに死んでなきゃ、いけないんですがねえ」— "it's strange, you really should have died long ago." Q4

3癌患者の生命を得ることgan kanja no seimei o eru kotogaining a cancer patient's lifeएक कैंसर मरीज़ का जीवन पानाકેન્સર દર્દીનું જીવન મેળવવું
4病人用の食事を食べることbyounin-you no shokuji o taberu kotoeating hospital-patient foodमरीज़ों वाला खाना खानाદર્દીઓનું ભોજન ખાવું

Why: The patient draws an explicit parallel: vampires "gained eternal life 血を吸いとられることによって" (by having their blood sucked out), then immediately says わたしも、全身を癌に侵されたため…これで永遠の生命を得たのかもしれない — "I too, because my whole body was invaded by cancer, may have gained eternal life this way." So the parallel to "having blood sucked" is precisely 全身を癌に侵されること (option 2) — the loss/invasion that, paradoxically, grants endless life. Options 1, 3 and 4 all describe things mentioned elsewhere in the story but not the specific parallel the patient is drawing.

Q1 Grammar of who-did-what-to-whom 申し出た needs its subject, not its recipient The patient is grammatically the one who "accepted" (受けた), not the one who "offered" (申し出た) — easy to swap the two roles.
