1 Reading Strategy

Key idea

筆者はどちら側の人間か、を先に見極める。Hissha wa dochira gawa no ningen ka, o saki ni mikiwameru.

This essay sets up a two-sided structure (送り手 vs. 受け手, "sender" vs. "receiver"). Two habits help with this kind of piece:

  • 1. Work out which side the author belongs to before answering anything else. A media critique like this only makes sense once you know whether the writer is speaking as an insider (a broadcaster/producer) or an outsider (a viewer) — small details, like who calls him a "traitor," reveal this.
    HI: जवाब देने से पहले पता करें लेखक किस पक्ष का है — यह "प्रसारक" पक्ष का इनसाइडर है या "दर्शक" पक्ष का बाहरी व्यक्ति, यह छोटे संकेतों (जैसे उसे "ग़द्दार" कौन कहता है) से पता चलता है। GU: જવાબ આપતાં પહેલાં જાણો લેખક કયા પક્ષનો છે — તે "પ્રસારક" પક્ષનો ઇનસાઈડર છે કે "દર્શક" પક્ષનો બહારનો, આ નાના સંકેતો (જેમ કે તેને "દગાબાજ" કોણ કહે છે) પરથી ખબર પડે છે.
  • 2. When asked to predict a continuation, stay consistent with the stated argument — don't introduce a new one. A question can ask what the writer would say next, even though that part isn't printed. The right answer always extends the same logic already laid out, never contradicts it.
    HI: अगर सवाल यह पूछे कि आगे क्या कहा जाएगा (भले वह हिस्सा न छपा हो), तो सही जवाब वही तर्क आगे बढ़ाता है जो पहले ही बताया गया — कोई नया, विरोधाभासी तर्क नहीं। GU: જો પ્રશ્ન પૂછે કે આગળ શું કહેવાશે (ભલે તે ભાગ છપાયો ન હોય), તો સાચો જવાબ એ જ તર્ક આગળ વધારે છે જે પહેલેથી કહેવાયો છે — કોઈ નવો, વિરોધાભાસી તર્ક નહીં.

Tairyou (masu) ni komyunikeeshon o okonau kara "masukomi" to ii, terebi wa sono baitai (media) to shite saidai no sonzai no hazu na no da ga, soko de genzentaru yakuwari buntan ga atte, ippou wa "okurite," tahou ga "ukete" to iu no de wa, hontou no komyunikeeshon to iigatai. Shikashi, kono kubetsu wa genjitsu ni meikaku ni sonzai shite iru.

この関係のなかでもっとも不幸な部分は、互いが互いを腹の底では軽蔑し合っていることである。受け手＝視聴者はたしかにテレビをよく視るが、それを俗悪なしろもの注2だと思っている。一方、送り手＝制作者たちは、俗悪だといいながら受け手たちがもっとも好んでみるのは俗悪な番組であることを見せ続けられて、視聴者を内心では軽蔑している。Kono kankei no naka de mottomo fukou na bubun wa, tagai ga tagai o hara no soko de wa keibetsu shiatte iru koto de aru. Ukete = shichousha wa tashika ni terebi o yoku miru ga, sore o zokuaku na shiromono da to omotte iru. Ippou, okurite = seisakushatachi wa, zokuaku da to iinagara ukete-tachi ga mottomo kononde miru no wa zokuaku na bangumi de aru koto o misetsuzukerarete, shichousha o naishin de wa keibetsu shite iru.

受け手の意向の量的表現＝視聴率がほとんどすべてである世界で、次に起きる現象は「悪貨が良貨を駆逐する注3」ことである*。視聴率さえとっておれば、どんな"やらせ注4"をやろうとも、メディアの責任を考えればモラルに反すると思うことをやっても、大手を振ることができる世界**で、それを見習っていく人間と、そんなことはしたくないと気力がなえてしまう注5人間とが生まれ、富み栄えるのは①前者ということになる。Ukete no ikou no ryouteki hyougen = shichouritsu ga hotondo subete de aru sekai de, tsugi ni okiru genshou wa "akka ga ryouka o kuchiku suru" koto de aru. Da ga, sore o nageitari, koutei suru koto kara wa nani mo umarenai. 長年のテレビによる"ごますり"注6に慣れ親しんだ視聴者は少しでもこういうことを言うと傲慢注7だと非難する。このテレビ状況で損をしているのは「受け手」のほうなのに……。Naganen no terebi ni yoru "gomasuri" ni narejitashinda shichousha wa sukoshi demo kou iu koto o iu to gouman da to hinan suru. Kono terebi joukyou de son o shite iru no wa "ukete" no hou na no ni……

EN: In the world of television, the words I dislike most are "sender" and "receiver." Because mass communication is carried out, it's called "mass media," and television ought to be its greatest embodiment — yet there's a rigid division of roles there, one side "sender," the other "receiver," which can hardly be called real communication. And yet this distinction concretely and clearly exists in reality. The most unfortunate part of this relationship is that each side secretly despises the other, deep down. The receivers — the viewers — certainly watch television a lot, but think of it as vulgar stuff. The senders — the producers — meanwhile, while calling it vulgar, keep being shown that what viewers most love to watch are vulgar programs, and inwardly despise the viewers for it. In a world where the quantified expression of the receivers' wishes — the ratings — is nearly everything, the next thing that happens is that "bad money drives out good." In a world where, as long as you get the ratings, you can walk tall no matter what fake staging you pull, no matter what you do that would seem immoral if you considered the media's responsibility — in that world, two kinds of people are born: those who learn to imitate that behavior, and those who lose the will to do such things. And it's the former who prosper and flourish. It's said that "there is no politics beyond the people's own level," and I feel the same could be said of television. But lamenting it, or simply accepting it, produces nothing. I don't mind at all being treated as a "traitor" by the "senders" for saying this, but what wears me out is having bullets fired at me from among the "receivers." Viewers who have grown used to television's long habit of flattering them criticize even the slightest such statement as arrogant — even though it's the "receivers" who are actually losing out in this whole television situation...
