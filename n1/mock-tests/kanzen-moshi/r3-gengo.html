ともあれ、趣味になり得るかどうかは、それを心地よい気持ちでやっているかどうかによるのであり、無理に趣味を持とうと［45］のはいうまでもない。Tomoare, shumi ni nariuru ka dou ka wa, sore o kokochi yoi kimochi de yatte iru ka dou ka ni yoru no de ari, muri ni shumi o motou to [45] no wa iu made mo nai.

Full sentence: …無理に趣味を持とうと意気込む必要などないのはいうまでもない。…muri ni shumi o motou to ikigomu hitsuyou nado nai no wa iu made mo nai.
EN: In any case, whether something can become a hobby depends on whether you do it with a pleasant feeling, and it goes without saying that there's no need to get worked up about forcing yourself to have a hobby.
HI: ख़ैर, कोई चीज़ शौक़ बन सकती है या नहीं, यह इस पर निर्भर है कि आप उसे सुकून से करते हैं या नहीं; और कहने की ज़रूरत नहीं कि ज़बरदस्ती शौक़ पालने के लिए जोश दिखाने की कोई ज़रूरत नहीं।

Answer 4. The 解説: the conclusion is the view that you should follow your real feelings and situation, so 「無理に〜必要はない」 fits. 無理に…ようと ("forcing yourself to…") already sets up a negative; 意気込む (be all fired up) echoes 意気込んで高いシューズを買った earlier in the passage (added, not in book).

Exam trap 1 — four real words, only one kanji: in Q2 (快く／著しく／甚だしく／潔く), Q4 (拝む／阻む／拒む／妬む) and Q5 (親密／精密／機密／緻密) every option is a genuine word with a similar shape. Meaning won't save you — you must know the reading of the kanji in front of you.
Exam trap 2 — 問題4 wrong sentences swap in a near-synonym. The book's fixes show the pattern: 送る for 配送, 不調／不足／不安 for 不備, 推薦／推測 for 推進, はっきり／さっぱり for きっぱり, 説明／説教 for 説得. Ask "what word would a native actually use here?" for each option.
Exam trap 3 — 〜ことはない appears twice: in Q37 (研究自体をあきらめることはない) and Q44 (チャレンジすることはない). It means "there's no need to", not "it never happens" — don't confuse it with 〜たことはない (have never done).
