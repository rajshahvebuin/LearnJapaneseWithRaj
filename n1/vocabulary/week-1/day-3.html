Theme
人の動作を表す言葉 (words/phrases describing a person's physical actions)
Answer key

Notes
Unlike Days 1–2, this day's page is itself organized into body-part categories (頭／目／口／手／体) rather than one flat list — that grouping is preserved below (as an extra "Part" column) since it's the book's own structure, not an invented sub-grouping. Most entries are full action phrases (verb + its object/particle), and several rows carry a second, related phrase using the same verb on a different object — kept in the Note column, same convention as →/・ items on other days.
※ = 「が」can be swapped for 「の」 (e.g. 愛想が いい ＝ 愛想の いい), more common in writing
→ = related word (usually the verb form)
⇔ = antonym
・ = a common example phrase using the headword, not a separate word
Warm-up

＿＿＿に入るのは？___ ni hairu no wa?
髪の毛を＿＿＿。Kami no ke o ___.
("What goes in the blank? '[I] ___ my hair.'" — candidates offered: めくる / いじる / 丸める)

髪の毛をいじる (kami no ke o ijiru) — "to fiddle/play with one's hair"

Q1

肩までお湯によく（　）。Kata made oyu ni yoku (___).

Full sentence: 肩までお湯によくつかりましょう。Kata made oyu ni yoku tsukarimashou.
EN: Let's soak well in the hot water up to our shoulders.
HI: कंधों तक गरम पानी में अच्छी तरह डूबें।
GU: ખભા સુધી ગરમ પાણીમાં સારી રીતે પલળીએ.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
aつかりましょうtsukarimashoulet's soakडूबेंપલળીએ
bすくいましょうsukuimashoulet's scoop up (wrong verb for bathing)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Why: 湯につかる is the fixed collocation for soaking in a bath; すくう is for scooping something up (like water in your hands), not immersing your whole body.

Q2

車の前に飛び出してきた猫を（　）電柱にぶつかってしまった。Kuruma no mae ni tobidashite kita neko o (___) denchuu ni butsukatte shimatta.

Full sentence: 車の前に飛び出してきた猫をよけたら電柱にぶつかってしまった。Kuruma no mae ni tobidashite kita neko o yoketara denchuu ni butsukatte shimatta.
EN: When I swerved to avoid a cat that jumped out in front of the car, I ended up hitting a utility pole.
HI: गाड़ी के सामने कूदी बिल्ली से बचने पर बिजली के खंभे से टकरा गया।
GU: ગાડી સામે કૂદેલી બિલાડીથી બચતાં વીજળીના થાંભલા સાથે અથડાઈ ગયો.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
aよけたらyoketaradodged/avoidedबचने परબચતાં
bそらしたらsorashitaradeflected/turned away (wrong object)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

The warm-up itself sets up the day's main trap — めくる／いじる／丸める all involve hands touching something, but only one fits 髪の毛を___:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
めくるmekuru To flip/turn over For flat, page-like things (calendar pages, book pages) — turning one leaf over to reveal the next. Turning a page, a calendar sheet, a flat cover.
いじるijiru To fiddle/play/tinker with Idle, repetitive touching or light tinkering — hair, a phone, a garden — with no goal of transforming its shape. Absent-minded touching (hair) or casual tending (a garden, a hobby project).
丸めるmarumeru To roll/ball up, curl Actively reshapes something into a round/curled form — paper into a ball, or one's own body into a curled position. Crumpling paper, curling up one's body.
つまむtsumamu To pinch, take a small pinch of Light touch with fingertips to pick up or hold something small (salt, one's own nose) — no force/pain implied. Picking up a pinch of something, pinching your own nose.
つねるtsuneru To pinch (skin) Squeezes someone's skin between fingers, usually causing mild pain — a specifically skin-on-skin action. Pinching a cheek or arm, often teasingly or to hurt slightly.
むしるmushiru To pull out/pluck Forceful removal of something rooted or attached — weeds, feathers, hair strands — pulled out by the roots. Pulling weeds, plucking something out completely.
Exam trap: 摘む (tsumu, "to pick/pluck," as in 花を摘む) and つまむ (tsumamu, "to pinch/take a pinch of," as in 塩をつまむ) share the same kanji root and both describe fingertip actions, but they are different verbs with different readings and objects — 摘む picks something off a plant/stem; つまむ pinches or picks up something small between finger and thumb. Also note うつむく (⇔ direction) and うなずく are opposite head motions, not synonyms — Q6 above tests exactly this contrast — while もがく／あがく in the word list are near-total synonyms (both "to struggle/flail"), not a contrastive pair.
