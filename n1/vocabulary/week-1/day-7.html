2 Quiz / Exercise Section

問題1 — choose the best word for the blank (1問4点)

Q1

（　）の発作が起きるとせきが止まらなくなる。(___) no hossa ga okiru to seki ga tomaranaku naru.

Full sentence: ぜんそくの発作が起きるとせきが止まらなくなる。Zensoku no hossa ga okiru to seki ga tomaranaku naru.
EN: When an asthma attack occurs, the coughing won't stop.
HI: जब दमे का दौरा पड़ता है, तो खाँसी रुकती नहीं है।
GU: જ્યારે અસ્થમાનો હુમલો આવે છે, ત્યારે ખાંસી અટકતી નથી.
#JapaneseEnglish
1うつ病utsubyoudepression (no "attack" with coughing)
2ぜんそくzensokuasthma
3花粉症kafunshouhay fever (sneezing, not a coughing "attack")
4癌gancancer (no "attack" pattern)

Why: Day 6's ぜんそくの発作 (asthma attack) is the fixed collocation for a sudden attack that causes uncontrollable coughing.

Q2

彼女と手をつないで歩いていたら、友達に（　）。Kanojo to te o tsunaide aruite itara, tomodachi ni (___).

Full sentence: 彼女と手をつないで歩いていたら、友達に冷やかされた。...tomodachi ni hiyakasareta.
EN: While I was walking hand-in-hand with my girlfriend, a friend teased me about it.
HI: जब मैं अपनी गर्लफ़्रेंड का हाथ थामे चल रहा था, तो दोस्त ने मुझे छेड़ा।
GU: હું મારી ગર્લફ્રેન્ડનો હાથ પકડીને ચાલતો હતો ત્યારે મિત્રએ મને ચીડવ્યો.
#JapaneseEnglish
1もてなされたmotenasaretawas hosted (wrong meaning)
2冷やかされたhiyakasaretawas teased
3見せびらかしたmisebirakashitashowed off (wrong voice — active, and wrong subject)
4しいられたshiiraretawas forced (wrong meaning)

Why: Day 4's 冷やかす describes playful teasing — exactly the reaction friends give when they see a couple holding hands.

Q3

気に入って買ったバッグを友達に（　）気分が悪かった。Ki ni itte katta baggu o tomodachi ni (___) kibun ga warukatta.

Full sentence: 気に入って買ったバッグを友達にけなされて気分が悪かった。...tomodachi ni kenasarete kibun ga warukatta.
EN: A friend badmouthed the bag I'd bought because I liked it, and it put me in a bad mood.
HI: जो बैग मुझे पसंद आकर ख़रीदा था, दोस्त ने उसकी बुराई की तो मेरा मूड ख़राब हो गया।
GU: મને ગમેલી અને ખરીદેલી બેગની મિત્રએ નિંદા કરી, એટલે મારો મૂડ ખરાબ થયો.
#JapaneseEnglish
1けなされてkenasaretehad it disparaged/badmouthed
2さらわれてsarawaretewas kidnapped/snatched away (wrong — too extreme for a bag comment)
3むかつかれてmukatsukarete(not a natural passive form)
4あざ笑われてazawarawaretewas sneered at (fits mocking a person, not a thing's quality)

Why: Day 4's けなす (to speak ill of/disparage) is the natural verb for a friend criticizing the quality of a thing you like, like a bag; あざ笑う mocks a person's action, not an object's quality.

Q4

3歳ぐらいの女の子が、（　）公園で遊んでいた。San-sai gurai no onna no ko ga, (___) kouen de asonde ita.

Full sentence: 3歳ぐらいの女の子が、無邪気に公園で遊んでいた。...mujaki ni kouen de asonde ita.
EN: A girl of about three was playing innocently in the park.
HI: क़रीब तीन साल की एक बच्ची मासूमियत से पार्क में खेल रही थी।
GU: આશરે ત્રણ વર્ષની એક છોકરી નિર્દોષતાથી બગીચામાં રમતી હતી.
#JapaneseEnglish
1温和にonwa nimild-manneredly (wrong fit for a toddler playing)
2気さくにkisaku niin a friendly/approachable way (wrong fit)
3無邪気にmujaki niinnocently, in a childlike way
4陰気にinki nigloomily (wrong tone for a child playing)

Why: Day 1's 無邪気な specifically describes childlike innocence — the natural word for describing how a small child plays.

Q5

彼はだれにでも優しい（　）人で、みんなに好かれている。Kare wa dare ni demo yasashii (___) hito de, minna ni sukarete iru.

Full sentence: 彼はだれにでも優しい思いやりがある人で、みんなに好かれている。...omoiyari ga aru hito de...
EN: He's a considerate person who's kind to everyone, and is liked by all.
HI: वह सबके प्रति दयालु और सबका ख़याल रखने वाला है, इसलिए सभी उसे पसंद करते हैं।
GU: તે બધા પ્રત્યે માયાળુ અને બધાની કાળજી રાખનાર છે, એટલે બધા તેને પસંદ કરે છે.
#JapaneseEnglish
1でしゃばりなdeshabari napushy, intrusive (contradicts being liked by all)
2思いやりがあるomoiyari ga aruconsiderate
3しぶといshibutoistubborn/tenacious (wrong fit)
4きざなkiza napretentious (contradicts being liked by all)

Why: Day 1's 思いやりがある (considerate) fits someone kind to everyone and universally liked; でしゃばり/きざ describe traits people usually dislike.

Q6

バスタオルを（　）枕の代わりにした。Basutaoru o (___) makura no kawari ni shita.

Full sentence: バスタオルをまるめて枕の代わりにした。Basutaoru o marumete makura no kawari ni shita.
EN: I rolled up a bath towel and used it in place of a pillow.
HI: बाथ टॉवल को गोल लपेटकर तकिए की जगह इस्तेमाल किया।
GU: બાથ ટુવાલને ગોળ વાળીને ઓશીકાની જગ્યાએ વાપર્યો.
#JapaneseEnglish
1つぶってtsubutteclosed (eyes) — wrong verb entirely
2めくってmekutteflipped (a page) — wrong object
3まるめてmarumeterolled/balled up
4そらしてsorashiteaverted/deflected — wrong object

Why: Day 3's 丸める means rolling something into a compact shape, exactly what you do with a towel to use it as a makeshift pillow.

Q7

母に（　）早く家を出た。Haha ni (___) hayaku ie o deta.

Full sentence: 母にうながされて早く家を出た。Haha ni unagasarete hayaku ie o deta.
EN: Prompted by my mother, I left home early.
HI: माँ के कहने पर मैं जल्दी घर से निकल गया।
GU: માતાએ ઉતાવળ કરાવી એટલે હું વહેલો ઘરેથી નીકળ્યો.
#JapaneseEnglish
1うながされてunagasareteprompted/urged
2かばわれてkabawareteprotected/covered for (wrong meaning)
3しかけられてshikakeraretehad a trap set on me (wrong meaning)
4たどられてtadoraretewas traced/followed (wrong meaning)

Why: Day 5's 促す (to urge/prompt) fits being encouraged by one's mother to hurry up and leave.

Q8

長い間立ちっぱなしだったので、足が（　）しまった。Nagai aida tacchippanashi datta node, ashi ga (___) shimatta.

Full sentence: 長い間立ちっぱなしだったので、足がむくんでしまった。...ashi ga mukunde shimatta.
EN: Since I was standing for a long time, my legs ended up swelling.
HI: लंबे समय तक खड़ा रहने से पैरों में सूजन आ गई।
GU: લાંબા સમય સુધી ઊભા રહેવાથી પગમાં સોજો આવી ગયો.
#JapaneseEnglish
1もうろうとしてmourou to shitebecame hazy (wrong subject — for 意識, not legs)
2むくんでmukundeswelled/bloated
3伸びてnobitestretched/grew (not idiomatic for tired legs)
4こじれてkojiretegot aggravated/complicated (for colds/relationships, not legs)

Why: Day 6's 足がむくむ is the fixed phrase for legs swelling after prolonged standing.

Q9

これはみそを（　）調理器具です。Kore wa miso o (___) chouri kigu desu.

Full sentence: これはみそをこす調理器具です。Kore wa miso o kosu chouri kigu desu.
EN: This is a cooking tool for straining miso.
HI: यह मिसो को छानने का रसोई उपकरण है।
GU: આ મિસો ગાળવાનું રસોઈ સાધન છે.
#JapaneseEnglish
1さらすsarasuto expose/soak (wrong verb for straining)
2すすぐsusuguto rinse (wrong — rinsing isn't straining)
3こすkosuto filter/strain
4ゆするyusuruto shake (wrong — unrelated to filtering)

Why: Day 5's こす (to filter/strain) is exactly what a strainer-type tool does to dissolved miso when making soup.

Q10

細いひもを何本も（　）太いロープを作った。Hosoi himo o nanbon mo (___) futoi roopu o tsukutta.

Full sentence: 細いひもを何本もたばねて太いロープを作った。...nanbon mo tabanete futoi roopu o tsukutta.
EN: By bundling together many thin strings, I made a thick rope.
HI: कई पतली डोरियों को एक साथ बाँधकर एक मोटी रस्सी बनाई।
GU: ઘણી પાતળી દોરીઓ ભેગી બાંધીને એક જાડું દોરડું બનાવ્યું.
#JapaneseEnglish
1つづってtsuzuttespelled/composed in writing (wrong meaning)
2すえてsueteinstalled/placed (wrong meaning)
3交えてmajieteincluded (a third party) — wrong meaning
4たばねてtabanetebundled/tied together

Why: Day 5's 束ねる (to bundle/tie up) is exactly the action of gathering many thin strings into one thick rope.

問題4 — which sentence uses the word correctly? (1問4点)

Q21

しぶといshibutoi

#JapaneseEnglish
1敵もしぶとく、なかなか負けを認めない。Teki mo shibutoku, nakanaka make o mitomenai.The enemy too is tenacious, and won't easily admit defeat.
2この柿はちょっとしぶとい。Kono kaki wa chotto shibutoi.This persimmon is a bit [shibutoi] — wrong word; astringent taste is 渋い(shibui), not しぶとい
3しぶといお茶はとてもおいしい。Shibutoi ocha wa totemo oishii.[Shibutoi] tea is delicious — same confusion with 渋い
4庭の木がとてもしぶとくなってきた。Niwa no ki ga totemo shibutoku natte kita.The garden tree became very [shibutoi] — doesn't fit a tree

Why: しぶとい means stubborn/tenacious about a person or agent refusing to give up — only sentence 1 fits. Sentences 2–3 confuse it with the unrelated word 渋い (shibui, "astringent/bitter-tasting"), a classic sound-alike trap.

Q22

近寄りがたいchikayorigatai

#JapaneseEnglish
1私の上司は気さくで近寄りがたい。Watashi no joushi wa kisaku de chikayorigatai.My boss is friendly and hard-to-approach — self-contradictory
2彼女は近寄りがたいほど美しい。Kanojo wa chikayorigatai hodo utsukushii.She is beautiful to the point of being hard to approach.
3泥棒が近寄りがたい柵を作った。Dorobou ga chikayorigatai saku o tsukutta.A thief made a hard-to-approach fence — wrong, describes a feeling toward people, not a physical barrier's own trait
4古本屋で近寄りがたい本を手に入れた。Furuhon'ya de chikayorigatai hon o te ni ireta.Got a hard-to-approach book at a used bookstore — doesn't fit an object

Why: 近寄りがたい naturally describes a person so impressive (often beautiful) that others feel hesitant to approach — sentence 1 is self-contradictory (気さく directly means approachable), and 3–4 misapply it to objects.

Q23

見せびらかすmisebirakasu

#JapaneseEnglish
1おばあちゃんは孫にお年玉を見せびらかした。Obaachan wa mago ni otoshidama o misebirakashita.Grandma showed off New Year's money to her grandchild — doesn't fit the boastful-to-a-peer connotation
2美容院で髪を見せびらかしてもらった。Biyouin de kami o misebirakashite moratta.Had my hair "shown off" at the salon — not idiomatic
3今日は私が食事を見せびらかします。Kyou wa watashi ga shokuji o misebirakashimasu.Today I will show off the meal — not idiomatic
4彼は新しい腕時計をみんなに見せびらかしていた。Kare wa atarashii udedokei o minna ni misebirakashite ita.He was showing off his new watch to everyone.

Why: 見せびらかす means boastfully showing off a possession to others, exactly like Day 4's own example 新しいバッグを見せびらかす — sentence 4 matches that pattern precisely.

Q24

むせるmuseru

#JapaneseEnglish
1そのパーティー会場には、むせるほど人が来ていた。Sono paatii kaijou ni wa, museru hodo hito ga kite ita.So many people had come to the party venue it was stifling.
2タバコの煙が目にむせた。Tabako no kemuri ga me ni museta.Cigarette smoke "choked" in the eyes — wrong; むせる is about throat/breathing, not eyes
3今日はよく晴れたむせる天気だ。Kyou wa yoku hareta museru tenki da.Today is clear, "choking" weather — doesn't fit clear weather
4捻挫した足がだんだんむせてきた。Nenza shita ashi ga dandan musete kita.The sprained foot gradually "choked" — wrong verb for swelling

Why: むせる (to choke, from smoke/crowding/humidity) extends idiomatically to an overwhelming, stifling crowd (人いきれでむせる); the other three misapply it to eyes, clear weather, and an injury, none of which involve choking/breathing difficulty.

Q25

片思いkataomoi

#JapaneseEnglish
1上司の不正を片思いして警察に通報した。Joushi no fusei o kataomoi shite keisatsu ni tsuuhou shita."Kataomoi'd" the boss's misconduct and reported it — nonsensical
2彼女の帰りを片思いして待っていた。Kanojo no kaeri o kataomoi shite matte ita.Was "kataomoi-ing" waiting for her return — wrong grammar/sense
3私の初恋は片思いで終わってしまった。Watashi no hatsukoi wa kataomoi de owatte shimatta.My first love ended in unrequited love.
4親はかわいいわが子に片思いするものです。Oya wa kawaii waga ko ni kataomoi suru mono desu.Parents "kataomoi" their cute children — wrong; parental love isn't romantic one-sided love

Why: 片思い specifically means one-sided romantic love that isn't reciprocated — only sentence 3 uses it in that exact romantic sense; the others misapply it to unrelated situations (whistleblowing, waiting, parental love).

Test-day strategy: This test deliberately mixes look-alikes from every day this week — sound-alike traps (しぶとい vs. 渋い in Q21), false friends sharing a root (もてる vs. もてなす), and transitive/intransitive pairs (Q8's むくむ, and the 早まる／強まる／弱まる／和らぐ family). When stuck, first ask "does this word describe a person's inner trait, an outward judgment, or a physical action?" — that question alone eliminates half the options on most items above. Since this book's official answers are in a separate booklet not included in this scan, treat the reasoning in each 「Why」box as the primary check, not just the marked correct row.
