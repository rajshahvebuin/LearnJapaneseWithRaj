Source

Theme

Answer key

Notes
The book itself splits this day into two particle-based groups — 〈人〉を＿＿＿ (verbs that take a person as direct object) and 〈人〉に＿＿＿ (verbs that take a person marked with に) — plus a small "もっと覚えましょう" (learn more) bonus list on p.19. All three groupings are preserved below (as a "Group" column) since they're the book's own structure, exactly like Day 3's body-part categories.
※ = 「が」can be swapped for 「の」 (e.g. 愛想が いい ＝ 愛想の いい), more common in writing
→ = related word (usually the verb form)
⇔ = antonym
・ = a common example phrase using the headword, not a separate word
Warm-up

＿＿＿に入るのは？___ ni hairu no wa?
友達を＿＿＿。Tomodachi o ___.
("What goes in the blank? '[I] ___ my friend.'" — candidates: もてる／けなす／もめる. Hint given: only one of these can take 友達を as its object, and it can be paraphrased as 非難する — "to criticize.")

友達をけなす (tomodachi o kenasu) — "to speak ill of / disparage a friend"

Q1

うちの犬は母にいちばん（　）います。Uchi no inu wa haha ni ichiban (___) imasu.

Full sentence: うちの犬は母にいちばんなついています。Uchi no inu wa haha ni ichiban natsuite imasu.
EN: Our dog is most attached to my mother.
HI: हमारा कुत्ता माँ से सबसे ज़्यादा हिल-मिल गया है।
GU: અમારો કૂતરો માતાથી સૌથી વધુ હળીમળી ગયો છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
aなついてnatsuiteattached to, fond ofहिल-मिल गयाહળીમળી ગયો
bもててmotetepopular with (wrong — not about romantic popularity)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Why: なつく describes an animal or child becoming attached/fond of someone; もてる specifically means being popular with the opposite sex, which doesn't fit a dog and its owner.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

The warm-up's contrast between もてる／けなす／もめる sets up the day's first cluster; the "criticize" verbs けなす／ののしる／なじる／とがめる form a second cluster the exam loves to test at different intensities:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
けなすkenasu To disparage, speak ill of Verbal put-down of a person or their work/quality — mildest of the four, often just unkind commentary. Badmouthing a friend, criticizing someone's work unkindly.
ののしるnonoshiru To curse at, revile Loud, abusive language directed at someone — much stronger and more aggressive than けなす. Shouting curses/abuse at someone in anger.
なじるnajiru To reproach, take to task Confronting someone directly about something they did wrong — accusatory, one-on-one. Confronting someone about a specific wrongdoing.
とがめるtogameru To reproach, blame Milder, can be a passing comment or one's own conscience "reproaching" oneself — not always spoken aloud. Blaming/questioning an action, including self-reproach (良心がとがめる).
もてるmoteru To be popular (romantically) About being liked/desired by the opposite sex — despite looking related to もてなす, it shares no meaning with it. Describing someone's romantic popularity.
もてなすmotenasu To host, entertain (a guest) About hospitality toward a visitor/guest — a "false friend" of もてる despite the shared もて- root. Describing how you treat a guest, not romantic appeal.
Exam trap: もてる ("popular with the opposite sex") and もてなす ("to host/entertain a guest") look like they should be related — same もて- root — but they are unconnected in modern meaning; don't infer one from the other. Separately, 言いつける and 告げ口する both translate as "tell on/tattle," but 告げ口する is the more natural choice in a plain 〜される passive frame (Q7), while 言いつける more often appears as 言いつけられる in a slightly different sentence shape — a subtle register/grammar difference the exam can exploit.
