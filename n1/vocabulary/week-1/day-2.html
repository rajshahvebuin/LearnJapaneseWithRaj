Q1

電車に乗り遅れそうになり（　）。Densha ni noriokuresou ni nari (___).

Full sentence: 電車に乗り遅れそうになりあせった。Densha ni noriokuresou ni nari asetta.
EN: I panicked because I was about to miss the train.
HI: ट्रेन छूटने वाली थी, इसलिए मैं घबरा गया।
GU: ટ્રેન છૂટવાની હતી એટલે હું ગભરાઈ ગયો.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
aあせったasettapanicked, felt rushedघबरा गयाગભરાઈ ગયો

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Today's word list has three clusters of near-synonyms that English flattens into one word ("annoyed", "annoying", "embarrassed") but Japanese keeps carefully distinct:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
気に障るki ni sawaru To offend, rub someone the wrong way Neutral/general term for causing offense — often used as a warning ("don't say things that..."). Talking about what might upset a third party in general.
しゃくに障る／かんに障るshaku ni sawaru / kan ni sawaru To be irritated, get on one's nerves More visceral, first-person irritation at something specific someone did — stronger and more colloquial than 気に障る. Describing your own gut-level annoyance at someone's attitude/behavior.
むかつくmukatsuku To feel angry / to feel nauseous Casual, physical-feeling word — can mean anger or literal nausea depending on context; not just "irritated." Describing a strong gut reaction — either at a person, or literal queasiness (seasickness, etc).
うっとうしいuttoushii Gloomy, oppressively annoying Describes a mood-dampening, oppressive kind of annoyance — classic collocation with weather (天気). Weather, a heavy atmosphere, or someone's constant nagging presence.
わずらわしいwazurawashii Troublesome, bothersome Describes complexity/hassle — procedures, relationships, or wiring that take unwanted effort to deal with. Paperwork, tangled relationships, complicated setups.
うざいuzai Annoying (slang) Informal, blunt catch-all for either of the above two — fine in casual speech, too casual for the exam's written register except as a vocabulary item to recognize. Casual conversation; recognize it on the exam, don't overuse it in formal writing.
きまり（が）悪いkimari (ga) warui Embarrassed, awkward An inward, personal feeling after one's own social slip (showing up late, etc) — about how you feel. Describing your own awkward/self-conscious feeling after a mistake.
見苦しいmigurushii Unsightly, disgraceful An outward judgment on how something looks to others — behavior or appearance judged as disgraceful. Criticizing someone else's (or your own, from outside) unsightly conduct.
恥をかくhaji o kaku To be embarrassed, lose face An event/action phrase — describes actively suffering public embarrassment in front of others, stronger than きまり悪い's inner discomfort. Describing a concrete embarrassing incident that happened in front of people.
Exam trap: むかつく is genuinely ambiguous between "makes me angry" (彼の顔を見るだけでむかつく) and "makes me feel sick/nauseous" (船に酔って、胸がむかつく) — context (a person vs. motion/food) decides which reading is meant, so don't assume anger by default. Separately, 心強い and 心細い are a fixed antonym pair (⇔) — 心強い needs a companion/support to lean on, while 心細い is the isolation that results from lacking one; they can't be used to describe the same situation.
