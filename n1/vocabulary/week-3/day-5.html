Q1

このカップ麺はあそこのラーメン屋より（a. よっぽど　b. まして）おいしい。Kono kappumen wa asoko no raamen-ya yori (a. yoppodo / b. mashite) oishii.

Full sentence: このカップ麺はあそこのラーメン屋よりよっぽどおいしい。Kono kappumen wa asoko no raamen-ya yori yoppodo oishii.
EN: This instant cup noodle is considerably tastier than that ramen shop.
HI: यह कप नूडल उस रामेन दुकान से कहीं ज़्यादा स्वादिष्ट है।
GU: આ કપ નૂડલ પેલી રામેન દુકાન કરતાં ઘણું વધારે સ્વાદિષ્ટ છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
aよっぽどyoppodoconsiderably, quite a lotकहीं ज़्यादाઘણું વધારે
bましてmashitelet alone (wrong meaning — for adding a more extreme case, not comparing degree)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Why: よっぽど intensifies a comparison (considerably more X than Y); まして is used to add "let alone [an even more extreme case]" to a preceding negative statement, a different sentence pattern entirely.

Q2

彼は何も言わないが、（a. さも　b. いたって）いやそうな顔をしている。Kare wa nani mo iwanai ga, (a. samo / b. itatte) iyasou na kao o shite iru.

GU: તે કંઈ કહેતો નથી, પણ તેના ચહેરા પર સ્પષ્ટ દેખાય છે કે તેને પસંદ નથી.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
aさもsamoevidently, with an obvious airसाफ़ तौर पर झलकते हुएસ્પષ્ટ દેખાય એ રીતે

Q3

HI: कीड़े के काटे हुए हिस्से को खुजलाने पर वह और भी ज़्यादा खुजली करने लगा।
GU: જંતુના ડંખવાળી જગ્યાએ ખંજવાળવાથી તે વધારે ખંજવાળવા લાગ્યું.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
aなおさらnaosaraall the moreऔर भी ज़्यादाવધારે
bまさしくmasashikudefinitely (wrong meaning — confirms a fact, doesn't intensify a resulting change)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Q4

あのおばあさんは、昔は（a. さぞかし　b. なんと）美人だったことでしょう。Ano obaasan wa, mukashi wa (a. sazokashi / b. nanto) bijin datta koto deshou.

HI: वह बुज़ुर्ग महिला अपने ज़माने में ज़रूर बहुत ख़ूबसूरत रही होंगी।
GU: તે વૃદ્ધ મહિલા પોતાના જમાનામાં ચોક્કસ ખૂબ સુંદર હશે.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
aさぞかしsazokashisurely, no doubtज़रूरચોક્કસ
bなんとnantowhat a...! (wrong grammar — an exclamation about something known/seen directly, not a guess about the unseen past)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Why: さぞかし expresses an empathetic guess about something you didn't witness yourself (matching ご両親はさぞお喜びでしょう); なんと is an exclamation of surprise about something directly observed, not a speculative guess with でしょう.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Today's emphasis words split into "especially" words, "guess/surely" words, and "very much so" words that overlap heavily in English gloss:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
とりわけtoriwake Particularly, among a group Singles one item out from an implied set (drinks, sweets, etc.). "Among sweets, I especially like cake."
ことにkoto ni Especially Interchangeable with とりわけ in most contexts, slightly more literary/written. Written descriptions: "this novel is especially interesting."
何よりnani yori More than anything, the best thing Not about singling one item from a group — it's a standalone superlative judgment ("nothing beats this"). Declaring something the most welcome/important thing, full stop.
さぞ／さぞかしsazo / sazokashi Surely, no doubt (guess) An empathetic guess about someone else's unseen feelings/situation, usually + でしょう. "Your parents must be so happy" (you weren't there to see it).
さもsamo Evidently, with an obvious air Describes a visible, observed appearance/manner conveying a feeling — not a guess about something unseen. "He smiled with evident/obvious pleasure" (you watched him do it).
よほど／よっぽどyohodo / yoppodo Considerably, quite a lot A degree intensifier for comparisons or strong inferences ("must be quite X"). "That luggage looks awfully heavy"; "tastier by far."
なおさらnaosara All the more (so) Specifically means a change/action increases an existing tendency — needs a preceding cause. "Being told no makes me want to do it all the more."
Exam trap: まして only works attached to an implied negative comparison ("adults can't even do it, まして children [definitely can't]") — it cannot open a sentence on its own the way やけに or いやに can, as Q5 above tests. If there's no earlier negative clause to attach to, まして is the wrong choice no matter how well its English gloss ("even more so") seems to fit.
