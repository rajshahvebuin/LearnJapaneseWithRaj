3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Today mixes frequency words, reluctance words, and repeated-kanji adverbs that overlap in English gloss but not in Japanese usage:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
こつこつkotsukotsu Steadily, little by little About the manner of sustained effort over time (saving, studying) — not how often something happens. Praising someone's patient, incremental progress.
ちょくちょくchokuchoku Every once in a while (casual) A frequency word for a recurring habit, casual register. Casual conversation about how often you visit somewhere.
しばしばshibashiba Often, frequently (formal) Same frequency meaning as ちょくちょく but formal/written register — fits news, reports, essays. Writing or formal speech about recurring events (e.g. earthquakes).
いやいやiyaiya Unwillingly Describes the internal reluctant attitude while doing something. "Doing homework unwillingly" — focus on the person's attitude.
しぶしぶshibushibu Reluctantly (after resistance) Implies the person resisted or hesitated first, then gave in — a process of yielding, not just a mood. "Reluctantly agreed/complied" after being pushed.
くよくよkuyokuyo Brood, fret Dwelling anxiously on a past event one cannot change. Telling someone to stop worrying about a mistake that's already done.
ふらふらfurafura Feel faint, unsteady A physical sensation of dizziness/lack of balance, unrelated to worry. Describing physical dizziness from illness or exhaustion.
Exam trap: むかむか carries two very different feelings that share one word: a queasy stomach (胃がむかむかする) and simmering anger at someone's attitude (彼の態度にむかむかする ＝ むかつく). Context — 胃 (stomach) vs. 態度 (attitude) — is what tells you which sense is meant, not the word itself.
