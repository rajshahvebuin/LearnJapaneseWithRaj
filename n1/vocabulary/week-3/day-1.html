JLPT N1 · Vocabulary · Week 3 — 副詞をじっくり学習しよう (Fukushi o jikkuri gakushuu shiyou — "Let's thoroughly study adverbs")

1日目 — こつこつ努力しよう (Kotsukotsu doryoku shiyou — Let's work hard, step by step)

Source
日本語総まとめ N1 語彙 (Nihongo Sou-Matome N1 Goi) — printed p.44–45 (PDF pages 35–36; offset holds at printed page = PDF index + 9).
Theme
様子・程度を表す言葉 (words describing a state/manner, plus words of degree) — this week's theme is 副詞 (adverbs), mostly mimetic ぎおん語・ぎたい語 words rather than the adjectives of Weeks 1–2.
Answer key
練習Ⅰ・Ⅱ answers for this day are printed in the official answer box at the bottom of p.47 — confirmed, not guessed.
Notes
This book's own page layout already pairs each headword with a full example sentence and an English + Chinese gloss printed right on the page — unlike Weeks 1–2's bare word lists. Hindi/Gujarati below are original translations for this site; English mirrors the book's own gloss. Example sentences are carried in the Note column with romaji, as this day's own "vocabulary" mostly consists of one-word-per-example entries rather than a flat list.
※ = 「が」can be swapped for 「の」, more common in writing

Warm-up

＿＿＿に入るのは？___ ni hairu no wa?
頭が＿＿＿する。Atama ga ___ suru.
("Which word fits the blank? 'My head is ___-ing.' The book's cats hold up three candidate answers: がんがん, ざあざあ, ばらばら.")

がんがん (gangan) — 頭ががんがんする "my head is pounding" — ざあざあ (pouring rain) and ばらばら (scattered/disassembled) don't fit a headache.

Q1

HI: बारिश के दिन लगातार होने से कमरा नम हो गया है।
GU: સતત વરસાદના દિવસોના કારણે રૂમ ભેજવાળો થઈ ગયો છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
aざあざあzaazaapouring (rain itself, not a room's state)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Several of today's mimetic words sound like they could swap for each other but occupy very different domains — exactly what Q2 and Q5 above are testing. Compare them:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
ずるずるzuruzuru Dragging (object or situation) A heavy thing being pulled along the ground, or a situation/decision dragging on unresolved. Luggage being dragged; a strike or negotiation that won't end.
だらだらdaradara Dripping; sluggish, aimless Liquid dripping continuously, or a person/activity lacking focus and dragging without purpose. Sweat/drool dripping; a lazy, unfocused way of working or living.
ばらばらbarabara Scattered into separate pieces Something taken apart into distinguishable, separate units. A disassembled machine, puzzle pieces, a family that has split up.
ぐちゃぐちゃguchagucha A disordered jumble/mess Contents mixed into a mess with no distinguishable pieces or order — not just "in pieces" but "in disarray." The inside of a messy bag; a crushed or squashed thing.
むちゃくちゃmuchakucha Unbelievably (intensifier); nonsensical A general-purpose degree intensifier for anything extreme (crowding, mess), or (as な-adjective) describes something that makes no logical sense. "The train gets unbelievably crowded"; "that opinion makes no sense."
さんざんsanzan Severely, thoroughly (a bad ordeal) Specifically about a harsh, unpleasant experience someone is directly put through (scolding, disaster) — not a general intensifier. "Be scolded severely"; "have a disastrous experience."
ほどほどhodohodo Moderately, with self-restraint A deliberate choice to hold back and not overdo something. "Drink alcohol in moderation" — advice or self-control.
そこそこsokosoko Reasonably well, so-so A result that is acceptable but unremarkable — about outcome quality, not restraint. "Got a so-so score on the exam."
Exam trap: ずるずる is easy to mistake for a single-meaning word, but the book itself gives it two unrelated-feeling glosses: dragging a physical object (重い荷物をずるずる引きずる) and a situation stalling with no resolution (ストがずるずる長引く). Both share the same core image — something being pulled out/along against resistance — so don't split them into "two words" when studying.
