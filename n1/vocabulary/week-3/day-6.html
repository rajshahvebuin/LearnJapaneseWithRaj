6日目 — どうにか最後までやろう (Dounika saigo made yarou — Let's somehow make it to the end)

OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
aどうにかdounikasomehow, manage to (wrong meaning — implies deliberate effort, not a hearsay observation)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Today's hedging/possibility words are exactly what tomorrow's review test opens with — get the boundaries clear now:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
どうにかdounika Somehow (manage to) Implies active effort by the subject to achieve or cope with something. "I somehow managed to graduate"; "I'll manage somehow" (about your own effort).
どうやらdouyara It seems, apparently A passive inference about a situation from outside evidence, not personal effort — also doubles for "barely" when paired with 間に合う. Reporting something you've deduced or heard secondhand.
かろうじてkaroujite Barely, just barely Describes a completed outcome achieved with the narrowest possible margin — no ambiguity about the result, only about how close it was. "Barely made the last train"; "barely passed."
仮にkari ni Hypothetically, suppose Sets up a scenario contrary to or independent of fact, usually with としても/たら. "Suppose you failed the exam — what then?"
ひょっとするとhyotto suru to Possibly, perhaps A speculative guess about an uncertain possibility, usually + かもしれない. "His story might possibly be a lie."
もしかしてmoshika shite By any chance Used when directly asking someone a guessing question — more conversational/direct than ひょっとすると. "Are you Mr. Tanaka, by any chance?"
Exam trap: どうにか and どうやら are the single easiest pair to confuse this week because both can gloss as "somehow" — but どうにか is about the subject's own effort producing a result, while どうやら is about an outside observer inferring what's probably true. Q1 above hinges entirely on this: divorce is something you hear about and infer (どうやら…らしい), not something the divorcing couple "managed to do" through effort from your point of view.
