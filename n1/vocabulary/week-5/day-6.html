Warm-up

（　）の中に入るのは？(___) no naka ni hairu no wa?
大学の授業料が（　）値上がりした。Daigaku no jugyouryou ga (___) neagari shita.
("Which word fits the blank? 'University tuition fees went up [___].' The cats hold up 軒並み, 応急に, and 独自に as candidates; the mascot's hint: it means 'each and every one, all alike.'")

軒並み (nokinami) — 軒並み値上がりした, "went up across the board, every single one."

Q1

朝の電車は（a. 過密な　b. 独自の）ダイヤで運行している。Asa no densha wa (a. kamitsu na / b. dokuji no) daiya de unkou shite iru.

EN: Morning trains run on an extremely packed schedule.
HI: सुबह की ट्रेनें बेहद भरे-पूरे समय-सारणी पर चलती हैं।
GU: સવારની ટ્રેનો ખૂબ ભરચક સમયપત્રક પર દોડે છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a過密なkamitsu naoverly packedबेहद भरा हुआઅતિ ભરચક
b独自のdokuji noone's own, unique (wrong meaning — a schedule can't be "unique," it can be "densely packed")(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

This flat-list day's traps are near-synonyms that an English gloss flattens into "the same word," exactly what Q2, Q3, and Q6 above test:

ClusterCore distinction
誤り／過ち誤り ayamari — a plain factual/typo error (e.g. a wrong date) vs. 過ち ayamachi — a moral wrongdoing/lapse in judgment one regrets
自己／自我自己 jiko — "self" in everyday compounds like 自己責任 (one's own responsibility) vs. 自我 jiga — the psychological "ego," used in discussions of identity/development
作戦／決意作戦 sakusen — a strategy/plan of action (paired with 練る, "to work out") vs. 決意 ketsui — one's inner resolve/determination (paired with 固める, "to firm up")
人並み／軒並みSame 並み ("in a row/on par") but different scope: 人並み hitonami compares one person to the average person, while 軒並み nokinami means "every single one, across the board" (originally "row of eaves/houses")
画期的／客観的／自発的／強制的All end in 的（な）but describe unrelated qualities: 画期的 = groundbreaking, 客観的 = objective (⇔主観的), 自発的 = voluntary, 強制的 = forced/compulsory — don't let the shared suffix suggest a shared meaning
Variant spelling note: This book prints 善し悪し yoshiashi ("right and wrong, good and bad") with okurigana on both halves; the more common spelling elsewhere is 良し悪し. Both are read the same way and mean the same thing — don't treat them as two different words.
