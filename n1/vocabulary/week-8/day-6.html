6日目 — よく使う表現② (Yoku tsukau hyougen ②)

Source
日本語総まとめ N1 語彙 — printed p.134–135 (PDF pages 124–125). Offset holds at printed page = PDF index + 10, confirmed by reading the printed page numbers directly.
Theme
生活でよく使う名詞 — everyday nouns grouped by short prefix/suffix families (～違い, ～放題, ～忘れ, ～嫌い, ～人, 逆～, 共～, （お）～), then a run of standalone everyday nouns (game/comparison words, romance & relationship vocabulary), and finally a further batch of single-word nouns each covering a very specific everyday nuance (つじつま, ひずみ, はり, つや, こつ, めど, ゆとり, ぐち, ねた).

Warm-up

("What goes in the blank? — 'His story doesn't add up.' Options shown: めど, こつ, つじつま.")

Q1

恩師の名前を（a.物忘れ b.度忘れ）した。Onshi no namae o (a. monowasure b. dowasure) shita.

Full sentence: 恩師の名前を度忘れした。Onshi no namae o dowasure shita.
EN: I suddenly blanked on my mentor's name.
HI: गुरुजी का नाम अचानक भूल गया।
GU: ગુરુજીનું નામ અચાનક ભુલાઈ ગયું.
#JapaneseEnglish
a物忘れmonowasuregeneral forgetfulness (wrong word — a trait, not a one-off slip)
b度忘れdowasuremomentarily blanking on something known

Why: 度忘れ describes forgetting something you actually know well, just for a moment — exactly a familiar mentor's name; 物忘れ describes a general tendency toward forgetfulness as a trait, not a specific slip.

Q2

ボーリングの投球の（a.こつ b.めど）をつかんだ。Booringu no toukyuu no (a. kotsu b. medo) o tsukanda.

Full sentence: ボーリングの投球のこつをつかんだ。Booringu no toukyuu no kotsu o tsukanda.
EN: I got the hang of how to throw a bowling ball.
HI: बॉलिंग की गेंद फेंकने का गुर आ गया।
GU: બોલિંગનો બોલ ફેંકવાની આવડત આવડી ગઈ.
#JapaneseEnglish
aこつkotsuthe knack/trick
bめどmedoa prospect/estimate (wrong word — about timing, not mastering a skill)

Why: こつをつかむ is the fixed idiom for "getting the hang of" a physical skill; めど describes having a sense of when or how something will be completed, unrelated to mastering a technique.

Q3

彼の話は時間的に（a.ひずみ b.つじつま）が合わない。Kare no hanashi wa jikanteki ni (a. hizumi b. tsujitsuma) ga awanai.

Full sentence: 彼の話は時間的につじつまが合わない。Kare no hanashi wa jikanteki ni tsujitsuma ga awanai.
EN: His story doesn't add up in terms of the timeline.
HI: उसकी बात समय के हिसाब से मेल नहीं खाती।
GU: તેની વાત સમયની દૃષ્ટિએ બંધબેસતી નથી.
#JapaneseEnglish
aひずみhizumistrain/distortion (wrong word — physical or structural, not logical)
bつじつまtsujitsumainternal consistency

Why: つじつまが合わない is the fixed idiom for a story or account being internally inconsistent; ひずみ describes a physical or structural strain (from an earthquake or a bad policy), not a logical contradiction.

Q4

ビルの完成のめどがやっと（a.合った b.たった）。Biru no kansei no medo ga yatto (a. atta b. tatta).

Full sentence: ビルの完成のめどがやっとたった。Biru no kansei no medo ga yatto tatta.
EN: A prospect for the building's completion has finally been established.
HI: इमारत के पूरा होने की कोई उम्मीद आख़िरकार दिखने लगी।
GU: ઈમારત પૂરી થવાની કોઈ શક્યતા આખરે દેખાવા લાગી.
#JapaneseEnglish
a合ったattamatched (wrong verb — めど doesn't pair with 合う)
bたったtattabecame established

Why: めどが立つ is the fixed collocation for "a prospect/estimate becomes established"; めど simply doesn't combine with 合う the way つじつま does.

Q5

彼らはとても（a.お似合い b.お見合い）のカップルですね。Karera wa totemo (a. oniai b. omiai) no kappuru desu ne.

Full sentence: 彼らはとてもお似合いのカップルですね。Karera wa totemo oniai no kappuru desu ne.
EN: They're such a well-matched couple, aren't they.
HI: वे बहुत जँचने वाला जोड़ा हैं, है ना।
GU: તેઓ ખૂબ શોભે એવું જોડું છે, ખરું ને.
#JapaneseEnglish
aお似合いoniaiwell-matched
bお見合いomiaian arranged meeting (wrong word — describes a formal matchmaking meeting, not an existing couple's compatibility)

Why: お似合い describes two people or things suiting each other visually/personality-wise, exactly a well-matched couple; お見合い refers to a formally arranged first meeting between prospective marriage partners, a different concept.

Q6

彼はシャツのボタンを（a.互い違い b.裏返し）に止めていた。Kare wa shatsu no botan o (a. tagaichigai b. uragaeshi) ni tomete ita.

Full sentence: 彼はシャツのボタンを互い違いに止めていた。Kare wa shatsu no botan o tagaichigai ni tomete ita.
EN: He had fastened his shirt buttons in the wrong order, alternating out of sync.
HI: उसने शर्ट के बटन उल्टे-सीधे, गड़बड़ क्रम में लगा रखे थे।
GU: તેણે શર્ટના બટન ઊંધા-છત્તા, ખોટા ક્રમમાં લગાવ્યા હતા.
#JapaneseEnglish
a互い違いtagaichigaialternating out of order
b裏返しuragaeshiinside-out (wrong word — describes an entire garment flipped, not buttons misaligned)

Why: 互い違いに describes something alternating out of its proper matching sequence, exactly buttons fastened one hole off; 裏返し means an entire item is turned inside-out, a different kind of mistake.

Q7

初対面の彼女に（　）してしまった。Shotaimen no kanojo ni (___) shite shimatta.

Full sentence: 初対面の彼女に一目ぼれしてしまった。Shotaimen no kanojo ni hitomebore shite shimatta.
EN: I fell in love at first sight with her the moment we met.
HI: पहली ही मुलाक़ात में उससे प्यार हो गया।
GU: પહેલી જ મુલાકાતમાં તેની સાથે પ્રેમ થઈ ગયો.
#JapaneseEnglish
1なれそめnaresomethe start of a romance (wrong part of speech — a noun describing an origin story, not a verb one "does")
2一目ぼれhitomeborelove at first sight
3見合いmiaian arranged meeting (wrong meaning — a formal matchmaking setup, not spontaneous attraction)
4気合kiaifighting spirit (wrong meaning entirely)

Why: 一目ぼれする is the fixed verb for falling in love at first sight, exactly matching "at our first meeting"; the other options describe unrelated concepts — how a relationship is later described, a formal matchmaking meeting, and willpower.

Q8

時間を（　）して待ち合わせに遅れてしまった。Jikan o (___) shite machiawase ni okurete shimatta.

Full sentence: 時間を勘違いして待ち合わせに遅れてしまった。Jikan o kanchigai shite machiawase ni okurete shimatta.
EN: I mixed up the time and ended up late for our meeting.
HI: समय को लेकर ग़लतफ़हमी हो गई और मीटिंग के लिए देर हो गई।
GU: સમય બાબતે ગેરસમજ થઈ અને મુલાકાત માટે મોડું થઈ ગયું.
#JapaneseEnglish
1食い違いkuichigaia discrepancy (wrong word — between two accounts, not one person's own error)
2行き違いikichigaicrossed paths (wrong word — two people missing each other, not a personal mix-up)
3勘違いkanchigaia mistaken belief
4見違いmichigainot a standard noun in this form

Why: 勘違いする is the fixed verb for mistakenly believing something incorrect, like the wrong meeting time; 食い違う and 行き違う describe two separate accounts or people failing to match up with each other, not a single person's own misunderstanding.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

With over 50 words in one day, here are the pairs most likely to blur into "the same word" under an English gloss:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
度忘れdowasure A momentary memory blank About something you DO know, just can't recall right now. 恩師の名前を度忘れした。
物忘れmonowasure General forgetfulness A broader trait/tendency, often used for age-related forgetfulness. 最近、物忘れがひどい。
お似合いoniai Well-matched (compatibility) Describes how well two things/people already suit each other. お似合いのカップル。
お見合いomiai An arranged meeting A specific formal event set up to potentially start a marriage. お見合いをする。
あべこべabekobe Reversed/the wrong way round Two things swapped into each other's correct position. 頭とシッポをあべこべにつける。
互い違いtagaichigai Alternating out of sync A repeating sequence misaligned by one step, not fully swapped. ボタンを互い違いに止める。
裏返しuragaeshi Inside-out An entire single object flipped to show its reverse side. シャツを裏返しに着る。
Slang worth knowing but using carefully: デブ, ナンパ, バツイチ, and ねた are all marked 俗語 (colloquial/slang) in the book itself. They're common in casual spoken Japanese and useful for listening comprehension, but デブ in particular can come across as rude — use it only when quoting or clearly informal, never to describe someone to their face.
