Q1

服装だけでなく髪型にも（a.気が利く b.気を配る）。Fukusou dake de naku kamigata ni mo (a. ki ga kiku b. ki o kubaru).

Full sentence: 服装だけでなく髪型にも気を配る。Fukusou dake de naku kamigata ni mo ki o kubaru.
EN: I pay careful attention not only to my clothes but to my hairstyle too.
HI: कपड़ों के अलावा हेयरस्टाइल पर भी ध्यान देना।
GU: કપડાં ઉપરાંત હેરસ્ટાઇલ પર પણ ધ્યાન આપવું.
#JapaneseEnglish
a気が利くki ga kikuquick-witted, tactful (wrong verb — a personality trait, not the act of paying attention to something specific)
b気を配るki o kubarupay careful attention to

Why: 気を配る describes actively directing one's attention/care toward several things — exactly clothes and hairstyle both; 気が利く describes an inherent quality of being perceptive/thoughtful, not an action performed on an object marked with に.

Q2

イカの刺身は（a.手が b.足が）早いので、今日中に食べたほうがいいです。Ika no sashimi wa (a. te ga b. ashi ga) hayai node, kyou juu ni tabeta hou ga ii desu.

Full sentence: イカの刺身は足が早いので、今日中に食べたほうがいいです。Ika no sashimi wa ashi ga hayai node, kyou juu ni tabeta hou ga ii desu.
EN: Squid sashimi spoils quickly, so it's best to eat it today.
HI: स्क्विड सशिमी जल्दी ख़राब हो जाती है, इसलिए आज ही खा लेना बेहतर है।
GU: સ્ક્વિડ સાશિમી ઝડપથી બગડી જાય છે, એટલે આજે જ ખાઈ લેવું સારું છે.
#JapaneseEnglish
a手が早いte ga hayaiquick to act/flirt (wrong idiom — describes a person, not food)
b足が早いashi ga hayaiperishable, spoils quickly

Why: 足が早い is the fixed idiom for food going bad quickly; 手が早い is an unrelated idiom describing a person who acts (or flirts) hastily.

Q3

私の学校は、やたら規律にうるさいので（a.息が切れる b.息が詰まる）。Watashi no gakkou wa, yatara kiritsu ni urusai node (a. iki ga kireru b. iki ga tsumaru).

Full sentence: 私の学校は、やたら規律にうるさいので息が詰まる。Watashi no gakkou wa, yatara kiritsu ni urusai node iki ga tsumaru.
EN: My school is excessively strict about rules, so it feels suffocating.
HI: मेरा स्कूल अनुशासन को लेकर बहुत सख़्त है, इसलिए दम घुटता है।
GU: મારી શાળા શિસ્ત બાબતે ખૂબ કડક છે, એટલે ગૂંગળામણ થાય છે.
#JapaneseEnglish
a息が切れるiki ga kireruout of breath (wrong meaning — physical exertion, not an oppressive mood)
b息が詰まるiki ga tsumarufeel suffocated/stifled

Why: 息が詰まる describes a stifling, oppressive atmosphere — exactly overly strict school rules; 息が切れる is purely about physical breathlessness after exertion like running.

Q4

その景色の美しさに思わず（a.息をのんだ b.息が切れた）。Sono keshiki no utsukushisa ni omowazu (a. iki o nonda b. iki ga kireta).

Full sentence: その景色の美しさに思わず息をのんだ。Sono keshiki no utsukushisa ni omowazu iki o nonda.
EN: I unconsciously held my breath at the beauty of the scenery.
HI: उस दृश्य की सुंदरता देखकर अनजाने में साँस थम गई।
GU: તે દૃશ્યની સુંદરતા જોઈને અજાણતાં શ્વાસ થંભી ગયો.
#JapaneseEnglish
a息をのんだiki o nondagasped, held breath in awe
b息が切れたiki ga kiretabecame out of breath (wrong meaning — exertion, not an emotional reaction)

Why: 息をのむ is the fixed idiom for gasping/catching one's breath in awe at beauty or shock; 息が切れる describes physical breathlessness from running or exertion, an unrelated cause.

Q5

GU: પ્રેક્ષકો વધુ હોય કે ઓછા, તે હંમેશાં કોઈ કસર છોડ્યા વિના પ્રદર્શન કરે છે.

EN: The aged mother waited eagerly for her child's return home.

GU: વૃદ્ધ મા પોતાના બાળકના પાછા આવવાની આતુરતાથી રાહ જોઈ રહી હતી.

Q7

こんな大きな犬を飼うのは、ちょっと私の（　）と思います。Konna ookina inu o kau no wa, chotto watashi no (___) to omoimasu.

Full sentence: こんな大きな犬を飼うのは、ちょっと私の手に余ると思います。Konna ookina inu o kau no wa, chotto watashi no te ni amaru to omoimasu.
EN: I think keeping such a big dog is a bit beyond what I can handle.
HI: इतना बड़ा कुत्ता पालना मेरे बस से थोड़ा बाहर है, ऐसा मुझे लगता है।
GU: આટલો મોટો કૂતરો પાળવો મારી ક્ષમતાની થોડો બહાર છે, એવું મને લાગે છે.
#JapaneseEnglish
1手を尽くすte o tsukusuexhaust every means (wrong meaning)
2足が出るashi ga derugo over budget (wrong meaning)
3手に余るte ni amarubeyond one's own capacity
4息がつまるiki ga tsumarufeel suffocated (wrong meaning)

Why: 手に余る is the fixed idiom for a responsibility exceeding what one can personally manage, exactly a dog too big to handle; the other options describe unrelated situations like exhausting resources or overspending.

GU: હાઇકિંગમાં આખો દિવસ ચાલ્યા પછી પગ લાકડાની જેમ જકડાઈ ગયા.

1長くなったnagaku nattagot longer (not an idiom here)

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

息 alone produces four unrelated idioms today, and 気 produces four more — the pair most likely to trip up a learner in each family:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
息が切れるiki ga kireru Out of breath (physical) Purely about exertion — running, climbing. 走って息が切れる。
息が詰まるiki ga tsumaru Feel suffocated (emotional/atmospheric) About an oppressive situation or crowding, not exercise. 満員電車で息が詰まる。
息をのむiki o nomu Gasp in awe A brief, involuntary reaction to beauty or shock. 息をのむほど美しい景色。
息が長いiki ga nagai Long-lasting (a career) Figurative only — has nothing to do with literal breathing. 息が長い役者。
気があるki ga aru To have romantic interest About feelings toward a specific person. 彼は田中さんに気があるらしい。
気が利くki ga kiku Tactful, quick-witted (trait) Describes someone's inherent personality, not an action. 彼女は気が利く人だ。
気を配るki o kubaru To pay attention to (action) An active, ongoing behavior directed at specific things/people. 周囲に気を配る。
Same body part, opposite direction of trouble: 手を焼く ("to struggle with something difficult," the trouble comes from outside — a mischievous kid) and 手を抜く ("to skimp on one's own effort," the trouble is self-inflicted laziness) both use 手 and both sound like complaints, but one describes being troubled BY something and the other describes cutting corners ON something. Keep the direction straight.
