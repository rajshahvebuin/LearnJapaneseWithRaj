Warm-up

（　）に入るのは？(___) ni hairu no wa?
それは、耳に（　）ができるほど聞いた。Sore wa, mimi ni (___) ga dekiru hodo kiita.
("What goes in the blank? — 'I've heard that until a [___] forms in my ear' — meaning being made to hear the same thing until sick of it. Options shown: ガン, 痛み, たこ, イカ.")

たこ (tako) — a callus; 耳にたこができるほど聞いた ＝ "I'm sick of hearing that"

Q1

祖父の自慢話は（a.顔から火が出るほど b.耳にたこが出来るほど）聞かされた。Sofu no jimanbanashi wa (a. kao kara hi ga deru hodo b. mimi ni tako ga dekiru hodo) kikasareta.

Full sentence: 祖父の自慢話は耳にたこが出来るほど聞かされた。Sofu no jimanbanashi wa mimi ni tako ga dekiru hodo kikasareta.
EN: I've been made to listen to grandpa's boastful stories so many times I'm sick of them.
HI: दादा की डींगें इतनी बार सुनी हैं कि कान पक गए।
GU: દાદાની બડાઈઓ એટલી વાર સાંભળી છે કે કંટાળો આવી ગયો.
#JapaneseEnglish
a顔から火が出るほどkao kara hi ga deru hodoembarrassed enough for fire to shoot from one's face (wrong simile — about shame, not repetition)
b耳にたこが出来るほどmimi ni tako ga dekiru hodosick of hearing something

Why: 耳にたこができる is the fixed idiom for having heard the same thing so many times one is sick of it, exactly repeated boastful stories; 顔から火が出る describes intense embarrassment, an unrelated feeling.

Q2

（a.差し支えなければ b.夜分遅くすみませんが）お名前をお教えくださいませんか。(a. Sashitsukae nakereba b. Yabun osoku sumimasen ga) onamae o oOshie kudasaimasen ka.

Full sentence: 差し支えなければ、お名前をお教えくださいませんか。Sashitsukae nakereba, onamae o oOshie kudasaimasen ka.
EN: If it's not too much trouble, could you tell me your name?
HI: अगर कोई असुविधा न हो, तो क्या आप अपना नाम बता सकते हैं?
GU: જો કોઈ અગવડ ન હોય, તો શું તમે તમારું નામ કહી શકશો?
#JapaneseEnglish
a差し支えなければsashitsukae nakerebaif it's no trouble
b夜分遅くすみませんがyabun osoku sumimasen gasorry to call this late at night (wrong context — no late-hour setting given)

Why: 差し支えなければ is the general polite hedge used before any request for information; 夜分遅くすみませんが is reserved specifically for apologizing about the lateness of a call or visit, a context not established here.

Q3

若いころなら（a.悪しからず b.いざ知らず）、今そんな無茶をすると体を壊すよ。Wakai koro nara (a. ashikarazu b. iza shirazu), ima sonna mucha o suru to karada o kowasu yo.

Full sentence: 若いころならいざ知らず、今そんな無茶をすると体を壊すよ。Wakai koro nara iza shirazu, ima sonna mucha o suru to karada o kowasu yo.
EN: Setting aside what you could get away with when young, doing something that reckless now will ruin your health.
HI: जवानी की बात छोड़ो, अब ऐसी लापरवाही करने से सेहत बिगड़ जाएगी।
GU: જુવાનીની વાત જવા દો, હવે આવી બેદરકારી કરવાથી તબિયત બગડી જશે.
#JapaneseEnglish
a悪しからずashikarazudon't take offense (wrong idiom — used when declining/refusing something, not contrasting past and present)
bいざ知らずiza shirazusetting aside, apart from

Why: ～ならいざ知らず is the fixed pattern for "that might be true/acceptable for ~, but apart from that..." — exactly the youth-vs-now contrast; 悪しからず only appears when politely refusing or declining something.

Q4

欲を言えば（a.ピン b.キリ）がないから、このくらいのアパートでいいんじゃないかな。Yoku o ieba (a. pin b. kiri) ga nai kara, kono kurai no apaato de ii n ja nai ka na.

Full sentence: 欲を言えばキリがないから、このくらいのアパートでいいんじゃないかな。Yoku o ieba kiri ga nai kara, kono kurai no apaato de ii n ja nai ka na.
EN: If we start being greedy there's no end to it, so an apartment about this size should be fine, don't you think?
HI: लालच करें तो कोई हद नहीं है, इसलिए इतना-सा फ़्लैट काफ़ी है, है ना?
GU: લોભ કરીએ તો કોઈ હદ નથી, એટલે આટલો ફ્લેટ પૂરતો છે, ખરું ને?
#JapaneseEnglish
aピンpinthe top/best quality (wrong word — belongs to ピンからキリまで, not 欲を言えば)

Q5

（a.去る b.来る）10月10日に体育祭が行われます。(a. Saru b. Kitaru) juu-gatsu tooka ni taiikusai ga okonawaremasu.

Full sentence: 来る10月10日に体育祭が行われます。Kitaru juu-gatsu tooka ni taiikusai ga okonawaremasu.
EN: The sports festival will be held on the upcoming October 10th.
HI: आगामी 10 अक्तूबर को खेल उत्सव आयोजित होगा।
GU: આગામી 10 ઓક્ટોબરે રમતોત્સવ યોજાશે.
#JapaneseEnglish
a去るsaruthe bygone/past (wrong direction — contradicts the future-tense 行われます)
b来るkitaruthe upcoming

Why: 来る marks an upcoming/future date, matching the future-tense 行われます ("will be held"); 去る marks a date already in the past, which would contradict the sentence's own tense.

Q6

（a.申し訳ありませんが b.差し支えなければ）その商品は弊社では取り扱っておりません。(a. Moushiwake arimasen ga b. Sashitsukae nakereba) sono shouhin wa heisha de wa toriatsukatte orimasen.

Full sentence: 申し訳ありませんが、その商品は弊社では取り扱っておりません。Moushiwake arimasen ga, sono shouhin wa heisha de wa toriatsukatte orimasen.
EN: I'm sorry, but our company does not carry that product.
HI: माफ़ कीजिए, हमारी कंपनी वह उत्पाद नहीं रखती।
GU: માફ કરશો, અમારી કંપની તે ઉત્પાદન રાખતી નથી.

Q7

（　）あのドレスがほしいです。(___) ano doresu ga hoshii desu.

Full sentence: のどから手が出るほどあのドレスがほしいです。Nodo kara te ga deru hodo ano doresu ga hoshii desu.
EN: I want that dress more than anything in the world.
HI: वह ड्रेस इतनी बुरी तरह चाहिए कि जैसे गले से हाथ निकल आए।
GU: તે ડ્રેસ એટલી બધી જોઈએ છે કે જાણે ગળામાંથી હાથ બહાર આવી જાય.

1雲をつかむほどkumo o tsukamu hodoas unrealistic as grasping a cloud (wrong simile)
2胸をえぐられるほどmune o egurareru hodoheartbroken enough to feel gouged (wrong simile)

Q8

Full sentence: お騒がせして申し訳ありません。Osawagase shite moushiwake arimasen.
EN: I'm sorry for causing such a commotion.
HI: हलचल मचाने के लिए माफ़ी चाहता हूँ।

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Several of today's set phrases share a word but point in opposite social directions:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
差し支えなければsashitsukae nakereba If it's no trouble (hedge before asking) Softens a request YOU are about to make of someone else. 差し支えなければ、お名前を…
申し訳ありませんがmoushiwake arimasen ga I'm sorry, but (apology before bad news) Softens a refusal or unwelcome answer YOU are about to give. 申し訳ありませんが、取り扱っておりません。
悪しからずashikarazu Please don't take offense Attached when declining/refusing, asking the listener not to be upset. 今回は難しいですが、悪しからず。
来るkitaru The upcoming (date) Points forward in time — pairs with future-tense verbs. 来る3月10日に…行われる。
去るsaru The bygone (date) Points backward in time — pairs with past-tense verbs. 去る3月10日に…行われた。
ピンからキリまでpin kara kiri made Ranging from best to worst キリ here means "the bottom/worst end" of a range. ワインもピンからキリまである。
キリがないkiri ga nai No end/limit to something キリ here means "a stopping point" — its absence means endlessness. 欲を言えばキリがない。
Same いざ, opposite grammar: いざという時 ("the critical moment/an emergency," a noun phrase describing a moment itself) and ～はいざ知らず ("setting aside whether ~ is true," a concessive pattern contrasting two cases) both use いざ but function completely differently in a sentence — one is a noun modifier, the other is a connective pattern. Don't try to swap them.
