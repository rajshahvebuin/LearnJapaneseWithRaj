Theme
似ている言葉 — words that look "the same" once translated into English but are NOT interchangeable in Japanese: deformation verbs (ゆがむ・ねじれる・こじれる・ほどける・ほころびる), verbs for a substance seeping/wilting/drying (にじむ・しみる・しなびる・かれる), a pair of sound-alike adverbs (じきに／じかに) plus three "everything/whatever" adverbs (何もかも／何でもかんでも／何だかんだ), いかに vs いかにも, and two nouns each covering ground English flattens into one word (ひび／すき間, 粒／滴, あか／あざ).

Warm-up

（　）に入るのは？(___) ni hairu no wa?
煙が目に（　）。Kemuri ga me ni (___).
("What goes in the blank? — 'Smoke [___] my eyes.' Options shown: にじむ, しみる, かれる.")

じきに・じかに and 何もかも・何でもかんでも・何だかんだ — sound-alike and look-alike adverbs

いかに・いかにも — "how much" vs. "every bit like"
それをいかに(＝どのように)実現するかが問題だ。Sore o ikani (=dono you ni) jitsugen suru ka ga mondai da.How we implement it is the question.इसे कैसे साकार किया जाए, यही सवाल है।તેને કેવી રીતે સાકાર કરવું, એ જ પ્રશ્ન છે.いかに = formal "how" — pairs with a question (か)
いかに(＝どんなに)急いでも間に合わないだろう。Ikani (=donna ni) isoide mo maniawanai darou.No matter how much we hurry, we won't make it.चाहे कितनी भी जल्दी करें, समय पर नहीं पहुँच पाएँगे।ગમે તેટલી ઉતાવળ કરીએ, સમયસર નહીં પહોંચાય.いかに～ても = "no matter how much ~"
それはいかにも残念です。Sore wa ikanimo zannen desu.It is certainly disappointing.यह वाक़ई बहुत निराशाजनक है।તે ખરેખર ખૂબ નિરાશાજનક છે.いかにも = "truly, every bit" — intensifies an adjective/description
彼はいかにも学者らしい。Kare wa ikanimo gakusha rashii.He surely looks like a scholar.वह बिल्कुल एक विद्वान जैसा लगता है।તે બિલકુલ વિદ્વાન જેવો લાગે છે.いかにも＋らしい is the fixed pattern for "every bit the stereotype of ~"

Q1

熱いなべを手で（a.じかに b.いかに）つかんで、やけどをしてしまった。Atsui nabe o te de (a. jikani b. ikani) tsukande, yakedo o shite shimatta.

Full sentence: 熱いなべを手でじかにつかんで、やけどをしてしまった。Atsui nabe o te de jikani tsukande, yakedo o shite shimatta.
EN: I grabbed the hot pot directly with my bare hand and got burned.
HI: मैंने गरम बर्तन को सीधे हाथ से पकड़ लिया और जल गया।
GU: મેં ગરમ વાસણને સીધું હાથથી પકડ્યું અને દાઝી ગયો.
#JapaneseEnglish
aじかにjikanidirectly, with nothing in between
bいかにikanihow (wrong word — needs a question or ～ても, not this slot)

Why: じかに describes touching/dealing with something with nothing in between (bare hand on a hot pot); いかに means "how" and grammatically can't fill this manner-adverb slot.

Q2

かゆいので肌をかきむしったら、血が（a.しみて b.にじんで）きた。Kayui node hada o kakimushittara, chi ga (a. shimite b. nijinde) kita.

Full sentence: かゆいので肌をかきむしったら、血がにじんできた。Kayui node hada o kakimushittara, chi ga nijinde kita.
EN: Since it was itchy, I scratched my skin raw until blood started to seep out.
HI: खुजली होने के कारण त्वचा को नोच डाला, तो खून रिसने लगा।
GU: ખંજવાળ આવવાથી ત્વચા ખોતરી નાખી, તો લોહી નીકળવા લાગ્યું.
#JapaneseEnglish
aしみてshimitestinging/soaking in (wrong verb — for smoke or sweat, not blood appearing)
bにじんでnijindeseeping/welling up

Why: にじむ is exactly the verb for a liquid (blood, tears, ink) slowly welling up to the surface; しみる describes a stinging sensation or a liquid soaking into fabric, not blood emerging from broken skin.

Q3

最近お酒を飲む量を減らそうと（a.心がけて b.心得て）います。Saikin osake o nomu ryou o herasou to (a. kokorogakete b. kokoroete) imasu.

Full sentence: 最近お酒を飲む量を減らそうと心がけています。Saikin osake o nomu ryou o herasou to kokorogakete imasu.
EN: Lately I've been making a conscious effort to cut down on how much I drink.
HI: आजकल मैं शराब पीने की मात्रा कम करने की सोच-समझकर कोशिश कर रहा हूँ।
GU: આજકાલ હું દારૂ પીવાનું પ્રમાણ ઘટાડવાનો સભાનપણે પ્રયત્ન કરું છું.
#JapaneseEnglish
a心がけてkokorogaketemaking a conscious effort toward
b心得てkokoroeteunderstanding/grasping (wrong verb — a fact/skill one already knows, not a goal one strives toward)

Why: 心がける means "to make it a habit/goal to try to ~," matching an ongoing resolution; 心得る means "to have understood/grasped" something, like manners or one's position, not a future intention.

Q4

転んだら、腰の骨に（a.あざ b.ひび）が入ってしまった。Korondara, koshi no hone ni (a. aza b. hibi) ga haitte shimatta.

Full sentence: 転んだら、腰の骨にひびが入ってしまった。Korondara, koshi no hone ni hibi ga haitte shimatta.
EN: When I fell, I ended up with a hairline crack in my hip bone.
HI: गिरने पर, कूल्हे की हड्डी में दरार आ गई।
GU: પડી જવાથી, થાપાના હાડકામાં તિરાડ પડી ગઈ.
#JapaneseEnglish
aあざazaa bruise (wrong noun — a surface discoloration, not a break in the bone)
bひびhibia hairline crack

Why: ひびが入る is the fixed phrase for a crack forming in something solid — a wall, dish, or bone; あざ is a bruise mark on the skin's surface, unrelated to a broken bone underneath.

Q5

私は（a.一粒の b.小粒の）納豆が好きです。Watashi wa (a. hitotsubu no b. kotsubu no) nattou ga suki desu.

Full sentence: 私は小粒の納豆が好きです。Watashi wa kotsubu no nattou ga suki desu.
EN: I like small-grain natto.
HI: मुझे छोटे दानों वाला नत्तो पसंद है।
GU: મને નાના દાણાવાળું નાત્તો ગમે છે.
#JapaneseEnglish
a一粒のhitotsubu noof one single grain (wrong meaning — describes a count of one, not a size variety)
b小粒のkotsubu nosmall-grained

Why: 小粒 describes a whole food as being the small-grain variety (小 = small + 粒 = grain), the natural way to describe a preference for natto's grain size; 一粒の literally means "of a single grain," which doesn't describe an entire dish.

Q6

（a.しなびた b.枯れた）白菜があったが、スープにしたらおいしかった。(a. Shinabita b. Kareta) hakusai ga atta ga, suupu ni shitara oishikatta.

Full sentence: しなびた白菜があったが、スープにしたらおいしかった。Shinabita hakusai ga atta ga, suupu ni shitara oishikatta.
EN: I had some wilted napa cabbage, but it turned out delicious made into soup.
HI: मुरझाई हुई पत्ता गोभी थी, पर सूप बनाकर स्वादिष्ट बनी।
GU: ચીમળાયેલી કોબી હતી, પણ સૂપ બનાવીને સ્વાદિષ્ટ બની.
#JapaneseEnglish
aしなびたshinabitawilted, gone limp
b枯れたkaretadied/withered (wrong verb — too final for a vegetable still fit to cook)

Why: しなびる describes a vegetable losing moisture/firmness while still edible — exactly cabbage headed for soup; 枯れる describes a plant dying outright, like a tree, and wouldn't describe something you then cook and enjoy.

Q7

彼は（　）政治家らしい口調で話す。Kare wa (___) seijika rashii kuchou de hanasu.

Full sentence: 彼はいかにも政治家らしい口調で話す。Kare wa ikanimo seijika rashii kuchou de hanasu.
EN: He speaks in a tone that really does sound just like a politician.
HI: वह बिल्कुल एक राजनेता जैसे लहजे में बोलता है।
GU: તે બિલકુલ રાજકારણી જેવા લહેકામાં બોલે છે.
#JapaneseEnglish
1いかにikanihow (wrong word — needs a question, not らしい)
2いかにもikanimoevery bit, truly
3何もかもnanimokamoabsolutely everything (wrong meaning)
4じかにjikanidirectly (wrong meaning)

Why: いかにも＋～らしい is the fixed pattern for "looking/sounding exactly like the stereotype of ~"; いかに alone means "how" and needs a question word or ～ても, not らしい.

Q8

靴のひもが（　）いるよ。Kutsu no himo ga (___) iru yo.

Full sentence: 靴のひもがほどけているよ。Kutsu no himo ga hodokete iru yo.
EN: Your shoelace has come untied.
HI: तुम्हारे जूते का फीता खुल गया है।
GU: તારા બૂટની દોરી છૂટી ગઈ છે.
#JapaneseEnglish
1しなびてshinabitewilted (wrong meaning — for vegetables)
2ほどけてhodoketecome untied
3ほころびてhokorobitecoming apart at the seam (wrong verb — for stitching, not a tied bow)
4にじんでnijindeseeping (wrong meaning)

Why: ほどける is exactly "to come untied" for a knot or bow, like a shoelace; ほころびる is reserved for stitching/a seam coming apart, a different kind of unraveling.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Since today's whole lesson IS a set of confusable words, here is the cheat-sheet distilling the trickiest clusters:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
にじむnijimu To seep/well up to the surface A liquid already inside something gradually shows through (ink, tears, blood). 涙がにじむ, 血がにじむ.
しみるshimiru To sting, or to soak into Either a sharp sensory sting (smoke in the eyes) or a liquid absorbing into fabric. 煙が目にしみる, 汗がシャツに染みる.
しなびるshinabiru To wilt/go limp Loses moisture and firmness but is still usable/edible. 野菜がしなびる.
枯れる／涸れるkareru To die (plant) / to dry up (water) / to go hoarse (voice) Same reading, three unrelated meanings depending on kanji and subject — a classic false-friend trap. 木が枯れる, 池が涸れる, 声がかれる.
じきにjikini Soon (time) About when something will happen. 雨はじきにあがる.
じかにjikani Directly (manner) Sound-alike but unrelated — about nothing standing in between two parties/things. 社長とじかに話す.
何もかもnanimokamo Absolutely everything An exhaustive, total sum — nothing left out. 何もかもいやになった。
何でもかんでもnandemokandemo Whatever it happens to be, indiscriminately Emphasizes a lack of selectivity, not a total sum. 何でもかんでもかじる。
Same word, three unrelated meanings: かれる is this lesson's sharpest trap — 木が枯れる ("a tree dies," written 枯れる), 声がかれる ("a voice goes hoarse," usually written かれる in kana or 嗄れる), and 池がかれる ("a pond dries up," written 涸れる) all sound identical but describe three completely different subjects drying out, dying, or straining. Never assume one かれる sentence tells you how to use another.
