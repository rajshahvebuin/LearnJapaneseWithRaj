Q1

子犬のしつけには（　）います。Koinu no shitsuke ni wa (___) imasu.

Full sentence: 子犬のしつけには手を焼いています。Koinu no shitsuke ni wa te o yaite imasu.
EN: I'm having a hard time training the puppy.
HI: पिल्ले को सिखाने में काफ़ी परेशानी हो रही है।
GU: ગલૂડિયાને તાલીમ આપવામાં ખૂબ મુશ્કેલી પડી રહી છે.
#JapaneseEnglish
1息が切れてiki ga kireteout of breath (wrong meaning)
2足が出てashi ga deteover budget (wrong meaning)
3手を焼いてte o yaitestruggling with, at one's wit's end
4目を離してme o hanashitelooking away from (wrong meaning)

Why (official): 手を焼いている(＝困っている) — training a puppy is exactly the kind of ongoing struggle 手を焼く describes.

Q2

彼の才能には社長も（　）置いています。Kare no sainou ni wa shachou mo (___) oite imasu.

Full sentence: 彼の才能には社長も一目置いています。Kare no sainou ni wa shachou mo ichimoku oite imasu.
EN: Even the company president acknowledges his talent with respect.
HI: उसकी प्रतिभा को कंपनी के अध्यक्ष भी सम्मान से स्वीकारते हैं।
GU: તેની પ્રતિભાને કંપનીના પ્રમુખ પણ આદરથી સ્વીકારે છે.
#JapaneseEnglish
1一目ichimokua glance (in 一目置く, "hold in high regard")
2一息hitoikione breath (wrong word)
3一筋hitosujisingle-mindedly (wrong word)
4一見ikkenat a glance (wrong word — pairs with 価値がある, not 置く)

Why (official): 一目置く(＝相手が優れているところを認め、敬意を払う) — to acknowledge someone's superior ability and show respect for it.

Q3

火事で家が焼け、彼は財産も（　）失ってしまった。Kaji de ie ga yake, kare wa zaisan mo (___) ushinatte shimatta.

Full sentence: 火事で家が焼け、彼は財産も何もかも失ってしまった。Kaji de ie ga yake, kare wa zaisan mo nanimokamo ushinatte shimatta.
EN: The house burned down in the fire, and he ended up losing absolutely everything, fortune included.
HI: आग से घर जल गया, और वह अपनी संपत्ति सहित बिल्कुल सब कुछ खो बैठा।
GU: આગથી ઘર બળી ગયું, અને તે પોતાની મિલકત સહિત બિલકુલ બધું જ ગુમાવી બેઠો.
#JapaneseEnglish
1何でもかんでもnandemokandemoindiscriminately, whatever it is (wrong meaning here)
2何もかもnanimokamoabsolutely everything
3何だかんだnandakandawhatever people say (wrong meaning)
4何でもかんでもnandemokandemo(identical to option 1 — printed this way in the source book, reproduced faithfully)

Why (official): 何もかも(＝すべて)失う — "to lose absolutely everything," matching a total, exhaustive loss. Note: options 1 and 4 are printed identically in the original book — an apparent errata, not a transcription error on this site.

Q4

秋の夜、老いた母は虫の声を（　）聞いていた。Aki no yoru, oita haha wa mushi no koe o (___) kiite ita.

Full sentence: 秋の夜、老いた母は虫の声を耳をすまして聞いていた。Aki no yoru, oita haha wa mushi no koe o mimi o sumashite kiite ita.
EN: On an autumn night, the aged mother was listening intently to the sound of the insects.
HI: शरद ऋतु की रात में, बूढ़ी माँ कीड़ों की आवाज़ ध्यान से कान लगाकर सुन रही थी।
GU: પાનખરની રાત્રે, વૃદ્ધ મા જીવડાંનો અવાજ ધ્યાનથી કાન માંડીને સાંભળી રહી હતી.
#JapaneseEnglish
1耳についてmimi ni tsuitestuck in one's ear/head (wrong meaning)
2耳をすましてmimi o sumashitelistening with concentrated attention
3耳にさわってmimi ni sawattegrating on the ear (wrong meaning)
4耳にはさんでmimi ni hasandeoverhearing by chance (wrong meaning)

Why (official): 耳をすまして(＝聞こうと集中して)聞く — to listen with one's full, concentrated attention, exactly straining to hear faint insect sounds at night.

Q5

彼女と待ち合わせしたが、どうも（　）になったようで、結局会うことができなかった。Kanojo to machiawase shita ga, doumo (___) ni natta you de, kekkyoku au koto ga dekinakatta.

Full sentence: 彼女と待ち合わせしたが、どうも行き違いになったようで、結局会うことができなかった。Kanojo to machiawase shita ga, doumo ikichigai ni natta you de, kekkyoku au koto ga dekinakatta.
EN: I had arranged to meet her, but we somehow seem to have crossed paths without meeting, so in the end I couldn't see her.
HI: उससे मिलने का समय तय था, पर लगता है हम एक-दूसरे को चूक गए, आख़िरकार मुलाक़ात नहीं हो पाई।
GU: તેની સાથે મળવાનું નક્કી હતું, પણ લાગે છે અમે એકબીજાને ચૂકી ગયા, છેવટે મુલાકાત ન થઈ.
#JapaneseEnglish
1互い違いtagaichigaialternating out of sync (wrong meaning)
2行き違いikichigaicrossed paths without meeting
3食い違いkuichigaia discrepancy (wrong meaning)
4人違いhitochigaimistaken identity (wrong meaning)

Why (official): 行き違い(＝すれ違いになる) — two people who each went to meet the other but missed each other.

Q6

朝の魚市場は（　）忙しい。Asa no uoichiba wa (___) isogashii.

Full sentence: 朝の魚市場は猫の手も借りたいほど忙しい。Asa no uoichiba wa neko no te mo karitai hodo isogashii.
EN: The morning fish market is so busy they'd take help from anyone, even a cat.
HI: सुबह की मछली मंडी इतनी व्यस्त होती है कि बिल्ली का पंजा भी उधार माँग लें।
GU: સવારનું માછલી બજાર એટલું વ્યસ્ત હોય છે કે બિલાડીનો પંજો પણ ઉછીનો માંગી લે.
#JapaneseEnglish
1顔から火が出るほどkao kara hi ga deru hodoembarrassed enough for fire to shoot from one's face (wrong simile)
2のどから手が出るほどnodo kara te ga deru hododesperately wanting something (wrong simile)
3猫の手も借りたいほどneko no te mo karitai hodoextremely busy
4胸をえぐられるようにmune o egurareru you niheartbroken (wrong simile)

Why: 猫の手も借りたいほど is the fixed simile for being so busy one would accept help from anyone, however unlikely — the classic image for a bustling early-morning market.

Q7

この電球は高いけれど、（　）、結局は節約になる。Kono denkyuu wa takai keredo, (___), kekkyoku wa setsuyaku ni naru.

Full sentence: この電球は高いけれど、長い目で見ると、結局は節約になる。Kono denkyuu wa takai keredo, nagai me de miru to, kekkyoku wa setsuyaku ni naru.
EN: This lightbulb is expensive, but taking the long view, it ends up saving money.
HI: यह बल्ब महँगा है, पर लंबे समय की सोच से देखें तो, आख़िरकार यह बचत ही है।
GU: આ બલ્બ મોંઘો છે, પણ લાંબા ગાળાની દૃષ્ટિએ જોઈએ તો, છેવટે તે બચત જ છે.
#JapaneseEnglish
1頭を下げて見るとatama o sagete miru tonot a fixed idiom (wrong meaning)
2長い目で見るとnagai me de miru totaking the long view
3気を配って見るとki o kubatte miru topaying attention (wrong meaning)
4あらゆる手を見るとarayuru te o miru tonot a fixed idiom (wrong meaning)

Why (official): 長い目で見る(＝今のことだけ考えず、長期的に考える) — to think long-term rather than fixating on the present cost.

Q8

お風呂場のリフォームをするため、業者に（　）をしてもらった。Ofuroba no rifoomu o suru tame, gyousha ni (___) o shite moratta.

Full sentence: お風呂場のリフォームをするため、業者に見積もりをしてもらった。Ofuroba no rifoomu o suru tame, gyousha ni mitsumori o shite moratta.
EN: To renovate the bathroom, I had a contractor give me a cost estimate.
HI: बाथरूम की मरम्मत के लिए, ठेकेदार से लागत का अनुमान लगवाया।
GU: બાથરૂમના સમારકામ માટે, કોન્ટ્રાક્ટર પાસેથી ખર્ચનો અંદાજ કઢાવ્યો.
#JapaneseEnglish
1見晴らしmiharashia scenic view (wrong meaning)
2見計らいmihakaraichoosing the right timing (wrong meaning)
3見積もりmitsumoria cost estimate
4見落としmiotoshian oversight (wrong meaning)

Why: 見積もり is the standard noun for a contractor's cost estimate/quote before work begins, exactly what's needed before a bathroom renovation.

Q9

前はおいしいと思ったのにそう思えないのは、（　）しまったのかもしれない。Mae wa oishii to omotta noni sou omoenai no wa, (___) shimatta no kamoshirenai.

Full sentence: 前はおいしいと思ったのにそう思えないのは、口が肥えてしまったのかもしれない。Mae wa oishii to omotta noni sou omoenai no wa, kuchi ga koete shimatta no kamoshirenai.
EN: The reason it doesn't taste as good as it used to might be that my palate has become more refined.
HI: पहले जो स्वादिष्ट लगता था अब वैसा नहीं लगता, शायद इसलिए क्योंकि मेरी ज़ुबान अब पारखी हो गई है।
GU: પહેલાં જે સ્વાદિષ્ટ લાગતું હતું તે હવે તેવું નથી લાગતું, કદાચ મારી જીભ હવે વધુ ચોખલિયું થઈ ગઈ છે.
#JapaneseEnglish
1口が肥えてkuchi ga koetedeveloped a refined/demanding palate
2鼻が高くなってhana ga takaku nattebecame proud (wrong meaning)
3気が散ってki ga chittebecame distracted (wrong meaning)
4目が疑ってme ga utagattenot a natural verb form here

Why (official): 口が肥えて(＝おいしいものを食べ慣れて、「おいしい」と思うレベルが上がっている) — having grown used to good food, one's standard for "delicious" has risen.

Q10

電気のコードが（　）いると、熱を持つから危ないですよ。Denki no koodo ga (___) iru to, netsu o motsu kara abunai desu yo.

Full sentence: 電気のコードがねじれていると、熱を持つから危ないですよ。Denki no koodo ga nejirete iru to, netsu o motsu kara abunai desu yo.
EN: If the electrical cord is twisted, it can build up heat, so that's dangerous.
HI: अगर बिजली का तार मुड़-तुड़कर उलझा हुआ हो, तो गर्मी पैदा होकर ख़तरा हो सकता है।
GU: જો ઈલેક્ટ્રિક કોર્ડ વળ ખાઈને ગૂંચવાયેલો હોય, તો ગરમી પેદા થઈને જોખમ થઈ શકે.
#JapaneseEnglish
1ほどけてhodoketecome untied (wrong verb — for knots/bows)
2ゆがんでyugandecrooked (wrong verb — for a fixed shape like a tie)
3ほころびてhokorobitecoming apart at the seam (wrong verb — for stitching)
4ねじれてnejiretetwisted

Why: ねじれる describes a long, flexible object twisting along its own axis, exactly a tangled cord — and a twisted cord really can build up dangerous heat.

問題4 — which sentence uses the word correctly? (1問4点)

Q21

あべこべabekobe

#JapaneseEnglish
1収入と支出のあべこべが合わない。Shuunyuu to shishutsu no abekobe ga awanai."The income-and-expense's あべこべ doesn't match" — wrong noun; this needs つじつまが合わない, a story/figures being consistent
2トラックが多く通る道路はあべこべしている。Torakku ga ooku tooru douro wa abekobe shite iru."A road with lots of trucks is あべこべ" — wrong word; a bumpy road needs でこぼこしている, not あべこべ
3工事で車線が狭く、車はあべこべに通行した。Kouji de sharen ga semaku, kuruma wa abekobe ni tsuukou shita."Lanes narrowed by construction, cars passed あべこべに" — wrong word; alternating one at a time needs 互い違い／交互に, not あべこべ
4ぬいぐるみの頭とシッポをあべこべにつけてしまった。Nuigurumi no atama to shippo o abekobe ni tsukete shimatta.I ended up attaching the stuffed animal's head and tail the wrong way round.

Why (official): 「あべこべ」は順序や位置が逆さまに入れ替わっている様子を表す — あべこべ describes two things swapped into each other's correct position (head and tail reversed); options 1, 2, and 3 each need a different word (つじつま, でこぼこ, 互い違い／交互).

Q22

度忘れdowasure

#JapaneseEnglish
1今まで覚えていたのに、度忘れしてしまった。Ima made oboete ita noni, dowasure shite shimatta.Even though I'd known it until now, it suddenly slipped my mind.
2今日の気温を度忘れしないように気をつけよう。Kyou no kion o dowasure shinai you ni ki o tsukeyou."Let's be careful not to 度忘れ today's temperature" — odd; you can't "momentarily blank" on something you never memorized in the first place
3今日は雨なので電車の中に傘の度忘れが多い。Kyou wa ame na node densha no naka ni kasa no dowasure ga ooi."Since it's raining, there are many umbrella 度忘れ on trains" — wrong noun; a left-behind item needs 忘れ物, not 度忘れ
4緊張して度忘れしたことが思い出せない。Kinchou shite dowasure shita koto ga omoidasenai."Nervous, I can't recall the thing I 度忘れした" — redundant/circular; 度忘れ already IS the state of being unable to recall

Why (official): 「度忘れ」は知っているはずのことを、その場で忘れて思い出せないこと — 度忘れ describes momentarily forgetting something you actually do know, exactly option 1; option 3's left umbrellas need 傘の忘れ物, a different concept.

Q23

気が散るki ga chiru

#JapaneseEnglish
1面接で予想していないことを聞かれて気が散った。Mensetsu de yosou shite inai koto o kikarete ki ga chitta."Asked something unexpected in the interview, I 気が散った" — odd; a sudden surprise needs 動揺した/慌てた, not the ongoing-distraction sense of 気が散る
2気が散るからテレビを消して。Ki ga chiru kara terebi o keshite.It's distracting, so turn off the TV.
35人も人を殺したという犯人は、気が散っているね。Gonin mo hito o koroshita to iu hannin wa, ki ga chitte iru ne."A criminal who killed 5 people is 気が散っている" — wrong word; describing someone as deranged needs 気が狂っている, not 気が散る
4こんなに作ったのにまだ予定の半分にも満たないとは、とても気が散る。Konna ni tsukutta noni mada yotei no hanbun ni mo mitanai to wa, totemo ki ga chiru."Made this much and still under half the target — very 気が散る" — wrong word; that frustration needs 気が滅入る/がっかりする, not 気が散る

Why (official): 「気が散る」は一つのことに気持ちを集中できないという意味 — 気が散る means being unable to concentrate on one thing, exactly why background noise like a TV is distracting; options 1, 3, and 4 each need a different emotional word entirely.

Q24

足が出るashi ga deru

#JapaneseEnglish
1今月は足が出るほどの買い物をしなければならない。Kongetsu wa ashi ga deru hodo no kaimono o shinakereba naranai."This month I must shop enough for 足が出る" — awkward phrasing; 足が出る describes a resulting state (over budget), not a purpose/degree clause like this
2いたずらな末っ子には本当に足が出る。Itazura na suekko ni wa hontou ni ashi ga deru."With the mischievous youngest child, 足が出る" — wrong idiom; struggling with a mischievous kid needs 手を焼く, not 足が出る
3彼女はインテリアの世界ではかなり足が出た存在です。Kanojo wa interia no sekai de wa kanari ashi ga deta sonzai desu."In the interior design world, she is a 足が出た presence" — wrong idiom; standing out needs 名の知れた／際立った, not 足が出る
4海外旅行でお土産を買いすぎて、予算から足が出てしまった。Kaigai ryokou de omiyage o kaisugite, yosan kara ashi ga dete shimatta.I bought too many souvenirs on the overseas trip and ended up going over budget.

Why (official): 「足が出る」は出費が多く、予算が足りなくなるという意味 — 足が出る means spending too much and running over one's budget, exactly buying too many souvenirs; option 2's mischievous child instead needs 手を焼く.

Q25

いかにもikanimo

#JapaneseEnglish
1彼女はいかにも自分が能力があるかを必死でアピールした。Kanojo wa ikanimo jibun ga nouryoku ga aru ka o hisshi de apiiru shita."She desperately appealed いかにも whether she was capable" — wrong word; a question like "whether/how much ability" needs いかに, not いかにも
2このドレッシングはいかにもおいしく食べられます。Kono doresshingu wa ikanimo oishiku taberaremasu."This dressing can いかにも be eaten deliciously" — odd collocation; いかにも intensifies a description/impression, not a potential-form verb phrase like this
3そのラーメン屋の看板にはいかにもおいしそうなラーメンの絵が描かれている。Sono raamen-ya no kanban ni wa ikanimo oishisou na raamen no e ga kakarete iru.That ramen shop's sign has a picture of ramen that looks every bit as delicious as can be.
4空がいかにも晴れてきた。Sora ga ikanimo harete kita."The sky いかにも cleared up" — odd; a simple factual change like weather clearing doesn't take いかにも's "every bit the stereotype of" nuance

Why (official): Option 1 should use いかに (＝いかに能力があるかを, "however much ability she had") — いかにも only intensifies a description that already carries a comparison/impression, like ～そうな ("looking like ~"), exactly option 3's "looking every bit as delicious."
