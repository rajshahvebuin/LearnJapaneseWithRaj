JLPT N1 · Vocabulary · Week 8 — まとめて覚えよう② (Matomete oboeyou ②)

3日目 — 体の部分を使った言葉① (Karada no bubun o tsukatta kotoba ①)

Source
日本語総まとめ N1 語彙 — printed p.128–129 (PDF pages 118–119). Offset holds at printed page = PDF index + 10, confirmed by reading the printed page numbers directly.
Theme
Body-part idioms, the first of two days on this theme: 顔 (face — reputation/connections), 目 (eyes — attention/perception), 耳 (ears — listening), 鼻 (nose — pride), 口 (mouth — speech/taste), and 頭 (head — thought/admiration). Each body part carries several fixed idioms that don't share a single translation pattern in English.

Warm-up

Q. 何という？Nan to iu?
よく知られていて特別な扱いをしてもらえること。Yoku shirarete ite tokubetsu na atsukai o shite moraeru koto.
("What's this called? — 'Being well known somewhere, and receiving special treatment there because of it.' Options shown: 目がこえる, 鼻にかける, 顔が利く.")

顔が利く (kao ga kiku) — "I'm a good customer of theirs, so they give me special treatment"

顔 (kao) — face: reputation, connections, standing
彼はこの辺りでは顔が売れている。Kare wa kono atari de wa kao ga urete iru.He's well connected around here.वह इस इलाक़े में काफ़ी जाना-पहचाना है।તે આ વિસ્તારમાં ખૂબ જાણીતો છે.顔が売れる = "to be a known/recognized face" (like a minor celebrity)

私はその店に顔が利く。Watashi wa sono mise ni kao ga kiku.I'm a good customer of theirs, so they give me special treatment.मेरी उस दुकान में अच्छी पहचान है, इसलिए वे ख़ास ख़याल रखते हैं।તે દુકાનમાં મારી સારી ઓળખાણ છે, એટલે તેઓ ખાસ ધ્યાન રાખે છે.today's warm-up answer — 顔が利く = "known well enough somewhere to get special treatment"

鼻 (hana) — nose: pride, arrogance
彼女は美人を鼻にかけている。Kanojo wa bijin o hana ni kakete iru.She's always boasting about her beauty.वह अपनी ख़ूबसूरती पर बहुत इतराती है।તે પોતાની સુંદરતાનું ખૂબ ગુમાન કરે છે.鼻にかける = "to boast/be smug about" — negative connotation
立派な息子を持って鼻が高い。Rippa na musuko o motte hana ga takai.I'm proud of my good son.अच्छे बेटे के होने पर गर्व महसूस होनाસારા દીકરાનું ગૌરવ અનુભવવું鼻が高い = "proud" — neutral-to-positive, unlike 鼻にかける

つい口が滑った。Tsui kuchi ga subetta.I let the secret slip.अनजाने में राज़ ज़ुबान से निकल गया।અજાણતાં રહસ્ય મોઢેથી નીકળી ગયું.口が滑る = "to let something slip by accident"
彼は口が肥えている。Kare wa kuchi ga koete iru.He is a gourmet.उसकी ज़ुबान बहुत नफ़ीस है, स्वाद परखने में माहिर है।તેની જીભ ખૂબ ચોખલિયું છે, સ્વાદ પારખવામાં માહિર છે.口が肥える = "a refined palate" (same 肥える as 目が肥える, applied to taste)

彼は頭がよく切れる。Kare wa atama ga yoku kireru.He is very sharp.वह बहुत तेज़-तर्रार दिमाग़ का है।તે ખૂબ તેજ દિમાગનો છે.頭が切れる = "sharp-minded, quick-witted"
彼の努力には頭が下がる。Kare no doryoku ni wa atama ga sagaru.I admire his effort.उसकी मेहनत के आगे सिर झुक जाता है।તેની મહેનત આગળ માથું ઝૂકી જાય છે.頭が下がる = "to feel genuine admiration/respect"

Q1

彼の人を見下した態度が、（a.鼻が高い b.鼻につく）。Kare no hito o mikudashita taido ga, (a. hana ga takai b. hana ni tsuku).

Full sentence: 彼の人を見下した態度が、鼻につく。Kare no hito o mikudashita taido ga, hana ni tsuku.
EN: His condescending attitude toward others really gets on my nerves.
HI: दूसरों को नीचा दिखाने वाला उसका रवैया बहुत खलता है।
GU: બીજાને નીચા દેખાડતું તેનું વલણ બહુ ખૂંચે છે.
#JapaneseEnglish
a鼻が高いhana ga takaiproud (wrong tone — positive, doesn't fit a complaint about someone)
b鼻につくhana ni tsukugrating, annoying

Why: 鼻につく describes a manner that becomes irritating the more one is exposed to it — exactly a condescending attitude; 鼻が高い is a positive feeling of pride about oneself or one's own family, the wrong tone entirely here.

Q2

あの人は口が（a.すべっている b.こえている）から、そのレストランの料理では満足しないでしょう。Ano hito wa kuchi ga (a. subette iru b. koete iru) kara, sono resutoran no ryouri de wa manzoku shinai deshou.

Full sentence: あの人は口がこえているから、そのレストランの料理では満足しないでしょう。Ano hito wa kuchi ga koete iru kara, sono resutoran no ryouri de wa manzoku shinai deshou.
EN: That person has a refined palate, so they probably won't be satisfied with that restaurant's food.
HI: उस व्यक्ति की ज़ुबान बहुत नफ़ीस है, इसलिए वह उस रेस्तराँ के खाने से संतुष्ट नहीं होगा।
GU: તે વ્યક્તિની જીભ ખૂબ ચોખલિયું છે, એટલે તે રેસ્ટોરન્ટના ખાવાથી સંતુષ્ટ નહીં થાય.
#JapaneseEnglish
aすべっているsubette iruletting something slip (wrong meaning — about accidentally revealing a secret)
bこえているkoete irurefined, discerning (of taste)

Why: 口が肥える describes someone who's eaten enough good food to become hard to satisfy, exactly the reason they'd be picky about a restaurant; 口が滑る is about accidentally revealing something in speech, unrelated to taste.

Q3

答えるのに頭を（a.かたむける b.かかえる）ような質問がたくさん来た。Kotaeru no ni atama o (a. katamukeru b. kakaeru) you na shitsumon ga takusan kita.

Full sentence: 答えるのに頭をかかえるような質問がたくさん来た。Kotaeru no ni atama o kakaeru you na shitsumon ga takusan kita.
EN: A lot of questions came in that were hard enough to make one hold one's head in despair trying to answer them.
HI: जवाब देने में सिर पकड़ लेने जैसे कई सवाल आए।
GU: જવાબ આપવામાં માથું પકડી લેવું પડે એવા ઘણા સવાલ આવ્યા.
#JapaneseEnglish
aかたむけるkatamukerutilt (wrong verb — 傾ける pairs with 耳, "to lend an ear," not 頭)
bかかえるkakaeruhold/cradle (be deeply troubled)

Why: 頭を抱える is the fixed idiom for being so stumped or troubled by something that one holds one's own head in one's hands; 傾ける is reserved for 耳を傾ける ("to listen attentively"), not 頭.

Q4

ありがとうございます。そうしていただけると私の（a.顔が立ちます b.顔がききます）。Arigatou gozaimasu. Sou shite itadakeru to watashi no (a. kao ga tachimasu b. kao ga kikimasu).

Full sentence: ありがとうございます。そうしていただけると私の顔が立ちます。Arigatou gozaimasu. Sou shite itadakeru to watashi no kao ga tachimasu.
EN: Thank you. If you could do that, it would save my reputation.
HI: धन्यवाद। अगर आप ऐसा कर दें, तो मेरी इज़्ज़त बच जाएगी।
GU: આભાર. જો તમે એમ કરી શકો, તો મારી આબરૂ જળવાઈ રહેશે.
#JapaneseEnglish
a顔が立ちますkao ga tachimasuone's reputation is upheld
b顔がききますkao ga kikimasuget special treatment somewhere (wrong idiom — about being a known customer, not reputation being saved)

Why: 顔が立つ means one's honor/reputation is preserved thanks to someone's action, exactly what's being thanked for here; 顔が利く describes being well-known enough at a specific place to receive favors, an unrelated situation.

Q5

彼の努力には（a.頭が下がる b.会わせる顔が無い）。Kare no doryoku ni wa (a. atama ga sagaru b. awaseru kao ga nai).

Full sentence: 彼の努力には頭が下がる。Kare no doryoku ni wa atama ga sagaru.
EN: I have to admire his effort.
HI: उसकी मेहनत के आगे सिर झुक जाता है।
GU: તેની મહેનત આગળ માથું ઝૂકી જાય છે.
#JapaneseEnglish
a頭が下がるatama ga sagarufeel genuine admiration/respect
b会わせる顔が無いawaseru kao ga naitoo ashamed to face someone (wrong tone — shame, not admiration)

Why: 頭が下がる is the fixed idiom for feeling real admiration toward someone's effort or character; 合わせる顔がない describes one's own shame at facing someone, the opposite emotional direction.

Q6

美しい歌声に（a.耳を傾けた b.耳を貸した）。Utsukushii utagoe ni (a. mimi o katamuketa b. mimi o kashita).

Full sentence: 美しい歌声に耳を傾けた。Utsukushii utagoe ni mimi o katamuketa.
EN: I listened attentively to the beautiful singing voice.
HI: सुंदर गायन को ध्यान से सुना।
GU: સુંદર ગાયનને ધ્યાનથી સાંભળ્યું.
#JapaneseEnglish
a耳を傾けたmimi o katamuketalistened attentively
b耳を貸したmimi o kashitalent an ear (wrong register — usually used for whether one will hear out a request/complaint, often negated)

Why: 耳を傾ける is the natural, appreciative idiom for listening closely to music or a speaker; 耳を貸す typically appears in sentences about whether someone will listen to a request, and often in the negative (耳を貸してくれなかった) — a mismatched register for enjoying singing.

Q7

彼女はそれを聞いたとたん、（　）驚きの声をあげた。Kanojo wa sore o kiita totan, (___) odoroki no koe o ageta.

Full sentence: 彼女はそれを聞いたとたん、目を丸くして驚きの声をあげた。Kanojo wa sore o kiita totan, me o maruku shite odoroki no koe o ageta.
EN: The moment she heard that, her eyes went wide and she let out a cry of surprise.
HI: उसने वह सुनते ही, आँखें फाड़कर हैरानी से चीख़ पड़ी।
GU: તેણે તે સાંભળતાં જ, આંખો પહોળી કરીને આશ્ચર્યથી ચીસ પાડી.
#JapaneseEnglish
1顔を立ててkao o tateteupholding a reputation (wrong meaning)
2口が滑ってkuchi ga subettea slip of the tongue (wrong meaning)
3目を丸くしてme o maruku shiteeyes going wide with surprise
4鼻をかけてhana o kaketenot a fixed idiom (鼻にかける means "boast," this form is wrong)

Why: 目を丸くする is the fixed idiom for one's eyes going wide with surprise, matching "the moment she heard it"; the other options describe unrelated actions like upholding a reputation or slipping up in speech.

Q8

女優の中でも、彼女は特に（　）美しい女性です。Joyuu no naka demo, kanojo wa toku ni (___) utsukushii josei desu.

Full sentence: 女優の中でも、彼女は特に目を引く美しい女性です。Joyuu no naka demo, kanojo wa toku ni me o hiku utsukushii josei desu.
EN: Even among actresses, she is an especially eye-catching, beautiful woman.
HI: अभिनेत्रियों में भी, वह ख़ासतौर पर ध्यान खींचने वाली ख़ूबसूरत महिला है।
GU: અભિનેત્રીઓમાં પણ, તે ખાસ ધ્યાન ખેંચે એવી સુંદર મહિલા છે.
#JapaneseEnglish
1目を引くme o hikueye-catching
2目に浮かぶme ni ukabupicture vividly (wrong meaning — for memories/scenes, not a person's looks)
3目を疑うme o utagaudoubt one's eyes (wrong meaning)
4目が回るme ga mawarudizzying/overwhelmingly busy (wrong meaning)

Why: 目を引く is the fixed idiom for something or someone that visually catches people's attention, exactly describing striking beauty; the other 目 idioms describe unrelated states like nostalgia, disbelief, or being overwhelmed.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Body-part idioms are notorious for flattening into one English word — here's where today's list actually splits:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
鼻が高いhana ga takai Proud (positive) A neutral-to-warm feeling of pride, often about family. 立派な息子を持って鼻が高い。
鼻にかけるhana ni kakeru To boast, be smug (negative) Same body part, but framed as an unattractive habit of showing off. 美人を鼻にかけている。
鼻につくhana ni tsuku Grating, annoying Not about the person's own pride at all — about how their manner affects others. 彼女の態度は鼻につく。
耳を傾けるmimi o katamukeru To listen attentively Positive/neutral — actively engaging with what's being said. 子どもの話に耳を傾ける。
耳を貸すmimi o kasu To lend an ear (to a request) Usually appears negated — about whether someone will hear a plea out at all. 彼は私の話に耳を貸してくれなかった。
口が重いkuchi ga omoi Tight-lipped, taciturn A person's general disposition toward speaking little. 彼は口が重い。
口が肥えるkuchi ga koeru To have a refined palate Unrelated to talkativeness — about taste discrimination, same 肥える as 目が肥える. 彼は口が肥えている。
Four different "face" idioms, four different meanings: 顔が売れる ("to become a known face"), 顔が広い ("to know many people"), 顔が利く ("to get special treatment somewhere because you're known there"), and 顔が立つ ("one's reputation/honor is upheld") all revolve around 顔 and reputation, but describe four distinct social situations — being famous, being well-connected, having pull at one specific place, and having one's honor preserved by someone else's action. Don't treat them as interchangeable synonyms for "well-known."
