問題1 — choose the best word for the blank (1問4点)

Q1

彼は芸能界の話題にとても（　）。Kare wa geinoukai no wadai ni totemo (___).

Full sentence: 彼は芸能界の話題にとても明るい。Kare wa geinoukai no wadai ni totemo akarui.
EN: He's very knowledgeable about entertainment-industry topics.
HI: वह मनोरंजन जगत की बातों की गहरी जानकारी रखता है।
GU: તે મનોરંજન જગતની બાબતોની ઊંડી જાણકારી ધરાવે છે.
#JapaneseEnglish
1明るいakaruiknowledgeable
2あまいamainaive/sweet (wrong meaning)
3かたいkataifirm/hard (wrong meaning)
4重いomoiheavy/serious (wrong meaning)

Why: Day 4's 彼は政治に明るい(＝詳しい) shows 明るい's "well-informed about" sense, extending naturally here to entertainment topics.

Q2

GU: તેની જીભ તીખી છે, પણ મૂળભૂત રીતે તે દયાળુ છે.
#JapaneseEnglish
1種taneseed/cause (wrong word)
2根neat the root/core
3身mibody/oneself (wrong word)
4実mifruit (wrong word)

Why: Day 5's 彼は根(＝本来の性質)は正直だ shows 根's "true underlying nature" sense, matching exactly this contrast between outward manner and inner character.

Q3

EN: My son is a bit stubborn — just like his stubborn father.

GU: દીકરો થોડો હઠીલો છે, બિલકુલ પોતાના હઠીલા પિતા જેવો.
#JapaneseEnglish
1かたいkataistubborn
2軽いkaruilight (wrong meaning)
3あまいamainaive/lenient (wrong meaning)
4強いtsuyoistrong (wrong word)

Why: Day 4's 頭がかたい(＝頑固だ) is the exact idiom for stubbornness, and 頑固な父親 in the second sentence confirms it.

Q4

うまい話に（　）ように気をつけよう。Umai hanashi ni (___) you ni ki o tsukeyou.

Full sentence: うまい話に乗らないように気をつけよう。Umai hanashi ni noranai you ni ki o tsukeyou.
EN: Let's be careful not to fall for offers that sound too good.
HI: लुभावने प्रस्तावों के झांसे में न आने का ध्यान रखें।
GU: લોભામણી ઑફરની જાળમાં ન ફસાવાનું ધ્યાન રાખીએ.
#JapaneseEnglish
1受けないukenainot receive (wrong verb)
2乗らないnoranainot fall for
3すべらないsuberanainot slip (wrong verb)
4通らないtooranainot pass through (wrong verb)

Why: Day 2's その手には乗らない shows 乗る's "fall for/buy into" sense, extending naturally to a suspiciously good offer.

Q5

何回も振り込みをすると、手数料が（　）。Nankai mo furikomi o suru to, tesuuryou ga (___).

Full sentence: 何回も振り込みをすると、手数料がバカにならない。Nankai mo furikomi o suru to, tesuuryou ga baka ni naranai.
EN: If you make transfers many times, the fees add up to a non-negligible amount.
HI: बार-बार पैसे ट्रांसफ़र करने पर, शुल्क कोई मामूली रक़म नहीं बनता।
GU: વારંવાર ટ્રાન્સફર કરવાથી, ફી નજીવી રકમ નથી રહેતી.
#JapaneseEnglish
1バカになるbaka ni narustrips/wears out (wrong sense — for screws, not fees)
2バカにならないbaka ni naranaiisn't negligible
3バカをみるbaka o miruends up losing out (wrong sense — for people, not fees)
4バカをみないbaka o minaidoesn't lose out (wrong sense)

Why: Day 5's 交通費がバカにならない is the exact same construction, now with 手数料 (fees) instead of 交通費 (transport costs).

Q7

Full sentence: もう少しスピードを出していたら、大事故につながるところだった。...daijiko ni tsunagaru tokoro datta.
EN: If I had driven a bit faster, it would have led to a serious accident.
HI: थोड़ी और तेज़ रफ़्तार होती तो बड़ी दुर्घटना हो सकती थी।
GU: થોડી વધુ ઝડપ હોત તો મોટો અકસ્માત થઈ શકતો હતો.
#JapaneseEnglish
1回るmawaruturn (wrong verb)
2引かれるhikarerube pulled/run over (wrong verb here)
3つながるtsunagarulead to
4結ぶmusubutie/conclude (wrong verb)

Why: Day 1's 事件につながる人物 and もうけにつながる話 show つながる's "lead to (a bad/notable outcome)" sense, exactly matching a near-miss leading to a serious accident.

問題4 — which sentence uses the word correctly? (1問4点)

Q21

波nami

#JapaneseEnglish
1彼は授業の波に乗って、すらすら答えた。Kare wa jugyou no nami ni notte, surasura kotaeta."He rode the wave of class and answered smoothly" — nonsensical collocation; 波に乗る doesn't pair with "class"
2その会社は不況の波を受けてついに倒産してしまった。Sono kaisha wa fukyou no nami o ukete tsui ni tousan shite shimatta.That company was hit by the wave of the recession and finally went bankrupt.
3セールスマンの波に乗らないように気をつけてください。Seerusuman no nami ni noranai you ni ki o tsukete kudasai."Be careful not to ride the salesman's wave" — wrong; a salesman's pitch needs 誘いに乗る, not 波に乗る
4台風の被害には波がいろいろある。Taifuu no higai ni wa nami ga iroiro aru."There are various waves to typhoon damage" — wrong; different kinds/degrees of damage need 種類, not 波

Why: 不況の波を受ける ("to be hit by the wave of a recession") extends this week's own 国際化の波に乗る(＝傾向に従う) pattern to a company being swept up by an economic downturn; 1, 3, and 4 each force 波 into a collocation it doesn't take.

Q22

すべるsuberu

#JapaneseEnglish
1コップを手ですべって、落としてしまった。Koppu o te de subette, otoshite shimatta."The cup slipped with the hand and I dropped it" — grammatically malformed; 滑る is intransitive and needs 手が滑って (the hand as subject), not 手で
2つい舌がすべってしまい、うそがばれてしまった。Tsui shita ga subette shimai, uso ga barete shimatta.My tongue accidentally slipped and my lie was found out.
3湖水の上を白鳥がすべるように泳いでいる。Kosui no ue o hakuchou ga suberu you ni oyoide iru.Grammatically fine as a simile ("gliding smoothly"), but doesn't test any of this week's taught senses of 滑る the way the other options do
4試験の時間にすべるように、落ち着いてください。Shiken no jikan ni suberu you ni, ochitsuite kudasai.Self-contradictory — should be すべらないように ("so that you DON'T fail"), not すべるように

Why: つい舌が滑る ("one's tongue slips") is a natural everyday variant of Day 1's つい口が滑ってしまった — both describe accidentally blurting something out; 1 breaks the verb's grammar, 4 says the opposite of what it means, and 3, while valid Japanese, is a decorative simile rather than one of this week's core taught senses.

Q23

通るtooru

#JapaneseEnglish
1彼の主張は、一応、筋が通っている。Kare no shuchou wa, ichiou, suji ga tootte iru.His argument is, at least, logically coherent.
2彼は恋人の肩に手を通した。Kare wa koibito no kata ni te o tooshita."He passed his hand through his girlfriend's shoulder" — wrong verb; putting an arm around someone needs 回した (Day 2's 肩に手を回す), not 通した
3車で駅まで通っていきましょう。Kuruma de eki made tootte ikimashou."Let's commute by car to the station" — wrong verb; a one-time drive needs 行きましょう or 送っていきましょう, not 通う (regular commuting)
4わからなかった問題がやっと通った。Wakaranakatta mondai ga yatto tootta."The problem I couldn't understand finally passed" — wrong verb; solving a problem needs 解けた (Day 3's とける group), not 通った

Why: 筋が通る ("to be logically coherent") is Day 2's own example (彼の言うことは筋が通っている) verbatim; 2, 3, and 4 each substitute 通る for a different verb that's actually correct in that context (回す, 行く/送る, 解ける).

Q24

飛ぶtobu

#JapaneseEnglish
1扇風機で風が飛んで、涼しくなった。Senpuuki de kaze ga tonde, suzushiku natta."The fan's wind flew and it got cool" — wrong verb; a fan needs 風を送る (Day 3's 送る group), not 風が飛ぶ
2暑さで氷が一気に飛んでしまった。Atsusa de koori ga ikki ni tonde shimatta."The ice flew away all at once from the heat" — wrong verb; ice needs 溶けて (melted), not 飛んで
3年とともに髪の毛が飛んで、はげてきた。Toshi to tomo ni kaminoke ga tonde, hagete kita."Hair flew away with age, and he went bald" — wrong verb; hair falling out needs 抜けて, not 飛んで
4ブレーキランプのヒューズが飛んでしまった。Bureeki ranpu no hyuuzu ga tonde shimatta.The brake light's fuse blew.

Why: ヒューズが飛ぶ ("a fuse blows") is Day 3's own example verbatim; 1, 2, and 3 each misuse 飛ぶ for wind, melting, and hair loss, which are each covered by a different verb.

Q25

明るいakarui

#JapaneseEnglish
1彼はとても責任感の明るい人です。Kare wa totemo sekininkan no akarui hito desu."He's a person with a very bright sense of responsibility" — wrong word; a strong sense of responsibility needs 責任感が強い, not 明るい
2父は世界の地理に明るい。Chichi wa sekai no chiri ni akarui.My father is very knowledgeable about world geography.
3この計算は明るい。Kono keisan wa akarui."This calculation is bright" — nonsensical; a calculation can't be 明るい
4おじいちゃん、おばあちゃんは孫に明るいものだ。Ojiichan, obaachan wa mago ni akarui mono da."Grandparents are bright toward their grandchildren" — wrong word; being indulgent toward grandchildren needs 甘い (Day 4's 孫に甘い-type usage), not 明るい

Why: 政治に明るい-type usage from Day 4 (彼は政治に明るい＝詳しい) extends directly to 世界の地理に明るい ("well-informed about world geography"); 1, 3, and 4 each substitute 明るい where 強い, no adjective at all, and 甘い actually belong.

Test-day strategy: This entire chapter is built around one insight: a single common word (受ける, 張る, バカ, 後, 筋…) can mean wildly different things purely based on what it's paired with.
