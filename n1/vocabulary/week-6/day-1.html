1日目 — 受ける・滑る・つながる (Ukeru / Suberu / Tsunagaru)

Theme
A brand-new format for this book: instead of a flat word list or kanji-compound groups, each headword is a single highly polysemous everyday verb, shown through 5–11 short example phrases that each isolate a different sense. Today's three titled headwords are 受ける (ukeru — to receive/catch/take on), 滑る (suberu — to slip/slide, incl. "fail an exam"), and つながる (tsunagaru — to be connected); the book also teaches a 4th, untitled bonus headword this same day: 挟む (hasamu — to sandwich/insert/pinch between).

練習Ⅰ (6 items, a/b choice, from p.93) and 練習Ⅱ (2 items, 4-option, from p.93). Answers confirmed from the official answer box printed at the bottom of p.95.

練習Ⅰ — circle the correct option

Q1

彼のギャグは、全然（a.つながらなかった b.うけなかった）。Kare no gyagu wa, zenzen (a. tsunagaranakatta b. ukenakatta).

Full sentence: 彼のギャグは、全然うけなかった。Kare no gyagu wa, zenzen ukenakatta.
EN: His joke didn't land at all.
HI: उसका मज़ाक बिल्कुल पसंद नहीं आया।
GU: તેની મજાક બિલકુલ ન ગમી.
#JapaneseEnglish
aつながらなかったtsunagaranakattadidn't connect (wrong meaning — a joke can't "connect")
bうけなかったukenakattadidn't go over well

Why: 受ける in the "be well-received" sense is exactly what a joke either does or doesn't do with an audience.

Q2

パンにハムを（a.はさんで b.つなげて）食べました。Pan ni hamu o (a. hasande b. tsunagete) tabemashita.

Full sentence: パンにハムをはさんで食べました。Pan ni hamu o hasande tabemashita.
EN: I ate bread with ham sandwiched inside.
HI: मैंने ब्रेड में हैम रखकर खाया।
GU: મેં બ્રેડમાં હેમ મૂકીને ખાધું.
#JapaneseEnglish
aはさんでhasandesandwiching
bつなげてtsunageteconnecting (wrong verb — ham isn't "connected" to bread)

Why: 挟む is the exact verb for putting a filling between two slices of bread.

Q3

つい口を（a.はさんで b.すべらせて）、彼の秘密をばらしてしまった。Tsui kuchi o (a. hasande b. suberasete), kare no himitsu o barashite shimatta.

Full sentence: つい口をすべらせて、彼の秘密をばらしてしまった。Tsui kuchi o suberasete, kare no himitsu o barashite shimatta.
EN: I accidentally let it slip and gave away his secret.
HI: अनजाने में मुँह से निकल गया और उसका राज़ खुल गया।
GU: અજાણતાં મોઢામાંથી નીકળી ગયું ને તેનું રહસ્ય ખૂલી ગયું.
#JapaneseEnglish
aはさんでhasandeinserting (wrong verb here)
bすべらせてsuberaseteletting slip

Why: 口を滑らせる ("to let one's mouth slip") is the fixed idiom for accidentally blurting out a secret.

Q4

そのときの経験が、今の自信に（a.うけている b.つながっている）。Sono toki no keiken ga, ima no jishin ni (a. ukete iru b. tsunagatte iru).

Full sentence: そのときの経験が、今の自信につながっている。Sono toki no keiken ga, ima no jishin ni tsunagatte iru.
EN: That experience is connected to my confidence today.
HI: उस समय का अनुभव आज के आत्मविश्वास से जुड़ा हुआ है।
GU: તે વખતનો અનુભવ આજના આત્મવિશ્વાસ સાથે જોડાયેલો છે.
#JapaneseEnglish
aうけているukete irureceiving (wrong verb)
bつながっているtsunagatte iruis connected to

Why: ～につながる describes an abstract cause leading to/feeding into a later result — exactly "past experience → present confidence."

Q5

彼女が結婚するといううわさを耳に（a.はさんだ b.受けた）。Kanojo ga kekkon suru to iu uwasa o mimi ni (a. hasanda b. uketa).

Full sentence: 彼女が結婚するといううわさを耳にはさんだ。Kanojo ga kekkon suru to iu uwasa o mimi ni hasanda.
EN: I happened to hear a rumor that she's getting married.
HI: यूँ ही कानों में पड़ा कि वह शादी करने वाली है।
GU: અફવા કાને પડી કે તે લગ્ન કરવાની છે.
#JapaneseEnglish
aはさんだhasandahappened to hear
b受けたuketareceived (wrong verb here)

Why: 耳に挟む is the fixed idiom for overhearing a rumor by chance.

Q6

ドアを開けるとき、手が（a.すべって b.はさんで）持っていた本を落としてしまった。Doa o akeru toki, te ga (a. subette b. hasande) motte ita hon o otoshite shimatta.

Full sentence: ドアを開けるとき、手が滑って持っていた本を落としてしまった。Doa o akeru toki, te ga subette motte ita hon o otoshite shimatta.
EN: When I opened the door, my hand slipped and I dropped the book I was holding.
HI: दरवाज़ा खोलते समय हाथ फिसल गया और पकड़ी हुई किताब गिर गई।
GU: બારણું ખોલતી વખતે હાથ લપસ્યો ને પકડેલું પુસ્તક પડી ગયું.
#JapaneseEnglish
aすべってsubetteslipped
bはさんでhasandesandwiching (wrong verb — nothing was inserted between two things)

Why: 手が滑る describes a hand physically slipping and losing its grip on something.

Q7

彼と彼女は戸籍上は兄弟だが、血は（　）いない。Kare to kanojo wa koseki-jou wa kyoudai da ga, chi wa (___) inai.

Full sentence: 彼と彼女は戸籍上は兄弟だが、血はつながっていない。...chi wa tsunagatte inai.
EN: Officially they're registered as siblings, but they're not related by blood.
HI: काग़ज़ों में वे भाई-बहन हैं, पर उनमें ख़ून का रिश्ता नहीं है।
GU: સત્તાવાર રીતે તેઓ ભાઈ-બહેન છે, પણ તેમની વચ્ચે લોહીનો સંબંધ નથી.
#JapaneseEnglish
1受けてuketereceiving (wrong verb)
2つながってtsunagatteconnected (by blood)
3受かってukattepassing (an exam) — unrelated word
4つなげてtsunageteconnecting (something) — wrong, needs the intransitive form since 血が is the subject

Why: 血がつながっている ("to be related by blood") needs the intransitive つながる, matching 血が as its subject — not the transitive つなげる.

Q8

彼の家は、うちの前の道路を（　）向こう側にある。Kare no ie wa, uchi no mae no douro o (___) mukougawa ni aru.

Full sentence: 彼の家は、うちの前の道路をはさんで向こう側にある。...douro o hasande mukougawa ni aru.
EN: His house is across the road from mine.
HI: उसका घर हमारे घर के सामने वाली सड़क के उस पार है।
GU: તેનું ઘર અમારા ઘર સામેના રસ્તાની સામી બાજુ છે.
#JapaneseEnglish
1はさんでhasandewith (the road) between
2すべってsubetteslipping (wrong meaning)
3うけてuketereceiving (wrong meaning)
4つなげてtsunageteconnecting (wrong meaning — a road isn't described as "connecting" two houses this way)

Why: ～を挟んで向こう側 ("across ~, with it in between") is the standard way to describe a location on the far side of a road/river/table.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

This "many-meanings" chapter's own trap is picking the right sense of the SAME headword, plus a couple of transitive/intransitive pairs hidden in today's list:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
受けるukeru To receive/take on (formal, broad) The general-purpose kanji form — aid, influence, a check-up, a shock. 検査を受ける, 影響を受ける, ショックを受ける.
ウケるukeru To be a hit, go over well (slang) Same word, written in katakana specifically for the "popular/well-received" sense. ギャグがウケる, 若者にウケる映画.
つながるtsunagaru To be connected (intransitive) Something IS connected — no separate agent doing the connecting. 血がつながっている, 電話がつながる.
つなげるtsunageru To connect something (transitive) Someone connects two things — takes an を-marked object. ケーブルをつなげる (not tested this day, but the trap in Q7/Q8's wrong options).
挟むhasamu To insert/pinch something (transitive) An agent deliberately puts something between two things. 本にしおりを挟む, 口を挟む.
挟まるhasamaru To get caught/stuck between (intransitive) Something ends up stuck by accident — the book shows this via the passive 挟まれる. 指をドアに挟んだ＝ドアに指を挟まれた.
Exam-day superstition: 試験に滑って ("to fail/'slip' an exam") is one of a small set of Japanese words — along with 落ちる ("to fall/fail") — that nervous test-takers avoid saying out loud before an exam, the same way some avoid saying certain things before a wedding. It's why "キットカット" (Kit Kat) is sold as an exam-season good-luck charm in Japan: its name sounds like きっと勝つ (kitto katsu, "surely win"), the cheerful opposite of すべる/落ちる.
