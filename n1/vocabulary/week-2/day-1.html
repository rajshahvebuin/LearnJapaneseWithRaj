Warm-up

Q. 何と言う？Nanto iu?
自分が実際以上に優れていると思って、得意になること。Jibun ga jissai ijou ni sugurete iru to omotte, tokui ni naru koto.
("What do you call thinking you're better than you actually are, and feeling proud of yourself about it?")

うぬぼれる (unuboreru) — "to be conceited, to have an inflated opinion of oneself"

Q1

駅で電車の乗り換えに（　）。Eki de densha no norikae ni (___).

Full sentence: 駅で電車の乗り換えにまごついた。Eki de densha no norikae ni magotsuita.
EN: I was flustered/confused transferring trains at the station.
HI: स्टेशन पर ट्रेन बदलते समय मैं घबरा गया।
GU: સ્ટેશન પર ટ્રેન બદલતી વખતે હું ગભરાઈ ગયો.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
aおびえたobietawas frightened (wrong emotion)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
bまごついたmagotsuitawas flustered, at a lossघबरा गयाગભરાઈ ગયો

Why: Not knowing how to transfer trains causes confusion/flustered fumbling (まごつく), not fear (おびえる) — there's no threat here, just unfamiliarity.

HI: जो फोटो का फोकस सही नहीं है, वह धुंधली तस्वीर।
GU: જે ફોટોનું ફોકસ બરાબર નથી, તે અસ્પષ્ટ તસવીર.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
aぼけたboketablurred, out of focusधुंधलाઅસ્પષ્ટ

GU: તે એવું માનીને ગુમાન કરે છે કે તેનામાં પ્રતિભા છે.

HI: उस इमारत की बनावट काफ़ी बारीक/जटिल है।

OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati

GU: એક કલાકથી રાહ જોઉં છું, બસ હજી આવી નથી. હવે રાહ જોઈ જોઈને થાકી ગયો!
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Several of today's words look interchangeable in English but aren't in Japanese:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
くたびれるkutabireru Tired, worn out General fatigue — mental or physical, mild-to-moderate, e.g. from waiting or walking. Everyday tiredness, including "tired of waiting" (待ちくたびれる).
ばてるbateru Exhausted, worn to the bone Stronger, physical exhaustion from sustained exertion (overtime, heat, exercise). Describing being wiped out from hard, continuous physical effort.
とぼけるtobokeru To play dumb Deliberately feigning ignorance of something you actually know. "I don't know" said knowingly, to dodge a question.
ごまかすgomakasu To deceive, cover up Actively distorting facts, numbers, or the truth — broader than just feigning ignorance. Lying about an age, a price, or dodging an uncomfortable topic.
戸惑うtomadou Bewildered, at a loss Confusion from encountering something unfamiliar or unexpected — an inner reaction. Facing a new job, custom, or situation you don't know how to handle.
まごつくmagotsuku Flustered, fumbling Visible, flustered fumbling while trying (and failing) to do something correctly. Fumbling through an unfamiliar procedure, like transferring trains.
嘆くnageku To lament, grieve A serious, sorrowful reaction — genuinely mourning a loss or failure. Grieving over a real failure or misfortune.
ぼやくboyaku To grumble Casual, habitual griping about something minor and ongoing (like low pay). Everyday complaining, said半-jokingly or out of habit.
こだわるkodawaru Fussy, particular about Being unusually picky/hung up on a detail — can be praise (a craftsman) or criticism (nitpicky). Someone who insists on doing one specific thing a specific way.
凝るkoru Absorbed in, enthusiastic about Positive immersion in a hobby or craft — spending real time/effort out of enjoyment. Describing a hobbyist's dedication (fishing, an elaborate design).
Exam trap: 老ける (ふける, "to look old for one's age" — about outward appearance) and 老いる (おいる, "to actually grow old" — the real aging process) share the kanji 老 but describe different things: someone can 老ける early (look older than they are) without having actually 老いた much yet. Also watch the sound-alike pair from Q2/Q4 above: ぼける (blur/senility) vs とぼける (play dumb), and 凝る (こる, be absorbed in) vs 懲りる (こりる, learn a lesson) — different verbs, easy to mix up by ear.
