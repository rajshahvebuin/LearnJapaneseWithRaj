Warm-up

Q. 下線部を言い換えると？Kasenbu o iikaeru to?
先日の地震は大きい被害を引き起こした。Senjitsu no jishin wa ookii higai o hikiokoshita.
("How would you paraphrase the underlined part? 'The recent earthquake caused/brought about major damage.'")

もたらした (motarashita) — "brought about" — 大きい被害をもたらした

OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Today's list has several pairs that look related but split into different meanings:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
途切れるtogireru A temporary lull, break Something pauses briefly and can resume — like a lull in conversation or a gap in clouds. A conversation goes quiet for a moment; clouds part briefly.
途絶えるtodaeru A complete stoppage Something stops entirely and doesn't resume — usually contact or communication. News or letters from someone stop coming altogether.
とどまるtodomaru Something itself stays/stops (intransitive) The subject remains in place or a trend continues by itself — no one is actively limiting it. "Prices keep rising" (自動詞, が): 上昇はとどまることがない.
とどめるtodomeru Someone keeps something in check (transitive) An agent actively limits or holds something to a certain degree, or keeps it in memory. "Keep expenses to a minimum" (他動詞, を): 経費を最小限にとどめる.
はまるhamaru Absorbed/hooked (often positive) Getting drawn into something enjoyable, like a hobby or game — neutral-to-positive. Being hooked on a video game or a new interest.
かぶれるkabureru Strongly influenced (often mildly negative) Being swept up in an outside influence to the point it changes you — carries a whiff of "too much," like a rash. Someone who's "gotten really into" a subculture in a way that changed their behavior.
ありふれるarifureru Common, unremarkable (still current) Describes something ordinary that exists everywhere right now. "A run-of-the-mill story" — nothing special, but still typical today.
廃れるsutareru Fallen out of use (used to be prevalent) Describes something that WAS popular/common and has since gone into decline. A fad or custom that people used to follow but no longer do.
Exam trap: とどまる (が, self-contained — "prices keep rising on their own") vs. とどめる (を, someone actively limits — "keep expenses to a minimum") is another 自動詞/他動詞 pair, joining かなう／かなえる and 染める／染まる from this week — whenever you see a を-marked object being actively controlled, look for the transitive -める/-える form; when the subject itself just continues or stays, expect the intransitive -まる form.
