4日目 — 田舎・景色・迷子 (Inaka, Keshiki, Maigo)

Source

Theme
33 words under one heading, 特殊な読み (special/irregular readings) — almost entirely 熟字訓 (jukujikun): whole-word readings that don't map onto the individual kanji at all (田舎 いなか, 迷子 まいご). Split below into 3 sets of 11 purely for readability
Answer key

Notes

数珠 (じゅず, prayer beads strung together) gives this day its built-in exam trap via 数珠つなぎ (juzu-tsunagi, "strung together like prayer beads") — the fixed simile for bumper-to-bumper traffic tested in 練習Ⅰ Q1 below.

2 Quiz / Exercise Section

練習Ⅰ (6 items) and 練習Ⅱ (4 items, 2 blanks each), both from p.67. This book's answer key is in a separate [別冊] booklet not included in the scan — answers below are worked out from the meanings on this same spread, flagged as reasoned rather than officially confirmed.

練習Ⅰ — choose the correct option

Q1

（　）のように車が何台もつながって、なかなか進まない。(___) no you ni kuruma ga nandai mo tsunagatte, nakanaka susumanai.

Full sentence: 数珠のように車が何台もつながって、なかなか進まない。Juzu no you ni kuruma ga nandai mo tsunagatte, nakanaka susumanai.
EN: Cars are strung together like prayer beads and barely moving forward.
HI: मालाओं की तरह कई गाड़ियाँ आपस में जुड़ी हैं और मुश्किल से आगे बढ़ रही हैं।
GU: જપમાળાની જેમ ઘણી ગાડીઓ જોડાયેલી છે અને માંડ માંડ આગળ વધે છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a数珠juzuprayer beadsमाला (जाप की)જપમાળા
b小豆azukiadzuki beans (wrong simile)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Why: 数珠つなぎ ("strung together like prayer beads") is the fixed simile for a long, connected line of things — exactly bumper-to-bumper traffic. 小豆 (adzuki beans) isn't part of this idiom.

Q2

HI: हम एक खोए हुए बच्चे की देखभाल कर रहे हैं। यदि आपको इसकी जानकारी हो...
GU: અમે એક ખોવાયેલા બાળકની સંભાળ રાખી રહ્યા છીએ. જો તમને આ અંગે ખ્યાલ હોય તો...
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a行方yukuewhereabouts (not a noun describing a child)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
b迷子maigoa lost childखोया हुआ बच्चाખોવાયેલું બાળક

Why: 迷子 directly means "a lost child" — the exact noun this public announcement needs. 行方 means "whereabouts" and cannot itself describe a person.

Q3

HI: जाने का मन नहीं है, पर अब विदा लेने का समय हो गया है।
GU: છૂટા પડવાનું મન નથી થતું, પણ હવે વિદાયનો સમય થઈ ગયો છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a名残nagoria lingering trace/feelingयादगार निशानયાદગાર નિશાન
b最寄moyorithe nearest (wrong word entirely)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

GU: હવામાન એટલું સરસ છે કે કપડાં સૂકવવા માટે એકદમ યોગ્ય દિવસ છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati

EN: To ask the police-box officer for directions.
HI: पुलिस चौकी के सिपाही से रास्ता पूछना।
GU: પોલીસ ચોકીના પોલીસવાળાને રસ્તો પૂછવો.

HI: लगता है आख़िरकार बरसात का मौसम ख़त्म हो गया है।
GU: લાગે છે કે આખરે ચોમાસું પૂરું થયું છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a雪崩nadarean avalanche (doesn't "end" with 明ける)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Because every word today is a jukujikun that must be memorized whole, the "confusion" is really about picking the right whole word for the context — pulled straight from 練習Ⅰ above:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
数珠（つなぎ）juzu (tsunagi) Prayer beads (strung together) The fixed simile for a long connected chain. "Cars bumper-to-bumper in traffic."
小豆azuki Adzuki beans A food ingredient — not part of any "chain" idiom. "Adzuki beans for sweet bean paste."
迷子maigo A lost child A noun describing the child themself. "We're looking after a lost child."
行方yukue Whereabouts Describes an unknown location, not a person. "Their whereabouts are unknown."
名残（惜しい）nagori (oshii) Reluctant to part, wistful An emotional reaction to an ending. "Sad to say goodbye."
最寄りmoyori The nearest (place) Describes physical proximity of a location. "The nearest station."
日和biyori Favorable conditions for X Combines with an activity: X日和. "Perfect laundry-drying weather."
浴衣yukata A summer kimono An article of clothing, no "conditions for" usage. "Wearing a yukata to the festival."
Exam trap: These are all 熟字訓 — readings assigned to the whole written word, not built kanji-by-kanji. On a 漢字読み question, resist the urge to sound out 田+舎 or 迷+子 piece by piece; if the compound looks like an everyday noun (a place, a person, a piece of clothing), check whether it's one of these fixed whole-word readings first.
