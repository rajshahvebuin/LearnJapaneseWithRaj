JLPT N1 · Kanji · Week 3 — 訓読みを覚えよう (Kun'yomi o oboeyou)

3日目 — 憎い・幼い・快い (Nikui, Osanai, Kokoroyoi)

Source
日本語総まとめ N1 漢字 (Nihongo Sou-Matome N1 Kanji) — printed p.48–49 (PDF pages 48–49; PDF index = printed page number for this scan)
Theme
The organizing principle shifts again: today the book groups words by part of speech, not by kanji or reading — 名詞 (nouns, 9 words), な形容詞 (na-adjectives, 4 words), and い形容詞 (i-adjectives, split across two sets of 13 and 10 words for the two printed pages). Kun'yomi is still the throughline, but the grouping itself is grammatical.
Answer key
This book's answers/explanations live entirely in a separate [別冊] 解答・解説 booklet, not included in the uploaded scan. 練習Ⅰ・Ⅱ answers below are worked out from the book's own definitions on this spread, not copied from an official key — flagged transparently, same policy as every other day in this module.
Notes
The day's title picks three i-adjectives with tricky, non-obvious kun'yomi: 憎い(にくい), 幼い(おさない), 快い(こころよい) — none of their readings can be guessed from the kanji's usual on-yomi.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Today's confusion pairs aren't same-kanji traps (each word uses a different kanji) — they're same-sounding-ending traps, exactly what 練習Ⅱ pairs up:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
醜いminikui Ugly (in appearance or character) Describes something visually or morally repulsive. "An ugly fight," "an ugly (mean) heart."
憎いnikui Hateful, detestable Describes a target of anger/hatred, not ugliness itself. "A hateful opponent."
悩ましいnayamashii Seductive, vexing, hard to resist A tempting or troubling stimulus that stirs conflicted feelings. "A seductive gesture/manner."
勇ましいisamashii Brave, valiant, stirring Describes courage — positive, martial energy. "A brave figure," "a rousing march."
著しいichijirushii Remarkable, striking (a change/degree) Neutral intensifier — describes the size of a change. "Remarkable progress," "striking fluctuations."
卑しいiyashii Mean, base, greedy A moral judgment on character/behavior. "A greedy, mean action."
Exam trap: 醜い (みにくい) and 憎い (にくい) are the single most testable pair today — 醜い literally contains にくい as its final three morae, so a reading question can trip you into supplying the wrong kanji for にくい alone. Always check whether the sentence describes something as visually/morally ugly (→醜い) or as a target of hatred (→憎い) before choosing.
