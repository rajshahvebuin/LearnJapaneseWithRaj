Q1

町の活性化のために、町民が知恵を（　）。Machi no kasseika no tame ni, choumin ga chie o (___).

Full sentence: 町の活性化のために、町民が知恵を絞る。...choumin ga chie o shiboru.
EN: The residents rack their brains to revitalize the town.
HI: शहर को फिर से जीवंत बनाने के लिए निवासी दिमाग़ लड़ाते हैं।
GU: શહેરને ફરી ધબકતું બનાવવા રહેવાસીઓ દિમાગ કસે છે.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a絞るshiboruto rack (one's brains)दिमाग़ लड़ानाદિમાગ કસવું
b縛るshibaruto tie with a rope (wrong — 知恵 can't be physically tied)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

Why: 知恵を絞る ("rack one's brains") is a fixed idiom using 絞る's "squeeze out" sense; 縛る only means physically tying something with rope/string.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Today's traps mostly pair verbs that sound like they could substitute for each other but take very different objects — exactly what 練習Ⅰ tests:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
縛るshibaru To tie up (with rope/string) A literal, physical binding action. "Tie a package with string."
絞るshiboru To wring out; to rack (one's brains) Squeezing — literal (a cloth) or figurative (mental effort). "Wring out a towel," "rack one's brains."
阻むhabamu To block, obstruct (a path/solution) An impersonal obstacle standing in the way of progress. "The law is blocking a solution."
絡むkaramu To be entangled with; to pick a quarrel Something (often money, or a person) gets tangled up in an affair. "Money is involved," "he picked a fight with someone."
濁るnigoru To become muddy/cloudy (liquid) Describes a literal liquid or atmosphere clouding over. "The river water turned muddy."
劣るotoru To be inferior (in ability/quality) A comparison of capability or quality between two things. "This model's performance is inferior to that one."
Exam trap: 絞る vs 縛る is the most testable pair — both involve tightening/restraining something, but 縛る is strictly literal rope-and-string binding, while 絞る covers both a literal wringing motion (a cloth) and the common figurative idiom 知恵を絞る ("rack one's brains"). If the object is abstract (知恵, 声, an idea), it's always 絞る, never 縛る.
