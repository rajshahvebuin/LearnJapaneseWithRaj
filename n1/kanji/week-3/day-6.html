Q1

お酒を（　）ようにと医者に言われた。Osake o (___) you ni to isha ni iwareta.

Full sentence: お酒を控えるようにと医者に言われた。Osake o hikaeru you ni to isha ni iwareta.
EN: The doctor told me to cut down on alcohol.
HI: डॉक्टर ने शराब कम करने को कहा।
GU: ડોક્ટરે દારૂ ઓછો કરવાનું કહ્યું.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a鍛えるkitaeruto train/toughen (the body) — wrong verb for "cutting down" on something(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
b控えるhikaeruto refrain from, cut down onकम करना, परहेज़ करनाટાળવું, ઓછું કરવું

Why: お酒を控える ("cut down on alcohol") is the set medical-advice phrase; 鍛える means to physically train/strengthen the body, an unrelated action.

EN: The new business achieved rapid growth.
HI: नए व्यवसाय ने तेज़ी से विकास हासिल किया।
GU: નવા વ્યવસાયે ઝડપી વિકાસ સાધ્યો.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a遂げたtogetaachieved, attainedहासिल कियाસાધ્યો
b設けたmouketaprovided/created (an opportunity) — wrong object, growth isn't "created"(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Today's traps pair verbs that could seem interchangeable in tone or shape but take opposite emotional registers or objects:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
慰めるnagusameru To console, comfort Responds to sadness, grief, or misfortune. "Find words to console a grieving friend."
褒めるhomeru To praise, compliment Responds to an achievement or good quality. "Praise a child for doing well."
控えるhikaeru To refrain from, hold back on Voluntarily cutting down on an action/habit. "Cut down on alcohol," "hold back from speaking."
鍛えるkitaeru To train, toughen Actively building up strength/skill through practice. "Train one's body/mind."
告げるtsugeru To announce, herald Signals or declares something (often poetically). "Flowers herald spring's arrival."
帯びるobiru To take on, carry (a quality) Describes acquiring an attribute (heat, color, urgency). "The debate took on a heated tone."
Exam trap: 慰める vs 褒める is the most testable pair — both describe kind words directed at another person, but the correct choice depends entirely on whether the surrounding context is sad (→慰める) or an accomplishment (→褒める). A reading passage's emotional tone, not just the vocabulary, decides the answer.
