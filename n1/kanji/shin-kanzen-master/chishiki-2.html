Notes
A short column, not a 回 — there is no word list and no テスト. The book's point: a two-kanji 熟語 (じゅくご, compound) is built in one of six ways, and spotting the way lets you guess the meaning of a word you have never seen. N1 does not test the labels a–f, but the skill helps with 漢字の意味 and 語形成 questions (chosen-suffix/prefix items like 指揮者, 矛盾点 in 第12回).
a–f = the book's six structure types; the quizzes answer with these letters
［ ］ = the book's gloss of how the model word is built: → describes, ← object of the verb, ＝ same meaning, ⇔ opposite/pair
⇒ (p.60) = two compounds joined into one longer word; ／ = where a long compound splits into dictionary words

1 The Rule — six ways a compound is built

ルール：知らない熟語は、語の構成を考えると意味が分かる。 (Shiranai jukugo wa, go no kousei o kangaeru to imi ga wakaru.)

p.59 · a–f

Type b is the one that reverses Japanese word order: 読書 = 書を読む, 開会 = 会を開く, 防火 = 火を防ぐ. Read the first kanji as a verb and the second as its object. Types c and d both stack two kanji of the same kind — c when they mean the same (道＝路), d when they are opposites or a pair (勝⇔敗). The English glosses of the "other examples" are ours.

The particle (の, を) and the する/こと disappear when two 熟語 are joined. The same structure types apply one level up: 日本経済 is "a describes b" (type a), 温度調節 is "verb ← object" read backwards (type b).

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

The quizzes deliberately put words with the same kanji but different structures side by side. The pairs below come from クイズ1–3.

Exam trap: type b reads backwards. 読書, 防火, 集金, 募金, 記名 all put the verb first and the object second (書を読む, 火を防ぐ). When you meet an unknown N1 word whose first kanji is a verb (就職, 遅刻, 握手 …), try "object を verb" — it usually gives the meaning (examples added, not in book).
