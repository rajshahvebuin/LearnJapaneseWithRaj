I 「国内旅行、友人より家族と」増加 (Kokunai ryokou, yuujin yori kazoku to zouka — "Domestic trips: more with family than friends")

p.156–157

Source given in the book: 日本経済新聞 2010年4月28日付 (Nihon Keizai Shinbun, 28 April 2010)

Passage summary (not the book's text):
EN: Statistics from the Japan Tourism Agency show that Japanese domestic trips with friends or colleagues have been falling for about 20 years, while family trips have grown to become the largest group (about 45% in 2008, roughly double the share for friends). Young people say they travel less because they have less money; company trips have faded, school trips are flat, and solo travel is slowly rising. The agency links this to weaker ties with colleagues and acquaintances.
HI: जापान पर्यटन एजेंसी के आँकड़ों के अनुसार, दोस्तों या सहकर्मियों के साथ घरेलू यात्राएँ लगभग 20 साल से घट रही हैं और परिवार के साथ यात्राएँ बढ़कर सबसे बड़ा समूह बन गई हैं (2008 में लगभग 45%, दोस्तों के हिस्से का लगभग दोगुना)। युवा कहते हैं कि पैसे कम होने से वे कम घूमते हैं; कंपनी-यात्राएँ घट गईं, स्कूल-यात्राएँ स्थिर हैं और अकेले घूमना धीरे-धीरे बढ़ रहा है। एजेंसी इसे सहकर्मियों व परिचितों से कमज़ोर होते रिश्तों से जोड़ती है।

p.157–159

Source given in the book: 読売新聞 2008年2月13日 (Yomiuri Shinbun, 13 February 2008) — 抜粋 / 基づく (excerpt, adapted)

Passage summary (not the book's text):
EN: Japan's warm-water bidet toilet seats, first sold in 1980 and now in about two-thirds of homes, began as Western products for medical use and for people with limited use of their hands. Their spread has slightly raised household water use, because the washing adds water to each flush; at the same time makers compete to build water-saving toilets that use far less water. But the seats also use electricity, including standby power, so the article asks whether these "evolved" toilets really save resources or waste water and energy.

p.159–161

Source given in the book: YOMIURI ONLINE 2009年5月28日 — 抜粋 / 基づく (excerpt, adapted)

Passage summary (not the book's text):
EN: A Fujitsu phone app reads the colour of whatever the camera points at and, on some models, speaks it aloud — useful for blind or colour-blind people, and handy for others too; it won a universal-design award and its team leader is visually impaired. The article explains universal design (design that helps disabled and elderly people is pleasant for everyone) and introduces an IBM researcher who lost her sight and built software that reads web pages aloud.
