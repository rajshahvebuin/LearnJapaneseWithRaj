Q1

国民に隠されていた事実が（　）になった。Kokumin ni kakusarete ita jijitsu ga (___) ni natta.

Full sentence: 国民に隠されていた事実が公になった。Kokumin ni kakusarete ita jijitsu ga ooyake ni natta.
EN: The fact that had been hidden from the public came to light.
HI: जनता से छिपाया गया तथ्य सार्वजनिक हो गया।
GU: જનતાથી છુપાવેલી હકીકત જાહેર થઈ ગઈ.
OptionJapaneseEnglishHindiGujarati
a穴anaa hole (wrong sense)(यहाँ ग़लत रूप)(અહીં ખોટું સ્વરૂપ)
b公ooyakepublicसार्वजनिकજાહેર

Why: 公になる is the fixed idiom for a hidden fact "becoming public"; 穴 (a hole) has no such idiomatic use with になる in this sense.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Because this week groups kanji by everyday word category rather than shared sound, the "confusion" here is between near-neighbors in meaning rather than in pronunciation — exactly what 練習Ⅰ above tests. Compare them side by side:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
公になるooyake ni naru To become public A hidden fact is revealed to everyone. A secret, scandal, or truth comes to light.
穴があくana ga aku A hole opens up A literal or budgetary gap forms — unrelated to secrets. "A hole appeared in the wall," "a budget shortfall."
源minamoto A source (abstract origin) Where energy, health, or motivation comes from. "The source of my energy/motivation."
塊katamari A lump (physical mass) A solid physical clump of something. "A lump of soil/dough," never an abstract origin.
恥をかくhaji o kaku To embarrass oneself A momentary loss of face from a mistake. Mispronouncing a word, tripping in public.
罪tsumi Guilt, a sin/crime A moral or legal wrongdoing, not simple embarrassment. "A sense of guilt," a criminal offense.
唇を噛むkuchibiru o kamu Bite one's lip (in frustration) An emotional reaction — regret or suppressed anger. "He bit his lip in frustration."
舌を噛むshita o kamu Bite one's tongue (literally) An accidental physical mishap, e.g. while eating/speaking. "I accidentally bit my tongue."
襟を正すeri o tadasu Straighten one's collar (become serious/respectful) A figurative gesture of respect or seriousness. Responding respectfully to admirable behavior.
丈take Length (of clothing) A purely physical measurement, no figurative use. "The length of a coat/skirt."
幻の酒maboroshi no sake A "phantom" (extremely rare) sake Describes something so scarce it feels almost mythical. Praising a hard-to-find product.
紫murasaki Purple (a color) A plain color word, no connotation of rarity. "A purple sweater."
Exam trap: 公 vs 穴 and 源 vs 塊 are this day's most testable pairs — one member of each pair only works in a fixed idiom (公になる, 元気の源), while its look-alike partner is a plain concrete noun with no such idiomatic reading. When a sentence uses an abstract "becomes ___" or "the ___ of ___" pattern, check for the idiom first.
