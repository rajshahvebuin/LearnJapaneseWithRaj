JLPT N1 · Kanji · Week 7 — いろいろな覚え方をしよう (Iroiro na oboekata o shiyou)

5日目 — 1羽・1個・1軒 (Ichiwa, Ikko, Ikken)

Source
日本語総まとめ N1 漢字 (Nihongo Sou-Matome N1 Kanji) — printed p.116–117 (PDF pages 117–118; same +1 PDF-to-printed offset confirmed on Days 1–4).
Theme
Yet another memorization angle: 助数詞 (josuushi, counters/classifiers used with numbers), 26 in total, organized by level — 初級 (9), 中級 (11), 上級 (6). Each kanji is memorized through what it counts, not a reading or a shape family.
Answer key
This book's answers/explanations live entirely in a separate [別冊] 解答・解説 booklet, not included in the uploaded scan. 練習Ⅰ・Ⅱ answers below are worked out from the book's own definitions on this spread, not copied from an official key.
Notes
Japanese counters are famously irregular — the "right" counter depends entirely on the shape/category of the thing being counted (long and thin, flat, alive and small, alive and large, machine, etc.). This day is a reference table to memorize by category rather than by reading.

Q1

GU: મેં સોયા સોસ એક મોટી (1.8 લિટર) પરંપરાગત બોટલમાં ખરીદ્યો.

3 Confusion Pairs / Nuance Notes

Counters confuse learners because several share a reading (けん, はい/ぱい, かい/がい) while counting completely different categories of thing:

FormMeaningKey differenceUse when
軒ken/gen Counts houses/shops/restaurants A physical building. "Three houses down the street."
件ken Counts matters, messages, cases An abstract case or item of business. "One email," "one traffic incident."
羽wa Counts birds and rabbits A specific small-animal category (note irregular 3羽=さんば). "Four swans," "two rabbits."
匹hiki Counts small animals/fish/insects generally The default small-animal counter, excluding birds/rabbits. "Six cats," "one dog."
頭tou Counts large animals Reserved for cattle, horses, and other large livestock. "Two pandas," "cattle on a ranch."
枚mai Counts thin flat objects Paper, plates, stamps — anything flat. "Ten stamps and two postcards."
冊satsu Counts bound books/magazines A bound, multi-page item, not a loose flat sheet. "Five workbooks."
階kai Counts building floors A vertical level within a structure. "The apartment on the 1st floor."
畳jou Counts tatami-mat units of floor area A measure of horizontal room size, not height. "A four-and-a-half mat room."
Exam trap: 軒 and 件 are true homophones (both けん) that count wildly different things — a physical house vs. an abstract case/matter. When a 助数詞 question gives you けん, check whether the noun is a building (→軒) or an intangible item like an email or incident (→件), exactly as tested in 練習Ⅱ Q10 above.
